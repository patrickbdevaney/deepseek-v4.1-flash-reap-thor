# Prune Half, Quantize to 2.9, Stream Engram

**The plan the evidence supports keeps 192 of 384 routed experts per layer in DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017. It selects them with HOPE (REAP-sum diagonal, re-measured `protect_frac`, per-modality vision ranking, Super-Expert guard) on a balanced 1.5-3M-token, 8k-sequence corpus with Engram switched on. The deletion is bit-exact in MXFP4. The pipeline then folds a measured router-replay gain into the next quantizer and requantizes the survivors once, from the MXFP4 source, to about 2.85 average expert bpw. That last step uses a per-layer mix with attention at Q8_0, shared experts at Q6_K, and router and hyper-connection tensors at BF16/F32. The 189 GiB Engram tables are read from NVMe as 48 O_DIRECT rows per token.** The total is about **113 GiB against the 117 GiB envelope**. The "~2.3 bpw" premise fails on both counts. At 256 experts, 2.3 bpw does not fit: 256 experts need ≤2.24 bpw. At 192 experts it fits but costs about +15-21% perplexity in quantization alone, against +0.4-1.8% for trellis at about 3 bpw on this exact checkpoint. Decode on Thor is bandwidth-bound at roughly **11.1-11.7 GB per token with the native FP8 dense path** (about 10 tok/s at the 117 GB/s our engines achieve today). An NVFP4 dense overlay brings that to about 7.3-8.0 GB (about 15 tok/s), and closing the kernel gap to 160-200 GB/s gives 20-26 tok/s. Expert bit-width moves decode by only about 6%. No runtime serves V4.1 on sm_110 today. vLLM's FlashMLA and DeepSelect ship no sm_110 code, llama.cpp's port is an unmerged PR capped near 16K context, and ik_llama.cpp has an open NaN bug with MXFP4 experts on CUDA. The serving path is therefore our own pure-CUDA server, retargeted from the DSV4-0731 engine, with a lean llama.cpp fork as the independent reference and GGUF distribution. The claim of "best teacher agreement" is provable. It needs a teacher-forced reference from a layer-streamed forward on Thor plus a rented B200 node, and a Model Confidence Set over paired top-32 KL on a frozen split. Before any of that, our own GLM-5.3 pass3-hope checkpoint has to be fixed and measured. It shipped with a known-biased heal and attention quantization we had rejected, and it has never been evaluated end to end. So the "HOPE beats our v2 mask" premise behind the selection recipe is still untested on our own model.

## The premises that the measurements overturn

Four numbers in the brief need correcting before any byte arithmetic. **Active parameters are about 16.4B per decode token, not 13B.** The 13B figure belongs to DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731. The V4.1 paper says "8B parameters per token during prefill and 16B during decode" ([arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969v1)). The header count agrees: 6 routed experts of 3×2304×5120 over 40 layers (8.49B), the shared expert (1.42B), attention (5.13B) and the head (0.66B) ([local serving note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/DeepSeek%20V4.1%20Flash%20REAP%20pipeline%20plan/thor_serving_speed.md)). **There is no MTP head.** vLLM's recipe states "MTP was dropped in V4.1. DSpark is the only speculative method" ([vLLM recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)). The `mtp.*` tensors are a three-block DSpark drafter with its own 128-expert, top-3 pool, totalling 7.39 GiB. A backbone keep-set does not touch it. The **parameter count of about 763B is roughly right only if the drafter is counted**: one community card totals 754.6B, with 543.6B in routed experts and 196.6B in Engram ([pipenetwork](https://huggingface.co/pipenetwork/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-REAP50-MLX-mixed-4_8bit)). The count is the wrong frame anyway. **40% of the 475.24 GiB checkpoint is Engram lookup tables that never need to enter RAM.** The quantity that has to fit is 268.95 GiB of MXFP4 experts plus a 9.77 GiB floor ([1017 architecture note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/DeepSeek%20V4.1%20Flash%20REAP%20pipeline%20plan/v41_1017_architecture_runtime.md)).

The **2.3 bpw premise** is the one that changes the recipe. With a 12 GiB runtime reserve, the 9.77 GiB floor and 0.83 GiB of KV at 1M tokens, the expert budget is about 94.4-95.1 GiB. That allows a maximum average expert width of **4.48 bpw at 128 experts, 2.98 at 192, 2.56 at 224 and 2.24 at 256** (arithmetic in the [trellis note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/DeepSeek%20V4.1%20Flash%20REAP%20pipeline%20plan/trellis_lowbit_formats.md)). "256 experts at 2.3 bpw" totals 120.2 GiB and does not fit. 192 at 2.3 fits, but it spends quality the budget does not require. The best quantization ladder for this model family has FP4-source experts too: DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 against its bit-exact BF16 dequant. It reads **+7.1% PPL at 2.91 expert bpw, +15.2% at 2.48 and +21.3% at 2.25**, with the knee between 2.79 and 2.58 ([AtomicChat ladder](https://huggingface.co/AtomicChat/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731-GGUF); [eval artifacts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AtomicChat/dsv4-eval-artifacts)). On 1017 itself, an EXL3 trellis build with experts at 3 bpw (layers 18-22 at 2) costs only **+1.8% code and +0.4% prose perplexity** ([LibertAI EXL3](https://huggingface.co/LibertAIDAI/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017-EXL3-2.9bpw)). An IQ2_XXS GGUF of all 384 experts costs +19.5% with top-1 76.9% ([acrogenesis](https://huggingface.co/acrogenesis/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017-IQ2_XXS-GGUF)).

Every large MoE measured at 2.0-2.3 expert bpw lands in the visibly damaged band of +20-45%. Trellis is worth about 0.2 bpw over i-quants at equal error ([ik_llama.cpp PR 113](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/113)), which is not enough to escape that band. INT4-QAT sources tolerate it worse, not better: Kimi-K2.5 is at +43.7% at 2.26 bpw ([ubergarm](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Kimi-K2.5-GGUF)). Assuming the two costs add (an upper bound, since locally they are sub-additive), **192 at about 2.85 bpw costs about +15-23% in total**, against **+27-35% for 256 at about 2.2**. The recommended operating point is therefore 192 experts at a per-layer mix averaging 2.85-2.9 bpw. 192 at about 2.46 (IQ2_KT gate/up with IQ3_KT down) is the fallback that frees 16 GiB, which is enough to make the DSpark drafter resident.

| User premise | What the evidence says | Recommended alternative |
|---|---|---|
| ~763B total | 754.6-763B depending on whether DSpark is counted; 40% of bytes are Engram that stay on NVMe | Budget against 268.95 GiB of experts + 9.77 GiB floor |
| 13B active | ~16.4B at decode (8B at prefill, encoder-decoder) | Use B_tok ≈ 7.3-11.7 GB in speed planning |
| MTP head for spec decode | No MTP; DSpark drafter (3×128 experts, 7.39 GiB) | Drop DSpark from the first build; re-fit it later only if the verify curve justifies it |
| Trellis ~2.3 bpw | Does not fit at 256; at 192 it buys 16 GiB of slack for +8-14 pp extra PPL | 192 experts at ~2.85 avg, per-layer mixed; 2.46 only to make DSpark resident |
| GGUF Q4_K_M-class mix | Any ≥4 bpw expert format exceeds the budget at 192 (MXFP4 alone is 134.5 GiB) | Q8_0/Q6_K on the dense path only; experts at IQ3/KT-class |

## Every GLM and MiMo lesson that survived measurement goes into the recipe

Our best measured 50% result is **GLM-5.3-Flash v2**: the pass-2 mask plus a per-layer scalar heal from router replay. It scored **FP8 top-1 0.84249, ΔNLL 0.1756 and top-32 KL 0.650** on 241,516 paired held-out tokens ([healing ablation](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research/HEALING_ABLATION.md)). Each of its components was established by a paired test, not by a proxy. The v2 mask was worth +0.00545 top-1 (McNemar z=9.3). Reverting per-expert healing to the scalar was worth +0.00556 (z=11.8). The per-expert heal had *lowered* held-out reconstruction residual in 41 of 42 layers while making agreement worse. Its coefficients reduce to gate-before/gate-after and suppress the promoted substitute experts. Expert outputs are near-orthogonal (‖μ‖²/E‖f‖² = 0.034), so the 0.27 residual is deleted information, not a misscaling ([healing wiki](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/70-healing.md)). The heal gain itself has to be **measured by replaying post-prune routing**: 0.9111 measured against 0.7149 from the first-moment derivation, a 30.8% disagreement that converged on a single chunk. Leaving attention out of NVFP4 was also measured. The KDA q/k/v arm cost −0.00230 top-1 (5.8σ), the o_proj+MLA arm −0.00551, and the "all" arm −0.00673 ([log](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/00-log.md)). An independent group then scored our published REAP-50 mask at **KL 0.393 and top-1 80.4% against FP8**, with the unpruned model at the same Q2/Q3 quant at KL 0.117 ([lte-ai Strata](https://huggingface.co/lte-ai/GLM-5.3-Flash-Strata-Q2Q3-Experts)). That confirms the local finding that **pruning is about 14× more damaging per byte than quantization** ([criterion audit](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md)). It is also why V4.1 should not prune past the point where quantization can carry the rest.

The proxies that predicted quality are narrow. Per-domain saliency retention tracked per-domain top-1 damage at Pearson r=0.942. Reconstruction residual had the right sign for masks but the wrong sign for weight-space corrections. A per-tensor cosine of 0.9972 coexisted with a 9.9% top-1 flip rate. Saliency-mass "wins" are circular, because a candidate selector maximises that ruler by construction ([evaluation wiki](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/97-evaluation.md); [internal playbook](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/DeepSeek%20V4.1%20Flash%20REAP%20pipeline%20plan/internal_learnings_playbook.md)). The calibration lessons were equally concrete. Pass 2 specified its mixture in samples, which left code at 5.7% of tokens against an intended 21% and finance at 0.5% against 8%, so pass 3 moved targets into token space. `MAX_LEN=2048` discarded 73.5% of the collected tokens. The worst-retained domain at every `protect_frac` was general text, and ballast top-1 bottomed at 0.579. Token count matters more than sequence length: keep-sets at S=8192 and S=2048 agree at 0.9745, while the same S on disjoint 131k-token samples agrees at only 0.838 ([seqlen ladder](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/93-seqlen-ladder-results.md)).

MiMo-V2.6-Flash is our precedent for pruning **MXFP4-source experts**, which is exactly V4.1's situation. Deletion was bit-exact and was the only lever available. `protect_frac` has a model-specific knee: 0.08 on MiMo, where it bought 53% of the worst-domain residual loss for +1.15% pᵀFp, against 0.04 on GLM. **pf=0.01 was worse than pf=0, so the curve is not monotone** and must be swept rather than inherited ([MiMo override](/home/patrickd/xiaomi-2.6-flash-REAP/conf/protect_frac_override.txt)). Layer-local router KD beat the sliced teacher router on 0 of 47 layers, because only 1.13% of routed slots hit a pruned expert. The correct noaux_tc formula is that the bias selects and the unbiased score weights. The naive version agreed on 1 expert in 8, and the bias has zero gradient ([noaux note](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/noaux-tc-router-formula.md)). MiMo's searched non-uniform allocation gained +0.0115 worst-domain but was not applied, so that the checkpoint stays loadable. **MiMo REAP-50 was published but never served or evaluated**, so "HOPE + protect_frac on MXFP4 at 50%" has no end-to-end quality number from us.

The October 2026 community evidence adds four items. First, on GLM-5.3-Flash at 144/288, a **REAP-sum mask beats REAP-mean and frequency on held-out KL (0.73 vs 0.85 vs 0.94)**, and that ranking matched agentic outcomes ([lapadatbogdan](https://huggingface.co/lapadatbogdan/GLM-5.3-Flash-REAP144-Coder-GGUF)). The same author found three failures. KL mask search with RCO, which did not re-route removed experts, reached KL 1.52, worse than frequency. LoRA top-20 logit distillation diverged in about 16 steps. Python-only calibration broke general arithmetic ([repo](https://github.com/lapadatbogdan/GLM-5.3-Flash-REAP144-Coder)). Second, HOPE (arXiv 2609.18916) is still the strongest published one-shot criterion at 40-50%. MoRA (2610.00367) adds cheap per-channel affine proxies for removed experts but was never compared with REAP or HOPE ([MoRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00367)). Third, `peasantsmith/GLM-5.3-Flash-Maya-GGUF` is **a quantization recipe only**: all 288 experts kept, no pruning ([Maya card](https://huggingface.co/peasantsmith/GLM-5.3-Flash-Maya-GGUF)). The transferable part is per-expert imatrix plus GPTQ-style error-feedback rounding of gate/up. The author reports about a quarter lower expert output error at equal size, unverified. Maya-S (about 2.4 bpw) scores top-1 83.3% and KL 0.428, Maya-M (about 2.9 bpw) 86.2% and 0.329. Fourth, lte-ai found that **requantizing from a 2-bit intermediate nearly doubled KL** (0.117 to 0.212). That is the rule to quantize once, from the source. Finally, the only public V4.1 50% baseline is weak: pipenetwork's wikitext-calibrated REAP-mean at 192/384 has PPL ×1.168, worse on 138 of 140 windows ([pipenetwork REAP50](https://huggingface.co/pipenetwork/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-REAP50-MLX-mixed-4_8bit)). LibertAI's 0909 curve reads +5.49% at 256 and **+14.66% at 192** on a 262k-token corpus ([LibertAI REAP-256E](https://huggingface.co/LibertAIDAI/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-REAP-256E)). As of 2026-10-19, no expert-pruned 1017 exists.

## The pipeline from unpruned teacher to three published artifacts

The binding constraint is **disk, not RAM or compute**. The repo is 510.3 GB in 48 shards. Shards 47 and 48 (101.5 GB each) hold one Engram table each, and shards 3-42 are about 7.39 GB, roughly one decoder layer each. The NVMe has **102 GB free today** ([HF tree](https://huggingface.co/api/models/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017/tree/main); measured `df`). **All 48 shards changed between 0909 and 1017, including both Engram shards**, while config, code, index and tokenizer are byte-identical. Any saliency, keep-set or Engram table built from 0909 is invalid, and that includes LibertAI's pointer to the 0909 shards 47/48. Every derivative must pin the 1017 shards by sha256 ([1017 architecture note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/DeepSeek%20V4.1%20Flash%20REAP%20pipeline%20plan/v41_1017_architecture_runtime.md)). The 1017 repo also dropped `chat_template.jinja`, so serving needs `encoding/encoding.py` or the identical 0909 template.

**Stage 0: memory conservation and preparation.** Every guard proven on GLM and MiMo carries over unchanged. They are: one layer or block per process, with `drop_caches 3` verified to return to baseline (in-process `empty_cache` returned 0.03 of 13.54 GiB) ([OOM root cause](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/96-the-real-oom-cause.md)); unconditional reclaim whenever a unit costs more than the floor; a memguard that kills only resumable named stages and treats a reclaim no-op as a failure; a `(chunk, block)` ledger against resume double-counting; and teacher routing and teacher evaluation captured before surgery deletes the source. Two operating rules also apply: never co-run anything beside a vLLM server, which hard-rebooted the box and cost 12 hours ([OOM memory](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/thor-unified-memory-oom.md)), and run every multi-hour stage under systemd or setsid. Calibration must use the checkpoint's own `inference/model.py`, because there is no `transformers` modeling file. The REAP hook belongs after `hc_pre`. Cross-layer state comes from the KV-source layers 2/8/14/20 and must be carried. The dense indexer score tensor is 4 GiB per row at 8k, so it has to be evaluated row-chunked. Done naively the peak is 148-167 GiB; row-chunked it is **28 GiB with MXFP4 resident or 47 GiB fully dequantized** ([e2e engineering note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/DeepSeek%20V4.1%20Flash%20REAP%20pipeline%20plan/e2e_pipeline_engineering.md)).

**Stage 1: run the teacher reference before anything is deleted.** Three teacher sources complement each other ([teacher-agreement note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/DeepSeek%20V4.1%20Flash%20REAP%20pipeline%20plan/teacher_agreement_proof.md)). Source A is the gold reference. It is the layer-streamed, teacher-forced forward on Thor in the checkpoint's own MXFP4/FP8 numerics, and it yields full logits, top-6 routing per layer and hidden-state taps. It is estimated at about 1 hour per 2M tokens, at ≤8k context, and it rides along with the saliency pass. Storage is about 140 B per token for top-32 logits and 480 B for routing. Source B is a rented 2×B200 or 4×H200 vLLM node returning `prompt_logprobs`. At about $50-100 per 10M tokens (extrapolated) it is the only source for 32k-128k references and for the capability panel's teacher scores. Source C is the official DeepSeek API, which serves 1017 with top-20 logprobs on generated tokens only, at about $0.60 per 1M output tokens off-peak ([DeepSeek pricing](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing)). It is useful only for free-running teacher trajectories, and only after an equality check against A or B, because hosted endpoints often deviate from reference weights: 11 of 31 Llama endpoints did ([arXiv 2410.20247](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20247v2)).

**Stage 2: layer-major saliency.** This is the opposite of GLM's chunk-major order, because the backbone is three times the free disk. Each step downloads one layer shard (the full pass takes about 49 minutes at the measured 105 MB/s) and runs it over the whole corpus with hidden states resident. At 40,960 B per token of 4-stream bf16, **about 1.5M tokens is the in-RAM ceiling**. 2M or more needs about 82 GB of NVMe paging, roughly 1.9 hours. Engram rows for the calibration tokens are a pure function of token ids, about 6.3 KB per token per Engram layer. They are precomputed off-box or on a second drive, and the gate·value term is applied before blocks 1 and 14. Engram must never be switched off: without it, perplexity goes from 1.91 to 4.16 on code and from 6.45 to 9.06 on prose ([LibertAI EXL3](https://huggingface.co/LibertAIDAI/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017-EXL3-2.9bpw)).

The same pass banks every statistic the later stages need, so no second 475 GiB forward is ever required. Those statistics are: REAP-sum and the sum/sq/norm/count accumulators per domain bucket; the 384² HOPE F matrix per layer (576 KiB per layer); the router cache for heal replay; a **per-expert max down_proj output for the Super-Expert guard**, which neither existing saliency script records; per-expert input amax for NVFP4 activation scales; per-column Σx² for the GGUF imatrix; and teacher top-k logits and routing. The router hook records `scores.gather(indices)` after renormalization and the ×1.5, and it uses `bias_vl` on image spans. The corpus should be token-space balanced, along the lines of general 20 / agentic 15 / code 15 / vision 14 / math 12 / science 12 / finance 10, with every pool at least 10%. It should use 8k sequences, interleaved domain order and probed source yields. Extrapolated cost is **3-4 hours per 1M tokens**, which may be 2-3× slower if the reference TileLang `fp4_gemm` will not build for sm_110a.

**Stage 3: selection and arbitration.** Rank with HOPE pᵀFp using the REAP-sum diagonal and renormalized top-k weights. Sweep `protect_frac` and take the measured knee. Rank vision per modality against `bias_vl`, because text-only ranking was **worse than random for vision** at 272 experts, and plain mean saliency at 256 gave +27.4% caption perplexity against +5.5% for text. Never prune the shared expert. Keep the allocation uniform at 192: that is a legal vLLM fused-router count, and it is the only count both GGUF and the CUDA server express as one scalar. Then build three to five candidate masks: HOPE at the knee, REAP-sum, routing mass, and random and frequency anchors. **Arbitrate them by held-out top-32 KL with proper re-routing over the survivors**, evaluated in memory without writing a checkpoint. Do not arbitrate on saliency mass.

**Stage 4: bit-exact surgery and the healing decision.** Surgery runs as a stream: download a shard, copy the kept experts' packed E2M1 and E8M0 tensors, slice `gate.weight`, `gate.bias` and `gate.bias_vl`, write, upload, delete. Then set `n_routed_experts` to 192, regenerate the index and verify kept indices against the source ([LibertAI pipeline](https://github.com/Libertai/deepseek-v41-flash-reap)). Healing is where the MXFP4 source changes the GLM recipe. GLM's heal multiplied F32 FP8 block scales. **E8M0 scales can only shift by powers of two, so a 0.91 gain cannot be applied losslessly to the MXFP4 artifact.**

My inference is that the gain does not need to live there. The measured per-layer gain can be folded **exactly into the NVFP4 artifact's per-tensor FP32 global scale**. For the GGUF and CUDA artifacts it can be **multiplied into the dequantized weights before the one requantization** that has to happen anyway, at zero extra cost. The MXFP4 source artifact then ships unhealed, as bit-exact deletion, and documents this. Router-bias refit on the pruned support, MoRA affine proxies, and block-wise frozen-expert LoRA (estimated at 1-2 hours per 2M tokens and about 75 GB resident, with no existing software) each enter only through a paired s09 gate. Community LoRA KD on the same model family diverged, and layer-local router KD was a measured no-op on MiMo.

**Stage 5: quantization, last and once.** NVFP4 is a deterministic, lossless repack of MXFP4: "all 16,986,931,200 weight blocks passed lossless conversion" ([nvidia NVFP4](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4)). It runs tensor by tensor in our s07 streamer, with input scales from the banked amax. The GGUF expert requant comes straight from the MXFP4 source, not from any intermediate. It uses our own streamed imatrix written in the legacy `.dat` layout and checked by `imatrix_verify.py`, and GPTQ-style error-feedback rounding of each expert against its own input Hessian, the Maya technique. The dense path stays protected: attention, compressor and indexer at Q8_0; shared experts, token embedding and output at Q6_K; router at BF16; and all hyper-connection, norm and sink tensors at F32. In BF16 the hyper-connection projections drift by 1.16e-2 against 1.43e-6 in F32 ([llama.cpp PR #28696](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28696)). A naive Q2_K that drags these tensors down collapses V4-Flash to PPL 9.11 and KLD 0.97 ([dsv4-eval-artifacts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AtomicChat/dsv4-eval-artifacts)).

Routed experts get a per-layer mix: about 35 layers near 3 bpw and the 4-6 least salient at about 2. This is LibertAI's EXL3 shape, with protected layers chosen from our saliency bank: layers 0-1, the Engram layers 1 and 14, the KV-source layers 2, 8, 14 and 20, and the DSpark taps 37-39. Keeping down_proj bit-exact MXFP4 and requantizing only gate/up loses at matched bytes (KLD 0.222 at 120.3 GB against 0.218 at 113.9 GB), so requantize all three projections.

## Speed is set by the dense path and kernel efficiency, not by expert bits

At 192 kept experts, pruning does not change the active bytes, because top-6 still reads six experts. Per-token reads are 8.49B routed, 1.42B shared, 5.13B attention, indexer and compressor, 0.66B of lm_head, and about 0.3B of `engram.wkv`. That last one is read every token and easy to forget. **Moving routed experts from 2.3 to 2.9 bpw changes B_tok by only 0.64 GB, about 6%**, whereas NVFP4 on the FP8 dense path removes about 3.7 GB ([serving note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/DeepSeek%20V4.1%20Flash%20REAP%20pipeline%20plan/thor_serving_speed.md)). Expert bits decide fit and quality. The dense path and kernel efficiency decide speed.

| Effective bandwidth | Native FP8 dense, 2.9-bpw experts (11.7 GB) | NVFP4 dense overlay, 2.9-bpw experts (8.0 GB) | Regime |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 117 GB/s | 10.0 tok/s | 14.6 tok/s | Both local engines today |
| 160 GB/s | 13.7 | 20.0 | After MoE/HC/glue work |
| 200 GB/s | 17.1 | 25.0 | ds4-on-GB10 class ceiling |

The 117 GB/s anchor is measured on the closest architecture we have. Our pure-CUDA DSV4-Flash-0731 REAP engine has MLA, the DSA indexer, the compressor, HC/Sinkhorn and MXFP4 MoE, and it runs at **9.51 tok/s with a real engine B_tok of 12.26 GB on this Thor**. Its FP8 projections stream at 89-94% of achievable, but its MoE GEMV ran at 38% until an occupancy fix ([0731 server memory](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/dsv4-0731-cuda-server.md)). On GLM, NVFP4-ing the dense path bought only 1.4% until each block reused the activation vector across R=5 rows. At 2.3-2.9 bpw an expert weight byte drives 11-14 B of activation traffic, so the sub-3-bit expert GEMV needs **R≥8 rows of x reuse** to stay near streaming ([activation-traffic finding](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-activation-traffic-finding.md)). The NVFP4 overlay on V4.1 is a double quantization of FP8 block-scaled sources, unlike GLM's BF16-source overlay (+1.56% PPL, 0.138% confident flips). It ships only behind its own flip and PPL gate.

Format choice for the expert kernel is an open engineering question. Hardware FP4 unpack is the only path with a proven ≥80%-of-streaming batch-1 kernel on Thor. Our warp-per-row NVFP4 MoE GEMV went from 13% to 82% of achievable. But FP4 does not fit at 192. Trellis types (IQ*_KT, EXL3) have the best quality per bit and the most ALU work per weight. IQ2_KT ran 7-10% slower than IQ2_XXS on an RTX 4080 ([PR 113](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/113)). Low-bit dequant on GB10 is compute-limited: a recent MMVQ rewrite gave 1.15× on iq2_s there and nothing on discrete cards ([llama.cpp PR #30517](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/30517)). Because routed experts are only 22-39% of B_tok, even a 30% slower expert kernel costs just 7-12% end to end. So the quality advantage of trellis is worth keeping if a microbenchmark shows it holding ≥70-75% of streaming. Otherwise use codebook IQ3_XXS/IQ2_XXS-class grids with in-register LUTs. The IQ1_S/IQ2_S/IQ3_S Blackwell packed-load bug did not reproduce on Thor under CUDA 13.2 ([PR #27902](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27902)), but `test-backend-ops` must confirm every type used.

Engram on NVMe costs almost nothing at decode. 48 random O_DIRECT reads took **1.13 ms p50 and 2.22 ms p99** on this Thor ([Engram offload note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/engram_off_device_thor.md)). The hashes depend only on token ids, so reads are issued at sampling time. The layer-14 fetch then has 13 layers of slack, and the cost hides behind layer 0. Pinned-host or UVA offload frees zero bytes on unified memory: a 4 GiB pinned allocation cost 4.09 GiB of GPU-visible memory ([josephdrose](https://github.com/josephdrose/joe-spark-patches/blob/main/dsv41/docs/fit-and-engram.md)). mmap pulls 64 KiB of page cache per touch. So both vLLM's `cpu_offload` and the llama.cpp ports' CPU-mmap tables are wrong for Thor. In the CUDA server, Engram is an io_uring row store into a `cudaHostRegister` arena that the lookup kernel reads directly, signalled through `cuStreamWaitValue32` so the decode graph stays capturable. In llama.cpp it becomes a graph input filled from the same row store. Prefill is where Engram binds. It needs 48 reads per prompt token, so at the measured 62-95k IOPS **Engram caps prefill at about 1,300-2,000 tok/s**. That is above any plausible Thor prefill rate, but only if the next chunk's rows are fetched while the current chunk computes.

Prefill is the weak side. V4.1's causal encoder-decoder runs only the 20 encoder layers over the prompt (8B active per token) plus a 128-token decoder replay. The extrapolated band is **200-450 tok/s for llama.cpp-class kernels**, so a 128K prompt takes 5-11 minutes. **300-1,000 tok/s** is plausible for a CUDA server with a W4A16 tcgen05 grouped GEMM: BF16 tcgen05 compiles for sm_110a, block-scaled FP4 tcgen05 does not ([flashinfer PR #5882](https://github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer/pull/5882)). Our engines today prefill at about 21 tok/s because they run per-token loops, so prefill kernels are a separate, explicitly scheduled build item. KV is irrelevant to fit, at 890 B per token, which is 0.83 GiB at 1M tokens ([arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969v1)).

DSpark is a late lever with a low ceiling. Native acceptance on Spark is about 3.6 tokens per step, but **acceptance fell from 53% to 12.4% on an EXL3-requantized body** ([tonyd2wild](https://github.com/tonyd2wild/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-vLLM-DGX-Spark)). Our 0731 engine, using the same drafter machinery, reached only 1.00× with verify at 80-83% of the step. That was traced to cold-weight coalescing in the M≥2 tile, and the fix is unbuilt. A width-6 verify touches about 33 distinct experts per layer out of 192. The expected gain is **1.0-1.3×, with a ceiling around 1.5×**, and only after the cold M=2..6 path is fixed and the drafter is re-fit to the pruned, requantized body. It also does not fit at 2.85 bpw unless the experts drop to about 2.46.

## A runtime nobody has booted is the real risk

The showstoppers are engineering, not modelling, and they should be stated plainly.

**Disk** comes first. 102 GB free cannot stage one Engram shard with margin, cannot hold the 163 GB MXFP4 output, and cannot hold the 97-189 GiB Engram row file that serving requires on local NVMe. The only attachment points are USB 3.2 Gen2 and the Key-E Gen5 x1 slot. Engram latency over either is unmeasured.

**sm_110 attention kernels** come second. In the local vLLM 0.32.0rc1 image, `_flashmla_C` and `_flashmla_extension_C` carry only sm_90a/sm_100 cubins, `_deepselect_C` only sm_100, and none embeds PTX. The DSv4.1 selector falls through to FlashMLA on compute capability 11. **vLLM cannot run V4.1 attention on Thor as shipped.** The router and MoE ops do carry sm_110 cubins.

**Runtime support** comes third. llama.cpp PR #28696 is still open. It has no two-level candidate mask (context is effectively capped near 16K), reuse layers attend to all compressed rows, and there is a CUDA MMQ out-of-bounds read ([PR #28696](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28696)). ik_llama.cpp merged `deepseek41` but has open issue #2519: NaN logits and illegal memory access with MXFP4 routed experts on CUDA sm_12x. Its trellis types have no Thor report ([ik_llama #2519](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/issues/2519)). ExLlamaV3 has no V4.1 model class. SGLang support is nightly-only, with no Thor build. **No source anywhere reports V4.1 running on sm_110.**

Below these sit the second-tier risks. The reference TileLang 0.1.12 kernels may not build for sm_110a, which is the calibration forward's dependency. No sub-3-bit expert GEMV exists for Thor. E8M0 cannot carry a non-power-of-two heal. vLLM accepts only 128/192/256/320 kept experts. The 1017 Engram tables must be pinned, because mixing them with 0909 loads silently and is wrong.

The serving recommendation follows from that list. **The primary path is the pure-CUDA server, retargeted from the 0731 engine.** That engine is 7,870 lines with bit-exact CUDA-graph decode and byte-exact DSML encoding. The port needs five additions: the Engram row store and gate kernel; the CSA2 roles (KV-source {2,8,14,20}, index-source {2,…,36}, reuse layers, and the 2,048×8 candidate pool needed past 16K); the encoder-to-decoder KV projection; the sqrtsoftplus router with `bias_vl` and ×1.5; and the sub-3-bit expert GEMV. It owns its attention kernels, so FlashMLA's missing sm_110 cubins do not matter to it. The expected result is about 10 tok/s on day one, about 15 with the gated overlay, and 20+ with kernel work. **A lean llama.cpp fork on PR #28696 is the secondary arm.** Its patches are F32 HC, the reuse-layer top-k fix, Engram as an O_DIRECT graph input, and a REAP-aware converter. Its value is as an independent correctness and quality reference and as the GGUF distribution format, at an expected 7-11 tok/s. It is not the fastest engine. On a single Spark, mature GGUF engines already beat our CUDA engine's effective bandwidth on the V4 family. Closing 117 to 160-200 GB/s is therefore the top CUDA-server lever, ahead of DSpark.

## Proving best agreement takes a frozen split and a confidence set

The claim has to rest on output agreement, scored identically for every candidate. Our regime is the lossy one: GLM REAP-50 sits at top-32 KL of about 0.65, 10-25× above the "silent zone" where fidelity metrics stop ranking quantizations. In that regime, KL and top-1 agreement correlate with benchmark scores at |ρ| 0.70-0.86 ([arXiv 2606.19558](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19558)). Flips track KL at Spearman 0.981 ([arXiv 2407.09141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09141)).

The **primary metric is paired mean KL(teacher‖student) on the teacher's top-32 support**, per token, with document-bootstrap intervals. The per-token Gaussian SE is not used, because dNLL's mean is about 160× its median. Secondary metrics are McNemar-paired top-1 agreement, the confident-flip rate where teacher p_max ≥ 0.9, p99 KL, and expected greedy accept length on teacher trajectories. Perplexity is reported only for comparability.

Our measured paired discordance of 5.4-8.3% gives a minimum detectable effect of 0.13-0.16% top-1 at 241k tokens. 0.25% resolution needs 68-104k tokens before document-cluster inflation. Ranking K=10 finalists with family-wise control multiplies that by about 1.85. **A 2-5M-token Source-A reference, one evening of streaming, covers per-domain claims at about 0.25%.** One cheap, high-value refinement: a reconstruction gain only reaches tokens routed through changed experts, so stratify the paired test to affected tokens using the banked routing. For small affected fractions f, this raises power by roughly 1/f.

Fairness takes five rules. First, score every competitor against the same cached 1017 teacher on the same token ids with no BOS or template drift. Second, run each competitor in its intended runtime and export top-k logprobs into one format. Third, bound cross-runtime numeric noise by running one model in two runtimes. Fourth, define "unified-RAM-viable" as **measured peak resident memory ≤117 GiB at a stated context with Engram on NVMe**, not file size. Fifth, populate the frontier with three random-mask seeds, a frequency mask, plain REAP, HOPE, every public 1017 compression that fits, and the lowest-bit unpruned quant that fits, if one exists. Label 0909 and 0731 derivatives as different-base.

Tune on a screening split. Freeze the finalists, hash the confirmation split, and only then compute a **90% Model Confidence Set** over per-document paired KL ([Hansen et al.](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2011/03/01/model-confidence-set)). "Best" can be claimed only if the set holds one model. Capability then comes as **non-inferiority**, not superiority, on a panel teacher-scored once on the rented node. The panel is BFCL v3 multi_turn_base (all 200 items; it is the column that collapses first under REAP, with Kimi-K2 going from 0.355 to 0.164 at 50%), PopQA (1,000), log-prob MMLU-Pro (2,000), EvalPlus, MATH-500, RULER multi-key/multi-value at 32k, and a 2,000-prompt free-running loop screen ([REAP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13999)). Generation config, reasoning effort and seqmax are pinned, and served identity is asserted in every result.

## Our published pass3-hope checkpoint has three defects that change the recipe

This section is separate because it concerns an artifact we already shipped, and because each defect rewrites a step above.

**Defect 1: v3 was healed with the first-moment derivation known to over-correct by about 30%, not the measured router-replay gain.** `heal_refit.json` had been measured on the pass-2 keep-set `9d2ed9e33db85f90`, while v3 applies `cfab1fb7c846ad41`. The keep-set guard raised `RuntimeError` correctly, but it sits inside a `try/except Exception` that downgraded the error to a WARN and fell back to the first-moment gain. The fallback has median 0.7664 and range 0.6673-0.8533, applied to 6,048 tensors with the layer scalar and to **0 tensors per-expert** ([s05 log](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/logs/s05_heal.log); [s05 artifact](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/artifacts/s05_heal.json)). The README describes v3's heal as "per-expert", which the log contradicts. If v3's true replay gain lands near pass 2's 0.91, the shipped MoE output is under-scaled by about 0.766/0.91 ≈ **0.84×** (inference, unverified).

**Defect 2: v3 has never been evaluated end to end.** `s09_eval` stayed pending in the pipeline log. The only `s09_eval.json` is dated 2026-08-29 and names a pass-2 arm ([pipeline log](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/logs/pipeline.log); [s09 artifact](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/artifacts/s09_eval.json)). **Nothing we have measures whether HOPE plus `protect_frac` 0.04 beats the v2 mask.** That is the very selection method this plan carries to V4.1.

**Defect 3: the v3 NVFP4 was built with `nvfp4_attn_mode="kda"`.** That mode quantizes the KDA q/k/v projections, an arm we had measured at −0.00230 top-1 (5.8σ) and declined for v2 ([state.db / s07 artifact](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/artifacts/s07_quantize.json); [log](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/00-log.md)). The published 93.6 GiB v3 NVFP4 therefore carries an attention cost that the 98.2 GiB v2 NVFP4 deliberately avoided.

The recipe changes follow directly. Every keep-set fingerprint mismatch becomes **fatal, never a WARN**, and a fallback that is "known to over-correct" is deleted from the code path, not merely logged. `heal_refit` is re-run on the final keep-set immediately before any gain is applied. On V4.1 that gain goes into the NVFP4 global scales and the requant inputs, as described above. No artifact ships without one paired s09 arm against the cached teacher. Attention precision is a recorded, gated decision per artifact, not a stage default. For V4.1 that means attention and indexer stay at Q8_0/FP8 unless the overlay passes its own flip gate. The card's numbers are filled from artifacts, never from literals. The GLM v3 repair is also the cheapest experiment available that tests the V4.1 selection method. Re-run `heal_refit` on keep-set `cfab1fb7`, re-heal the FP8 scales (invertible on F32 block scales), and score v3-FP8 against v2-FP8 on the cached 241,516-token teacher with McNemar. That answers whether HOPE plus `protect_frac` earns its place before 475 GiB of V4.1 is ever downloaded.

Engineering follows the gates. It starts with streamed surgery and upload of the MXFP4 master, then the NVFP4 repack with folded gains, then the CUDA-server port, whose first milestone is a golden-token match and per-layer cosine against `inference/model.py`. Next come the GGUF requant with error-feedback rounding and a matched-byte A/B of trellis against i-quant mixes, then the overlay gate, and DSpark last.

## Conclusion

The brief treats this as a compression problem, but it is closer to an exactness problem. Every step that is bit-exact is free: expert deletion, the MXFP4-to-NVFP4 repack, folding the heal gain into a scale or a quantizer input, and moving Engram onto NVMe. Every step that is not exact has bitten us. A guard downgraded to a WARN shipped a 0.84× under-scaled model. A per-expert heal that improved its proxy made agreement worse. Attention quantization that looked harmless cost 5.8σ. So the plan spends its single lossy operation, one requantization from the source, where the curve is flattest, at about 2.85 bpw, and refuses the 2.3-bpw point, which buys bytes the budget does not need at three times the quantization damage.

What remains uncertain is narrower than it looks. Selection quality hinges on a GLM measurement we can make this week. Speed hinges on two microbenchmarks, the one-layer sm_110 forward and the sub-3-bit GEMV, not on any modelling question. "Best teacher agreement" is a claim we can actually win, because the only public V4.1 50% baselines are a wikitext REAP-mean and a 262k-token PPL-only prune, and neither has ever been scored against the teacher.
