# Router-side post-prune adjustments (no expert training): rules, formulas, measured effects

Research date: 2026-10-17. Scope: every router-side lever that can be applied after MoE expert pruning without touching expert weights, assessed as cheap/free steps for each stage of a progressive REAP/HOPE pipeline on a 763B-1T noaux_tc model (GLM-5.3 lineage: sigmoid scores, `e_score_correction_bias` SELECTS, unbiased sigmoid WEIGHTS, `norm_topk_prob`, `routed_scaling_factor`). Context from our own memory notes is cited as "[local memory]". **[extrapolation]** marks anything not measured in a source. Sibling note `exfold_routing_recovery.md` covers the ExFold fold itself in depth; here only its interaction with renormalization is treated.

Notation used throughout: for token x, router pre-activations z = W_r x; softmax routers p = softmax(z); sigmoid routers s = sigmoid(z); T_k(x) = selected top-k set; m_k(x) = sum of scores over T_k(x) ("reference mass"); w_i = per-expert gating weight actually multiplied into the expert output.

## KQ1. arXiv 2609.04575: which renormalization rules were compared, what "k2" is, the 4.3-point MMLU swing, and transfer to sigmoid/noaux_tc routers

### Takeaway
The paper ("Training-Free Halving of Activated Experts in Fine-Grained MoE", Chen & Yao, Sep 2026) decouples the number of activated experts k1 from the reference mass used to normalize them, k2: w_i = p_i / sum_{j in T_k2} p_j. On Qwen3.6-35B-A3B going from k=8 to k1=4, standard renormalization (k2=4) costs 4.65 MMLU points while k2=16 costs 0.35 (not significant) — a 4.3-point swing from the normalization rule alone, with identical experts running. The paper is softmax-only; nothing in it tests sigmoid/noaux_tc routers, and it studies reducing k, not deleting experts from the pool, so transfer to pool pruning is an inference (made below), not a result.

### Cited Findings
- Formula: softmax probabilities p over all E experts; activate top-k1; weights w_i = p_i / sum_{j in T_k2} p_j for i in T_k1, with k2 >= k1 so T_k1 is a subset of T_k2. Special cases: k2=k1 is standard renormalization; k2=k_train anchors the gain to what training calibrated; k2=E is "no renormalization" (weights sum to m_k1 instead of 1); k2>k gives a per-token gain below the trained value. The weights sum to m_k1(x)/m_k2(x) < 1, an "input-adaptive down-scaling". One integer, no parameters, no training, no measurable compute. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (trained k=8, E=256), MMLU 5-shot n=2000, paired McNemar vs native 81.65%: (k1=6,k2=6) 79.05 (−2.60, p=9e-7); (6,8) 80.55 (−1.10, p=0.021); (6,16) 82.40 (+0.75, p=0.27); (6,256) 54.20 (−27.45); (4,4) 77.00 (−4.65, p=1.7e-9); (4,8) 78.55 (−3.10); (4,16) 81.30 (−0.35, p=0.66). — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (trained k=10, E=512), MMLU n=2000, native 89.45%: (8,8) 89.60 (+0.15, p=0.78); (8,10) 89.00 (−0.45); (8,20) 88.45 (−1.00, p=0.024); (5,5) 87.35 (−2.10, p=1e-4); (5,10) 88.90 (−0.55, p=0.24); (5,20) 87.75 (−1.70); (5,512) 70.90 (−18.55). The optimal k2 is model-dependent: 16 (2x k_train) on the 35B, 10 (= k_train) on the 397B. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- GSM8K 0-shot n=500: 35B (4,4) 88.40 (−6.60, 10.4% truncation) vs (4,16) 94.40 (−0.60, 1.4% truncation); 397B (5,5) 92.40 (−4.20, 7.0% truncation) vs (5,10) 95.80 (−0.80, 1.6% truncation). The wrong normalization rule roughly triples the rate of runaway/truncated generations. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Perplexity disagrees with downstream: on the 35B, WikiText perplexity is minimized at k2=8 (−1.61% vs native) but MMLU peaks at k2=16; the perplexity-optimal setting is significantly worse on MMLU (−1.10, p=0.021). Perplexity is U-shaped and MMLU inverted-U in k2. The authors state that compression settings should not be chosen on unlabeled text alone. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Why no-renormalization is catastrophic: the top-8 of 256 hold only 0.182 of softmax mass on WikiText (0.170 code), top-16 hold 0.262; renormalization therefore applies a ~5.5x gain to the expert branch, and "the gain, not the weights, is what breaks" when the reference set is wrong. On Gemma-4-26B-A4B the top-8 mass is 0.302 (gain 3.31x) and removing renormalization at k1=6 raises WikiText ppl only +0.7% (9.158 -> 9.224) vs +356% on Qwen. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Gemma-4-26B-A4B variant (global scalar gain, w_i = alpha * p_i / m_k1(x), instruction-tuned, MMLU n=1000, baseline 80.40): k1=6 alpha=1.00 78.10 (−2.30, p=0.0018); 0.90 78.60; 0.85 78.60; 0.80 79.10 (−1.30, p=0.079, the only non-significant one); 0.75 78.80. Authors call this preliminary/directional. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Expert identity dominates weighting: at fixed 8 experts, uniform weights cost +49.7% ppl, sampling +310%, random selection +1667%. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Routers covered: softmax only; the paper notes GLM-style configs apply a fixed `routed_scaling_factor` after renormalization but does not test sigmoid gating or `norm_topk_prob` variants. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- The paper recommends against pool pruning for Qwen3.6-35B: median 0 dead experts/layer; keeping the top 50% of experts retains 88.8% (WikiText)/81.3% (code) of routing mass, worst layer 64.2%; code-derived top-128 keeps 81.3% of code mass but WikiText-derived top-128 keeps only 38.0%; union of the two domains' top-128 covers 198/256. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Independent corroboration that renormalizing to 1 over survivors inflates the branch: ZEDA (Qwen3-30B-A3B, SFT-only variant, Table 6) scores 73.3 average without renormalization vs 71.6 with it; the authors' explanation is that the original top-K weights are calibrated in pre-training and renormalizing the active normal experts' weights to sum to one "artificially amplifies them, which inflates the effective scale of the MoE residual branch". (Setting: zero-expert injection with fixed top-K, not pool pruning.) — [arXiv 2605.18643](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18643)

### Inferences
- **Mapping k-reduction to pool pruning.** Deleting expert j from a softmax router rescales every surviving probability by 1/(1−p_j(x)); if the token's top-k set is unchanged, the renormalized top-k weights w_i = p_i / m_k are exactly invariant (REAP's Eq. 2, g_k/(1−g_j), is this). The damage mechanism in 2609.04575 therefore only bites for pool pruning on tokens whose top-k set changes (a deleted expert is replaced by a promoted, lower-score expert): the new m_k is smaller, the gain 1/m_k larger, and the survivors are over-amplified relative to training. That is the same mechanism ZEDA measured (−1.7 avg from renormalizing) and the same mechanism the k2 rule corrects. **[extrapolation]**
- **Sigmoid routers differ in a way that helps.** For sigmoid scoring (GLM-5.3, DeepSeek-V3/V4, Kimi-K2) the surviving scores s_i are per-expert and unchanged by deletion; only T_k and m_k change. The k2 analogue is w_i = s_i / sum_{j in T_k2} s_j (then * routed_scaling_factor). For GLM-5.3 a "k2 = original top-k over the unpruned pool" rule means: normalize by the mass the ORIGINAL router would have assigned to its own top-k (including deleted experts). This needs the deleted experts' scores at inference, which are available only if the router rows are masked rather than deleted (see KQ6/KQ9). **[extrapolation]**
- The Gemma alpha-gain result (alpha=0.80 best) and the k2 result are the same lever in two forms: a constant global gain vs a per-token adaptive gain. The per-token form won on Qwen (ns at k2=16); the constant form did not fully recover on Gemma. For our pipeline, prefer the adaptive form (needs no sweep per layer) and treat alpha as a fallback when deleted-expert scores are unavailable. **[extrapolation]**
- Zero-GPU status: choosing k2 by perplexity on banked statistics is explicitly unsafe (paper). The rule itself costs nothing at inference, but SELECTING k2 requires a downstream eval per candidate (MMLU-style, n~2000 with McNemar). Banked top-40 scores let us compute the implied gain distribution m_k1/m_k2 per layer for any k2<=40 offline, which bounds the sweep to 2-3 candidates. **[extrapolation]**

### Gaps
- No experiment in 2609.04575 or elsewhere applies the k2 rule after pool pruning (experts deleted) rather than k reduction; the 4.3-point swing is for k 8->4 with all 256 experts resident.
- No sigmoid-router measurement exists; the claim that the rule transfers to sigmoid/noaux_tc routers is inference only.
- The paper's abstract was reported by the caller as "k2=16 vs k2=4"; this is correct for the 35B model but the optimal k2 is k_train on the 397B, so "k2=2k" is not a universal rule.

## KQ2. The REAP renormalization fix (CerebrasResearch/reap, 2026-03-11/13): exact formula, numbers, and what it is NOT

### Takeaway
The fix is a one-line default flip (`ObserverArgs.renormalize_router_weights` False -> True) that changes only the SALIENCY STATISTIC collected on the unpruned model: gates used in S_j = mean g_j ||f_j|| are now softmax(z) gathered at the model's own top-k and divided by the top-k sum, i.e. the weights the model actually multiplies into experts (for norm_topk_prob models). It does not touch inference; the pruned checkpoint still re-selects top-k over survivors with the model's native gate. Mean non-agentic-coding drop across five models fell from 2.6% to 1.9%. The observer hard-codes softmax, which is a mismatch for sigmoid routers (GLM-4.5-Air was one of the five models).

### Cited Findings
- PR #13 "renorm logits bugfix" (commit 9033055, author mklasby, approved ivanl-cerebras, merged 2026-03-13; listed as commit 3a44d0c "Renorm router logits by default (#13)" on main): "Implements intended behaviour of normalizing the top-k logits to sum to 1 for REAP saliency scoring" plus README/figure updates. — [github PR 13](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap/pull/13); [commit list](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap/commits/main)
- Diff: `src/reap/args.py` `renormalize_router_weights: bool = field(default=False` -> `default=True`; help text "Whether to renormalize topk router weights to sum to 1 if the model.config.norm_topk_prob is True"; `src/reap/prune.py` drops the `-renorm_<bool>` suffix from the pruned-model directory name; two result PNGs replaced. No change to any inference code path. — [commit 9033055 diff](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap/commit/9033055.diff)
- The statistic (src/reap/pruning_metrics.py, `update_pruning_state`): `routing_weights = F.softmax(router_logits, dim=1, dtype=float)`; `topk_weights = gather(routing_weights, 1, selected_experts)`; `if renormalize_router_weights and selected_experts.numel() > 0: routing_weights = routing_weights / topk_weights.sum(dim=-1, keepdim=True)`; per expert i, `active_router_weights = routing_weights[active_mask, i]`, `ean_norm = ||selected_activations||_2`, `reap[i] = (ean_norm * active_router_weights).mean()`. The code never reads `norm_topk_prob` and never uses sigmoid: scoring is hard-coded softmax. — [pruning_metrics.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CerebrasResearch/reap/main/src/reap/pruning_metrics.py)
- The observer selects experts by `torch.topk(router_logits, top_k)` on the logits returned by the MoE module (or recomputed via `module.router(flat_input)` in the fused path, "does not apply router_scores"), and passes `selected_experts` + `router_logits` to `update_pruning_state`. — [observer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CerebrasResearch/reap/main/src/reap/observer.py)
- Pruning mechanics (src/reap/prune.py): `router.weight.data = router.weight.data[retained_expert_indicies, :]`; `router.bias` sliced if present (guarded by a tensor-truthiness check that would raise on multi-element tensors); `e_score_correction_bias` sliced by `hasattr`; Ernie's `moe_statics.e_score_correction_bias` sliced on dim 1; config expert count updated via `MODEL_ATTRS`; `top_k`/`num_experts_per_tok` NOT changed; `n_group`/`topk_group` not referenced. — [prune.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CerebrasResearch/reap/main/src/reap/prune.py)
- README (2026-03-11 entry): "For REAP saliency, top-k router logits are now correctly renormalized to sum to 1"; expected "a modest improvement for most model/datasets"; prior large-scale results and released Cerebras checkpoints were already calibrated with normalized logits. Non-agentic coding evals on ERNIE-4.5-21B-A3B-PT, Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1, GLM-4.5-Air, Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct: mean accuracy decrease 1.9% with normalization vs 2.6% without. No per-model or per-benchmark breakdown is published. — [reap README](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap)
- REAP paper: pruning "re-normalizes the router outputs over the remaining K−1 experts", survivors weighted g_k(x)/(1−g_j(x)); saliency S_j = (1/|X_j|) sum_{x in X_j} g_j(x)||f_j(x)||_2 with g the post-top-k normalized weight; all models evaluated one-shot with no fine-tuning; no load-balance analysis after pruning. — [arXiv 2510.13999v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v3)
- Local record of the same fix (consistent with the above). — [local memory: reap-logit-renorm-fix]

### Inferences
- It is "renormalize the WEIGHTS used in the saliency over the ORIGINAL model's top-k"; it is neither "re-select top-k over survivors" (that happens anyway at inference) nor a post-prune inference change. The 2.6% -> 1.9% number is the effect of scoring with the gate the model actually uses instead of raw softmax mass (which under-weights every expert by the factor m_k ~ 0.18 and, worse, makes the score depend on how flat the token's softmax was rather than on the expert's realized contribution).
- **For sigmoid/noaux_tc routers the upstream observer is still wrong in two places**: (a) it softmaxes the sigmoid pre-activations instead of using sigmoid(z), so the "renormalized" gate is softmax(z)/sum_topk softmax(z), not s/sum s — a different weighting across tokens (monotone within a token, but the renormalized vector differs because exp(z) != sigmoid(z)); (b) `topk(router_logits)` ignores `e_score_correction_bias`, so unless the `models/` patch for GLM returns bias-adjusted scores, the conditional set X_j is the UNBIASED top-k, which our own measurement shows can agree with the true selection on only 1 of 8 experts when the bias is realistic. **[inference; the GLM patch in reap/models was not read]** Our own saliency pass must gate against the checkpoint's gate module (as [local memory: noaux-tc-router-formula] already prescribes).
- Zero-GPU: with banked unbiased top-40 sigmoid scores and true routing captures, the correct REAP/HOPE gate (s_i / sum_{T_k} s * routed_scaling_factor, X_j = true biased selection) is recomputable offline for any stage without a forward pass.

### Gaps
- Per-model before/after numbers for the fix are not published (only the 5-model mean).
- Whether the reap `models/` patches for GLM/DeepSeek return sigmoid scores, logits, or bias-adjusted scores is unverified here.

## KQ3. Router temperature / score scaling after pruning

### Takeaway
No source measures a router temperature or logit-scale sweep after expert pruning. The closest measured levers are the per-token gain (k2 rule; see KQ1) and a global scalar gain alpha (Gemma, alpha=0.80 cuts loss from −2.30 to −1.30); for training-time temperature, tau=1.0 is best with a flat 0.5-1.5 plateau (PEFT setting), and inference-time Gumbel temperature shows a concave curve. Theory says pool pruning does not change the sharpness of the surviving scores for sigmoid routers at all, and for softmax routers it only rescales them uniformly; what changes is the effective gain on tokens whose top-k set changed.

### Cited Findings
- Inference-time routing temperature (RoE): `Indices = TopK(R + tau*G, k)` with Gumbel noise G; swept in 0.05 increments uniformly across layers; accuracy "rises from tau=0, peaks at an optimal task-specific value, then declines" because high tau selects less relevant experts; numeric values only in a figure. Temperature here acts on selection only, not on weights. — [arXiv 2509.17238](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17238)
- Training-time router temperature (Monkey Jump, MoE-style PEFT): tau=1.0 best, stable across 0.5-1.5; very low tau approaches hard routing and loses soft top-k weighting; very high tau washes out specialization. — [arXiv 2601.06356](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06356)
- Zero-temperature limit theory: softmax routing approaches hard routing as tau -> 0 but the limit is singular near routing ties; risk bounds only, no accuracy study. — [arXiv 2605.02124](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02124)
- Pruning "de-disperses" routing: with fewer experts, "top-4 selection shifts toward the strongest remaining experts, which lowers perplexity" while task accuracy can fall; under aggressive load-balancing (gpt-oss-20b, lambda_aux=0.9, per-token routing entropy 4.54 bits = 90.8% of max) router probabilities stop being a reliable importance signal. — [arXiv 2609.04453](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04453)
- Global scalar gain alpha on Gemma-4-26B-A4B: alpha=0.80 best (−1.30, p=0.079) vs alpha=1.00 (−2.30); see KQ1. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- MoRA uses a calibration temperature tau inside its learned-bias ranking, r' = (r + alpha*s)/tau, but only for choosing which experts to prune; biases/temperature are discarded afterwards and no inference-time temperature is applied. — [arXiv 2610.00367](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00367)

### Inferences
- Why removing experts does not sharpen or flatten a softmax: for survivors, p'_i = p_i/(1 − m_del(x)); the ratio p'_i/p'_j is unchanged, so the renormalized top-k weights are identical whenever T_k is unchanged. The effective-temperature question is therefore moot for softmax+norm_topk_prob and for sigmoid routers (scores untouched). What does change is (i) which experts are in T_k (promotion of a weaker expert) and (ii) the top-k mass m_k used as denominator, which shrinks on promoted tokens and raises the gain. A temperature cannot fix (i); (ii) is fixed by the reference-mass rule, not by temperature. **[extrapolation from the formulas]**
- For routers WITHOUT top-k renormalization (DeepSeek-V2-Lite style, or GLM configs with norm_topk_prob=false), deletion changes nothing for sigmoid (branch scale just loses the deleted experts' contribution, which is what the ExFold fold restores) and for softmax applies a uniform gain 1/(1−m_del) to the branch — there a global alpha = (1−m_del_mean) per layer is the right first-order correction and is computable from banked captures with zero GPU. **[extrapolation]**
- Rank: temperature is the lowest-value lever in this list; do not sweep it before the k2/alpha gain and bias re-fit are in place.

### Gaps
- No paper measures logit temperature or scale on a pruned MoE. The RoE figure values were not extractable.

## KQ4. noaux_tc / bias-selected routers after pruning: delete rows, re-fit the bias, or leave it

### Takeaway
The only published handling is row deletion: REAP's prune.py slices `e_score_correction_bias` with the gate rows and leaves top-k unchanged; no paper or model card measures re-fitting the bias after pruning. The DeepSeek aux-loss-free rule (b_i += u * sign(c_mean − c_i), u=0.001, bias used only for selection) is a pure function of per-expert load, so it can be replayed offline on banked routing captures with zero GPU; whether it should target uniform load over survivors is unmeasured.

### Cited Findings
- Aux-loss-free balancing (DeepSeek, Algorithm 1): after each batch, count tokens per expert c_i, mean c_bar; error e_i = c_bar − c_i; update b_i <- b_i + u * sign(e_i); biases start at 0; bias is added to scores ONLY for top-K selection ("only used to adjust the routing strategy"); the layer's output uses the original gating scores. u in {1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2}: 1e-4 converges slowly, 1e-2 fluctuates late; 1e-3 is "appropriate". Sign rule beat u*e_i (ppl 9.50 vs 9.51-9.53) and multiplicative biases (9.52-9.54). Balance metric MaxVio_global: loss-controlled 0.72/0.52 (1B/3B) vs loss-free 0.04/0.04. No statement of steps/tokens needed to converge. — [arXiv 2408.15664](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15664)
- noaux_tc inference formula (verified against the checkpoint gate to 2.98e-8): scores = sigmoid(h W^T) fp32; choice = scores + e_score_correction_bias (selection only); w = scores.gather(idx); w = w / w.sum() (norm_topk_prob); w *= routed_scaling_factor; the bias has exactly zero gradient under straight-through top-k; the wrong (folded-bias) formula agrees on 1 of 8 selected experts. — [local memory: noaux-tc-router-formula]
- REAP prune.py: `e_score_correction_bias` sliced to retained indices via `hasattr`; `router.bias` sliced under a buggy truthiness guard; `n_group`/`topk_group` not handled. — [prune.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CerebrasResearch/reap/main/src/reap/prune.py)
- Model cards of pruned noaux models do not describe bias handling: 0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B (REAP keep-168 of 256 incl. MTP layer; Router-KD on the 75 gate matrices only) — bias handling unstated. — [HF 0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B); noooop/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-REAP128-FP4 (128 of 256, top-k 6 kept, "86 recomputed router tensors") — bias/group handling unstated. — [HF noooop/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-REAP128-FP4](https://huggingface.co/noooop/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-REAP128-FP4)
- vLLM needed a patch for non-power-of-two expert counts (K160/K144) because fused CUDA router kernels do not template-instantiate them; fix forces the general fallback router path. — [friendli listing of 0xSero DeepSeek-V4-Flash-162B](https://friendli.ai/models/0xSero/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-162B)
- MoRA (learned selection bias for PRUNING, not serving): r' = (r + alpha*s)/tau, frozen router, biases trained 1000 iterations on C4 with L_LM + 0.8*L_Div, then discarded. Ranking by bias-adjusted gate mass vs plain gate mass (no approximation): Qwen3-30B 50% 58.81 vs 58.09; DeepSeek-V2-Lite 50% 51.38 vs 50.98; Moonlight 50% 54.58 vs 54.33 — gains of 0.25-0.7 points; the Div loss (+7.2 at 50%) and LM loss (+10.7 at 50%) are what matter. — [arXiv 2610.00367](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00367)
- Router-sensitivity pruning: router-only LoRA (0.002% of params) beats all-module LoRA at matched rank with half the experts removed on Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct (27.54% vs 24.42%). — [alphaxiv 2608.07890](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07890.md)

### Inferences
- Row deletion is the correct minimum: the bias entry of a deleted expert has no consumer. Leaving the survivors' biases untouched preserves the SELECTION the unpruned model made among survivors for every token whose top-k was unaffected; that is exactly what REAP's error analysis assumes, so "delete rows, leave survivors' biases" is the REAP-consistent default. **[inference]**
- A re-fit is only warranted if the post-prune load is concentrated (KQ7). Replaying the sign rule offline: simulate selection on captured unbiased scores (top-40 per token suffices when >= k survivors are in the top-40, which holds for most tokens at 50% pruning; tokens that fail the condition are dropped from the replay) in batches of e.g. 4096 tokens with u=1e-3 and stop when MaxVio < ~0.05 — the DeepSeek target. Cost: CPU seconds per layer. Risk: the training-time balance target was the training mixture; re-balancing to uniform on OUR calibration mix changes selection on tokens the unpruned model routed correctly, which is a form of mis-routing REAP never introduces. Treat as an opt-in diagnostic-triggered lever, gate on a downstream eval, and keep the un-re-fit bias as the control arm. **[extrapolation]**
- Because the bias has zero gradient, any Router-KD/Router-FT (KQ8) on a noaux_tc model trains only W_r; a trained W_r shifts sigmoid scores and hence selection even with a frozen bias, so Router-KD does implicitly re-balance, but through weights not bias. 0xSero's GLM-5.2 run is the only noaux_tc Router-KD in the wild and reports no bias handling. **[inference]**
- n_group/topk_group: pruning leaves groups ragged (unequal survivors per group). GLM-5.3 and MiMo have n_group=1 ([local memory]), so this is a no-op for our target but must be asserted per checkpoint.

### Gaps
- No measurement anywhere of bias re-fit after pruning (effect size, token budget). The "few hundred calibration tokens" question is unanswered; the DeepSeek paper gives no convergence count.
- No source states how many tokens the sign rule needs; at u=1e-3 and sigmoid scores in ~[0.05,0.5], ~100-500 batch updates move a bias by 0.1-0.5, which is the scale of typical score gaps — so hundreds of batches, not hundreds of tokens. **[estimate]**

## KQ5. Routed-weight-sum rescale (ExFold-style) vs plain renormalization

### Takeaway
"Rescale survivors so the summed weight matches the original" is, in a renormalized model, exactly the k2 = original-top-k-mass rule of 2609.04575 (weights sum to m_k1/m_k_orig, the gain training saw), and it is also what ZEDA's "no renormalization" arm does; both beat plain renormalize-to-1 (ZEDA +1.7 avg; Qwen3.6-35B k1=4: −3.10 at k2=k_train vs −4.65 plain, and −0.35 at k2=2k). ExFold itself goes further and redirects each deleted expert's weight onto a retained expert via a calibrated scalar (REAP-D64 61.50 -> ExFold-D64 68.75 of 69.04 on Qwen3-30B), which is a routing-recovery lever, not a renormalization choice.

### Cited Findings
- ExFold: budget-excluded experts' contributions are projected onto retained experts with calibrated scalar projectors fit on unlabeled data; plug-in vLLM kernel; up to 1.41x TTFT, 2.45x TPOT, ~99% quality retained. Qwen3-30B-A3B average over 9 benchmarks: original 69.04; REAP-D64 (permanently delete 64 of 128, top-8 within survivors) 61.50; REAP-D32 22.25; ExFold-D64 68.75; ExFold-D32 68.97; SERE-K4 64.15. — [arXiv 2608.24938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938); table transcribed in sibling note exfold_routing_recovery.md from [arXiv html 2608.24938](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24938)
- k2 = k_train rule ("gain anchored to what training calibrated"): Qwen3.6-35B (4,8) −3.10 vs (4,4) −4.65; (6,8) −1.10 vs (6,6) −2.60; Qwen3.5-397B (5,10) −0.55 vs (5,5) −2.10; (8,10) −0.45 vs (8,8) +0.15 (ns). — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- ZEDA Table 6 (Qwen3-30B-A3B): no-renorm 73.3 vs renorm 71.6. — [arXiv 2605.18643](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18643)
- REAP merging-error bound: error ~ E[(g_i+g_j)^2] Var[r(x)] G_ij, "irreducible for any constant alpha"; pruning error bounded by g_j(x)(||f_j|| + ||f_i||) with survivors renormalized g_k/(1−g_j). — [arXiv 2510.13999v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v3)
- MoRA's per-pruned-expert channel-wise affine fold (a (.) f_proxy + b by weighted ridge regression, 2 d_o params): +0.33 (25%) / +0.53 (50%) on Qwen3-30B; +0.38 DeepSeek-V2-Lite; +0.54 Moonlight at 50%. — [arXiv 2610.00367](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00367)

### Inferences
- Three distinct rules are conflated in practice; keep them separate in the pipeline spec:
  1. **Plain renorm** w_i = s_i / sum_{T_k^new} s (model default after row deletion). Inflates the branch on promoted tokens.
  2. **Reference-mass renorm (k2 / "sum matches original")** w_i = s_i / sum_{T_k2} s where T_k2 is taken over the ORIGINAL pool (needs deleted experts' scores -> keep masked router rows) or over the survivors with k2 > k (works after deletion; the 2609.04575 form). Zero-GPU at inference; selection of k2 needs a downstream eval.
  3. **Fold / redirect** (ExFold, MoRA affine): deleted expert's weight w_s goes to a retained target with a scalar; needs a calibration pass with per-pair co-activation (sibling note), ~0.3-0.5 pt (MoRA) up to +7 pt (ExFold at D64 on a 128-expert model where plain REAP had lost 7.5).
- Expected ordering of benefit on a 50%-pruned fine-grained router, from the numbers above: fold >> reference-mass renorm > plain renorm; reference-mass renorm is the only one of the three that is both zero-GPU and zero-calibration. **[extrapolation across papers/models]**
- Interaction: a fold already restores the deleted mass (w_s * S[s] added to the target), so applying reference-mass renorm on top double-counts: use rule 2 only on tokens/experts the fold does not cover (no target, or sentinel pairs). **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No head-to-head of "rescale to original sum" vs "plain renorm" after POOL pruning on the same model; the evidence is from k-reduction (2609.04575) and zero-expert injection (ZEDA).
- ExFold's exact per-token weight formula was not extractable from the abstract page (sibling note has the code-level description).

## KQ6. Changing top-k after pruning: constant vs reduce vs increase

### Takeaway
Every pruning paper keeps k constant (REAP, MESA, Half the Experts, Router-KD, MoRA, ExFold-D). No source tests raising k to compensate for deleted experts. Reducing k is nearly free IF the reference mass is held (−0.35 to −0.6 at 50% k-reduction with k2 fixed; −2 to −4.65 without), and increasing k with k2 fixed at 16 on Qwen3.6-35B (k1=6, k2=16: +0.75, ns) suggests k can be tuned post-prune as long as the gain is anchored.

### Cited Findings
- REAP keeps `top_k`/`num_experts_per_tok` unchanged in prune.py; the paper's substitution analysis assumes a previously inactive expert is promoted into the active set. — [prune.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CerebrasResearch/reap/main/src/reap/prune.py); [arXiv 2510.13999v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v3)
- MESA: pruned experts removed, top-k unchanged; with fewer experts top-k shifts to the strongest survivors, lowering perplexity; a 2026 paper notes removing experts leaves top-k unchanged so per-token decode latency is essentially unaffected. — [arXiv 2609.04453](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04453); [alphaxiv 2608.07890](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07890.md)
- Half the Experts: top-8 implied constant; "restricting a softmax to survivors and renormalizing over the selected top-k commute", used as the invariant that the pruned model's logits equal the base model with deleted experts masked out of the router. Gemma's per-expert learned scale after top-k renormalization must be sliced with the router rows. — [arXiv 2607.16721v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721v1)
- k reduction with anchored gain: Qwen3.6-35B 8->4 at k2=16: MMLU −0.35 (ns), GSM8K −0.60 (ns); Qwen3.5-397B 10->5 at k2=10: MMLU −0.55 (ns), GSM8K −0.80 (ns). Increasing k relative to k1 while holding k2: (6,16) +0.75 ns vs (4,16) −0.35 ns. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Elbow routing (OLMoE-1B-7B, 64 experts, top-8): per-token k(x) = min(elbow, 8), mean k 7.615 (delta=0.05), accuracy within 0.33 pt on 6 tasks, 5.3% latency saving; load shift L1 0.034 vs bound 0.10; randomizing post-elbow experts leaves MMLU at baseline while randomizing pre-elbow experts hurts. — [arXiv 2608.04401](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04401)
- ZEDA vs naive truncation (halving active experts) on Qwen3-30B-A3B: NET(SFT) 72.3, NET(SFT->OPD) 73.0, ZEDA 74.2 (51.2% zero-expert activation); GLM-4.7-Flash NET 70.9 vs ZEDA 71.8. — [arXiv 2605.18643](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18643)
- Routing-mass loss at 50% pool pruning: 88.8%/81.3% retained (WikiText/code), worst layer 64.2%, on Qwen3.6-35B. Base model sent 49-60% of gate mass to deleted experts on failing prompts (Half the Experts). — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1); [arXiv 2607.16721v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721v1)

### Inferences
- Increasing k after pruning adds experts that the original router ranked BELOW the promoted replacement, i.e. lower-score survivors; 2609.04575's identity-vs-weight ablation (random selection +1667% ppl) and the elbow result (post-elbow experts are interchangeable with random) say those extra experts carry little signal, while each one costs a full expert read on Thor's bandwidth-bound decode. The expected gain is small and the cost is linear in k. Do not raise k. **[extrapolation]**
- Reducing k proportionally at a prune stage is a compute lever, not a quality lever, and is safe only with the reference-mass rule; on a memory-bound decode the saving is real (fewer expert weight reads per token), so k-reduction + k2 anchoring is worth a single downstream check at the final stage. **[extrapolation]**
- Interaction with renormalization: any k change MUST be paired with a k2 decision; the measured ±4 pt swings are all from k2, not k1.

### Gaps
- No published result for raising k after pool pruning; no result for k changes on sigmoid/noaux_tc routers.

## KQ7. Load concentration and dead-router outputs after >50% pruning: detection and mitigation

### Takeaway
Detection statistics are standard and all computable offline from routing captures (per-expert load share, MaxVio = max_load/mean_load − 1, coefficient of variation, per-token routing entropy); the only published post-prune routing statistic is a mass-retention curve (worst layer 64.2% at 50% keep) and the observation that failures coincide with 49-60% of gate mass having gone to deleted experts. No paper reports a measured mitigation (bias re-fit, temperature, router FT) for post-prune load concentration; ReMoE gives the only calibrated example of how much router FT moves these metrics (CV 0.04 -> 0.16, entropy 0.9998 -> 0.9971) without accuracy loss.

### Cited Findings
- MaxVio_global as the balance metric; loss-free balancing reaches 0.04 vs 0.52-0.72 with an aux loss. — [arXiv 2408.15664](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15664)
- Routing entropy baselines (unpruned): gpt-oss-20b 4.54 bits (90.8% of max), above 82% in every layer; Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct 2.23 bits (74.3%), 64-68% in later layers; OLMoE normalized 0.925; Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it ~0.69. The paper reports no dead-expert or load statistics after pruning; pruning "forces the router to redistribute mass to the remaining experts" (de-dispersing); random pruning gave the LOWEST perplexity (40.95) with among the worst accuracy on gpt-oss-20b. — [arXiv 2609.04453](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04453)
- Coverage before pruning: Qwen3.6-35B every expert in every layer received calibration traffic (100%); Gemma-4 99.9% (a handful of 3,840 experts saw no token) — consistent with aux-loss balancing. Keep-set overlap REAP vs routing-frequency/gate-mass 94.4% (Qwen) but only 52% on Gemma. Failure attribution: base model sent 49-60% of gate mass to deleted experts on average. Cliff: Qwen REAP 50% HE+ 0.896, 37.5% 0.860, 25% 0.787 with general ppl 12.0 -> 31.0 -> 30.9; Gemma REAP 50% 0.762, 25% 0.000 (collapse). — [arXiv 2607.16721v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721v1)
- Dead experts: median 0 (max 1) per layer on Qwen3.6-35B; 50%-keep mass retention 88.8%/81.3%, worst layer 64.2%. — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Router-FT concentration numbers (ReMoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, router-only, L_aux=0): baseline entropy 0.9998 / CV 0.0409 / EOR 27.3% -> ReMoE 0.9971 / 0.1608 / 34.5% (CV +293%); unique experts per sequence 64.000 -> 63.997 ("step-level concentration, not global collapse"); without the KL trust anchor entropy 0.9950, CV 0.2110, ppl 3.2280 -> 3.2629; accuracy effects within noise (MMLU +0.09, HumanEval +2.44, GSM8K −0.7, IFEval strict −1.1). — [arXiv 2605.27081](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27081)
- Routing shift after compression (Router-KD, Qwen3-30B-A3B, 100 ELI5 prompts): pruning shows larger L1 divergence of routing probabilities than editing or merging because pruning masks indices; top-8 overlap with the original declines in deeper layers for all methods. — [arXiv 2603.02217](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217)
- Lost-mass accounting is domain-dependent (code-derived keep set retains 81.3% of code mass but WikiText-derived set retains 38.0%). — [arXiv 2609.04575 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575v1)
- Loop/termination after pruning + Router-KD on GLM-5.2 keep-168: attractor/loop rate 0.072 vs 0.036 teacher, natural-EOS 0.928 vs 0.965, distinct-4 0.880 vs 0.921 (n=2000, raw sampling); sampler settings bring loop rate to 4.9%/2.3%. — [HF 0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B)

### Inferences
- Zero-GPU detection recipe from banked top-40 scores + true routing captures, per layer per stage: (1) simulate post-prune selection (survivors' unbiased scores + sliced bias, top-k); (2) load share l_i, MaxVio, CV, per-token entropy of the renormalized weights; (3) "lost mass" = mean over tokens of sum of scores of deleted experts in the ORIGINAL top-k, and its per-domain breakdown; (4) fraction of tokens whose new top-k contains an expert outside the original top-40 (unobservable -> flag). Thresholds the literature supports: worst-layer lost mass > ~35% coincides with the 25%-keep cliff; a 4x CV increase is the ReMoE "accepted" regime; MaxVio > ~0.5 is the aux-loss-only regime DeepSeek calls imbalanced. **[extrapolation; thresholds are read across different models]**
- Dead-router OUTPUTS (tokens whose top-k mass is near zero after deletion) are a different failure from dead EXPERTS: the renormalize-to-1 rule hides them by amplifying noise; the reference-mass rule exposes them as near-zero branch output, which is the behaviour of a dense residual skip and is the safer default. **[inference]**
- Mitigation priority when concentration is detected: bias re-fit (zero-GPU, selection-only, reversible) -> Router-KD with a KL trust anchor (ReMoE shows the anchor is what prevents collapse) -> un-prune the worst layer (layer-wise non-uniform allocation; sibling notes). Temperature is not a documented mitigation.

### Gaps
- No published before/after load-concentration table for any pruned model, and no measured mitigation. All thresholds above are cross-model readings.

## KQ8. Router-only fine-tuning: when it helps, when it is a no-op, data and compute

### Takeaway
Router-only training is decisive when the ROUTER is what broke (quantization mis-routing: GEMQ 1.5-bit Mixtral WikiText2 ppl 9.66 -> 7.69 in 59 s on 128x2048 tokens; a bad criterion: CFES at 75% retention +18.6 avg points) and a no-op on REAP-pruned models (Router-KD on REAP Qwen3-30B 62.5%: avg 0.5456 -> 0.5454, 16 wins/9 losses; Mixtral REAP −0.003), matching our own ±0.5 pt result; it cannot fix collapse or looping. Cost is ~0.5-2 GPU-hours on a 30B class model and ~1 min for Mixtral-class, with 3000 C4 samples x 512 tokens (Router-KD) or 128 x 2048 (GEMQ).

### Cited Findings
- Router-KD (arXiv 2603.02217): only router params theta_R trained; token-level KL(teacher || student) on softened next-token distributions p = softmax(z/tau), scaled tau^2, padding masked; distills output logits rather than gate values so teacher/student need not share expert sets. Hyperparameters (identical for all): C4, 1 epoch, batch 2, grad-accum 4, lr 5e-5, max seq 512, tau 1.0, max 3000 samples. Router share 0.04% (Qwen3-30B) / 0.002% (Mixtral). Time ~2 h Qwen3 / ~40 min Mixtral on A100. Retention 62.5% (128->80, 8->5) and 75%. — [arXiv 2603.02217](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217)
- Router-KD on REAP, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 62.5% (Table 2, 25 benchmarks): unweighted mean REAP 0.5456 -> REAP-R 0.5454 (delta −0.0002; 16 benchmarks up, 9 down; max +0.016 WinoGrande, min −0.033 AIME-2025 which is n=30); MMLU 0.6483 -> 0.6507; HumanEval 0.9146 -> 0.9207; MBPP 0.6220 -> 0.6180. MoBE (edit) gains are large where MoBE had collapsed a task (BBH-fewshot 0.0023 -> 0.4715; MATH 0.2438 -> 0.4608). — [arXiv 2603.02217 appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217) (means computed here from the table)
- Router-KD on Mixtral-8x7B 62.5% (Table 4, 7 benchmarks): REAP avg delta −0.003 (REAP already collapsed: MMLU 0.2829, GSM8K 0.031); MoBE +0.003; HC-SMoE +0.005. Qwen3 75% (Table 6): CFES +0.186 avg (MMLU 0.5472 -> 0.6288, GSM8K 0.4117 -> 0.6475, HumanEval 0.1341 -> 0.7012, MBPP 0.1140 -> 0.4720); MoBE +0.028; HC-SMoE +0.003. — [arXiv 2603.02217 appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217)
- Router-KD failure modes stated by the authors: no improvement on catastrophic collapse ("damage beyond misrouting"); regressions where the compressed student had beaten the teacher on a task. Gains are larger on fine-grained (C(128,8) ~ 1.4e12 routing choices) than coarse (C(8,2)=28) routers; coarse teachers' routing is peakier, weakening the KD signal. — [arXiv 2603.02217](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217)
- GEMQ router fine-tuning (RFT): next-token CE on the calibration set (NOT KL to the FP router; "FP-matching is suboptimal"); router only, starting from dequantized weights; routers 0.002-0.041% of params; 128 x 2048 WikiText2 sequences (same set as GPTQ), 1 epoch, AdamW lr 1e-4, bs 1, wd 1e-4; Mixtral-8x7B ~59 s (3.5% of GPTQ time, peak 136.3 GB), DeepSeek-V2-Lite ~77 s. Table 5, Mixtral 1.5-bit: FP 3.84/7.40 (WikiText2/C4); no RFT 9.66/23.53; FP router logits at inference 9.29/22.72; RFT 7.69/17.18. Routing flips: >40% of tokens at 1.5-bit; change ratio 41.31% (1.5-bit), 34.69% (2-bit), 38.38% (2-bit + RFT, ppl 9.01/21.88). Converges within one epoch; more samples help only marginally. 4-bit router quantization after RFT is lossless (7.71 vs 7.72). — [arXiv 2605.23078](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23078); [ICML 2026 page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/deng26t.html)
- Half the Experts recovery tune (NOT router-only: LoRA r64 on attention/linear-attention/shared expert + FULL router matrices, routed experts frozen byte-identical; 95.2M = 0.86% params; self-distillation on 1,863 base-model answers; < 3 h on-device): 25%-keep Qwen3.6-35B HE+ 0.787 -> 0.841 (52% of gap), MBPP+ 0.659 -> 0.685 (23%), general ppl 30.9 -> 9.4; pooled McNemar p=0.027, +3.5 mean. — [arXiv 2607.16721v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721v1)
- Practitioner Router-KD on a noaux_tc 744B model (0xSero GLM-5.2 REAP keep-168 -> 504B): only the 75 gate matrices (0.016%) trained, KL to the unpruned teacher, lr 5e-5, uniform position weighting, 8x B200; Aider Polyglot 90.1% aggregate (per-language deltas vs base −3.0 Py, 0.0 JS, 0.0 Java, +3.9 C++, −2.5 Go, −3.4 Rust); Terminal-Bench 2.1 70.5%; loop rate 7.2% vs 3.6% teacher — pruned-only (pre-KD) numbers not reported. — [HF 0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B)
- ReMoE: router-only CE+KL-anchor+locality training, 100k OpenHermes samples, 2000 steps, lr 5e-5, seq 2048, GA 8; accuracy within noise; CE-only control makes expert reuse WORSE than baseline (22.9% vs 27.3%). — [arXiv 2605.27081](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27081)
- Router-only LoRA after removing half the experts (Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct): 27.54% vs 24.42% for all-module LoRA at matched rank. — [alphaxiv 2608.07890](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07890.md)
- Our own measurement: router-only KD = ±0.5 pt on non-collapsed masks and cannot fix looping. — [caller context / local]

### Inferences
- Decision rule: run Router-KD only when a routing-divergence diagnostic fires (post-prune top-k overlap with the captured original selection drops well below the REAP regime, or the mask came from a non-REAP criterion, or a quantization stage flipped > ~30% of routings, the GEMQ regime). On REAP masks the expected value is ~0 and it consumes the only GPU in the pipeline.
- Loss choice: KL to the unpruned teacher's output distribution (Router-KD) is the right objective for a pruned model whose teacher is still available; GEMQ's CE objective was chosen for quantization where "FP router matching" is explicitly worse — note GEMQ's finding also implies that forcing the pruned router to reproduce the ORIGINAL routing (gate-level KD) is the wrong target after pruning. **[inference from GEMQ Table 5]**
- Always include a KL/trust anchor or a very small step count: ReMoE shows unconstrained router CE concentrates routing (CV 5x) and 0xSero's loop rate doubled vs teacher even with KD.
- On noaux_tc only W_r moves (bias gradient is zero); if load balance is the concern use the offline bias replay (KQ4) instead of GPU time.

### Gaps
- No Router-KD result at <= 50% retention on a fine-grained model (Router-KD stops at 62.5%); no router-only result on a 1T-class model other than the 0xSero card, which lacks the pre-KD arm.
- GEMQ's per-sample-count ablation values (Fig. 7) were not extractable.

## KQ9. Ordering inside a progressive prune pipeline: what goes before saliency re-ranking and what after

### Takeaway
Because every lever here changes either the gate weights g (which enter REAP/HOPE saliency directly) or the selection set X_j, the levers split cleanly: gate-definition fixes go BEFORE each observation pass; row deletion + reference-mass renorm + bias replay go immediately AFTER each prune and BEFORE the next observation pass (so the next saliency is measured on the model that will actually run); Router-KD, if used at all, goes last at a stage and forces a fresh capture.

### Cited Findings
- Per-stage statistics are only estimates of post-surgery behaviour: "flows measured under the base routing are only estimates of post-surgery behavior." — [arXiv 2607.16721v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721v1)
- Exact removal delta includes a renormalization/promotion term rho_{j,t} that all practical one-shot scores drop; evaluating it per candidate requires re-routing, which is costlier than a calibration pass. — [arXiv 2606.15716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15716) (as summarized in the search result and sibling note)
- REAP saliency uses the renormalized top-k gate (fix of 2026-03-11, 2.6% -> 1.9%). — [reap README](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap)
- Router-KD changes routing (that is its purpose) and is applied after compression with the teacher available. — [arXiv 2603.02217](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217)
- MoRA's learned bias is used only to RANK experts for pruning and then discarded; its own ablation shows the ranking tweak is worth 0.25-0.7 pt while the losses that shape it are worth 5-11 pt. — [arXiv 2610.00367](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00367)
- The bias affects only selection; gate weights come from unbiased scores. — [arXiv 2408.15664](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15664); [local memory: noaux-tc-router-formula]

### Inferences (the ranked recipe; effect sizes are the best available analogues, not measurements on our model)
Ranked by (measured effect) x (confidence that it transfers) / cost:

1. **Correct gate in the saliency observer** (before EVERY observation pass). g = sigmoid(z) gathered at the TRUE biased top-k, divided by its sum, times routed_scaling_factor; X_j = true selection. Measured analogue: +0.7 pt mean coding (REAP fix). Zero-GPU: recomputable from banked unbiased top-40 scores + captures. Failure mode: softmax-on-sigmoid or bias-free top-k (upstream reap code) silently mis-scores every expert.
2. **Row deletion of W_r and e_score_correction_bias, top-k unchanged, n_group asserted = 1** (immediately after each prune). This is the REAP-consistent baseline; keep it as the control arm for everything below. Zero-GPU.
3. **Reference-mass renormalization (k2 rule / "sum matches original")** (after deletion, before next capture). Prefer masking the router rows (keep the E x d gate, mask deleted logits to −inf for selection) so the original-pool mass is available per token; the k2 > k form over survivors is the fallback after true deletion. Measured analogues: −4.65 -> −0.35 MMLU (k-halving, Qwen3.6-35B); +1.7 avg (ZEDA). Zero-GPU at inference; choosing k2 needs 2-3 downstream evals (perplexity is explicitly unsafe for this choice). Interaction: must be fixed BEFORE the next saliency pass because it changes g; must not be stacked on tokens a fold already repairs. Failure mode: k2 too large (k2=E) collapses the branch (−27 pt).
4. **Load diagnostic from captures** (after every prune): MaxVio, CV, entropy, lost-mass per domain, worst-layer lost mass. Zero-GPU. Triggers 5 and 6; also feeds non-uniform layer allocation.
5. **Bias replay (sign rule, u=1e-3) toward MaxVio < 0.05 over survivors** — only if 4 fires. Zero-GPU on captures. Unmeasured after pruning; keep as an A/B arm. Changes X_j, so re-capture before the next saliency pass.
6. **Router-KD (KL to unpruned teacher, 1 epoch, 3000 x 512 C4-like tokens, lr 5e-5, with a KL-to-initial-router anchor)** — only on routing-divergence or quantization-flip triggers, last step of a stage, GPU (~hours at 30B scale; the 0xSero GLM-5.2 run used 8x B200). Expected ~0 on REAP masks (±0.5), +0.2-0.7 ppl-points-equivalent where quantization flipped >30% of routings. Cannot fix collapse or looping. Forces a fresh capture.
7. **Fold (ExFold/MoRA scalar) and k-reduction** are not renormalization levers; they sit between 3 and 4 and are covered by sibling notes. If a fold is used, apply reference-mass renorm only to uncovered tokens.
8. **Temperature / global alpha** — last, and only if the per-token k2 rule cannot be served (e.g. a kernel without a masked-gate path): Gemma alpha=0.80 recovered ~1 of 2.3 points.

Capture discipline: items 1, 3 and 5 all change the gate statistics the NEXT stage's saliency/HOPE F-matrix is computed from; therefore the only valid order per stage is prune -> (2,3,5) -> re-capture with the corrected gate (1) -> rank for the next stage. Re-using the previous stage's bank after applying 3 or 5 measures a model that no longer exists.

### Gaps
- No paper runs a multi-stage pipeline with any of these levers; the ordering is derived from which statistics each lever changes, not from a measured schedule.
- No measurement of any lever on a sigmoid/noaux_tc router except row deletion (REAP) and Router-KD without a control arm (0xSero).
