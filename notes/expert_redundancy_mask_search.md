# Expert redundancy structure in giant MoEs and prune-mask search vs one-shot ranking

Scope note: research run 2026-10-17. Every numbered finding carries the model scale it was measured on. "8-expert" means Mixtral-class (8 experts/layer, 2 active); "fine-grained" means 64–384 experts/layer with top-6/8 routing. Nothing below was measured on an actual 1T-class checkpoint except the REAP paper's Kimi-K2 (384 experts) and Qwen3-Coder-480B (160 experts) rows and the DBES routing statistics on DeepSeek-R1-0528 (256 experts), Qwen3-235B (128 experts) and GLM-4.6 (160 experts). Extrapolations are marked **[extrapolation]**.

Our own measured context, for the report writer: HOPE beat REAP by +2.8–6.1% on agentic at 40–50% in our runs, and our "v2" mask improved a reconstruction proxy by 2.5% with zero end-to-end change.

## KQ1. Expert utilisation statistics (routing mass, "dead" experts, depth pattern) in DeepSeek-V3/R1/V4, Kimi-K2, Qwen3-235B, GLM-4.5/5, MiMo-V2

### Takeaway
No published per-expert routing-mass histogram exists for any of the named frontier checkpoints; the only quantitative utilisation tables are on 7B–16B fine-grained models, where 5–29% of experts per layer are "cold" (<50% of uniform share) and the top quarter of experts carries 37–53% of routing, with concentration peaking in mid-to-late layers. The best direct evidence at 1T scale is indirect: on Kimi-K2 and Qwen3-Coder-480B, pruning the *least-frequent* 50% of experts collapses code accuracy to ~0.01, so rarely-routed experts are not dead.

### Cited Findings
- MoE-Sieve (2026) Table 2, averaged over 10 datasets. OLMoE-1B-7B (64 experts, top-8): global CV of per-expert totals 0.216, mean within-layer CV 0.869, Cold% (experts per layer receiving <50% of uniform share) 28.5%, Cov@25% (share of activations captured by the top-25% experts) 53.0%. Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B (60 experts): 0.077 / 0.374 / 5.2% / 37.4%. DeepSeek-MoE-16B (64 experts): 0.106 / 0.514 / 11.0% / 42.6%. The load-balancing loss equalises each expert's *total* workload (global CV ≤0.22) while individual layers stay 4–5× more skewed. The paper reports no expert with zero activations — only "cold" ones. — [arXiv 2603.24044](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24044)
- Same paper, depth pattern: for OLMoE routing concentration is "substantially higher from mid-layers onward" — layer CV 0.82 at layer 0, rising to ~1.4–1.6 from layer 4, peaking at layer 11 (CV ≈1.63, top-16 coverage 75%); the first three layers are "markedly more balanced". DeepSeek-MoE-16B peaks at layer 20 (CV 1.19), Qwen1.5-MoE at layer 20 (CV 0.93). — [arXiv 2603.24044](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24044)
- LPR (2025), 0.6B-parameter re-implementations of the DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3-MoE and Mixtral routers trained on FineWeb and measured on C4-val: Gini coefficient of expert load 0.790 (DeepSeekV3-arch), 0.707 (Qwen3Moe-arch), 0.635 (Mixtral-arch); min/max load ratios as low as 6.4e-9 and 1.3e-16, i.e. effectively dead experts in these small replicas. The introduction cites industrial-model Gini of 0.8–0.9 without auxiliary losses and 0.7–0.8 with them, "attributed to DeepSeek" — I could not trace that attribution to a DeepSeek primary source. — [arXiv 2506.21328](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21328v1)
- OLMoE paper: without a load-balancing loss, early in training all first-layer tokens went to a single expert and most other experts were "dead weights"; with the loss the imbalance "slowly decreases over the next few billion tokens". (Training-time observation, 7B model.) — [arXiv 2409.02060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02060)
- HOPE (AWS, 2026) states as background that despite load-balancing losses, "empirically, utilization remains highly non-uniform across experts (and this enables expert pruning)" — measured on Qwen3.5-122B-A10B / Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (256 experts, top-8) and GLM-4.5-Air (128 experts), but no histogram is given. — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- REAP (ICLR 2026) Table 3: on Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B (160 experts) at 50% pruning, Frequency-based pruning (drop least-routed experts) collapses Code Avg to 0.010 vs 0.619 for REAP and 0.636 baseline; "frequency-based pruning suffers from a sharp degradation in quality at 50% compression". Kimi-K2-Instruct-W4A16 (384 routed + 1 shared, top-8, 97.66% sparsity) and Qwen3-Coder-480B are the only ≥256-expert-class models with published prune results. — [arXiv 2510.13999v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- REAP appendix: the paper defines usage frequency ν_i per expert but reports "no usage histograms, no measure of usage imbalance, and no fraction of rarely-used experts" for any model. — [arXiv 2510.13999v2 (appendix)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- Lynx (2024, Mixtral and Qwen2-MoE): "at the workload level, no expert is consistently underutilized" even though per-iteration activation is highly heterogeneous — aggregate utilisation looks uniform while per-batch usage is sparse. (Search-snippet level; full text not read.) — [arXiv 2411.08982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.08982)
- DBES (May 2026) measures routing on DeepSeek-R1-0528 (256→8), Qwen3-235B-Thinking (128→8), GLM-4.6 (160→8), Qwen3-30B. It reports **Normalized Effective Rank (R_eff)** of the routing matrix rather than per-expert mass: Qwen3-235B R_eff 0.24–0.31 ("typically ranging between 0.24 and 0.31"), vs 0.45–0.55 for Qwen3-30B, DeepSeek-R1 (avg 0.48) and GLM-4.6 (avg 0.49). The authors interpret the low Qwen3-235B value as "massive expert redundancy exists within giant MoE models... they often form fixed 'cliques' to work collaboratively, leading to a collapse in effective routing dimensions". — [arXiv 2605.18498 (PDF, Table 1 and §4.2.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- DBES Table 2 (same models): n-gram expertise ratio (fraction of 10-grams whose tokens all land on the same expert path) 0.46–0.73%, which is 10^7–10^10× the i.i.d. baseline; DeepSeek-R1-0528 has the lowest Rademacher routing complexity (1.74e-5) of the five models, interpreted as a "highly structured and concentrated" routing hypothesis space. — [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- DeepSeek-V3 technical report: the only "redundant experts" discussed are *deployment* replicas (32 redundant experts in prefill; a dynamic-redundancy scheme hosting 16 experts per GPU with 9 activated) chosen from measured online load — it confirms DeepSeek measures load imbalance in production but publishes no per-expert distribution. — [arXiv 2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.19437)
- Mixtral training: one secondary source reports "overloaded experts emerge almost at every training iteration when training Mixtral 8x7B" (unverified secondary claim, from the MemFine paper search snippet). — [arXiv 2511.21431](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21431)

### Inferences
- The fraction of experts receiving <1% / <0.1% routing mass on a broad corpus is **not reported anywhere** for the frontier models. The closest proxy, MoE-Sieve's Cold% on 7–16B fine-grained models (5–29% of experts below half the uniform share), suggests **[extrapolation]** a comparable or larger cold tail at 256–384 experts, since the uniform share itself (8/256 = 3.1%) is small and DBES's R_eff drop at 235B points to more clique-like, lower-rank routing at scale.
- "Dead" experts in the strict sense (zero routing) are not documented on any production checkpoint; the REAP frequency-pruning collapse at 480B shows that the least-routed half of experts still carries essential code capability, so a routing-mass threshold alone cannot be the prune criterion.
- Routing concentration rises with depth on every model it was measured on (7–16B), and GRAPE/DiEP similarity analyses (below) agree redundancy rises with depth, so **[extrapolation]** layer-wise budgets should remove more experts from later layers, with the caveat that DBES observed a "terminal surge" of specialised routing in the last layers of thinking-tuned models.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives routing-mass histograms, cold-expert counts or Gini for DeepSeek-V3/R1/V4, Kimi-K2, Qwen3-235B, GLM-4.5/5 or MiMo-V2. Searches for DeepSeek-V3 expert-load analyses returned only architecture explainers. This would have to be measured locally (one calibration pass records it).
- The LPR Gini numbers are for 0.6B replicas and are **not** measurements of the real DeepSeek-V3.
- Kimi-K2: no utilisation or imbalance analysis found at all; only architecture facts.

## KQ2. Functional similarity between experts and effective number of distinct experts per layer

### Takeaway
Within-layer functional redundancy is real but structured as cooperative cliques rather than near-duplicate pairs: on fine-grained models hierarchical clustering yields mostly singletons plus a few "mega-clusters", HOPE's pairwise interaction terms average 33% of the diagonal, and causal-ablation audits find that no single-expert observational score predicts an expert's causal importance. No paper reports an "effective number of distinct experts" for a 256-expert layer.

### Cited Findings
- MoE Lens (ICLR 2025 SLLM workshop; DeepSeekMoE-16B, 64 routed experts, top-6): hidden-state cosine between the top-weighted single expert (plus residual) and the full top-6 ensemble is "extremely high... some layers having cosine similarity as high as 0.95", perplexity rises only ~5% when using a single expert across English/code/French; "very few experts handle over 50% of routing decisions" per domain and "most experts demonstrate minimal domain-specific activity". — [arXiv 2603.05806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05806)
- REAP (ICLR 2026): hierarchical clustering of experts by output (HC-SMoE) on high-granularity models produces "a high prevalence of singleton clusters" and compresses the rest "into a few mega-clusters containing tens of experts"; "in some cases, N/2+1 experts in a layer with N experts end up in one cluster". Merging is argued to incur irreducible "functional subspace collapse" because the router loses independent control; PCA (Table A4) shows merged layers have *higher* PC1+PC2 variance concentration than baseline on Qwen3-30B-A3B (layer 47: 0.744 merged vs 0.720 baseline vs 0.686 pruned). — [arXiv 2510.13999v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- HOPE (2026, Qwen3.5-122B/35B 256 experts, GLM-4.5-Air 128 experts): the per-layer expert interaction matrix F has off-diagonal entries whose "average magnitude is 33% of the diagonal", with visible block structure after hierarchical clustering "indicating groups of experts with high pairwise interaction scores"; interaction terms "vary by layer but are consistently present throughout the network". Expert co-selection PMI is strongly non-random (Figure S2, Qwen3.5-35B), "most pronounced at layer 0". HOPE with zeroed off-diagonals recovers exactly REAP's prune set. — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- GRAPE (2026; Mixtral-8x22B, DeepSeek-MoE-16B, gpt-oss-20b): redundancy measured as mean pairwise expert similarity (CKA/MSE) per layer, min-max normalised; "earlier MoE layers generally show lower redundancy than later ones, but the trend is not strictly monotonic"; "the final layer of DeepSeek-MoE shows disproportionately high redundancy"; "severe layerwise redundancy imbalance in DeepSeek-MoE can cause global pruning to collapse the model". — [arXiv 2604.06542 (alphaxiv mirror)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.06542.md)
- DiEP (NeurIPS 2025; Mixtral-8x7B): intra-layer expert CKA similarity "varies strongly by layer... most pronounced in layer 31, and layers 28–29 are more similar than layers 8–10". — [arXiv 2509.16105](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16105v1)
- Causal audit (ICML 2026 workshop; OLMoE-1B-7B, Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite — all 60–64 experts): token-level interventional audit across 60 metric×layer combinations finds every observational metric (utilisation rate, activation norm, routing-weight distribution) has effect size d<0.23 on causal importance; "no observational metric predicts causal expert importance in any model"; a power control recovers one signal only at OLMoE's final MoE layer (d=+0.231). The authors conclude existing pruning methods work "not because they identify dispensable experts" but because "early-layer redundancy renders most selection criteria interchangeable". — [arXiv 2606.10703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10703)
- Frontier modularity test (2026; Command A+, 218B total / 25B active, 128 experts, top-8 + 1 shared): six pre-registered expert "families" built from a routing-mass atlas were ablated against size-matched random nulls; only 1 of 6 (Arabic) survived as a clean selective module under a conservative bar (3 of 6 under a permissive rule); other families had real causal effects "but were not selective". A positive control on Qwen3-30B-A3B recovered its published disjoint structure. — [arXiv 2606.25092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25092)
- ConMoE (2026; Qwen3-30B-A3B 128 experts, DeepSeek-MoE-16B, OLMoE): treats weight-space proximity "only as a proxy"; replacing nearest-weight reassignment of removed experts with a router-conditioned functional assignment lowers layer-output error 17.19%, prediction KL 12.34%, top-1 disagreement 7.71%, expert-reconstruction MSE 17.43%, and raises six-task macro accuracy +1.82 pp at 50% retention (16 of 18 model–task cells improve). — [arXiv 2605.29350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29350)
- DBES R_eff (routing-matrix normalised effective rank) 0.24–0.31 on Qwen3-235B vs 0.45–0.55 on Qwen3-30B/DeepSeek-R1-0528/GLM-4.6; DeepSeek-R1 keeps high R_eff (0.49) despite low specialisation, meaning its expert *combinations* are diverse rather than repetitive. — [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- PES (Pairwise Expert Similarity, 2025) is proposed as a per-layer/per-checkpoint redundancy metric; most redundancy "appears early in training, when routing is least stable" (training-time study, scale not captured in snippet). — [arXiv 2506.14038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14038)

### Inferences
- The structure that enables >50% pruning on fine-grained models is less "duplicate experts" than "cooperative cliques plus a long tail of low-contribution experts": singleton-heavy clustering, 33%-of-diagonal interaction terms, and the collapse of frequency-pruning all point the same way. This is consistent with our own HOPE-over-REAP gain being largest at 40–50%.
- Because observational single-expert scores have no measurable causal validity at the token level (d<0.23) on 64-expert models, any proxy built from per-expert statistics — including reconstruction error summed per expert — is expected to saturate quickly; this matches our v2 mask improving a reconstruction proxy 2.5% with no end-to-end change.
- **[extrapolation]** DBES's lower R_eff at 235B than at 30B suggests effective routing dimensionality grows sub-linearly with expert count, i.e. a 256-expert layer may contain fewer than ~0.3×256 ≈ 77 effectively independent routing directions; this is a routing-rank statement, not a count of mergeable experts, and has not been validated by pruning.

### Gaps
- No paper reports an "effective number of distinct experts" (e.g. clusters at a cosine threshold) for a 256- or 384-expert layer; HC-SMoE singleton statistics in REAP are only plotted (Figure A7), not tabulated.
- Weight-space cosine similarity between experts in frontier models: no measurement found; all similarity numbers above are output/CKA-based on ≤141B models.
- The ConMoE search snippet claimed "on Qwen3-30B-A3B about half of routed experts have a nearest neighbor in a different layer, with some middle layers above 70%"; I could not find this sentence in the v2 PDF text and could not verify it (see KQ3).

## KQ3. Cross-layer redundancy and cross-layer sharing/merging

### Takeaway
Evidence for cross-layer near-duplication is thin and small-scale: adjacent-layer expert dependencies are visible on Mixtral, and one unverified snippet claims ~50% of Qwen3-30B experts have their nearest neighbour in another layer, but every pruning method that scales to 100B+ prunes layers independently, and the only cross-layer sharing methods are training-time architectures, not post-hoc merges.

### Cited Findings
- DiEP (Mixtral-8x7B): "adjacent-layer expert pairs show cross-layer dependence, such as expert 6 in layer 30 correlating with expert 5 in layer 31"; learned per-layer scores "don't fully match the adjacent-layer similarity maps". — [arXiv 2509.16105](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16105v1)
- HOPE: "like most other methods, HOPE prunes experts in each layer independently, and does not account for cross-layer interactions"; a cross-layer extension with non-uniform per-layer budgets is sketched (Appendix C, "preliminary results") but "may introduce non-uniform budget-allocation instability (e.g. over-pruning layers with weak cooperative structure)". — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- ConMoE search snippet (unverified against PDF): "about half of routed experts have a nearest neighbor in a different layer, with some middle layers above 70%" on Qwen3-30B-A3B, with the caveat that "parameter-space proximity doesn't mean cross-layer sharing is always preferable". The v2 PDF I read builds candidate pools within-layer and never states this figure. — [arXiv 2605.29350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29350)
- C-PRUNE (2025) clusters experts within each layer by parameter similarity then prunes redundant clusters *globally*, "accounting for cross-layer homogeneity" (snippet only; scale not captured). — [arXiv 2504.07807](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.07807)
- GRAPE allocates one global budget across layers via a block-diagonal similarity matrix (no cross-layer merging; interactions are within-layer only) and gains +1.40% average, up to +2.45% (Mixtral-8x22B, 4 experts/layer pruned) over the strongest per-layer baseline. — [arXiv 2604.06542](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.06542.md)
- UniPool (2026, 182M–1.5B pretraining): a shared expert pool with per-layer routers during a 2K-step ownership phase (3.3% of training), after which each expert is locked to one layer; held-out loss improves 0.024–0.037. Sharing is training-time only; the final model is layer-private. — [arXiv 2605.06665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06665)
- GMoE (ACL 2026) argues "separate expert sets per layer limit the diversity of computations across layers" and shares experts across depth — again a training architecture (snippet only). — [ACL 2026 long 2065](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.2065.pdf)
- PCoMoE (2026): pruning the same number of experts in every layer "produces very uneven quality loss", and errors "compound when reductions are applied across the whole network" (snippet). — [arXiv 2609.01024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01024)

### Inferences
- There is no published post-hoc method that merges or shares an expert across layers in a trained 100B+ model, and REAP's "functional subspace collapse" argument against within-layer merging applies at least as strongly across layers (different input distributions per layer). Cross-layer sharing is a pretraining design choice, not a compression lever for us.
- The actionable cross-layer structure is budget allocation (KQ7), not duplication.

### Gaps
- No measurement of adjacent-layer expert weight or output similarity on any model above Mixtral-8x7B.
- The ConMoE "nearest neighbour in another layer" statistic could not be verified; treat as unconfirmed.

## KQ4. Domain specialisation of experts and how many experts per domain are load-bearing

### Takeaway
Specialisation style is model-family-dependent at frontier scale: Qwen3-235B routes domains to nearly disjoint expert hotspots (routing-specialisation scores 0.94–0.99 for legal/medical), while DeepSeek-R1-0528 and GLM-4.6 use distributed, overlapping expert sets (scores 0.10–0.47, domain isolation <0.4). Either way, the wrong one-shot criterion can erase a whole domain (coding to 0.000–0.003 at only 25% pruning on Qwen3-30B), and prune sets differ ~40% between calibration domains on 122B models, which is the direct justification for per-domain protection.

### Cited Findings
- DBES (May 2026) routing-specialisation S_spec per domain: Qwen3-235B-Thinking Medical 0.99, Legal 0.94, Science 0.70, Math 0.74, Knowledge 0.49, Code 0.64; DeepSeek-R1-0528 ranges 0.10 (Knowledge) to 0.31 (Medical/Legal), avg 0.24; GLM-4.6 0.15–0.47, avg 0.35. Domain isolation S_iso: DeepSeek and GLM "generally <0.4"; all models lowest in the Knowledge domain (Qwen 0.37–0.44, DeepSeek 0.22). The authors describe "Qwen-series exhibit modular specialization with high domain isolation, while DeepSeek and GLM employ distributed collaboration". Thinking-tuning raises S_spec (Qwen3-30B Medical 0.90→1.09). — [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- DBES activation heatmaps for Qwen3-235B: "Coding exhibits sparse, highly specialized activation", science "relatively balanced", medical "moderate clustering"; the Qwen series shows "highly concentrated activation hotspots that are strictly partitioned by domain". Intervention: steering domain post-training along DBES-identified high-specialisation expert paths lifted medical/legal accuracy from 66% to 94.48% using 15% of the original training resources. The paper stresses specialisation is "necessary but not sufficient for downstream performance". — [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- Probing semantic routing (2025; DeepSeek-R1 671B/256 experts, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Mixtral 8x7B/8x22B, Llama-4 Scout/Maverick): expert overlap drops significantly when a target word's sense changes (WiC, p<0.001, all models) and rises for equivalent lexical substitutions (SWORDS, p<0.0001); effect is strongest in intermediate layers and clearest for DeepSeek-R1; a reasoning pattern "is most frequently routed to a small subset of experts" (e.g. experts 81, 89, 133, 136 recurring for "Wait"-type tokens in layer 7). No language/domain-level counts given. — [arXiv 2502.10928](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10928)
- MoE Lens (DeepSeekMoE-16B): "only a small number of experts show strong specialization (significantly higher than uniform routing frequency) for any domain. Second, most experts demonstrate minimal domain-specific activity." — [arXiv 2603.05806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05806)
- Unified scoring paper (2026; OLMoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, ERNIE-4.5-21B-A3B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507): on Qwen3-30B at only 25% task-agnostic (C4) pruning, Frequency, SEER, EAN and MoNE criteria score 0.000–0.003 on EvalPlus coding vs 0.871 for the full model, while the gate-free mean-activation-norm criterion (MAN) scores 0.868 — i.e. a small set of coding experts is load-bearing and is destroyed by routing-frequency-weighted criteria. Recommendation: task-agnostic pruning should use routed-token-averaged, gate-free scores (MAN/MSAN); task-specific pruning should keep gate and frequency information ((0,1,1) or (0,2,2) in their (b,α,β) family). Cross-calibration (C4 vs Evol-CodeAlpaca vs Tulu-math) bottom-expert overlap rises when frequency is removed and when gate exponent α=0. — [arXiv 2606.15716](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15716)
- HOPE calibration sensitivity (Qwen3.5-122B/35B, GLM-4.5-Air): Jaccard index between prune sets from Evol-CodeAlpaca vs SWE-Bench-verified calibration averages 0.60 (HOPE) vs 0.61 (REAP), i.e. ~40% of pruned experts change with calibration domain, while within-domain trial-to-trial Jaccard is >0.95 for all methods. — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- REAP: fine-grained models (Qwen3-30B, ERNIE-4.5-21B) "degrade more when calibrated on C4" than when calibrated on code/math/writing mixes (Figure A8); REAP's large-model calibration uses 12,228 samples up to 16,384 tokens plus xlam-function-calling and SWE-smith trajectories, and Kimi-K2 multi-turn tool use still drops from 0.355 to 0.263 at only 25% pruning. — [arXiv 2510.13999v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- Command A+ (218B): expert "families" for capabilities/languages had real causal effects on ablation but were mostly not selective; only the Arabic family was a clean module. — [arXiv 2606.25092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25092)
- Generic TB-Coverage (2026) argues a single aggregated importance score "can bias the retained set" and uses per-corpus coverage over generic corpora instead (snippet only). — [emergentmind 2607.01710](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2607.01710)

### Inferences
- For GLM-class models (DBES: distributed collaboration, isolation <0.4) per-domain protection is harder than for Qwen-class: load-bearing experts for a domain are spread across many experts with overlap, so a domain-coverage constraint must be expressed on routing-mass retention per domain rather than on a small named set. For Qwen-class models the hotspot structure makes per-domain sets identifiable.
- The ~0.60 cross-calibration Jaccard at 122B scale quantifies the stake: a calibration mix that under-represents a domain changes roughly 40% of the prune set, which is consistent with our earlier finding that the REAP gap was corpus imbalance, not criterion.
- "How many experts per domain are load-bearing" is not answered numerically anywhere; the Qwen3-30B coding collapse at 25% implies the number is small enough to be entirely captured in the bottom quartile of a frequency ranking, i.e. **[extrapolation]** on the order of a few experts per layer for a narrow domain like code on a 128-expert layer.

### Gaps
- No paper counts load-bearing experts per domain per layer on any ≥128-expert model.
- DBES's DeepSeek-R1 and GLM-4.6 results cover 9 datasets / 7 domains, all English; no per-language analysis on these models.
- Nothing on GLM-5, MiMo-V2 or DeepSeek-V4 specialisation.

## KQ5. Mask search (evolutionary, RL, differentiable, Bayesian, greedy-with-interactions) vs one-shot ranking: gains, cost, proxy optimised

### Takeaway
Search over masks gives large gains only where the baseline is weak (8-expert Mixtral, Frequency/SEER criteria, task-specific fitness); against a strong one-shot criterion (REAP) on fine-grained models the measured gains shrink to roughly 0–3 points and sometimes go negative, while HOPE's interaction-aware QP delivers its +2.8–6.1% at 40–50% for ~7% extra calibration time. No RL-based or Bayesian-optimisation expert-pruning method was found.

### Cited Findings
**Evolutionary (full mask, EEP)**
- EEP (2024): gradient-free evolutionary search over which experts to keep plus merge coefficients; fitness = accuracy on a training subset of each downstream dataset (task-specific); 40 pruning iterations then 160 merging iterations; population size and GPU-hours not stated, limitations admit "a potentially costly search process". Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct 10-dataset average: full 62.4; keep 4 of 8 experts — EEP 74.2, NAEE 60.5, Frequency 45.8, Random 44.9; keep 2 — EEP 65.6, NAEE 47.0, Frequency 24.9. Also tested Qwen1.5-MoE (60→30/15) and Qwen2-57B-A14B (64→8/4/2/1) on fewer datasets. Note EEP's pruned model *beats the full model* because the fitness is the target task's own data. — [arXiv 2407.00945](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00945v1)

**Evolutionary (layer budget only, EvoESAP)**
- EvoESAP (ACM MM 2026): holds within-layer order fixed (REAP/EAN/SEER/Frequency) and evolves only the per-layer integer budget under a global constraint; population 32, 50 generations (OLMoE), 20–30 (ERNIE/DeepSeek-V2-Lite), 10 (Qwen3-30B); fitness ESAP = teacher-forced expected speculative-acceptance between pruned and full model on 64 samples. Cost: OLMoE-7B 5.8 h on 1×L40S; DeepSeek-16B 6.7 h on 1×L40S; ERNIE-21B 5.0 h on 2×L40S; Qwen3-30B-A3B 5.2 h on 2×L40S (10 generations). — [arXiv 2603.06003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06003)
- EvoESAP gains over uniform allocation **with REAP as the within-layer order**: OLMoE 25% Code Avg 0.160→0.189, Math Avg 0.398→0.426, MC 0.579→0.581; ERNIE-4.5-21B 25% Code 0.527→0.521, WildBench 0.354→0.376, Math 0.775→0.783; ERNIE 50% Code 0.461→0.462, Math 0.646→0.648, MC 0.575→0.575; Qwen3-30B-A3B 50% Code 0.585→0.595, WildBench 0.299→0.267, Math 0.835→0.830, MC 0.596→0.585. Gains with weak criteria are much larger: ERNIE 50% Frequency MATH-500 +19.6% (0.272→0.468 Math Avg 0.397→0.549); Qwen3-30B 50% Frequency Code +6.6%, WildBench +7.2%, Math +9.5%, MC +6.0%. Authors: "when the within-layer pruning order already preserves the key experts... the additional gains are naturally more modest". — [arXiv 2603.06003 (Tables 1–2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06003)
- EvoESAP vs DiEP (OLMoE-7B, 25%, both 1,024 calibration samples, 10 generations vs 10 epochs): DiEP 11.25 h on 2×L40S, Code Avg 0.114, WildBench 0.208, MC 0.495; EvoESAP 1.92 h on 1×L40S (0.75 h calibration + 1.17 h search), Code 0.178, WildBench 0.275, MC 0.565. — [arXiv 2603.06003 (Table 4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06003)

**Differentiable masks**
- DiEP (NeurIPS 2025): softmax-relaxed per-expert logits α plus per-layer scale β, loss = CE + 0.01·‖pruned−original output‖_F, 128 C4 samples, 10 epochs, global ranking by α·β; pruning time 0.23–0.34 h on 4×A800 (vs NAEE 1.31 h on Mixtral and ~10^5 GPU-days infeasible for 64-expert models); peak memory 139 GB for Mixtral-8x7B. Mixtral-8x7B MMLU 25%/50%: DiEP 64.9/57.9 vs NAEE 58.7/47.3 vs S-MoE 61.5/55.9; Mixtral-8x22B task avg 50%: 62.9 vs NAEE 59.7; Qwen2-57B-A14B after removing 244 experts: 68.7% avg, −0.4 pt. Calibration sweep peaks at 128 samples (63.8 avg) and *declines* at 1,024 (61.9). — [arXiv 2509.16105](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16105v1)
- MC# (2025; Mixtral 8x7B/8x22B, DeepSeek-VL2-L): Gumbel-Softmax straight-through to learn a target mask matrix for dynamic expert pruning (snippet only; no numbers captured). — [arXiv 2510.10962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10962)
- Deterministic differentiable structured pruning (2026) learns experts×channels masks for MoE experts only, deterministic rather than sampled (snippet only). — [arXiv 2603.08065](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08065)

**Greedy with interactions / global greedy**
- HOPE: binary QP over per-layer E×E interaction matrix F, solved by continuous relaxation + rounding; at 40–50% pruning average rank 1.58 of 5 (REAP 2.42), beats REAP in every agentic (SWE-Bench Pro) condition at 40–50% with mean +2.8% and max +6.1%; over all 54 conditions average rank 2.07, top-1 in 39%, head-to-head win rate 73%; last place in 1/54 conditions. Cost: calibration 2.68 vs 2.53 ms/token (35B), 3.47 vs 3.31 ms/token (122B); QP solve 75–92 s total; overall 1.067–1.077× REAP time; 10k vs 8k prompts to reach 99% prune-set agreement at 50%. After 1B-token SFT on LiveCodeBench traces, HOPE still leads REAP ("fine-tuning narrows the gap but does not close it"). — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- GRAPE: greedy global redundancy-driven merging with an entropy guard, no fine-tuning: +1.40% average, max +2.45% (Mixtral-8x22B), +1.84%/+1.43% (DeepSeek-MoE-16B), +0.44%/+0.45% (gpt-oss-20b) over strongest local baseline; compute cost not reported. — [arXiv 2604.06542](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.06542.md)
- Shapley-MoE (NeurIPS 2025): Monte-Carlo Shapley values with early truncation and router-guided importance sampling (claimed quadratic complexity); Qwen2-57B-A14B at 25% expert reduction: perplexity +0.92, >96.4% of zero-shot accuracy retained; no numeric baseline comparison in the abstract. — [NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/c66a9db149261435664284a20b6f1d42-Abstract-Conference.html)
- MoE Pathfinder (2025): graph search for the top-m global expert *paths* and keep only experts on them (snippet only). — [arXiv 2512.18425](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18425)

**RL / Bayesian**
- Two targeted searches found no RL-based expert-pruning mask method; the nearest is ExpertCache (ICSME 2025), an RL policy for expert *caching/loading*, not pruning. — [ExpertCache](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/65750/1/ICSME25-ExpertCache.pdf)
- No Bayesian-optimisation method over layer-wise expert ratios surfaced; the layer-allocation methods found are evolutionary (EvoESAP), differentiable (DiEP), tolerance-based (FAIR-Pruner), entropy-based (C-GNN-PRUNE) and Fisher/outlier-based (COMPEL). — [FAIR-Pruner](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02291); [COMPEL, ACL Findings 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1521/)

### Inferences
- Ordering by measured gain-per-cost on fine-grained models with a REAP-class baseline: (1) interaction-aware QP (HOPE: +2.8–6.1% agentic at 40–50%, ~1.07× REAP cost, scales to 256 experts because F is only E×E per layer) ≫ (2) layer-budget search (EvoESAP: 0–3 pt, sometimes negative on Qwen3-30B at 50% REAP, 5–7 h on 1–2×L40S at ≤30B) ≈ (3) global greedy merging (GRAPE +1.4%) > (4) differentiable full-mask (DiEP; strong on Mixtral, 139 GB peak memory for 47B, no comparison against REAP, beaten by EvoESAP on OLMoE). The EEP +12–20 point results are task-specific fitness on 8-expert Mixtral and do not transfer to our task-agnostic, 256-expert setting.
- **[extrapolation]** EvoESAP-style search on a 1T model: each fitness evaluation is a teacher-forced pass of 64 samples through the pruned model; with population 32 × 10 generations that is ~320 full-model forward passes over 64 samples — feasible on a single 122 GiB machine only if the pruned candidate can be instantiated by masking without reloading weights, and the expected gain over REAP/HOPE order is in the 0–3 point band seen at 21–30B.
- Our measured HOPE gain (+2.8–6.1%) matches the paper's numbers exactly, which suggests we are already at the "cheap search" frontier; what remains is cross-layer budget allocation (KQ7), where HOPE itself has no published solution.

### Gaps
- No search method of any kind has been evaluated on a model with ≥256 experts other than HOPE's QP; EvoESAP/DiEP/EEP top out at 60–128 experts and ≤57B.
- No paper reports a mask-search method's gain *over HOPE* (all compare against uniform or REAP/NAEE).
- EEP's search compute (evaluations, GPU-hours) is not disclosed.

## KQ6. Proxy fidelity: which cheap proxies track final benchmark quality

### Takeaway
No paper reports a correlation coefficient between a cheap prune-mask proxy and final benchmark score across many masks; the available evidence is indirect and mostly negative — token-level causal audits find observational expert scores carry no predictive signal (d<0.23), CAMERA finds layer-output approximation error does not always match downstream accuracy, and EvoESAP shows KL/NLL/SAP/ESAP fitness functions all land within ~1.5 points of each other on downstream tasks. The one positive case (ConMoE) got +1.8 pp accuracy from a 17% layer-output-error reduction, but only because it changed assignment, not the retained set.

### Cited Findings
- EvoESAP Table 5 (OLMoE-7B, 25%, REAP order): fitness choice — KL: Code Avg 0.174 / Math 0.405 / MC 0.582; NLL: 0.175 / 0.424 / 0.576; SAP (single sampled-token acceptance): 0.172 / 0.420 / 0.584; ESAP (expected acceptance, closed-form over vocabulary): 0.189 / 0.426 / 0.581; uniform REAP reference 0.160 / 0.398 / 0.579. Using *true* speculative-decoding acceptance as fitness costs 29.49 h on 2×L40S vs 1.64 h on 1×L40S for ESAP, with Code Avg 0.171 vs 0.173, WildBench 0.269 vs 0.256, MC 0.565 vs 0.557. Search data: 32–64 samples suffice; 128 samples give no further gain. — [arXiv 2603.06003 (Tables 5–6)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06003)
- CAMERA (2025): "approximation error on the pruned layer outputs does not always match downstream task performance"; suggests "the share of tokens with lossless outputs may matter more" (snippet). — [arXiv 2508.02322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02322)
- Causal audit (OLMoE, Qwen1.5-MoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite): all 60 metric×layer combinations of utilisation rate, activation norm and routing-weight statistics have |d|<0.23 against token-level interventional importance; conclusion that inferring interventional claims from population-level observational summaries is "not valid". — [arXiv 2606.10703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10703)
- Unified scoring paper: their per-expert "proxy damage score" (Eq. 8) vs exact single-expert-removal damage gives bottom-expert-set overlap ≥0.95 on most models, ~0.85 on Qwen3-30B at 25%; i.e. single-expert proxies track single-expert damage but the paper does not test multi-expert masks and reports no correlation with benchmarks. — [arXiv 2606.15716](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15716)
- ConMoE: functional reassignment (pool fixed) reduces layer-output error 17.19%, prediction KL 12.34%, centered-logit MSE 12.23%, top-1 disagreement 7.71% and yields +1.82 pp macro accuracy at 50% retention, +0.35 pp at 75%; relative reconstruction-error reductions are positive in all six model–task settings (avg 10.71%). — [arXiv 2605.29350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29350)
- HOPE: the objective Z is a provable upper bound on squared substitution error, but the paper reports no correlation between Z and benchmark scores; what it reports is that the prune set is stable (Jaccard >0.95 across trials) and that HOPE-retained experts have higher mean F with other survivors. The theoretical advantage over REAP is entirely the off-diagonal terms. — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- DiEP calibration sweep: 32→128→1,024 C4 samples gives 61.1→63.8→61.9 average (Mixtral-8x7B, 25%), i.e. the gradient proxy overfits with more calibration data. — [arXiv 2509.16105](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16105v1)
- DBES: specialisation metrics "correlate heterogeneously" with benchmarks across Qwen3-235B/DeepSeek-R1/GLM-4.6 (Figure 5) and are "necessary but not sufficient for downstream performance". — [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- Biomedical reliability study (2026) comparing six training-free criteria incl. Frequency, Gate, REAP: "benchmark utility alone does not capture hallucination risk" (snippet). — [emergentmind 2607.01444](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2607.01444)

### Inferences
- Across EvoESAP's four fitness functions the downstream spread is ~1.5 Code-Avg points and ~2 Math points on a 7B model; a proxy improvement of 2.5% (ours) is below the resolution at which any paper has shown proxy-to-benchmark transfer. This is consistent with our "zero end-to-end change".
- Teacher-forced distribution-match proxies on real task prompts (ESAP, KL on answer tokens) are the only proxies shown to pick allocations that beat uniform; per-expert reconstruction sums are the proxies that failed the causal audit. **[extrapolation]** Switching our proxy from reconstruction error to a teacher-forced top-k/ESAP-style agreement on a domain-stratified prompt set (32–64 prompts per domain) is the cheapest change that the literature supports.
- Small-eval top-1 agreement: ConMoE's top-1 disagreement moved 7.7% alongside +1.8 pp accuracy, the only paired observation found; no paper validates it as a search fitness.

### Gaps
- No study reports Pearson/Spearman between any cheap proxy and final benchmark over a population of masks; this is a genuine hole in the literature as of Oct 2026.
- Retained routing mass and pᵀFp (HOPE objective value) as proxies: no fidelity measurement published.

## KQ7. Layer-wise pruning-ratio allocation: methods and measured gain

### Takeaway
Every allocation study agrees uniform per-layer ratios are a weak default and that redundancy (and routing concentration) rises with depth, but the measured gain of searched allocation over uniform with a strong criterion is small (0–3 points at 25–50% on 7–30B models, occasionally negative), and both GRAPE and HOPE warn that unconstrained non-uniform allocation can over-prune low-redundancy layers and collapse the model.

### Cited Findings
- EvoESAP: with REAP order, allocation search changes Qwen3-30B-A3B 50% results by Code +1.0, WildBench −3.2, Math −0.5, MC −1.1 points and ERNIE 50% by ≤+0.2 points; with Frequency order the same search recovers +6–19 points (see KQ5). Searched schedules are shown in Appendix D; the paper notes NAEE-style importance "reports nearly uniform layer-wise sparsity for Mixtral-8×7B and Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, but a highly non-uniform pattern for DeepSeek-V2-Lite". A plausible frequency-based heuristic allocation *degraded* OLMoE relative to uniform (Figure 1). — [arXiv 2603.06003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06003)
- DiEP learned allocation (Mixtral-8x7B): the text contains conflicting statements — §5.3.2 says α scores are higher in layers 1–15 than 16–32 (so deeper layers pruned more), Appendix A.1 says pruning rates increase from shallow to deep; layer 2 retains only two high-α experts. — [arXiv 2509.16105](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16105v1)
- GRAPE: redundancy generally rises in later layers; global allocation gains +1.40% average / +2.45% max; requires an entropy guard (freeze layers whose retained fraction would push global allocation entropy below E(1−γ)) because direct optimisation "can produce degenerate, imbalanced solutions" and "severe layerwise redundancy imbalance in DeepSeek-MoE can cause global pruning to collapse the model". — [arXiv 2604.06542](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.06542.md)
- HOPE: 10% prune rate is defined per layer (uniform); cross-layer HOPE with non-uniform budgets is future work with "preliminary results" and a flagged risk of "over-pruning layers with weak cooperative structure"; disagreements between HOPE and first-order methods are "distributed across all layers", interactions are "consistently present throughout the network" and co-selection PMI is strongest at layer 0. — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- MoE-Sieve: routing concentration (within-layer CV) is lowest in the first 3 layers and peaks mid-to-late (OLMoE layer 11, DeepSeek-MoE-16B and Qwen1.5-MoE layer 20). — [arXiv 2603.24044](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24044)
- DBES: thinking-tuned models show a "Terminal Surge" of domain-specialised routing in the deepest layers (e.g. Medical on Qwen3-30B-Thinking), "suggesting that deep layers are repurposed for specialized reasoning"; recommends "shallow layers for generic feature extraction, deep layers for specialized reasoning". — [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- REAP: "catastrophic functional collapse" from merging appears in late layers where gate-weight variance is highest; late-layer representation contraction on Qwen3-30B-A3B is "dramatic". — [arXiv 2510.13999v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- PCoMoE: equal per-layer expert removal "produces very uneven quality loss" (snippet). — [arXiv 2609.01024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01024)
- FAIR-Pruner derives layer-wise allocations automatically from a "tolerance of difference" criterion; its Qwen1.5-MoE profile assigns different expert counts per layer (snippet; no gain number captured). — [arXiv 2508.02291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02291)

### Inferences
- The depth signals conflict for our use: routing concentration and pairwise similarity say later layers are more prunable, but DBES's terminal surge and REAP's late-layer collapse say the last few layers hold the specialised, high-variance circuits that generative/agentic tasks need. **[extrapolation]** A safe non-uniform schedule protects layers 0–2 (balanced, strongly cooperative) and the final 2–3 layers, and shifts budget into the middle-to-late-middle band; GRAPE's entropy guard (γ) is the published mechanism for bounding how far from uniform the schedule may drift.
- Expected value on a single 122 GiB machine: at most ~1–3 points on generative tasks over uniform-HOPE, with real downside risk if the schedule is searched with a weak proxy (EvoESAP's own heuristic-schedule regression). The evolutionary layer-budget search is cheap enough to try (hours, not days, at ≤30B; cost at 1T unknown) but only if the fitness is a teacher-forced task-prompt agreement rather than reconstruction error.

### Gaps
- No layer-allocation method has been run on a ≥128-expert, ≥100B model; HOPE's cross-layer appendix has only "preliminary results" that I could not extract from the PDF.
- No published per-layer redundancy profile for DeepSeek-V3/R1, Kimi-K2 or GLM-4.5/5.
