# Healing / recovering quality after aggressive MoE expert pruning — methods, compute, data, and single-Thor feasibility

Scope note: all figures below are from the cited papers unless tagged **[extrapolation]** or **[local project evidence]**. Thor compute sizing uses the assumptions stated in the final section ("Ranking"). "RP" = relative performance retained vs. the unpruned model. Sources were retrieved 2026-10-17; most are 2026 preprints and were read in full text (PDF→text) unless noted as abstract-only.

## Key question 1 — Per-expert / per-layer output-scale or affine correction (closed-form, no training): how much does it recover, and does it saturate?

### Takeaway
Closed-form scalar corrections recover a lot **only when they are applied at the routing level (gate mass of an omitted expert folded onto a retained expert with a per-pair scalar), not as a static per-expert output rescale**: ExFold's weighted-least-squares pair scalars lift a REAP-style 50% expert pool from 61.50 to 68.75 average (original 69.04) on Qwen3-30B-A3B with ~131k calibration tokens and zero gradients. Richer projectors (diagonal, scalar+low-rank) lower perplexity but do not beat the scalar on downstream tasks — the same "reconstruction improves, task metric doesn't" saturation you observed in v2.

### Cited Findings
- ExFold (Aug 2026) is "training-free" and "folds the contributions of excluded experts into retained ones using scalar projectors calibrated on unlabeled data"; the deployed form is a scalar because "it can be folded into router weights without an online vector transform" — [ExFold, arXiv 2608.24938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938)
- The scalar is closed-form weighted least squares: for a co-routed source expert s and retained target t with outputs u_i = E_s(x_i), v_i = E_t(x_i) on m calibration tokens and weights w_i = ||u_i||², `s*_{s→t} = Σ w_i⟨v_i,u_i⟩ / (Σ w_i||v_i||² + λ)` (Eq. 8), with λ = 1e-3, scalars clipped to [−4, 4], self-pairs fixed to 1; a per-pair relative loss table ℓ_{s→t} (Eq. 9) picks the minimum-loss target. "Calibration therefore returns one directed scalar table S(l) and loss table L(l) per MoE layer, without labels, gradients, or model updates." — [ExFold §Method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938)
- Calibration cost: "we calibrate once on 32 unlabeled sequences of at most 4,096 tokens" (≤131k tokens) from the Nemotron Post-Training Dataset chat/code/STEM slices; GLM-4.5-Air used 16 sequences × ≤1,024 tokens. Storage for expert-pair scalars on Qwen3-30B: 6.00 MiB (layer-scalar: 192 B; global: 4 B). — [ExFold Table 3 and calibration protocol](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938)
- Measured recovery, Qwen3-30B-A3B (avg over MATH500/AIME24/IFEval/IFBench/GPQA/LCB/EvalPlus/MMLU-Pro): Original Top-8 = 69.04; **REAP-D64** (decode pool capped at 50% of experts) = 61.50; **ExFold-D64** = 68.75; REAP-D32 (25% pool) = 22.25 vs ExFold-D32 = 68.97. GLM-4.5-Air: Original 67.78; REAP-D64 55.45; ExFold-D64 66.71. — [ExFold Tables 1–2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938)
- Projector-family ablation (Qwen3-30B, Top-4 prefill): global scalar MMLU-Pro 65.60 / ppl 3.373; layer scalar 64.70 / 3.350; per-expert-pair scalar 66.94 / 3.352. "Pairwise scalars outperform global and layer-wise scaling, while diagonal and low-rank projectors require substantially [more storage]"; "[the diag]onal projector lowers perplexity, [but] the pairwise scalar performs [best on tasks]… Pairwise scalars give the best trade-off." — [ExFold Table 3 / §Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938)
- Calibration-domain ablation: "Mixed calibration [is near] optimal. This stability suggests that calibration captures expert geometry rather than benchmark-specific behavior. No task-specific calibration is required." — [ExFold Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938)
- Important architectural caveat: ExFold "changes neither expert parameters nor the original router" — the full router is kept and omitted routes are remapped at inference ("each omitted route is instead remapped in place" via a scalar-table lookup before the fused MoE kernel). It is therefore a serving-time fold, not a checkpoint with fewer router rows. — [ExFold §Method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938)
- REAP itself applies only one scale correction: top-k router logits are "renormalized to sum to 1" after pruning (added 2026-03-11, `ObserverArgs.renormalize_router_weights`); mean accuracy drop across five models on non-agentic coding 1.9% with vs 2.6% without. — [CerebrasResearch/reap README](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap)
- COMPEL (Findings of ACL 2026) adds "a Fisher-guided step [that] adjusts remaining expert weights to offset the damage from pruning"; on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B it reports near-lossless at 25% expert pruning and "within a 4% margin at 50%" (abstract only; formula not verified). — [COMPEL, ACL Findings 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1521/)
- MoNE replaces pruned experts with "lightweight novices": "unbiased estimates of the original outputs, computed from access frequency and output variance on a calibration set" (input-agnostic constant vectors), reporting gains over baselines at 25% and 50%. — [MoNE, arXiv 2507.00390](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00390v2)
- EEP (2024) tunes the surviving routing/merge weights with a gradient-free evolutionary search that "relies only on model inference, with no gradient computation", pruning "up to 75% of experts in Mixtral 8×7B-Instruct … with minimal performance loss"; the authors name search cost as the limitation. — [EEP, arXiv 2407.00945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00945)
- The user's v2 result: per-expert output-scale correction gave a heal-gain median of 0.766 on GLM-5.3-Flash REAP-50, but reconstruction-residual improvements did not move end-to-end top-1 agreement. **[local project evidence, stated in the assignment]**

### Inferences
- The gap between your static scalar (heal gain 0.766 on reconstruction residual, no top-1 movement) and ExFold's +7–11 average points is most plausibly the *where* of the correction: ExFold keeps the deleted experts' gate values alive and routes their mass (× a pair-specific scalar) to the retained expert the loss table says is closest, per token. A static per-retained-expert scalar cannot represent "this token wanted expert 37, send it to 112 scaled by 0.8". With your own CUDA server (`N_ROUTED_EXPERT` is a compile-time constant) this is implementable: keep the full 288-row router, delete only expert weights, and add a 288×144 scalar+target table per layer (~0.3 MB/layer). **[inference]**
- Saturation: ExFold's own ablation is the closest published evidence that going beyond scalar (diagonal/low-rank affine) does not pay on downstream tasks even when perplexity/reconstruction improve — consistent with your v2 observation. Expect a static affine (per-expert bias + scale) to plateau similarly. **[inference]**
- All closed-form methods here are layer-local and gradient-free, so they stream layer-by-layer trivially (one layer's expert outputs on the calibration tokens is all that is needed). This is the only class of healing that is unconditionally feasible for a 500B–1T model on one Thor. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No paper reports a *static* per-expert output scale on a REAP-pruned checkpoint with router rows deleted (your exact setting); ExFold's numbers are for folding with the router intact, so the 7–11 point gain is an upper bound for what a routing-level correction could add, not a measurement of your configuration.
- COMPEL's compensation formula and compute were not obtainable (abstract only).
- No source measures saturation of scalar corrections as a function of prune ratio beyond ExFold's D64 vs D32 (both ~68.8–69.0 avg on Qwen3-30B, i.e. no saturation visible there, but that is with the full router retained).

## Key question 2 — LoRA / adapter healing on calibration data: rank, tokens, steps, recovered quality; can it run layer-streaming?

### Takeaway
Across four 2026 papers the recipe converges: **LoRA rank 16–64 on attention (and shared-expert) projections, routed experts frozen, router either frozen or trained in full, 1–8M tokens, one epoch, hours on a single node** — and this recovers most of the pruning loss (e.g. 77.9% → 97.7% RP at 25% on GPT-OSS-20B; general-text ppl 30.9 → 9.4 at 25%-keep on Qwen3.6-35B). It does **not** stream layer-by-layer: every variant needs an end-to-end forward/backward through the student, so the student must be resident (in FP4 with frozen experts, LoRA states only).

### Cited Findings
- MAESTRO recovery fine-tuning (RFT): "LoRA adapters are inserted on each layer's attention projections ({W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O}) while the expert weights and router parameters are kept frozen"; data = "2,000 SlimOrca conversations with 1,024 tokens each" (≈2M tokens), rank r=16, α=16, one epoch, AdamW peak LR 2e-4, batch 2; all experiments on 8×A100-80GB. — [MAESTRO, arXiv 2607.08601 §3.7, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08601)
- MAESTRO ablation, GPT-OSS-20B at 25% compression: REAP **without** RFT = Log-PPL 5.02, Avg RP 77.91%, Std RP 25.60%; REAP **with** RFT = Log-PPL 2.21, Avg RP 97.67%, Std 2.70% (base Log-PPL 2.17). MAESTRO −RFT 78.54% → +RFT 98.90%. "RFT is a vital part of the pruning pipeline." At 50% on Qwen3-30B, REAP+RFT = 90.25% RP, MAESTRO+RFT = 93.40%. — [MAESTRO Tables 1–2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08601)
- "Half the Experts, All the Code" (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B pruned to 25%-keep, 10.9B params): healed by self-distillation on the base model's answers to the 2,047 calibration prompts (1,863 used), loss masked to the assistant turn; "the routed experts are stored as fused three-dimensional parameters that the LoRA implementation cannot wrap, so we adapt every other linear projection (attention, the linear-attention projections, the shared expert) with LoRA rank 64, train the router matrices in full, and the routed experts stay exactly frozen (verified byte-identical)". Trainable params 95.2M (0.86%); "healing runs in under three hours on the target device" — "one 119 GB unified-memory device". — [arXiv 2607.16721 §5.7, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16721)
- Same paper's recovery: held-out code ppl 5.45 → 4.85 (base 5.38); general-chat ppl 30.9 → 9.4 (base 5.20); HumanEval+ 0.787 → 0.841 (52% of gap), MBPP+ 0.659 → 0.685 (23% of gap); pooled McNemar p = 0.027. Contrasts with Martin et al. (2026) in translation, where "parameter-efficient methods are insufficient to recover entire pruned parameter blocks" — authors read it as "domain-dependent MoE plasticity". — [arXiv 2607.16721 §5.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16721)
- Methodological warning from the same paper: twice a recovery run "silently evaluat[ed] the unhealed model" (a save format re-pruned over the adapters; a precision path rounded small updates away at merge); "A byte-level tensor diff plus a frozen-parameter check is therefore a required gate before believing any recovery number." — [arXiv 2607.16721 §5.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16721)
- Router-only LoRA signal before pruning (arXiv 2608.07890): trains 0.002% of parameters; Mixtral-8×7B-Instruct with 50% experts removed retains 27.54% MMLU-Pro vs ~16% for magnitude/random; "the signal weakens as adaptation spreads beyond the router" (abstract only; rank/tokens not stated). — [alphaxiv 2608.07890](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07890)
- Full fine-tuning (not LoRA) in "Finding Fantastic Experts": task-agnostic C4, AdamW, cosine, max LR 1e-6, batch 8, 8×A100; token schedule doubling from 0.2M; "Appendix B tested 0.25M, 0.51M, 1.13M, and 2.27M tokens, and found about 1M tokens sufficient, with gains saturating after that". — [arXiv 2504.05586](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05586v2)
- MoE-I² (cited in Router-KD survey) prunes experts, low-rank-decomposes the rest and "recovers performance using LoRA fine-tuning". FlexMoE fine-tunes LoRA on expert FFNs under a fixed mask with task loss + teacher distillation term. — [Router KD App. E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217); [FlexMoE, arXiv 2606.27866](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27866)

### Inferences
- Data volume that matters is ~1–8M tokens; every paper saturates well below 10M. On Thor the binding constraint is therefore not tokens but **student residency**: the whole student must sit in memory for backprop. GLM-5.3-Flash REAP50 at NVFP4 is 96 GiB **[local memory note]**, leaving ~26 GiB for activations, LoRA states and the dequant workspace — tight but plausible with gradient checkpointing and ≤2k-token sequences. A REAP50 of a 1T model (~500B total) at the measured ~0.62 B/w for real NVFP4 checkpoints **[local memory note]** is ~310 GB and cannot be resident; LoRA healing of that student requires a custom layer-streamed backward (see Ranking section) — no cited paper does this. **[extrapolation]**
- The "experts frozen, heal via router + attention + shared expert" design is also the one that is compatible with FP4-at-source experts (Q7), which is why it should be the default on Thor. **[inference]**
- MAESTRO's un-fine-tuned REAP numbers (Log-PPL 5.02 vs base 2.17 at only 25%) are far worse than REAP's own paper claims for similar ratios, so part of the 78% → 98% "recovery" may be an evaluation-protocol artefact (chat-template/harness mismatch that RFT papers over); treat the *magnitude* with caution, the *direction* is corroborated by 2607.16721. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No paper reports LoRA healing on a model larger than ~47B (Mixtral) / 36B (Qwen3.6); nothing at the 300B–1T scale.
- No source attempts layer-streamed (non-resident student) LoRA training for MoE; DeepSpeed ZeRO-Infinity-style NVMe offload on an aarch64 Thor with FP4 base weights is unverified.
- 2608.07890's rank, token budget and hardware are not in the abstract.

## Key question 3 — Short continued pre-training after pruning: token budgets and recovered quality; feasible on one Thor?

### Takeaway
Published post-prune continued-pretraining budgets range from **~1M tokens (saturating, Mixtral full-FT)** through **2B tokens (OLMoE, 25% pruned)** to **120–400B tokens (Nemotron Puzzle, SlimMoE, SlimQwen)**. For a 1T-class student (~32B active) on one Thor, ≤10M tokens is hours, 100M tokens is days, 1B tokens is weeks, and ≥100B is years — only the sub-100M regime is feasible, and the literature says that regime already captures most of the recoverable loss for expert-level (not neuron-level) pruning.

### Cited Findings
- MoNE / OLMoE: after pruning 25%, "each pruned model was trained with 2B tokens, i.e., 512 steps [4M-token global batch], and the peak and minimum learning rate were 5e-5 and 5e-6"; "MC-SMoE and RS reclaim 8.54 and 14.02 average accuracy, indicating that expert pruning is … relatively easier to recover performance with continued pretraining". — [MoNE, arXiv 2507.00390](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00390)
- "Finding Fantastic Experts": ~1M C4 tokens per fine-tuning round suffices; gains saturate after (0.25M/0.51M/1.13M/2.27M sweep); full FT on 8×A100. — [arXiv 2504.05586](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05586v2)
- MoE-Pruner: full-parameter fine-tuning preserving the sparsity mask on 1,000 C4 samples, 3 epochs, LR 2e-5 cosine, on 2×8 H100-80GB, "about 1 hour"; Mixtral-8×7B 50% sparsity: 69.16 (dense) / 67.23 (pruned) / 68.40 (after) ≈ 98.9%. — [MoE-Pruner, arXiv 2410.12013](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12013v1)
- SlimMoE (Phi-3.5-MoE 42B → 7.6B / 3.8B, expert *slimming* not expert dropping): "400B training tokens, under 10% of the original training data"; compression training on 64 A100 GPUs; reduced-budget run 90B tokens total. — [SlimMoE, arXiv 2506.18349](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18349v1)
- SlimQwen (Qwen3-Next-80A3B → 23A2B): ablations at 120B tokens (global batch 512), main runs at 400B tokens; GPU count/hours not reported. — [SlimQwen, arXiv 2605.08738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08738)
- Nemotron-Labs-3-Puzzle-75B-A9B (from Nemotron-3-Super 120.7B/12.8B): three Puzzle stages healed for 24B, 43.2B and 52.8B tokens (~120B total) plus a recovery phase "up to 100B tokens"; data 30% pretraining / 70% SFT; "short-context KD brings accuracy across most benchmark categories to over 97% of Nemotron-3-Super". GPU hours not reported. — [arXiv 2607.04371](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04371)
- Jetson AGX Thor T5000 (128 GB) dense FP8 ≈ 517 TFLOPS, dense FP4 ≈ 1035 TFLOPS per a third-party spec aggregator; BF16/FP16 "No verified data available". — [flopper.io](https://flopper.io/gpu/nvidia-jetson-agx-thor-t5000-128gb) (third-party, unverified against NVIDIA datasheet)

### Inferences
- Thor training throughput **[extrapolation]**: assume ~100 TFLOPS sustained (≈20% of the 517 FP8 figure, or ≈40% of a BF16 figure that is presumably half of FP8). LoRA/frozen-base training costs ≈4·N_active FLOP/token (forward 2N, activation-gradient backward 2N, no weight-gradient GEMMs for frozen weights). For N_active = 32B (Kimi-K2 class): 1.28e11 FLOP/token → ~780 tok/s → **1M tokens ≈ 21 min; 10M ≈ 3.6 h; 100M ≈ 36 h; 1B ≈ 15 days; 10B ≈ 150 days; 120B–400B ≈ 5–14 years.** Full FT (6N) is 1.5× these. Replace 32B with the actual active-parameter count of the target; the figures scale linearly.
- The budgets that the pruned-MoE literature finds sufficient for *expert-level* pruning (1–10M tokens) are therefore cheap on Thor **if the student is resident**; the budgets used for *neuron-level* slimming (SlimMoE/SlimQwen/Puzzle, 10^11 tokens) are infeasible by 2–3 orders of magnitude. Do not expert-slim; expert-drop. **[inference]**
- Your own measurements (pruning ≈14× more damaging per unit compression than quantization) **[local memory note]** argue for spending any Thor-feasible token budget on healing the prune, not on quant recovery. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No published post-prune CPT budget exists for a model in the 300B–1T range (DeepSeek-V3/R1, Kimi-K2); the community "DeepSeek-V3 256 → 160 experts" artifact reports no recovery training. — [Ollama listing](https://registry.ollama.ai/huihui_ai/deepseek-v3-pruned/tags)
- Thor's BF16 training throughput is unverified; the 100 TFLOPS sustained assumption could be off by 2× either way.

## Key question 4 — Knowledge distillation from the unpruned teacher: logit-KD, hidden-state KD, router-KD; caching teacher outputs so the teacher need not be resident

### Takeaway
Every large-scale MoE compression recipe that recovers >97% uses **end-to-end logit KD** (top-8 to full-vocab), and two papers state teacher logits can be pre-computed and cached (SlimMoE: top-8; 2608.03796: top-100 with exact partial-mass handling, offline matches online loss at 29% faster / 25 GB less memory). **Router-KD** (distil the teacher's next-token distribution into the router only, 0.002–0.04% of params, ≤1.5M tokens, ~2 h) is the cheapest gradient method but on a well-chosen REAP prune its gains are marginal (±0.5 pt) and it "yields no performance improvements when the model suffers from catastrophic collapse". Pure hidden-state/expert-output KD appears only as an auxiliary MSE term (MoE-Pruner) — no paper heals with a layer-local objective alone and reports end-to-end gains.

### Cited Findings
- Router KD: "updates only … the router by distilling the original model's next-token distribution on unlabeled calibration data"; the design choice is "distilling output logits rather than matching router gate values explicitly, [so] Router KD avoids requiring the teacher and student to share identical expert sets". Hyperparameters (identical across all cases): C4, 1 epoch, batch 2, grad-accum 4, LR 5e-5, max seq 512, T = 1.0, max 3,000 calibration samples (≤1.5M tokens); "approximately 2 hours for Qwen3 [30B-A3B] and about 40 minutes for Mixtral" on A100-class GPUs. — [Router KD, arXiv 2603.02217 §4, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217)
- Router KD measured effect on REAP (62.5% retention, 128 → 80 experts, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507): HumanEval-instruct 0.9146 → 0.9207, MMLU 0.6483 → 0.6507, GSM8k-Platinum 0.8999 → 0.9090, WinoGrande 0.5683 → 0.5841, but BBH-fewshot 0.4866 → 0.4810, CoQA 0.4107 → 0.4015, AIME-2025 0.2667 → 0.2333. On the badly-collapsed CFES prune the gains are large (HumanEval 0.0183 → 0.0854, MBPP 0.104 → 0.186, AIME83-24 0.0986 → 0.1565); on MoBE (editing) MATH 0.2438 → 0.4608. — [Router KD Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217)
- "Router KD yields no performance improvements when the model suffers from catastrophic collapse … the failure likely stems from damage beyond misrouting (e.g., degraded expert representations), which router-only calibration cannot recover." Also "counterproductive" where compression paradoxically improved a benchmark. — [Router KD §5.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217)
- Architecture dependence: effective for fine-grained MoE (E=128, k=8, |Ω| ≈ 1.43e12 subsets) and "relatively marginal for Mixtral" (E=8, k=2, 28 subsets); "the router accounts for approximately 0.04% of parameters in Qwen3-30B-A3B … and 0.002% in Mixtral". Robust across ratios (62.5% and 75% retention). — [Router KD §5.4–5.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217)
- MoE-Pruner expert-wise KD: loss = CE + λ·Σ_layers Σ_experts MSE(teacher expert output, student expert output), λ initialised to the loss-magnitude ratio; 1,000 C4 samples × 3 epochs, full FT, 16×H100, ~1 h; Mixtral-8×7B 50%: 67.23 → 68.40 (dense 69.16). Reported for Mixtral-8×7B only. — [MoE-Pruner §3.3, Eq. 10](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12013v1)
- SlimMoE: "Logit-based KD: KL divergence against the teacher's top-8 next-token probabilities (other probabilities masked), plus an auxiliary load-balancing loss"; "teacher logits can be pre-computed and cached, which significantly reduc[es] memory requirements"; no hidden-state distillation. — [SlimMoE, arXiv 2506.18349](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18349v1)
- SlimQwen: KD matches the full-vocabulary soft distribution (KL), KD weight λ decays 1.0 → 0.75, plus an MTP-distillation term (β cosine 0.3 → 0.1) that "gives consistent additional gains"; at 120B tokens, pure KD MMLU 74.16 / MMLU-Pro 50.97 vs KD+LM 74.93 / 51.44 (BBH 71.63 → 73.00) but GSM8K 84.27 → 82.98, EvalPlus 67.32 → 66.07. — [SlimQwen, arXiv 2605.08738 Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08738v2)
- Nemotron Puzzle: "the student model is trained to match the logits of the original Nemotron Super model"; no hidden-state KD; KD vs plain CPT not compared. — [arXiv 2607.04371](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04371)
- Offline top-K KD (arXiv 2608.03796): cache K = 100 largest probabilities per position, **not renormalised**; the retained mass M enters as an M·log Z term with gradient M·q − p; fused chunked KL never materialises the full-vocab logit tensor. Offline vs online on one H200 (8K ctx): training-loss curves "near-identical", peak memory 103 → 78 GB, iteration 25.9 → 18.5 s (~29% faster). No K sweep, bias not analysed. — [arXiv 2608.03796](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03796)
- Caching top-K probabilities "yields biased estimates of the teacher distribution"; Random Sampling KD (importance sampling) needs far sparser logits than top-K (pre-training study, not pruning). — [Sparse Logit Sampling, arXiv 2503.16870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16870)
- Self-data distillation (unpruned model rewrites the SFT targets; pruned model trained on them, LoRA r=8, 5 epochs, 50k OpenMathInstruct samples): Llama3.1-8B with 6 blocks pruned — no FT 74.67%, SFT 81.66%, self-data FT 91.24% of baseline; at 4 blocks SFT (84.52%) is *worse* than no FT (92.31%) while self-data FT gives 93.29%. — [arXiv 2410.09982 Table](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09982v4)
- FP8 teacher logits for the GLM-5.3-Flash eval set are already banked (`s09_eval.json` has `teacher_from_cache: true`; `artifacts/eval`, `artifacts/router_cache`), so KD on that token set does not need the 321 GB teacher re-run. **[local project evidence, memory note reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit]**

### Inferences
- Teacher-caching storage **[extrapolation]**: top-8 (SlimMoE) ≈ 8×(4 B idx + 2 B prob) = 48 B/token → 5M tokens = 240 MB, 100M tokens = 4.8 GB; top-100 ≈ 600 B/token → 100M tokens = 60 GB. Both fit on Thor's disk; the cache replaces teacher residency entirely, so student-side KD costs the same as LoRA-SFT (Q2/Q3 sizing).
- Producing the cache for a 500B–1T teacher that is not resident requires a layer-streamed *forward* (no gradients): per pass, stream ~620 GB (NVFP4) or ~1 TB (FP8) of weights once, and read/write one hidden-state tensor per layer boundary (N_tokens × d_model × 2 B; 5M tokens × 7168 × 2 B ≈ 72 GB per boundary). At NVMe-class 3 GB/s this is hours for 5M tokens and ~a day for 100M tokens — feasible, and the same mechanism your REAP calibration pass already uses for the 321B teacher. **[extrapolation; the layer-wise observer added to the REAP repo 2026-03-30 does the same for pruning statistics — [REAP README](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap)]**
- Router KD on cached logits is the most Thor-friendly gradient method after closed-form scalars (router params only, ≤1.5M tokens), but the evidence says to expect ±0.5 pt on a REAP mask that is not collapsed, with gains concentrated in domains where the prune mis-routed heavily. Given your audit shows the damage is concentrated in the thin `general` bucket (retained saliency 0.49 vs 0.72–0.75 elsewhere) **[local memory note]**, router KD calibrated on a *general-heavy* corpus is where it could pay. **[inference]**
- Hidden-state KD is the only KD variant that can be made layer-local (and hence streamable), but nobody reports healing with it alone, and MoE-Pruner only uses it as a regulariser on top of CE. Combined with your v2 finding, the expectation is that local reconstruction gains do not propagate to top-1 agreement; the reason is that each layer's local optimum ignores downstream routing shifts, which logit-KD corrects implicitly. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No paper compares logit-KD vs hidden-state-KD vs router-KD head-to-head on an expert-pruned MoE.
- No paper distils a pruned MoE from a teacher that could not be loaded; the cached-logit papers all had the teacher available for cache generation on multi-GPU nodes.
- Top-K truncation bias for KD of pruned students is unquantified (2608.03796 does no K sweep).

## Key question 5 — Does healing between pruning stages (progressive prune→heal→prune) recover more than one heal at the end? Direct measurements

### Takeaway
Yes, in every direct comparison, at **equal total token budget**: SlimMoE multi-stage beats one-stage by +1.3 MMLU / +5.0 WinoGrande / +3.0 GSM8K (Phi-mini) and converges in 0.74× the time; SlimQwen depth-first progressive 77.39 vs one-stage 75.86 MMLU at 400B; Nemotron Puzzle iterative 69.05 vs single-step 68.48; and in the cheapest setting (Mixtral, ~1M tokens of FT per 12.5% round) iterative+FT reaches C4 ppl 9.76 vs 14.74 one-shot at 50% dropped (dense 7.44). Fully iterative (one expert per round) costs ~2.4× the compute of a 2–3-stage schedule for the same result.

### Cited Findings
- SlimMoE Table 3 (same 400B total): Phi-mini-MoE one-stage MMLU 68.58 / Arc-C 60.84 / WinoGrande 70.85 / GSM8K 74.52 vs multi-stage 69.87 / 62.29 / 75.85 / 77.48; Phi-tiny-MoE one-stage 58.40 MMLU vs iterative 60.05 vs multi-stage 60.08; iterative "costs about 2.4× compute during initialization". Multi-stage "reaches the one-stage baseline's final performance sooner, at 0.74× the computation time for Phi-mini-MoE and 0.91× for Phi-tiny-MoE"; one-stage pruning causes "model collapse at the initial pruning step"; intermediate stages use ~30–35% of total steps; recommended trigger: "start the next stage when the performance improvement in the current stage becomes minimal". Reduced-budget (90B, 25B in stage one): one-stage 64.11 vs multi-stage 65.59 MMLU. — [SlimMoE, arXiv 2506.18349](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18349v1)
- SlimQwen Table 5 (400B total, 23A2B): one-stage MMLU 75.86 / MMLU-Pro 52.97 / MMLU-Redux 75.41; depth-first progressive (40B + 360B) 77.39 / 53.22 / 78.01; width-first 77.14 / 52.80 / 77.07; "progressive pruning schedules outperform one-shot compression … gradual architecture transitions produc[e] better optimization trajectories". — [SlimQwen, arXiv 2605.08738](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08738v2)
- Nemotron Puzzle: three stages (75% → 60% of teacher MoE capacity → 50% active budget), healed 24B / 43.2B / 52.8B tokens; ablation "iterative Puzzle averaged 69.05 vs. 68.48 for single-step". — [arXiv 2607.04371](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04371)
- Finding Fantastic Experts, Mixtral-8×7B C4 ppl (dense 7.44), Min-EAN criterion: 50% dropped — one-shot 14.74, iterative (12.5%/round, no FT) 10.44, "MoE Lottery" (iterative + ~1M-token task-agnostic FT per round, optimizer reset each round) 9.76; 75% — 30.59 / 17.39 / 13.05. "There exists a significantly high disagreement between one-shot and iterative pruning while selecting least dominant experts." — [arXiv 2504.05586 Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05586v2)
- REAP paper evaluates "in the one-shot setting, with no additional fine-tuning after compression" and cites "iterative pruning benefits from Jaiswal et al. (2025)" without testing them. — [REAP, arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)

### Inferences
- The Mixtral result is the one that transfers to Thor: the *per-round* budget is ~1M tokens and the benefit at 50% is ~1/3 of the one-shot gap closed *before* any end heal (10.44 vs 14.74 with no FT at all — i.e. re-estimating saliency after each 12.5% removal is itself most of the gain). Re-ranking after partial removal is a layer-streamed forward pass (your existing calibration machinery), so a 4-stage 12.5%-per-stage REAP with re-banked saliency is feasible on Thor even for a non-resident 1T model; the *heal* between stages is only feasible if the student is resident (Q2). **[inference]**
- Your pipeline already stores per-expert accumulators and replays routing offline **[local memory notes]**; the deadline property noted for HOPE applies here too — a progressive schedule needs a fresh accumulation pass per stage because routing shifts after each removal. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No measurement isolates "heal between stages" from "re-rank between stages" at equal total heal tokens (SlimMoE's iterative row is the nearest, and it is neuron-slimming).
- No progressive-pruning result exists for ≥100B models.

## Key question 6 — Which healing methods rescue thin/minority domains (general-text "ballast") that pruning damages most?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence that thin domains come back is self-distillation from the unpruned model on a corpus that *includes the thin domain*: general-chat ppl 30.9 → 9.4 (base 5.20) with experts frozen, while plain SFT on an off-distribution set can make a pruned model *worse* than no fine-tuning. Nothing in the literature rescues a domain whose experts were deleted because the calibration corpus never showed them — the fix for that is on the pruning side (balanced calibration/guarded selection), with healing applied afterwards.

### Cited Findings
- Pruning a general MoE into a coding specialist drives "perplexity on held-out general chat … from 5.201 to 12.004, an increase of 131%" at 50%-keep and 30.9 at 25%-keep (code ppl only 5.38 → 5.49); self-distillation healing (LoRA r64 on non-expert projections + full router, experts frozen, 1,863 base-generated calibration exchanges, 70/30 code/general) brings general ppl "most of the way back to 9.4". — [arXiv 2607.16721 §5.1, §5.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16721)
- Pruning-side mitigations in the same paper: on-policy calibration (profiling on the base model's own greedy outputs) lifts the 25%-keep specialist 0.787 → 0.817 HumanEval+; "protecting the top general-traffic experts before filling the budget by saliency (a union guard) reaches 0.829". — [arXiv 2607.16721 §5.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16721)
- Self-data distillation vs SFT (Llama3.1-8B, 4 blocks pruned): no-FT 92.31% > SFT 84.52% < self-data FT 93.29%; authors attribute it to SFT "shift[ing] the learned data distribution and caus[ing] forgetting"; self-data FT keeps "embedding similarity to the original model's outputs tighter and higher than SFT". — [arXiv 2410.09982](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09982v4)
- SlimQwen: adding the LM loss to KD helps knowledge-heavy MMLU/MMLU-Pro/BBH but hurts GSM8K/EvalPlus; the weakest-recovering benchmarks after 400B-token KD are BBH (−12.8 vs teacher) and MMLU-Pro (−11.7), best are EvalPlus (−4.8) and GSM8K (−6.9). — [SlimQwen Tables 1, 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08738v2)
- Finding Fantastic Experts: "SMoEs' instruction-following capabilities are predominantly hurt during expert dropping", while pretraining knowledge and reasoning largely remain; k-shot prompting and SFT on an instruction set "impressively minimize" the zero-shot gap (figures only). — [arXiv 2504.05586](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05586v2)
- MAESTRO calibration-size ablation: small calibration sets "induce a frequency-based distributional bias towards domains that are most commonly seen", so 250 samples were chosen as a cross-domain middle ground; 50 samples raised Std RP by 15.79%. — [MAESTRO Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08601)
- Router KD: no help under catastrophic collapse (expert representations gone, not misrouting). — [Router KD §5.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217)
- Replay-ratio literature (non-MoE): general replay "reduced forgetting for some capabilities and increased it for others"; simple replay "does not fully stop forgetting". — [CapTrack, arXiv 2603.06610](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06610); [arXiv 2411.07175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07175v1)
- Your audit: `general` is 4.76% of calibration routed tokens and keeps 48.7% saliency mass vs 71–75% for other buckets; a bucket-balanced re-mask recovers only +0.028 — "re-masking cannot create information the calibration never collected"; s09_eval ballast dNLL 1.0027 / top-1 0.579 vs code 0.057 / 0.916. **[local memory note reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit]**

### Inferences
- The two general-text rescues that are measured (2607.16721, 2410.09982) share one ingredient: targets generated by the *unpruned* model on prompts that cover the thin domain. On Thor that means (a) generate general-domain responses with the teacher via the layer-streamed forward (slow but one-off; generation is decode, so expect your measured ~8–18 tok/s class throughput **[local notes]** — 1,000 × 1k-token responses ≈ 1–2 days), or (b) cache teacher top-K logits on an existing general corpus (prefill-only, far cheaper, Q4). Option (b) is the pragmatic route. **[inference]**
- Because routing to the surviving experts is what the frozen-expert LoRA recipe re-learns, a general-text heal can only redirect general tokens to experts that still exist; your retained-mass numbers say half of the general saliency is gone, so expect partial recovery (the 30.9 → 9.4 ppl result still ends ~1.8× base). Pair healing with a general-heavier recalibration or union guard at prune time. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No paper sweeps the general/target data ratio in the heal set for a pruned MoE; 70/30 (2607.16721) and 30/70 pretrain/SFT (Puzzle) are single points.
- No source measures per-domain recovery curves (tokens vs. per-domain NLL) for expert-pruned models.

## Key question 7 — Healing when experts are already FP4/MXFP4 at source: can you fine-tune quantized experts (QLoRA-style) or must corrections live in separate BF16 tensors?

### Takeaway
No public framework trains natively in MXFP4/NVFP4 ("currently no framework supports fp4 or MXFP4 training" — Unsloth; "native MXFP4 precision hasn't yet proven stable accuracy" — NVIDIA); the documented paths are upcast-to-BF16 → SFT → QAT back to FP4 (needs BF16 residency, infeasible for a 500B student on Thor) or QLoRA with the base re-quantised to bitsandbytes NF4 (dequant-on-the-fly, adapters in BF16). Therefore on Thor the corrections **must** live outside the FP4 expert tensors: router (BF16, ~0.002–0.04% of params), attention/shared-expert LoRA, and per-expert/pair scalars — exactly the frozen-expert recipes of Q1/Q2, which were in fact forced on 2607.16721 because its LoRA stack "cannot wrap" fused 3-D expert tensors.

### Cited Findings
- NVIDIA on gpt-oss: "its native MXFP4 precision hasn't yet proven stable accuracy"; recipe = upcast to BF16/FP16, SFT, `mtq.quantize()`, then QAT at LR ~1e-5 "to return the model to FP4"; "skipping the upcast and going straight to QAT yields lower accuracy"; SFT+QAT took two tasks from 16%/30% to 98%. LoRA not mentioned. — [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/fine-tuning-gpt-oss-for-accuracy-and-performance-with-quantization-aware-training)
- Unsloth FAQ: "currently no framework supports fp4 or MXFP4 training"; QLoRA path loads `load_in_4bit = True` (bitsandbytes 4-bit) and exports either BF16 (via on-demand MXFP4 dequant at merge) or `save_method="mxfp4"`; VRAM: QLoRA 14 GB (20b) / 65 GB (120b), BF16 LoRA 44 GB / 210 GB. — [Unsloth gpt-oss tutorial](https://unsloth.ai/docs/new/gpt-oss-how-to-run-and-fine-tune/tutorial-how-to-fine-tune-gpt-oss)
- "the routed experts are stored as fused three-dimensional parameters that the LoRA implementation cannot wrap, so we adapt every other linear projection … and the routed experts stay exactly frozen (verified byte-identical after training)"; a "precision path that rounded small updates away at merge time" silently produced an unhealed model. — [arXiv 2607.16721 §5.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16721)
- MAESTRO: "LoRA adapters are inserted on each layer's attention projections … while the expert weights and router parameters are kept frozen". — [arXiv 2607.08601 §3.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08601)
- Router KD trains only the router (0.04% / 0.002% of params) with experts untouched. — [arXiv 2603.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217)
- ExFold's scalar tables are router-side metadata (6 MiB for Qwen3-30B pair scalars) and "change neither expert parameters nor the original router". — [ExFold](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938)
- Low-bit quantisation itself shifts routing ("expert-shift"); EAC-MoE calibrates the router with a TopK-MSE objective and GEMQ shows "a lightweight global router fine-tuning step (updating only router parameters) provides substantial gains". — [Router KD App. E.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217)
- LoRA-on-4-bit literature covers NF4 only; no source found on LoRA over NVFP4 base weights. — [search summary, e.g. arXiv 2509.25214](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25214)
- Your NVFP4 overlay for GLM-5.3 costs +1.56% ppl / 0.138% confident flips and is applied as a 3.70 GiB overlay, not a rewrite. **[local memory note glm53-nvfp4-dense-overlay]**

### Inferences
- The frozen-FP4-expert design needs one kernel you may not have yet: the expert backward w.r.t. its *input* (dequant-FP4 GEMM with the transposed weight) so gradients reach earlier layers' LoRA/router. No weight gradients are needed for the experts. Your FP4→FP8 hardware path **[local note]** makes this a dequant+FP8 GEMM. **[inference]**
- If you ever do want to edit expert weights (e.g. COMPEL-style Fisher compensation or MoE-Pruner expert-MSE), do it layer-by-layer in BF16 on the streamed layer and re-quantise that layer immediately (PTQ, your existing NVFP4 path); this is the only way expert-side corrections can be absorbed on Thor, and it incurs the quant error again per edit. **[inference]**
- bitsandbytes QLoRA (NF4) is the only off-the-shelf "train through 4-bit" path; it requires re-quantising from a BF16/FP8 source layer-by-layer and whether bitsandbytes' 4-bit kernels run on Thor's aarch64/sm_110 is unverified. **[gap]**

### Gaps
- No published accuracy numbers for LoRA adapters trained against an NVFP4/MXFP4 frozen base (as opposed to NF4).
- Whether QAT-free merging of LoRA deltas into FP4 experts (re-quantise after merge) loses the heal has not been measured for MoE experts; 2607.16721's silent-rounding incident is a warning that small deltas vanish under re-quantisation.

## Ranking — quality recovered per unit of compute/data, and single-Thor feasibility for a 500B–1T student

### Takeaway
Ranked by recovered quality per Thor-hour and token, with the 122 GiB residency test applied: (1) routing-level closed-form scalar folding (ExFold-style) — gradient-free, ~10^5 tokens, layer-streamable, +7–11 avg points in the published case; (2) progressive re-ranking (prune 12.5%/stage, re-bank saliency) — forward-only, streamable, ~1/3 of the one-shot gap at 50%; (3) frozen-expert LoRA + full-router self-distillation on cached teacher logits/responses — 1–8M tokens, hours, but requires a *resident* student (feasible for GLM-5.3-Flash REAP50 at 96 GiB; **not** for a 500B student without a custom layer-streamed backward); (4) Router-KD on cached logits — cheapest gradient method, marginal on a good REAP mask; (5) continued pre-training ≥1B tokens and all neuron-slimming recipes (120–400B tokens) — infeasible on one Thor by 10–1000×.

### Cited Findings
- Sizing anchors used: Thor T5000 dense FP8 ≈ 517 TFLOPS (third-party) — [flopper.io](https://flopper.io/gpu/nvidia-jetson-agx-thor-t5000-128gb); a 10.9B-param pruned student healed with LoRA r64 "in under three hours" on "one 119 GB unified-memory device" — [arXiv 2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16721); Router KD ≈ 2 h for a 30B-A3B student at ≤1.5M tokens — [arXiv 2603.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217); ExFold ≤131k calibration tokens, no gradients — [arXiv 2608.24938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24938); ~1M tokens/round saturates for Mixtral full FT — [arXiv 2504.05586](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05586v2); 120–400B tokens for slimming recipes — [SlimMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18349v1), [SlimQwen](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08738v2), [Puzzle](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04371); REAP's layer-wise single-GPU calibration observer (2026-03-30) — [REAP README](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap).

- Per-heal-stage cost for a progressive schedule on a resident student: 4 stages × (streamed re-calibration + ~2M-token LoRA heal) ≈ 4 × (calibration pass + ~2–6 h) — a weekend job for GLM-5.3-REAP50. For a non-resident 500B student the heals need the custom streamed backward; the re-rankings do not.
- Ordering for your pipeline given the evidence: fix the corpus/criterion first (your audit: corpus imbalance, mass criterion); prune progressively with re-banked saliency; apply routing-level pair scalars (deployable in your server); then, only if the student is resident, run frozen-expert LoRA + router on cached teacher logits with a general-heavier mix. Skip router-only KD unless a per-domain check shows mis-routing rather than deleted capacity.

- No framework supports layer-streamed backprop with FP4 frozen experts on aarch64; this would be custom work comparable in scope to your CUDA server.
