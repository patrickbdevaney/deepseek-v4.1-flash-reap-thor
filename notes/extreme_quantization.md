# Extreme (2–3 bit) and mixed-precision quantization of MoE LLMs, composition with expert pruning, and the pre-quantized-FP4-experts special case

Scope note: numbers below are quoted from primary sources where possible. "TRUE bytes/weight" means on-disk bytes per parameter including scales; nominal bpw is flagged as nominal. Local measured facts from this project's memory are cited as `[local memory: file]` and are the only non-URL sources. Items marked **(extrapolation)** or **(my arithmetic)** are not in any source.

---

## KQ1. 2–3 bit methods: what they achieve on the largest models tested, true bits/weight, and whether a layer-streaming implementation exists for a 122 GiB box

### Takeaway
On dense models the 2-bit PTQ frontier is trellis/vector quantization (QTIP ≈ EXL3 ≈ ik_llama IQ*_KT) with QuIP#-style post-quant fine-tuning: Llama-3.1-405B goes 3.52 → 5.08 wikitext ppl at 2 bit (+44%) and 3.52 → 4.12 at 3 bit (+17%). On the largest MoEs actually measured (DeepSeek-R1/V3 671B, Kimi-K2 1T), the practical tools are GGUF K/IQ quants with imatrix, and the measured curve is: ~3.6 bpw +2.4% ppl, ~2.9 bpw +8%, ~2.4 bpw +25%, ~1.9 bpw +35% (Kimi-K2-Instruct, ubergarm). The one method demonstrably ported to 1T MoE layer-by-layer on commodity hardware is llama.cpp/ik_llama.cpp quantize (CPU, streaming); QTIP/EXL3 conversion runs layer-at-a-time on a single GPU but no 500B+ MoE EXL3/QTIP checkpoint with a published quality table was found.

### Cited Findings

**QTIP (trellis-coded quantization, NeurIPS 2024; basis of EXL3 and ik_llama KT types)**
- Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct wikitext2 ppl: BF16 3.52; QTIP 4-bit 3.73, 3-bit 4.12, 2-bit 5.08 (Table 8). Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct: BF16 6.50; 4-bit 6.61, 3-bit 6.80, 2-bit 7.82. Largest model quantized is 405B (dense). — [QTIP arXiv 2406.11235](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v4)
- Llama-2-70B wikitext/C4 at 2-bit: AQLM 3.83/5.62, QuIP# 3.91/5.71, QTIP 3.70/5.48 (FP16 3.12/4.97); at 3-bit QTIP 3.26/5.08. Llama-3-70B 2-bit: QuIP# 5.77 vs QTIP 4.97 (BF16 2.59) — i.e. Llama-3 is far harder to 2-bit than Llama-2. — [QTIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v4)
- The hybrid (HYB) QTIP results use QuIP#-style blockwise fine-tuning of codebook and unquantized weights; the pure computed-codebook variants (1MAD/3INST) use no fine-tuning and still beat QuIP#/AQLM at most sizes. Scale/Hadamard storage overhead is not accounted for in the nominal k bits/weight. Decode: Llama-2-70B 2-bit at 23.5 tok/s on RTX 6000 Ada, matvec at >80% of peak bandwidth. — [QTIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v4)
- A May-2026 preprint (BCJR-QAT) describes QTIP as the current frontier for 2-bit PTQ of **dense** transformers; a March-2026 study applying QTIP 2-bit to an 11B dense model reports preserved factual knowledge but "garbage tokens at sequence starts and sporadic language mixing". — [Bielik-Q2-Sharp arXiv 2603.04162](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04162); [QTIP search summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.10655)
- No source applying QTIP itself to a MoE was found (see Gaps).

**EXL3 / exllamav3 (QTIP-derived)**
- README: EXL3 is "based on QTIP"; conversion takes a target bitrate; `-hq` flag raises bitrate on attention and shared-expert layers, adding ~0.05–0.10 bpw on MoE models; MoE expert CPU offloading supported; architecture list includes DeepSeek V3, GLM 5.2, Qwen3 MoE, MiniMax-M2, GPT-OSS. Requires CUDA ≥12.4, torch ≥2.6; build extras cu124…cu132. No compute-capability list is published. — [exllamav3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/HEAD/README.md); [EXL3 doc search](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/turboderp-org/exllamav3)
- Community MoE EXL3 checkpoints exist: GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3 4.0 bpw decoder / 6 bpw head (needs exllamav3 ≥1.4.5); Inkling-Small EXL3 at 2.5 and 3.0 bpw routed experts ("runtime validation pending"); Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B official exl3 from 2.00 to 6.00 bpw. None of these cards carries a perplexity/KLD table. — [JANGQ-AI/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3](https://huggingface.co/JANGQ-AI/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3); [0xSero/Inkling-Small-EXL3-2.5bpw](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/Inkling-Small-EXL3-2.5bpw); [turboderp/Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct-exl3](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct-exl3/commit/e645279af09f9ce3b3b6b616a8d82f7f6a3b3669)
- The exllamav3 issue tracker shows three sm_120 (consumer/workstation Blackwell) **runtime** failures (illegal memory access in coop_autotune.cu #307 closed; gibberish with 2-GPU split #333 open; illegal memory access in pg_gather_kernel #245 open) and no issue mentioning Jetson/Thor/sm_110 at all. — [exllamav3 issues](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/issues?q=is%3Aissue+jetson+OR+thor+OR+sm_110+OR+sm_120+OR+blackwell)

**AQLM / QuIP# / PV-Tuning (vector quantization)**
- AQLM paper's Mixtral-8x7B table only covers 3–4 bits: at ~4 bits AQLM (3.915 b) 3.57 vs QuIP# (4.0 b) 3.60 wikitext ppl. Llama-2-7B 2-bit AQLM 7.61 (6.57 with FT) vs FP16 5.12. A community QuIP# 2-bit Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct card reports 5.28 vs 3.88 fp16 (ctx 4096). — [AQLM arXiv 2401.06118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.06118); [keyfan/Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-2bit](https://huggingface.co/keyfan/Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-2bit/blob/main/README.md?code=true)
- PV-Tuning 2.01-bit Llama-3.1-70B is listed at 5.70 in QTIP's Table 8 but in a different group (not a clean comparison). — [QTIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v4)
- Largest MoE in any AQLM/QuIP#/PV-Tuning paper table: Mixtral-8x7B (47B). No 100B+ MoE found.

**VPTQ**
- Package claims it compresses "70B, even the 405B model, to 1–2 bits without retraining"; community Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct VPTQ checkpoints exist at 1.375–1.875 bits, but no 405B perplexity table was located in the abstract/listing; paper tables cover LLaMA-2, Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3 8B/70B. — [VPTQ arXiv 2409.17066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.17066); [vptq on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/vptq)

**GPTAQ (a.k.a. GPTQv2, ICML 2025)**
- Extends GPTQ by matching each quantized layer's output to the **full-precision** model's output (asymmetric calibration) instead of the already-quantized input stream; closed-form from OBC; "20 more lines of code than GPTQ"; quantized a 405B transformer on a single GPU. No MoE-specific or 2-bit MoE numbers in the abstract. — [GPTAQ arXiv 2504.02692](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02692)

**HQQ / HQQ+**
- HQQ 2-bit g16 Llama-2-70B card: ppl 4.13 vs 4.63 for fp16 Llama-2-13B (model deprecated, old hqq version). HQQ+ (HQQ + LoRA adapter) is documented only on 1-bit Llama-2-7B-chat ("1-bit doesn't work well applied directly … when fine-tuned, output significantly improves"). No HQQ+ 70B/MoE numbers found. — [mobiuslabsgmbh/Llama-2-70b-hf-2bit_g16_s128-HQQ](https://huggingface.co/mobiuslabsgmbh/Llama-2-70b-hf-2bit_g16_s128-HQQ); [mobiuslabsgmbh 1-bit HQQ+](https://huggingface.co/mobiuslabsgmbh/Llama-2-7b-chat-hf_1bitgs8_hqq/commit/3ebba7dfc3090750013578db96a6fe16eb6d453c)

**Rotations (QuaRot/SpinQuant) on MoE**
- In MxMoE's own table, QuaRot W4A4 on MoE is catastrophic: Qwen2-MoE wikitext ppl 110.66 (fp16 5.84), Qwen1.5-MoE 18.44 (6.79), Mixtral 9.06 (3.88), DeepSeek-V2-Lite 8.44 (5.92); RTN W4A4 grid on Qwen1.5-MoE: 68079 ppl. Rotations are not a weight-only 2–3 bit lever and do not rescue W4A4 on MoE. — [MxMoE arXiv 2505.05799](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05799v1)

**GGUF K/IQ quants on 671B–1T MoEs (the only published 2–3 bit curves at this scale)**
- **Kimi-K2-Instruct (1T, BF16-source) ubergarm/ik_llama.cpp**, attention q8_0, shared expert q8_0, first dense layer q8_0 in every quant; baseline Q8_0 8.504 bpw 1016.6 GiB ppl 2.9507. IQ4_KS 4.638 bpw / 554.4 GiB / 2.9584 (+0.3%); IQ3_KS 3.604 / 430.9 / 3.0226 (+2.4%); IQ2_KL 2.923 / 349.4 / 3.1813 (+7.8%); smol-IQ2_KL 2.758 / 329.7 / 3.4086 (+15.5%); IQ2_KS 2.429 / 290.3 / 3.6827 (+24.8%); IQ1_KT 1.959 / 234.1 / 3.9734 (+34.7%); smol-IQ1_KT 1.835 / 219.4 / 4.2187 (+43%). (Percentages are my arithmetic vs Q8_0.) — [ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Instruct-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Instruct-GGUF)
- **DeepSeek-R1 (671B)** benchmark averages vs FP8 API (Table 2): Q4_K_M 4.82 bpw 377 GB −0.68%; Q3_K_M 3.81 bpw 298 GB −1.80%; UD-Q2_K_XL 2.70 bpw 212 GB −0.94%; DQ3_K_M (their mixed 3-bit) 3.59 bpw 281 GB −0.34%. On DeepSeek-V3, uniform Q2_K_L collapses (AIME 15.41 vs 38.34; average 61.51 vs 70.05) while DQ3_K_M is at parity (70.47). — [arXiv 2505.02390](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02390v1)
- The DQ3_K_M recipe: routed ffn_gate/up q3_k; routed ffn_down mostly q3_k with q4_k every 4th layer and q6_k on first two layers (75.9% q3_k / 20.7% q4_k / 3.4% q6_k); attn_kv_a_mqa, attn_kv_b, output, dense ffn_down, shared ffn_down q6_k; attn_q_a/q_b/o, shared gate/up q4_k; token_embd q4_k. — [arXiv 2505.02390](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02390v1)
- Unsloth's measured Gemma-3-27B KLD curve (the only full KLD table Unsloth publishes): IQ1_S 0.973 (6.06 GB), IQ2_XXS 0.521 (7.31), IQ2_M 0.258 (8.96), Q2_K_XL 0.221 (9.95), Q3_K_XL 0.081 (12.76), Q4_K_XL 0.024 (15.64). 5-shot MMLU for the same: IQ2_M 66.5, Q2_K_XL 68.7, Q3_K_M 70.7, Q4_K_XL 71.5, Q8_0 71.6. For MoEs Unsloth gives only prose: DeepSeek-V3.1 Dynamic 3-bit = 75.6% Aider Polyglot; Kimi-K3 Dynamic 1-bit ≈78.9% top-1 agreement at 62% smaller. — [Unsloth Dynamic 2.0 docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs)

**True bits/weight of the 4-bit block formats**
- MXFP4 = E2M1 + one E8M0 scale per 32 → 4.25 bits/weight; NVFP4 = E2M1 + one E4M3 scale per 16 → 4.5 bits/weight, plus a per-tensor FP32 global scale (negligible at real tensor sizes). — [Golden Ruler arXiv 2606.09686](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09686); [NVFP4 intro](https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/2025/07/introducing-nvfp4-for-efficient-and-accurate-low-precision-inference/)
- gpt-oss MoE weights "quantized to 4.25 bits per parameter" and are ~90% of parameters. — [centron gpt-oss explainer](https://www.centron.de/tutorials/openai-gpt-oss-explained-architecture-mxfp4-quantization-120b-20)
- Measured whole-checkpoint NVFP4 figures: Motif-3 0.594 B/w, Inkling-Small 0.642, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 0.737, our own GLM-5.3-Flash-REAP50 NVFP4 109.4 GB / 165B = 0.663 B/w; "use 0.62" for estimates. The gap to 0.5625 (4.5 b) is attention/embeddings/lm_head left at higher precision. — [local memory: thor-base-model-shortlist.md]
- GGUF bpw figures above are true on-disk (ik_llama reports size/params), e.g. Q4_0 experts = 4.5 bpw, Q8_0 = 8.5 bpw. — [ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Thinking-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Thinking-GGUF)

### Inferences
- At the 1T-MoE scale, the measured quality cliff sits between ~2.9 and ~2.4 bpw on routed experts (Kimi-K2: +7.8% → +24.8% ppl), and uniform Q2 on DeepSeek-V3 collapses while a 3.59-bpw mixed recipe is lossless on benchmarks. The safe zone for routed experts without healing is ≈3.0–3.6 true bpw; 2.4–2.8 bpw is usable only with the "smol"/dynamic recipes and costs 15–25% ppl.
- Trellis types (QTIP/EXL3/IQ*_KT) are ~0.3–0.5 bit more efficient than K-quants on dense models, but that advantage is not demonstrated above Mixtral size for MoE; the 1.83–1.96 bpw IQ1_KT rows on Kimi-K2 (+35–43% ppl) are the only ≥500B trellis datapoints.
- Everything in this class that has been run on 1T MoE was produced by llama.cpp/ik_llama.cpp `quantize` streaming over a BF16 GGUF on CPU; this is the only pipeline proven at 1T on a 122 GiB-class box.

### Gaps
- No QTIP, AQLM, QuIP#, PV-Tuning, VPTQ, HQQ+ or GPTAQ paper reports any MoE above Mixtral-8x7B; no EXL3 checkpoint ≥500B with a quality table was found.
- EXL3 scale overhead (true vs nominal bpw) is undocumented (doc/exl3.md returned 404 on both raw and blob URLs).
- exllamav3 on sm_110 (Thor) is unverified anywhere; sm_120 has open runtime-crash issues.

## KQ2. Per-expert mixed precision (fewer bits for rarely-routed / low-saliency experts)

### Takeaway
Expert-level or block-level mixed precision consistently beats uniform bits at matched average budget (MxMoE: −2.4 ppl vs GPTQ at 2.25 bit on Qwen1.5-MoE; Collaborative Compression: 130 GB mixed DeepSeek-V3 ≈ 230 GB uniform Q2_K_L on MMLU/GSM8K), but all academic methods are demonstrated on ≤107 GB models except one llama.cpp-based pipeline on DeepSeek-V3/R1; frequency is the common proxy and newer work argues it is wrong (router shift, calibration mismatch).

### Cited Findings
- **MxMoE (ICML 2025)**: allocates bits per linear block (gate/up/down) within each expert, not per expert — block-level beats expert-level (DeepSeek-V2-Lite W5A5: 6.11 vs 6.32 ppl). Wikitext ppl at 2.25-bit weight-only: DeepSeek-V2-Lite GPTQ 8.49 → MxMoE 7.01; Qwen1.5-MoE 11.19 → 8.79; Qwen2-MoE 7.98 → 7.57; Mixtral 5.69 → 5.63. At 3.25-bit: 6.18/6.08, 7.15/7.02, 6.11/6.18, 4.17/4.15 (GPTQ/MxMoE). Avg accuracy at 2.25 bit: DeepSeek-V2-Lite 58.04 → 62.04, Qwen1.5-MoE 49.07 → 55.39, Qwen2-MoE 58.58 → 64.57. Uses expert activation frequency from calibration (>10× variation within one block) in a cost model and generates mixed-precision group-GEMM kernels; 1.6–2.7× throughput memory-bound. Largest model Qwen2-MoE-Instruct (106.9 GB). Code: github.com/cat538/MxMoE. — [MxMoE arXiv 2505.05799](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05799v1)
- **MoPEQ (Sept 2025)**: per-expert bit width via Hessian-trace sensitivity (not frequency) + expert clustering; 2/3/4-bit mixes; models DeepSeek-VL2 tiny/small/base and MolmoE (VLMs). No numbers in abstract. — [MoPEQ arXiv 2509.02512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02512v1)
- **Collaborative Compression (Sept 2025)** — the only mixed-precision pipeline demonstrated on 671B: prune experts (score 0.5·frequency + 0.5·mean routing score, keep 75% → ~508B), set top-k 8→6, then llama.cpp imatrix quant with IQ1_M base and tensor-level upgrades to Q6/Q8 under a memory budget (routed experts never upgraded, may be downgraded to IQ1_S; k_b, v_b, token_embd, output = Q8). Full DeepSeek-V3: IQ1_M uniform 140 GB → MMLU 75.45 / GSM8K 83.4; Q2_K_L uniform 230 GB → 85.57 / 93.4; their mixed 130 GB → 84.55 / 93.56. Combined pruned+quant model 103 GB runs >5 tok/s on a 128 GB Strix Halo laptop. Sizes in the paper are internally inconsistent (103 vs 130 GB; 210 vs 220 GB for R1). No pruning-only or quant-only ablation. — [arXiv 2509.25689](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25689v1)
- **DQ3_K_M (arXiv 2505.02390)** is a layer/tensor-level (not expert-level) mixed recipe on R1/V3; at 3.59 bpw it beats Q4_K_M (4.82 bpw) on average accuracy (83.03 vs 82.70 on R1). — [arXiv 2505.02390](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02390v1)
- **GEMQ (May 2026)**: expert bit allocation as an LP using activation frequency on the FP model as importance proxy; argues layer-wise estimates ignore router shifts caused by quantization and adds router fine-tuning; main model Mixtral-8x7B. — [GEMQ arXiv 2605.23078](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.23078)
- **BitsMoE (May 2026)**: SVD shared-basis + ILP; 2-bit Qwen3-30B-A3B = 64.29% avg on 7 tasks, +2.80 pp over GEMQ, 16.5× faster quantization than GEMQ, up to 6.46× GPTQ decode throughput. — [BitsMoE arXiv 2606.00079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00079)
- **AlphaQ (ICLR 2026)**: calibration-free — scores experts by heavy-tailed spectral statistics of their own weights (HT-SR), arguing calibration sets are poor proxies for proprietary frontier training mixes; Qwen1.5-MoE at 3.5 avg expert bits "near full-precision" with >4× compression; models DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Qwen1.5-MoE, Mixtral. — [AlphaQ ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10017148)
- **MODE (June 2026)**: modality-aware frequency ranking + sensitivity term + ILP for multimodal MoEs; notes frequency "does not fully align with how much its quantization degrades model performance". — [MODE arXiv 2606.17118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17118)
- Unsloth Dynamic: "dynamically adjusts the quantization type of every possible layer"; Kimi-K2.6 Dynamic 2-bit "upcasts important layers to 8-bit", 350 GB (−43%); no per-expert allocation is described. — [Unsloth Kimi K2.6](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/kimi-k2.6.md); [Unsloth Dynamic 2.0](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs)
- Local: non-uniform per-layer expert COUNTS are unshippable (single scalar `num_local_experts`), but per-expert/per-layer **bit width** has no such constraint in GGUF (per-tensor types) — the ubergarm/DQ3_K_M recipes are exactly that. — [local memory: reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md]

### Inferences
- The deployable form of mixed precision on Thor today is GGUF per-TENSOR type selection (llama.cpp `--tensor-type`, ik_llama custom-q regex), which is per-layer-per-projection granularity: GGUF stores all routed experts of a layer fused in one 3-D `ffn_{gate,up,down}_exps` tensor with a single quant type, so per-EXPERT bit width within a layer is not expressible without a format change; MxMoE/GEMQ-style per-expert allocation therefore needs a custom kernel (MxMoE ships one for Ada, nothing for sm_110). **(inference from the GGUF layout used in the ubergarm/DQ3_K_M recipes above)**
- Our own banked saliency (`artifacts/saliency/*.pt`, out_sum, norms per expert) is exactly the input MxMoE/GEMQ/MoPEQ need; a REAP-saliency-driven per-expert bit map could be evaluated offline with no GPU the same way the mass-criterion audit was.
- Reported savings from expert-level allocation at matched budget are ~0.2–2.4 ppl at 2.25 bit (≤107 GB models); at 671B the only datapoint says mixed 130 GB ≈ uniform 230 GB.

### Gaps
- No paper evaluates per-expert mixed precision on any ≥200B MoE except via llama.cpp tensor-level upgrades; expert-level allocation on DeepSeek-V3/Kimi-K2 is unreported.
- MoPEQ/GEMQ full numeric tables were not retrievable (abstract-only pages).

## KQ3. Pre-quantized FP4/MXFP4/INT4 experts at source (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, MiMo-V2.6, gpt-oss, Kimi-K2-Thinking)

### Takeaway
(a) Pruning a quantized checkpoint is confirmed as plain tensor removal (REAP ran on Kimi-K2-W4A16 and Qwen3-Coder-480B-FP8; NVIDIA ModelOpt writes pruned gpt-oss with packed MXFP4 experts). (b) Nobody converts FP4 → 2-bit without going through BF16, but the BF16 round trip is lossless for block-FP4; the real cost is the second quantizer, and the only measured proxy (ubergarm Kimi-K2-Thinking INT4-source vs Kimi-K2-Instruct BF16-source, same recipes) shows ≈ +2–6 pp extra ppl at 2.75–2.9 bpw and ≈ +30 pp extra at 1.83 bpw. (c) The byte floor is not fixed at 4 bpw — ggml-org requantized MiMo-V2.6-Flash MXFP4 gate/up experts to Q2_K (167 → 126 GB) — but no one has published quality for it; Unsloth explicitly refused to requantize gpt-oss experts and only shrank attention. Non-expert BF16 parts of a 1T model are ~11–12B params (~23 GB BF16) and NVFP4 on them recovers ~15–16 GB.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Pruning quantized checkpoints**
- REAP: "pruning can be easily applied to quantized models without any additional steps"; calibrated, pruned and evaluated Qwen3-Coder-480B in FP8 and Kimi-K2 in W4A16; 50% expert pruning of the W4A16 Kimi-K2 gives 87.5% total size reduction vs BF16. Merging, by contrast, "necessitates re-quantization for block quantization formats" because shared block scales must be reconciled. Kimi-K2-W4A16 50% REAP: Eval+ 0.828 → 0.819, Code Avg 0.631 → 0.624, SWE-bench 0.554 → 0.576, but MC Avg 0.780 → 0.643 and BFCL multi-turn 0.355 → 0.164. — [REAP arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v3)
- NVIDIA ModelOpt `gpt_oss_pruned_to_mxfp4`: "Extracts MoE expert weights from the original gpt-oss-120b in MXFP4 format … Outputs a new pruned (heterogeneous) checkpoint with PACKED MXFP4 expert weights"; experts are matched by comparing weights; only `convert_moe_packed_tensors` dequantizes (for the forward pass). — [ModelOpt docs](https://nvidia.github.io/Model-Optimizer/pr-preview/pr-1190/reference/generated/modelopt.torch.puzzletron.anymodel.models.gpt_oss.gpt_oss_pruned_to_mxfp4.html)
- NeMo AutoModel keeps frozen routed-expert base weights packed as fp4-e2m1 + e8m0 block scales and dequantizes on the fly in training. — [NeMo quantized_experts](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/automodel/latest/nemo-automodel/nemo_automodel/components/moe/quantized_experts)
- Local: MiMo-V2.6-Flash `quantization_config.store_dtype: "mxfp4"`, experts = 158 GB of U8 (92.9%); `o_proj` in `ignored_layers` (3.2 GB BF16 read/token); REAP-50 = 94.7 GB confirmed by an existing MLX REAP50 artifact of the same model, i.e. pruning the MXFP4 checkpoint worked. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash `expert_dtype: "fp4"`, 510.3 GB = routed experts 288.8 + Engram 203.1 (FP8) + MTP 7.9 + everything else 10.4. — [local memory: mimo-v26-flash-thor.md; thor-base-model-shortlist.md]

**(b) FP4 → 2-bit, double quantization**
- ubergarm Kimi-K2-Thinking (native INT4 QAT source) went "from the original model to full bf16 before further quantization"; the Q4_X baseline uses q4_0 routed experts "patched to better fit the original QAT target quantization", no imatrix, and matches a full 1 TB Q8_0 test quant. All lower quants use an imatrix computed from Q4_X. — [ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Thinking-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Thinking-GGUF)
- Unsloth: Kimi-K2-Thinking "was originally released in INT4 format" so 4–5-bit dynamic GGUFs are already full precision. — [Unsloth K2 Thinking guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/kimi-k2-thinking-how-to-run-locally)

- Kimi-K2 Q4_X decomposition (routed q4_0 = 4.5 bpw, everything else q8_0 = 8.5 bpw) totals 543.6 GiB. **(my arithmetic from the public Kimi-K2 config: hidden 7168, 61 layers, 64 heads, q_lora 1536, kv_lora 512, qk 128+64, v 128, moe_inter 2048, 384 routed + 1 shared, dense inter 18432, vocab 163,840)**: routed experts = 384 × 60 × 3 × 7168 × 2048 ≈ 1.015 T params → 531 GiB at 4.5 bpw; non-routed ≈ attention 6.2 B + shared experts 2.6 B + dense layer-0 MLP 0.4 B + embed+head 2.35 B + routers 0.17 B ≈ **11.7 B params** → 11.6 GiB at q8_0; sum 543 GiB, matching ubergarm's 543.6 GiB. At BF16 that is 23.4 GB; at the measured 0.62 B/w NVFP4 rate ≈ 7.3 GB → **≈16 GB recovered**, i.e. ~3% of the checkpoint. — [ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Thinking-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Thinking-GGUF); [local memory: thor-base-model-shortlist.md]
- DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash measured: "everything else" (attention, dense, embeddings) = 10.4 GB of 510.3 GB; Engram (203 GB) is FP8 per paper and only offloadable to host, which on Thor is the same pool. — [local memory: thor-base-model-shortlist.md]; [DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969)
- Local measured on GLM-5.3-Flash: NVFP4 overlay on the BF16 dense/attention weights = 3.70 GiB saved, B_tok −50.6%, +1.56% ppl, 0.138% confident flips; MiMo `o_proj` overlay would take 3.2 → 0.8 GB/token read. — [local memory: glm53-nvfp4-dense-overlay.md; mimo-v26-flash-thor.md]

### Inferences
- **(a) Confirmed**: pruning experts from FP4/MXFP4/INT4 checkpoints is tensor removal with per-expert block scales removed alongside; both REAP (W4A16, FP8) and ModelOpt (MXFP4) do exactly this. The only thing that breaks is merging, not pruning.
- **(b)** The BF16 "round trip" is numerically free: an E2M1 value (≤2 significant bits) times an E4M3 (3-bit mantissa) or E8M0 scale has ≤5 significant bits, which BF16's 8-bit mantissa represents exactly (NVFP4's extra FP32 tensor scale adds ≤2⁻⁸ relative rounding). So "FP4 → 2-bit without a BF16 round trip" buys nothing; the loss is entirely the second quantizer operating on a 16-level-per-block input. **(extrapolation)**
- **(b)** Double-quantization penalty, using the two ubergarm Kimi tables as the only matched-recipe proxy (caveat: Thinking vs Instruct are different fine-tunes with different baseline ppl, 2.08 vs 2.95, and different imatrices): at smol-IQ2_KL (2.755 vs 2.758 bpw, identical recipe) +17.9% vs +15.5% → ~2.4 pp extra; at IQ2_KL (2.92) +14.0% vs +7.8% → ~6 pp extra; at smol-IQ1_KT (1.83) +72.6% vs +43% → ~30 pp extra. Reading: a 4-bit-QAT source costs little down to ~2.75 bpw and a lot below ~2.3 bpw, consistent with the second quantizer's grid being coarser than the 16-level source only above ~2.3 bits. **(inference)**
- **(c)** For MiMo-V2.6-Flash the ggml-org Q2_K precedent shows the expert byte floor is NOT 4.25 bpw mechanically, but it is unmeasured, imatrix-less, and still 126 GB; the lever that is both measured and shippable on Thor is REAP-50 (94.7 GB) + NVFP4 overlay on `o_proj`/attention. Routed experts at native MXFP4 on 50% of experts beats requantizing 100% of experts to Q2_K by the 14× pruning-vs-quant rule only if the Q2_K penalty on a 4-bit source exceeds ~21% ppl — which the Kimi proxy says it does at ≤2.3 bpw (+41–73%) but not at 2.75 bpw (+18%). The decision hinges on where MiMo's `Q2_K` lands, which nobody has measured.
- **(c)** On a 1T model the non-expert BF16 share is ~2–3% of bytes; NVFP4-ing it recovers ~15–20 GB, which is a KV-budget-sized win, not an envelope-sized one. It is worth doing for bandwidth (B_tok), per the local overlay result, more than for capacity.

### Gaps
- No published perplexity/KLD for any requantized-below-4-bit MXFP4-source expert set (gpt-oss, MiMo). The ggml-org MiMo Q2_K is the only artifact and it has no numbers and no imatrix.
- DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash released expert FP4 block format (scale type/block size) was not found in the paper text fetched; the local `expert_dtype: "fp4"` + 288.8 GB for routed experts is the only hard datum.
- No paper measures double quantization (FP4-source → 2–3 bit) directly; the Kimi-K2 proxy above is cross-model.

## KQ5. Quantization-aware healing: does the order (heal→quantize vs quantize→heal) matter, with measurements?

### Takeaway
Measured evidence says: heal (distill/fine-tune) the pruned model in high precision, then quantize last, and if the target is ≤2-bit, finish with QAT/post-quant fine-tuning initialized from the healed weights. The one systematic ordering study found P→KD→Q at ppl 5.05 vs any quantize-first order at 24–53 (Qwen2.5-3B, NF4), and ParetoQ shows ≥3-bit QAT is a small "compensation" (10–20% weight movement, ~10B tokens) while 2-bit is "reconstruction" (~40% movement, ~30B tokens) — so 2-bit healing is a different, much larger job than 3-bit. Frontier practice (Kimi-K2-Thinking INT4 QAT, DeepSeek-V4.1 FP4-KV QAT, Multiverse Quasar prune→heal→QA-quant) is consistent with this.

### Cited Findings
- Systematic compression-ordering study (Qwen2.5-3B student, Qwen2.5-7B teacher; 30% structured FFN pruning; NF4 4-bit; KD α=0.3, T=4): ppl Base 3.418; KD 3.226; P 5.086; Q 3.955; **P-KD-Q 5.048; KD-P-Q 5.553; P-Q-KD 5.597; KD-Q-P 53.37; Q-KD-P 24.07; Q-P-KD 34.49**. Conclusion: quantization must come last; early quantization injects "irreversible" noise into subsequent training (and NF4 models must be dequantized to train at all). — [arXiv 2511.19495](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19495v1)
- Olive (Microsoft) quantize-before-vs-after LoRA fine-tuning on Phi-3.5-mini and Llama-3.1-8B (AWQ/GPTQ, 4-bit implied): quantizing **before** fine-tuning gives better results than quantizing after, for both models; "quantization has no significant impact on quality relative to the fine-tuned unquantized model" — chart-only, no numbers in text; this is QLoRA-style (quantized base + trainable adapter), i.e. the adapter heals around a frozen quantized base. — [onnxruntime Olive blog](https://onnxruntime.ai/blogs/olive-quant-ft)
- ParetoQ (NeurIPS 2025, models 125M–3B): QAT above 2-bit is "compensation" (weights move 10–20%, near-FP accuracy after ~10B tokens); at ≤2-bit it is "reconstruction" (weights move ~40%, saturates ~30B tokens); best budget split ≈90% FP pretraining / 10% QAT; QAT fine-tuning from FP beats both PTQ and QAT-from-scratch at every bit width; LLaMA-3B avg zero-shot: FP 65.2, 4-bit 65.4, 3-bit 64.5, 2-bit 63.2, 1.58-bit 61.9 (wikitext ppl 8.0/8.4/9.1/9.9). — [ParetoQ arXiv 2502.02631](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02631v2)
- QTIP/QuIP#: post-quantization blockwise fine-tuning of codebooks + unquantized params is what makes the 2-bit "HYB" numbers; computed-codebook variants without FT are slightly worse. — [QTIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v4)
- HQQ+: 1-bit Llama-2-7B is unusable direct from PTQ and "significantly improves" with a LoRA adapter trained on the quantized model. — [mobiuslabsgmbh HQQ+](https://huggingface.co/mobiuslabsgmbh/Llama-2-7b-chat-hf_1bitgs8_hqq/commit/3ebba7dfc3090750013578db96a6fe16eb6d453c)
- Multiverse Quasar-438B (from GLM-5.2): order = expert pruning (265 → 148 experts/layer, ~44%) → one healing pass targeted at agentic/coding → "quantization-aware compression" to FP8 and NVFP4 (explicitly not PTQ). No data/token counts or benchmarks given. Company blog. — [Multiverse Quasar 438B](https://multiversecomputing.com/papers/inside-quasar-438b-by-multiverse-computing)
- Kimi-K2-Thinking: "native INT4" via QAT in the post-training stage, credited with a lossless ~2× low-latency speed-up. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: FP4 global KV via QAT "during post-training", "only marginal performance degradation". — [DeepInfra K2 Thinking](https://deepinfra.com/moonshotai/Kimi-K2-Thinking); [DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969)
- Gemma-3 QAT (Google) vs Unsloth PTQ at matched GGUF type: at low bits QAT does not help PTQ-GGUF (IQ2_XXS 59.20 PTQ vs 56.57 QAT+PTQ MMLU; Q2_K_XL 68.70 vs 67.77) — the QAT target (Google's int4) does not transfer to a different 2-bit quantizer. — [Unsloth Dynamic 2.0](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs)
- GEMQ argues quantization shifts the router and adds router fine-tuning to the quantized experts. — [GEMQ](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.23078)
- Local: every quant produces a different final hidden state (IQ3_M vs IQ4_XS cosine 0.891, NVFP4 vs IQ3_M 0.901, vs IQ4_XS 0.928, same-quant control 0.999); a draft head trained on one quant does not transfer. FP8 teacher logits are banked so KD healing on the REAP50 token set is feasible without re-running the 321 GB teacher. — [local memory: glm53-quant-hidden-state-drift.md; reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md]

### Inferences
- Order for our pipeline: REAP (on the BF16/FP8 checkpoint) → KD heal in BF16 against banked FP8 teacher logits → quantize last. If the target is ≤2.5 bpw on experts, add a short post-quant heal (QuIP#/QTIP-style blockwise FT of unquantized tensors + norms, or a LoRA on the frozen quantized base per Olive/HQQ+) — but ParetoQ's ~30B-token "reconstruction" regime at 2-bit is far beyond a Thor budget; the Thor-feasible heal is the ≥3-bit "compensation" regime (10–20% weight motion).
- The Gemma QAT datapoint plus the local hidden-state drift finding both say a QAT target is quantizer-specific: healing for NVFP4 does not heal IQ2_KS, and a Kimi-style INT4 QAT does not protect a later 2-bit requant (consistent with the +30 pp extra ppl at 1.83 bpw in KQ3).
- The only measurement that directly ranks orders (2511.19495) is 3B-dense with NF4; its 10× ppl penalty for quantize-first is dominated by NF4 dequant-to-train, so the magnitude is not transferable, the direction is.

### Gaps
- No MoE-scale (≥100B) ablation of heal→quant vs quant→heal exists; Quasar's pipeline is undisclosed.
- No source quantifies how many tokens a 2–3 bit post-quant heal needs on a ≥100B MoE.

## KQ6. KV-cache quantization (FP8/INT4 KV, MLA latent quantization) as a long-context lever

### Takeaway
FP8 on the MLA latent is production-proven at 671B (SnapMLA: ≤2.5 pt worst-case benchmark delta, 1.91× decode throughput; RoPE part must stay BF16 or be handled per-token), FP4 KV is proven only with QAT (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, 890 B/token), and post-training INT4/FP4 KV on MLA is measured only on a 16B model (+3.0% ppl) — larger than Llama (+2.8%) and Gemma (−0.7%). On Thor, vLLM offers fp8_e4m3/e5m2 KV only; SGLang has experimental fp4_e2m1; llama.cpp has q8_0/q4_0 cache types but MLA-cache behaviour there is undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- SnapMLA (Meituan, Feb 2026): DeepSeek-V3.1 (671B/37B) and LongCat-Flash-Thinking (560B); MLA latent (content) part → FP8 E4M3 with per-token scales, RoPE part kept BF16 because FP8 raised RoPE MSE ~10×; deltas vs BF16 FlashMLA on DeepSeek-V3.1: MMLU-Pro +0.02, IFEval +0.93, Arena-Hard −1.60, MATH-500 −0.60, AIME-24 −0.20, AIME-25 −2.50, GPQA-Diamond −1.58, LiveCodeBench −0.72; up to 1.91× end-to-end decode throughput at 16k–128k context. INT4/sub-FP8 latent "future work". — [SnapMLA arXiv 2602.10718](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10718)
- DeepSeek-V3 MLA KV = 70 KB/token vs 192–328 KB/token for GQA models (2.7–4.7× less), but "architectural compression alone is insufficient in high-concurrency inference" hence quantization on top. — [DeepSeek-V3 hardware insights arXiv 2505.09343](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09343)
- Post-training Q4 KV cache (safetensors, restored into attention): Gemma-3-12B −0.7% ppl, Llama-3.1-8B +2.8%, DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite (16B MoE, MLA, 27 layers) +3.0%; ~4× more agent contexts per device. — [arXiv 2603.04428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04428)
- DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: global KV cache FP4 "E2M1 with one E4M3 scale per 16 channels" (NVFP4 minus the global scale, "no measurable decrease in accuracy" from omitting it), quantized after RoPE, trained with QAT in post-training, "only marginal performance degradation"; SWA KV stays FP8; 890 B/token global KV (~1/4 of V4-Flash), persistent cache ~1/8 via SWA Bounded Replay. — [DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969)
- vLLM `kv_cache_dtype`: auto / fp8 / fp8_e4m3 / fp8_e5m2 only; scales default to 1.0 unless calibrated with llm-compressor; per-head scales only with the Flash Attention backend; FA3 runs attention in FP8 with FP8 queries; sliding-window layers are "more sensitive" and can be skipped via `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers`. No INT4/FP4, no MLA statement. — [vLLM quantized KV cache docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/quantization/quantized_kvcache.html)
- SGLang `--kv-cache-dtype` adds `fp4_e2m1` (experimental); accuracy "depends on model architecture, sequence length, and format", smaller models show larger FP4 drops. — [SGLang quantized KV cache](https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/quantized_kv_cache.html)
- Vultr DeepSeek-V3.2 guide: on-the-fly FP8 KV via vLLM; "standard KV cache offloading is not supported (different cache format) for MLA models". — [Vultr DeepSeek V3.2](https://docs.vultr.com/inference-cookbook/cuda/model-guides/deepseek-v32)
- KV-offloading survey: INT8 KV ≈2× with minimal loss; FP4 ≈4×. — [arXiv 2601.19910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19910)
- Local KV budgets: MiMo-V2.6-Flash 11,520 B/token (9 global-attention layers; 39 SWA layers window-capped) → 11.5 GB at 1M; DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 ~8.8 KiB/token; V4.1-Flash 890 B/token; on Thor our FA3 build crashes and MLA requires TRITON_MLA. — [local memory: mimo-v26-flash-thor.md; thor-base-model-shortlist.md; mistral-small4-thor-stack.md; dflash-thor-build.md]
- Local: GLM-5.3 DSA is a no-op below 2048 ctx; sparse attention (DSA/QSA) "cuts COMPUTE not KV STORAGE". — [local memory: glm53-cuda-server-build.md; thor-base-model-shortlist.md]

### Inferences
- For MiMo-V2.6-Flash the 1M-context KV is 11.5 GB; FP8 latent would bring it to ~5.8 GB (a ~6 GB envelope gain, enough to move REAP-40 from "misses by 0.6 GB at 1M" into fit), FP4-with-QAT to ~3 GB. The measured accuracy cost of FP8 on 671B MLA is ≤2.5 pt worst case; PTQ-FP4 on MLA has no ≥100B measurement and the 16B datapoint already shows MLA is the most sensitive of the three families tested. **(my arithmetic on local KV figures)**

## Cross-cutting synthesis for the 1T-on-122-GiB decision (inferences, not findings)

- Measured compression economics (local): REAP50 = +21.0% ppl; one GGUF bit-width step (Q4_K_S → Q3_K_M) = +1.46%; NVFP4 = +1.56%; pruning ≈14× more damaging per unit of compression. The public 1T curve above (Kimi-K2: 4.6→3.6 bpw costs +2.1 pp ppl; 3.6→2.9 costs +5.4 pp; 2.9→2.4 costs +17 pp; 2.4→1.96 costs +10 pp) says the quant axis stays cheaper than REAP50 down to ≈2.9 bpw (+7.8% total) and roughly breaks even with REAP50 at ≈2.4 bpw (+24.8%). So for a BF16/FP8-source 1T model the cheapest route to ~0.37 B/w is **IQ2_KL-class (2.9 bpw) experts + q8_0 attention, no pruning**, and pruning should only start once experts are already at ~2.9 bpw. — [local memory: reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md]; [ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Instruct-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Instruct-GGUF)
- For a 1T model at 2.9 bpw: 1.015 T routed × 2.92/8 ≈ 370 GB + 12 GB non-expert — still 3× the envelope; even smol-IQ1_KT (1.83 bpw, +43%) is 219 GiB. A 1T MoE therefore needs BOTH ~2.9 bpw experts AND ≥65% expert pruning to reach 122 GiB, which is above the >50%-prune rejection line; this is consistent with the local fit-frontier conclusion that frontier open weights are 3–12× over the envelope. — [local memory: thor-model-fit-frontier.md; thor-base-model-shortlist.md]
- For FP4-at-source models (V4.1-Flash, MiMo-V2.6), the quant lever is not "spent" mechanically — gate/up requant to Q2_K is demonstrated by ggml-org — but it is spent **economically**: the only matched proxy says a 4-bit-QAT source loses ~2–6 pp extra at 2.75–2.9 bpw and ~30 pp extra at 1.83 bpw, and nothing below 4.25 bpw on these experts has a published quality number. Treat sub-4-bit requant of MXFP4 experts as an experiment to run (MiMo Q2_K gate/up + MXFP4 down, imatrix, KLD vs MXFP4), not a plan input.
