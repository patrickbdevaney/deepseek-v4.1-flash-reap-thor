# Calibration-corpus domain proportions: dose-response from data-mixing laws and calibration-composition studies

Scope note: every data point below carries model scale, compression method and ratio. "Measured" = reported in a paper or our own run; "Model/Extrapolation" = our inference. The companion note `calibration_and_evaluation.md` (KQ1/KQ2) already covers token-budget saturation and licence-clean sources; this note is only about the share → retention relationship and the allocation it implies.

## KQ1. Functional form: what pretraining data-mixing laws say about proportion → per-domain loss, and whether it transfers to calibration-for-pruning

### Takeaway
Every published mixing law agrees that per-domain loss is a smooth, saturating, (near-)convex function of that domain's share — exponential-of-linear (Ye et al.), power law in own share (BiMix), power-law bias term (Apple), or a capacity-competition term where a domain's "allocated capacity" scales sublinearly with its weight (Dai & Zheng, ICML 2026). The capacity-competition law is the one that transfers to pruning: a fixed expert budget is a fixed capacity N, and the calibration share decides how that capacity is allocated; the "noise-reduction" term (statistical estimation of a domain's saliency) saturates within ~10k prompts / 25 demos and is irrelevant at our budgets.

### Cited Findings
- Data Mixing Laws (Ye et al.): per-domain validation loss L_i(r) = c_i + k_i·exp(Σ_j t_ij·r_j), "an exponential function over the linear combination of the proportions"; two-domain form L_i(r_i) = c_i + k_i·exp(t_ii·r_i). Fit on 70M/160M/305M/410M models trained on 30B tokens (RedPajama → Pile validation), target 1B/100B tokens; 2, 3 and 5 Pile domains. The law is designed to permit non-monotone behaviour: "a monotonic relationship between losses and the proportion of any domain indicates that a lopsided mixture can achieve minimum loss." A negative t_ij "indicates that training data of domain j helps reduce validation loss on domain i." MAE on held-out mixtures 0.004–0.037 nats. Optimal mixture is scale-dependent: "the optimal mixture at experimented scales can be suboptimal at the target scale." No minimum-share statement. — [arXiv 2403.16952](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16952)
- BiMix: L_i(r_i, s) = (A_i / r_i^α_i)·(B_i / s^β_i + C_i); at fixed steps "L_i(r_i | s) = Ã_i / r_i^α̃_i ... This straight line on a logarithmic scale strongly supports the standard power-law function." Each domain's loss depends only on its OWN proportion (cross-domain coupling only through the unit-sum constraint). 280M and 1B models (DoReMi configs), Pile 22 domains and SlimPajama 7 domains; relative error at final step 0.16–0.19% mean, worst 0.95%; R² 0.97–0.99. — [arXiv 2405.14908v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14908v3)
- Apple "Scaling Laws for Optimal Data Mixtures": additive law L = E + 1/(Σ_i C_i h_i^γ_i) + A/N^α + B/D^β (5+2k params) — weights enter as a power h_i^γ_i; joint law lets A and B depend on h (5+4k). Scales 186M–7B (150B tokens at 7B), multimodal 100M–8B, vision up to 1B. Under the additive law the optimal weights are "independent of the model size N and the number of tokens D"; under the joint law the optimum is compute-dependent ("interleaved data gets less important as we increase D, whereas bigger models tend to rely more on text"). ~10 runs for 4 domains, ~20 for 6–8 domains to reach optimal MRE; MRE 0.6–2.0%. Single-target optimum "typically lie[s] at that domain's corner of the simplex" (h*_i ≈ 1). No analysis of weights near zero. — [arXiv 2507.09404](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09404)
- "Explaining Data Mixing Scaling Laws" (Dai & Zheng, ICML 2026): L_i(h | N, D) ≈ c_i·x_i*(h,N)^(−b_i) + A_i·(D·h_i)^(−a_i) + E_i, where x_i* is domain i's share of finite capacity from min_x Σ_i h_i c_i x_i^(−b_i) s.t. Σ(x_i − H) ≤ N − H; closed form x_i*(h) ≈ (b_i c_i h_i N^(b̄+1))^(1/(b_i+1)) / (Σ_k (b_k c_k h_k)^(1/(b_k+1)))^((b̄+1)/(b_i+1)) — i.e. allocated capacity grows sublinearly (≈ h_i^(1/(b+1))) with weight and is coupled to every other domain's weight through the denominator ("Capacity Competition, where the allocation of finite model capacity couples domain losses globally"). The noise term (D h_i)^(−a_i) "breaks the symmetry," "shifting weight toward domains that are 'harder to learn'." Fit on 64 × 1B-param models (25B tokens, 17 Pile domains): MRE 1.53% (5K params) vs Exponential/Ye 6.99%, BiMix 2.96%, RegMix 6.48%, Additive 2.21%. Observations with h_i = 0 are excluded from fits "to prevent division by zero." — [arXiv 2606.08167](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08167)
- RegMix: 512 proxy models of 1M params × 1B tokens; LightGBM beats ridge regression on every metric; Spearman ρ of predicted vs true mixture ranking 98.45% (1M), 98.64% (60M), 97.12% (1B / 25B tokens) — "directly validates" rank invariance across scale. Proxy gains saturate "after approximately 0.25B tokens." "The relationship between domain weights and validation loss is more complex than scaling laws might imply" (non-linear interactions; PhilPapers "appears to provide gains for all other domains" under a linear fit). Derived mixture put 0.87 on Pile-CC vs 0.142 human-selected. — [arXiv 2407.01492](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01492)
- Aioli: linear dynamic mixing law L_i^(t+1)(p) = L_i^t(p) − Σ_j A_ij^t p_j^t; both static log-linear (MSE 8.9e-4, R² 0.991) and linear dynamic (MSE 1.0e-4, R² 0.947) "capture the true loss-proportion relationship"; prior methods lose to stratified (uniform) sampling because "existing methods set their mixing law parameters inaccurately." 160M models, SlimPajama 7 groups; Aioli beats stratified on 6/6 datasets by 0.274 ppl on average. — [arXiv 2411.05735](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05735)
- DoReMi: 280M proxy under Group DRO sets weights for an 8B model; +6.5 points few-shot accuracy, baseline reached with 2.6× fewer steps; "DoReMi improves perplexity across all domains, even when it downweights a domain." — [arXiv 2305.10429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10429)
- ODM (Online Data Mixing): bandit over domains; reaches next-best method's perplexity "with 19% fewer training iterations", +1.9% relative 5-shot MMLU. — [arXiv 2312.02406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02406)
- Survey framing: the exponential form is a "log-linear static mixing law"; Data Mixing Laws and RegMix "were designed for a fixed (N, D) and do not include any scale features." — [Data Mixing survey, arXiv 2604.16380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.16380)

### Inferences
- Transfer mapping (model, not measured): in pretraining the mixture changes the weights; in calibration-for-pruning the weights are fixed and the mixture changes only (i) the statistical estimate of each expert's saliency and (ii) which experts clear the prune line. (i) is Dai & Zheng's noise term (D·h_i)^(−a): it saturates at ~25 demos (99% of domain experts captured, [2504.06792]) / ~128 × 2048 tokens (0.1–0.2% fluctuation, [2410.17711]) / ~10k prompts (HOPE mask stability) and is negligible at ≥250k tokens per domain. (ii) is exactly the capacity-competition term with N = surviving experts: under a frequency-weighted criterion (our `mass` criterion) an expert's score is Σ_d s_d·M_dj, linear in the shares, and the number of experts a domain "keeps" is a sublinear, saturating function of its share coupled to every other share through the common prune line. So the pruning analogue of a mixing law is: retention_d(s) = R_d,max − (R_d,max − R_d,0)·g(s_d / s*_d) with g saturating, where s*_d is a domain-specific half-saturation share set by how concentrated (specialist) that domain's routing is.
- Which published form to use: BiMix's own-share power law is the simplest and is what the 2606.03328 points (KQ2) look like above the knee; but below the knee the pruning curve is steeper than any pretraining law because expert survival is a hard threshold (an expert is either kept or not) — pretraining laws have no discontinuity, pruning does. Expect sigmoid-like behaviour at low share, power-law/saturating above it.
- RegMix's rank-invariance (97% Spearman from 1M-param proxies to 1B) is the published justification for choosing a calibration mix on a cheaper MoE (e.g., a 35B or our 122B) and transferring the ranking of mixtures, not the absolute numbers, to the 1T target. Not verified for pruning.
- Aioli's diagnosis ("laws are right, parameters are mis-set") is the argument for fitting the share→retention curve from our own banked per-bucket accumulators rather than from literature priors.

### Gaps
- No paper fits any mixing law to calibration-for-pruning. The transfer above is a model.
- Data Mixing Laws' Appendix G error figures and BiMix's fitted exponents were not in the fetched text; the exponent that would set the slope of the "own-share power law" is unreported.

## KQ2. Measured sensitivity of each domain's eval to its calibration share (pruning and quantization)

### Takeaway
The only true multi-point dose-response in the literature is arXiv 2606.03328 (LLaMA-3.1-8B, SparseGPT 60%, 128 × 2048 tokens): math retention vs math share runs 14.5 (0%) → 21.8 (16.7%) → 37.8 (25%) → 45.1 (50%) → 52.2 (100%), code 0.4 (0%) → 23.5 (16.7%) → 52.3 (25%) → 59.8 (50%), while general is flat at 58–61 for any share ≥16.7% and only drops (to 46–51) at 0%. The curve is steep between ~17% and 25% and flat above — but at that budget 16.7% is only ~21 sequences, so share and absolute count are confounded. MoE-specific single-source results (REAP, HOPE, MoE-XBench, biomedical, coding-specialist) agree on the asymmetry: specialist capabilities (code, math) collapse when their share → 0, and general knowledge collapses when ITS share → 0 at ≥50% pruning but barely moves at 30%.

### Inferences
- Shape (from the 2606.03328 points, SparseGPT 60%, 8B): for a specialist capability the response to its own share is sigmoid-then-plateau — knee between ~17% and 25%, +7 pts from 25% → 50%, and only +7 more from 50% → 100%. For general text the response is flat above ~17% (58–61 for 16.7%, 25%, 100%) and drops ~10–15 pts only at 0%. Commonsense is insensitive everywhere (81.8–85.7). Because the 16.7% points sit at ~21 sequences / 43k tokens, part of the knee may be the dense-pruning sample floor (saturation is reported only from 64 sequences up); the share effect proper is cleaner at 25% vs 50% vs 100%, which is a concave saturating curve.
- Asymmetry rule: displacing a specialist pool from 25% to 16.7% cost −29 (code, Math-heavy) and −16 (math, Code-heavy), whereas raising a pool from 25% to 50% gained +7–7.5. Loss from going below the knee is 2–4× the gain from going above it, so the sum-of-retentions objective is maximised near equal shares (Jensen on a concave curve) — this is the mechanism behind "uniform is near-optimal."
- Ratio dependence of the general-share collapse in MoE (different models, same criterion family): at REAP r = 0.30 on Qwen3.6-35B, 0% general share (code-only) cost 1.5 MMLU-R points; at 50% on Qwen3.5-122B it cost 44 MMLU points (65.15 → 20.87); at 50% keep on Qwen3.6-35B even a 30% general share left general ppl +131% (and +496% at 37.5% keep). Off-domain damage grows superlinearly with prune ratio at fixed share.
- Our two internal points (4.76% → 0.49; 26–28% → 0.71–0.75) are consistent with the literature shape: 4.76% is far below the knee and 26–28% is on the plateau. We have no point between 5% and 26%; the pass-3 rebalance will be the first.
- GPTQ-style second-order methods are far more calibration-sensitive than activation-statistic methods (SparseGPT spread 18.8 vs Wanda 4.4; GPTQ +9.8 vs AWQ +1.8). Expert pruning by routed-token saliency is an activation-statistic method, but the collapse numbers above show it is on the SparseGPT side of sensitivity because expert survival is all-or-nothing.

### Gaps
- No MoE paper sweeps a domain's share at more than two levels; all MoE evidence is single-source vs mixture.
- The 2606.03328 Math-heavy/Code-heavy weights are ambiguous (1:1:1:3 per Table 12 vs "4:3:3" config names); the per-dimension sparsity sweep (Table 15/E.9) and the 70B per-source table are not in the fetched text.
- REAP Figs A8/A9 (C4 vs domain vs "general" 3× mix) are image-only; the general-mix numbers are not extractable.
- Our note does not record the code/science/vision/finance token shares of the shipped GLM-5.3-Flash run, so only two of seven internal points are usable.

## KQ3. Minority-domain collapse: the threshold share below which a domain's experts stop being selected

### Takeaway
No paper measures a share threshold for expert selection. The measured anchors are: 0% share collapses the domain's experts at ≥50% pruning (code at C4 calibration: 0.0 accuracy instances; general at code-only: MMLU 21); 4.76% share retained only 49% of the domain's saliency mass (ours, 50%); ~17% share (at a tiny absolute budget) cost a specialist pool 16–29 points vs 25% (dense, 60%); ≥25% is on the plateau. A routing-statistics model puts the threshold at the share where a pure specialist's share-weighted mass falls below the layer's median score.

### Cited Findings
- REAP: a global frequency-weighted average "may be dominated by usage frequency and risks pruning specialist experts which are rarely activated but contribute significantly to the layer output when selected"; frequency pruning "prunes experts with the lowest usage frequency across the calibration dataset" (usage = number of calibration tokens that activate the expert). — [arXiv 2510.13999v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v3)
- REAP-pruned model cards: performance "may be uneven across tasks, domains, or languages not well covered in the calibration set"; 25% pruning "removes the least-used experts; rare-domain performance may be affected." — [OpenMOSE Qwen3-VL-REAP card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSE/Qwen3-VL-REAP-145B-A22B)
- Coding-specialist paper: with 2,047 samples / 3.15M tokens, "Every expert of every layer received traffic during profiling on Qwen (minimum layer-expert coverage 100%); on Gemma the minimum is 99.9%" — so zero-evidence experts are not the mechanism at ≥3M tokens; ranking is. — [arXiv 2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- Few-shot: single-sample calibration unstable; 25 demos reach 99% overlap with the full domain expert set (DeepSeek-R1). — [arXiv 2504.06792](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06792)
- Ours: at 4.76% share, a bucket-balanced re-mask recovered only +0.028 retained mass for general — "re-masking cannot create information the calibration never collected"; the `bio` bucket got 0 tokens. — [memory: reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md)
- Dense evidence at 0% share: C4-only calibration gives Code 0.41 (LLaMA SparseGPT 60%) and 1.31 (Wanda); MetaMath-only gives General 46.20 vs 60.74. — [arXiv 2606.03328](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03328)
- Pretraining analogue: Dai & Zheng exclude h_i = 0 from fits because the (D·h_i)^(−a) term diverges; the gradient of the noise term scales as h_k^(−a_k−1) (strong upward pressure on small-weight domains). — [arXiv 2606.08167](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08167)

### Inferences
- Model (routing statistics, mass criterion). Let s_d be domain d's share of routed tokens and m_dj the mean per-token saliency of expert j on domain-d tokens times its routing probability. Score_j = Σ_d s_d·m_dj. A pure d-specialist (m_dj ≈ 0 for d' ≠ d) survives iff s_d·m_dj ≥ θ_L, the layer's prune line (the median score at 50%, the 70th percentile at 70%). Its collapse share is s*_j = θ_L / m_dj. Because θ_L is the median of the share-weighted scores of all experts, θ_L itself scales with the majority domains' shares, so raising any domain lowers every other domain's survival margin — the capacity-competition coupling of KQ1. With 7 equal pools, a specialist needs m_dj ≥ 7·θ_L, i.e. must be ~7× as salient per token as the median share-weighted expert; at 4.76% it needs 21×. Our 0.49 retained mass at 4.76% says roughly half of general's mass sat in experts that failed the 21× test.
- Under the frequency-invariant mean criterion (shipped mask), share still matters, via within-expert composition: an expert shared by general and agentic tokens has its mean dominated by the majority domain's token norms, and experts that are general-only are evaluated on few tokens (noisier means). This is why the mean criterion also collapsed general at 4.76%.
- Threshold estimate for our setting (model): the knee in 2606.03328 lies at 17–25% of a 4-pool budget; expressed as "share relative to equal share" that is 0.67–1.0× the uniform share. Translating to 7 pools: a domain below ~10% (0.7× uniform) is at risk, below ~5% is measured to lose half its mass, and 0% loses the specialists outright. Minimum share recommendation: no domain below 10%, general not below the largest other pool.
- Absolute floor (measured, different models): ≥25 demonstrations (99% domain-expert capture) ≈ 50–100k tokens; dense saliency saturates by 64 × 2048 ≈ 131k tokens. At 5M total tokens even a 10% pool (500k) is 4–10× above these floors, so for us the threshold is a ranking (share) effect, not a sampling effect.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of share → fraction-of-domain-experts-surviving for any MoE; the model above is unverified. The pass-3 corpus and the banked per-bucket accumulators are the only way to measure it (see KQ7).

## KQ4. Equal-by-domain vs usage-proportional; cases where equal weighting hurt the majority domain

### Takeaway
Equal-by-domain dominates usage-proportional and every single source on the total (dense: +4.6 to +18.8 over C4, +0.4 to +8.8 over the best single source; MoE: the REAP "official mixture" is "the safest general-purpose default"), and the price paid by the majority domain is small and bounded: general loses 0.5–2.8 points and math loses 2–14 points relative to its own single-source optimum. The one systematic loser under uniform mixing is the domain with the steepest single-source curve (math), not the majority domain.

### Cited Findings
- LLaMA-3.1-8B SparseGPT 60%: Balanced General 59.69 vs C4 60.74 (−1.05); Math 37.76 vs MetaMath 52.16 (−14.40); Wanda General 40.89 vs 41.72 (−0.83), Math 15.75 vs GSM8K 18.03 (−2.28). Qwen2.5-7B SparseGPT: General 72.97 vs 75.77 (−2.80), Commonsense 91.45 vs 92.56 (−1.11), Math 68.65 vs 79.20 (−10.55); Wanda General −0.51, Math −5.24. Code improves over every single source in every block. Total: Balanced 58.78 vs best single 50.02 (LLaMA), 74.00 vs 65.34 (Qwen); 70B SparseGPT 75.15 vs 69.33, Wanda 66.63 vs 64.39. — [arXiv 2606.03328](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03328)
- Held-out benchmarks (SparseGPT 60% total): C4 44.37 / MetaMath 56.56 / Balanced 64.40 (LLaMA); 56.11 / 64.46 / 72.34 (Qwen). — [arXiv 2606.03328](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03328)
- Stratified-random baseline (uniform over pools, random within) 49.20 vs IGSP 57.08 vs Balanced 58.78 (LLaMA SparseGPT 60%); IGSP ablation "no stratification" −12.28. — [arXiv 2606.03328](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03328)
- MoE-XBench REAP r = 0.30: the official mixture is best on none of the seven benchmarks individually (C4 wins GSM8K/MMLU-R, math-only wins MuSR/HumanEval/Math-Hard/IFEval, code-only wins MBPP) yet "avoids the largest single-domain failures" (HumanEval 0.281 under C4, MBPP 0.148 under the official mixture is its own worst cell). — [arXiv 2608.21693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21693)
- DeepSeek-R1 few-shot: mixed-domain 61.47 vs domain-specific 66.55 vs full 67.20 — mixing costs 5 points of average vs calibrating on the single evaluated domain. — [arXiv 2504.06792](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06792)
- Biomedical 75%: adding an equal general slice raised general utility .387 → .597 but lowered MedINST .476 → .424 and doubled biomedical hallucination (.179 → .358). — [arXiv 2607.01444v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01444v1)
- Pretraining: DoReMi "improves perplexity across all domains, even when it downweights a domain"; Aioli: prior adaptive methods fail to beat stratified (uniform) sampling. — [DoReMi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10429); [Aioli](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05735)
- REAP's own recipes are not usage-proportional: 12,228 agentic/tool samples + 4,096 Mixture-of-Thoughts (code/math/science) for ≥110B models; practitioner VL-REAP used 6,144 / 6,144 / 4,096 / 2,730 / 2,730 / 2,732 across six text/code sources (roughly 25/25/17/11/11/11%). — [REAP appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2); [atbender card](https://huggingface.co/atbender/Qwen3.6-VL-REAP-26B-A3B-W4A16)

### Inferences
- "Equal hurt the majority" exists but is small: general gives up ≤3 points (≤5% relative) to buy +20 to +50 points of code/math. The domain that pays most under equal weighting is the one whose single-source curve is steepest at the top (math: −10 to −14 vs MetaMath-only) — because its own share drops from 100% to 25%, not because it is the majority.
- Usage-proportional weighting has no published test; our 4.76%-general run is effectively a usage-proportional corpus (agentic+math = 54%) and it is the worst general-retention outcome in this note. The 2606.03328 result that 1:1:1:3 tilts are strictly worse on total than 1:1:1:1 is the closest published refutation of usage-proportional allocation.
- At ≥75% pruning the trade becomes zero-sum (biomedical result): equal weighting then costs the priority domain measurably, which is the one regime where a deliberate tilt toward the deployment domain is justified.

### Gaps
- No MoE paper compares equal-by-domain against a usage-proportional mix on the same model; the comparison is inferred from single-source vs mixture cells.

## KQ5. Interaction with prune ratio: does the mix need to shift as ratio rises 50% → 70%?

### Takeaway
Measured: the value of balance rises with ratio (Balanced − C4: +4.6 at 30% → +18.8 at 60%; MoE: 0% general share costs 1.5 MMLU at 30% but 44 at 50%), and off-domain damage at fixed share grows superlinearly with ratio (general ppl +11% / +131% / +496% at 25/50/62.5% pruned with 30% general share). At ≥75% the pools become zero-sum (adding general recovers general but costs the domain). So from 50% → 70% the mix should NOT tilt away from general; it should hold general at parity and, if anything, drop the lowest-priority specialist pools rather than thin all of them — but our own 14× pruning-vs-quant damage ratio says the right move is to stay at 50%.

### Cited Findings
- Balanced − C4 (LLaMA-3.1-8B SparseGPT): +4.62 (30%), +9.62 (40%), +17.17 (50%), +18.79 (60%); Qwen2.5-7B +3.10 → +23.89. — [arXiv 2606.03328](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03328)
- Source gap "from a 0.5% difference at 50% sparsity to 2.3% at 60% sparsity" (DCLM-7B, Wanda). — [arXiv 2410.17711](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17711v2)
- Qwen3.6-35B-A3B REAP r = 0.30, code-only: MMLU-R 0.796 vs official 0.811 (−1.5 pts). — [arXiv 2608.21693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21693). Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, 50%, code-only: MMLU 20.87 vs base 65.15 (−44). — [HOPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v3)
- Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, 70/30 code:general, general-chat ppl 5.20 → 5.80 (75% keep) → 12.00 (50%) → 30.99 (37.5%) → 30.92 (25%); code ppl 5.38 → 5.42 → 5.49 → 5.89 → 5.45. — [arXiv 2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- Biomedical: in-domain "highly robust up to 50%–75% pruning before collapsing suddenly"; general declines "immediately"; Qwen3.6 EASY-EP general .689 (50%) → .387 (75%); dual-domain at 75% general .597 but MedINST .424 (vs .476). — [arXiv 2607.01444v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01444v1)
- HOPE's advantage over REAP grows with prune rate (mean +2.8%, up to +6.1% at 40–50%; "clearest gap at aggressive rates"). — [HOPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v3)
- Pretraining: optimal mixture is scale-dependent under Ye et al. and under Apple's joint law; scale-independent under Apple's additive law. — [2403.16952](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16952); [2507.09404](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09404)
- Ours: REAP50 = +21.0% ppl vs one GGUF bit-width step +1.46% / NVFP4 +1.56%; non-uniform layer allocation gain shrinks with ratio (−4.98% at 50%, −3.83% at 65%, −3.24% at 75%). — [memory: reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md)

### Inferences
- In the capacity-competition picture, raising the ratio lowers N (surviving experts) so every domain's allocated capacity x_i* shrinks and the prune line θ_L rises; the specialist survival condition s_d·m_dj ≥ θ_L tightens for every domain at once. There is no share re-allocation that keeps all seven on the plateau at 70% — only a choice of which domains fall off it. The measured zero-sum behaviour at 75% is this.
- Direction: because general degrades first and fastest (biomedical "immediate" decline; +131% ppl at 30% share), it is the domain whose plateau is narrowest in ratio, so a 70% run must raise general's share, not lower it, to hold the same retention. The published numbers suggest general at the largest-bucket level is necessary but may not be sufficient at 70%.
- Practical: 50% → 70% at fixed mix would reproduce the HOPE/REAP MMLU-21 regime on general unless general is held at ≥ parity; given our 14× damage ratio the recommended answer is to spend the extra compression on quantization and keep the mix for 50%.

### Gaps
- No paper varies both ratio and mixture on one MoE model; the ratio interaction is assembled from different models and criteria.

## KQ6. Vision / multimodal share

### Takeaway
No source quantifies how much image-text calibration keeps vision experts. Practitioner VL-REAP releases calibrate on text/code only and report no multimodal benchmarks; LIMoE found image tokens spread across nearly all experts (so vision experts are hard to isolate and text experts are the prunable ones), MAEP (NeurIPS 2026) shows modality-agnostic importance mis-ranks experts that serve both modalities, and our own run retained only 0.682 of vision mass — the second-lowest bucket after general.

### Cited Findings
- LIMoE appendix: experts ranked per modality by tokens processed; least-used text experts pruned first; "Image tokens are spread across nearly all experts, so image experts are much harder to prune." — [arXiv 2206.02770](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.02770)
- MAEP (InternVL3.5-30B-A3B, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, +1 unnamed): "Existing pruning methods aggregate importance uniformly across tokens, which overlooks that one expert can serve different roles for vision and language processing"; text-side importance preserves, vision-side importance prunes; ~27 GB (~47% of memory) of BF16 expert weight removed; "outperforms modality-agnostic baselines on the majority of multimodal benchmarks while remaining competitive on text-only tasks." No calibration counts or ratios in the abstract. — [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/148334)
- MoDES (CVPR 2026, Qwen3-VL-30B): shallow MoE layers far more sensitive to reduced top-k; 97.33 avg at 88% expert skipping vs 86.66 best prior. — [arXiv 2511.15690](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15690v2)
- FastMMoE: visual tokens need fewer activated experts; up to 55% FLOPs cut at ~95.5% performance (DeepSeek-VL2, InternVL3.5). — [arXiv 2511.17885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17885)
- OpenMOSE Qwen3-VL-REAP-145B-A22B: ~40% experts pruned with a "mostly synthetic" text calibration set generated by Qwen3-235B-Instruct; image-text counts not reported; multimodal benchmarks "still work in progress"; "Complex compositional visual reasoning or extremely high-resolution images may not work reliably." — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSE/Qwen3-VL-REAP-145B-A22B)
- atbender Qwen3.6-VL-REAP-26B-A3B: 25% pruned, 24,576 samples at seqlen 16,384 from six text/code sources, no image-text samples; "Vision encoder is preserved structurally but was not re-calibrated post-prune"; "VL quality may differ from the base model." — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/atbender/Qwen3.6-VL-REAP-26B-A3B-W4A16)
- Ours: vision bucket retained 0.682 (mean criterion) / 0.755 (mass criterion) at 50%; blocked chunk order had placed image/audio/video at 74–85% of the pass. — [memory: reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md); [memory: calibration-corpus-visit-order](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/calibration-corpus-visit-order.md)
- Apple multimodal mixing law: "interleaved data gets less important as we increase D, whereas bigger models tend to rely more on text." — [arXiv 2507.09404](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09404)

### Inferences
- If LIMoE's finding generalises (visual tokens diffuse across many experts), vision mass is spread rather than concentrated, so vision has few pure specialists and is LESS share-sensitive than code/math under a mass criterion — but it also means vision tokens vote for many experts and, counted by routed tokens, an image-heavy sample contributes far more routed tokens than a text sample. Both effects argue for budgeting vision by routed LLM tokens (image tokens included), not by sample count, and for a share no lower than the text domains' (≈ 1/7).
- Crowd-out: because vision tokens are diffuse, raising vision's share raises θ_L broadly rather than promoting a vision-specialist set; above parity it mostly costs the text specialists. No measurement supports going above ~15%.

### Gaps
- No paper reports image-text vs text-only calibration shares, nor multimodal benchmark deltas for a text-only-calibrated expert-pruned VL-MoE. Our vision share and its token count are not in the memory note.

## KQ7. Concrete allocation for ~5M calibration tokens across 7 domains, and the per-domain minimum

### Takeaway
Evidence supports a near-uniform mix with general at parity-plus: general 20%, agentic 15%, code 15%, vision 14%, math 12%, science 12%, finance 10% (≈1.0M / 0.75M / 0.75M / 0.7M / 0.6M / 0.6M / 0.5M routed tokens), every pool ≥10% and ≥500k routed tokens, stratified chunk order, and shares measured in routed tokens not bytes. Tilts beyond ~1.4× uniform buy ≤7 points on the tilted domain and cost 16–29 on the displaced ones, so the allocation stays inside the measured plateau; the exact curve should be fitted offline from the banked per-bucket accumulators before pass 3.

### Cited Findings
- Uniform 1:1:1:1 beats 1:1:1:3 and 1:1:3:1 on total at 60% (58.78 vs 53.46 / 55.90 LLaMA; 74.00 vs 69.47 / 68.05 Qwen); "multi-dimensional coverage ... not precise ratio tuning"; "The uniform default is both simple and near-optimal"; IGSP uses 32 samples per dimension. — [arXiv 2606.03328](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03328)
- Saturation floors: 64–2048 × 2048 tokens fluctuate 0.1–0.2% (dense, DCLM-7B) — [2410.17711](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17711v2); 25 demos capture 99% of domain experts (DeepSeek-R1, 671B) — [2504.06792](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06792); HOPE/REAP masks "stabilize within 10k prompts" (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, 50%) — [HOPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v3); 3.15M tokens gave 100% layer-expert coverage on Qwen3.6-35B (256 experts) — [2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721).
- REAP ≥110B recipe is ~75% agentic / ~25% code+math+science by sample (12,228 + 4,096) and still ships; practitioner mix 25/25/17/11/11/11 across six text sources. — [REAP appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2); [atbender card](https://huggingface.co/atbender/Qwen3.6-VL-REAP-26B-A3B-W4A16)
- Our banked artifacts hold per-bucket accumulators (`norm_sum/norm_sq_by_bucket`, `sq_by_bucket`, `out_sum`) and `sum_saliency/count` reproduced the shipped mask 6048/6048, so any re-weighting of bucket shares can be re-masked at zero GPU cost; but "a bucket-balanced re-mask recovers only +0.028 for general" because the 4.76% slice collected too little. — [memory: reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md)
- Chunk order must be stratified so every bucket's first chunk precedes any bucket's second; worst-domain objectives are degenerate when any bucket is empty. — [memory: calibration-corpus-visit-order](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/calibration-corpus-visit-order.md)
- Mass criterion "makes the mask more corpus-dependent than the published mean, so it INTERACTS with the pass-3 corpus rebalance and the two must be validated jointly." — [memory: reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md)

### Inferences
- Allocation A (strict evidence, no deployment prior): general 16%, each of the other six 14% — the published "uniform near-optimal" rule with general raised to the largest bucket so it cannot be the minority.
- Allocation B (recommended; deployment tilt kept inside the plateau): general 20% / agentic 15% / code 15% / vision 14% / math 12% / science 12% / finance 10%. Rationale per pool: general at 20% is the largest bucket by construction (ours: 4.76% → 0.49; HOPE 0% → MMLU 21; 2606.03328 general flat above 17%, so 20% is on the plateau with margin). Agentic and code at 15% because they are the deployment target and the specialist curves gain ~+7 for a 2× share increase (so 1.07× uniform buys little but costs nothing). Vision at 14% (parity) budgeted in routed tokens including image tokens (KQ6). Math at 12%: it has the steepest single-source curve in 2606.03328 but GSM8K held at 94.7 under code-only calibration in HOPE, i.e. math experts are co-activated by code; 12% is 0.84× uniform, above the ~0.7× risk line. Science 12% and finance 10%: smallest priority, held at the floor; finance at 10% is 0.7× uniform, the lowest share the 2606.03328 knee permits (and 500k tokens, 4–10× the sampling floors).
- Minimum per-domain: ≥10% share AND ≥250k routed tokens (≈2× the dense saturation floor, ≈2.5–5× the 25-demo floor). Below 5% a domain is measured to lose half its mass; 0% loses its specialists at ≥50%.
- Fit the curve before committing: with per-bucket accumulators, synthesise retention_d(s) for s ∈ {5, 10, 14, 20, 30%} by re-weighting buckets offline and re-masking (minutes, zero GPU). Caveat from our own audit: this measures the ranking (share) effect only; a bucket that was under-collected (general at 4.76%) will under-report its true retention at higher synthetic shares, so the offline curve is a lower bound for under-sampled buckets and exact for well-sampled ones.
- Iterate like DoReMi/ODM, not like a static law: after pass 3, upweight buckets by retained-mass deficit (w_d ∝ exp(η·(1 − retention_d))) with a floor of 10% and a ceiling of 25%, and carry an untouched equal-share control (Aioli's lesson that mis-set parameters, not the law, is what loses to uniform).
- Measure shares in routed tokens through the MoE (what the saliency actually integrates), not bytes or samples; reasoning traces and image-heavy samples are 5–20× longer than chat turns.

### Gaps
- No published allocation for a 7-domain MoE calibration; B is a synthesis, and its tilts (1.4× for general, 0.7× for finance) are the only parameters without a direct measurement.
- Whether vision's routed-token count should include image tokens at full weight is unmeasured; LIMoE-style diffuseness could make image tokens over-vote.
- The RegMix rank-invariance (proxy → target) has not been tested for calibration mixtures, so a cheaper-model sweep is a hypothesis, not a plan.
