# Expert pruning of MoE LLMs beyond 50%: measured ratio-vs-quality frontier, criteria, schedules, allocation, router handling

Scope note: all numbers below are from the primary papers / model cards named, read directly (arXiv HTML or pdftotext of the arXiv PDF) on 2026-10-17. "pt" = absolute benchmark points; "rel" = relative to the unpruned base. "Prune ratio" = fraction of routed experts removed per layer unless stated. Baseline to beat for this project: GLM-5.3-Flash 321B, 288 routed experts top-8, REAP-50 (144 kept) + HOPE + per-expert healing.

One sentence up front: no primary source reports a general-purpose (multi-domain, MC/knowledge-preserving) MoE surviving >50% expert pruning within 10% of base; every >50% result in the literature is a domain specialist (code, math, translation) evaluated only in-domain, and the best of those (75% pruned) needed a healing pass and still paid 4–6% rel on the target domain while general ability collapsed (ppl +131% to +500%). No paper measured an iterative prune→heal→prune schedule for MoE experts.

## Q1. Highest reported prune ratio at ≤5% and ≤10% degradation, per model family, and which method

### Takeaway
One-shot, uniform-per-layer REAP/HOPE tops out at 50% and is "near-lossless" only on the calibration domain (code/agentic): Qwen3-Coder-480B loses 1.7 pt Code Avg and 5.8 pt MC at 50%, while fine-grained general models (Qwen3-30B-A3B, GLM-4.5-Air, Kimi-K2) lose 14–22 pt on multiple-choice at the same ratio. Beyond 50% the only measured points are domain specialists: DeepSeek-R1 math specialist at 62.5% (MATH-500 −0.6 pt, but GPQA −8.6 pt and LiveCodeBench −8.1 pt), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B code specialist at 62.5% (HumanEval+ −3.0 pt n.s., MBPP+ −3.9 pt) and 75% (HumanEval+ −10.3 pt, recovered to −4.9 pt after a LoRA+router self-distillation heal), and GPT-OSS-20B translation specialist at 75% after synthetic-distillation recovery (xCOMET −0.039, −4.1% rel) and 87.5% (−0.071, −7.5% rel).

Other relevant cited points:
- REAP tested only 25% and 50%; "All models are evaluated in the one-shot setting, with no additional fine-tuning after compression." Calibration: ≤110B models 1,024 samples × 2,048 tokens; ≥110B 12,228 samples up to 16,384 tokens — [REAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- REAP's text summary: Qwen3-Coder-480B mean non-agentic coding decrease 0.16% (25%) and 1.2% (50%); mean MC decrease for Qwen3-30B and GLM-4.5-Air "approximately 4% and 13%" at 25% and 50% — [REAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- Cerebras blog quotes 97.6% retained non-agentic coding and 96.7% SWE-Bench on Qwen3-Coder-480B at 50%; no results above 50%; "iterative pruning schedules" listed as future work — [Cerebras blog](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/reap)
- HOPE tested 10/20/25/30/40/50%; maximum 50%; "At 50% pruning, HOPE ... average rank of 1.58 out of 5 methods (versus 2.42 for REAP), with gains of up to +6.1% on agentic coding"; HOPE beat REAP in all agentic experiments at 40–50%, mean +2.8% — [HOPE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- Half-the-Experts: "The sharp break comes only at 25% keep"; "perplexity ... can rate a broken model above an intact one"; pruning beats quantizing the full model only where quantization would have to drop below 3 bits/weight — [Half the Experts](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- PreMoe: "math is the most robust domain ... GPQA and LiveCodeBench degrade first"; "the safe pruning budget varies by model, so 50% is not universally lossless"; DeepSeek-R1 at 75% runs on 16 NPUs vs 64 (180.5B params) — [PreMoe](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17639)
- Martin et al.: weight VRAM −45.6% at 50%, −68.5% at 75%, −79.9% at 87.5%; on Qwen3-30B-A3B "results at higher pruning rates favored REAP and Koishekenov over the full method" — [Martin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28042)

### Inferences
- Thresholds, general-purpose model, no healing: ≤5% rel on MC/knowledge is only met at 25% (Qwen3-Coder-480B, Kimi-K2, Llama-4-Scout, Mixtral); ≤10% is met at 50% only for Llama-4-Scout (−6.6% rel MC), Mixtral (−8.6%), Qwen3-Coder-480B (−7.7%). Fine-grained 128–256-expert general models (Qwen3-30B, GLM-4.5-Air, Kimi-K2) exceed 10% MC loss already at 50%. Extrapolation: GLM-5.3-Flash (288 experts) is in the fine-grained class, so its general-domain loss at 50% is expected to be in the 15–20 pt MC range, matching the observed ballast dNLL 1.00.
- Thresholds, single-domain specialist: ≤5% in-domain is met at 50% without healing (code on Qwen3-Coder/Qwen3.6-35B, math on DeepSeek-R1, MT on GPT-OSS), at 62.5% for math only (MATH-500 −0.6 pt, but the paired GPQA/LCB already lose 8–12 pt), and at 75% only with healing (MT −4.1% rel; code −5.5% rel). ≤10% in-domain is met at 68.75% one-shot for MT (−8.7%) and at 87.5% for MT with distillation (−7.5%). Extrapolation: 60–65% is the ceiling for a one-shot code/math specialist inside 5%, and 75% is the ceiling with a heal.
- The dominant loss above 50% is off-domain: general-chat perplexity on the code specialist rises 131% at 50% and ~500% at 62.5–75%, GPQA on DeepSeek-R1 drops 28 pt at 75%. For a Thor model expected to serve general chat plus code, these >50% specialists are not acceptable artifacts.
- Criterion matters more as ratio rises: at 75% on Qwen3.6-35B, REAP 0.787 vs MAN 0.720 vs routing-mass/random collapse; at 50% on DeepSeek-R1, REAP-like Act-Logits 62.9 vs Frequency 49.9 vs random 5–27. But the winner flips by family (routing mass beats REAP on Gemma-4 and GPT-OSS; REAP beats routing mass on Qwen3.6 and Qwen3-30B), so no criterion can be assumed to hold for GLM-5.3-Flash without a measured head-to-head.

### Gaps
- No primary source reports a general-purpose (multi-domain) pruned model above 50% with any benchmark table; Hy3-REAP-48e (75%) and Kimi-K3-REAP (50%) have no evaluations.
- HOPE/REAP never tested above 50%; HOPE's authors state the advantage grows with ratio but there is no >50% data point.
- GLM-4.6-REAP-218B (40%) card says evals "TBD"; the FP8 card was not fetched.
- Martin et al.'s no-retraining numbers at 75% and 87.5% were not in the fetched text (only recovery-tuned numbers); the Qwen3-30B-A3B per-ratio table was not extracted.

## Q2. Does iterative/progressive prune→heal→prune reach a higher total ratio at equal quality than one-shot?

### Takeaway
No paper or repo measured prune→heal→prune for MoE experts. The only controlled progressive-vs-one-shot comparison (SlimQwen, Qwen3-Next-80A3B→23A2B, 400B tokens continual pretraining) staged depth/width and found +0.4 to +2.6 pt on MMLU-family benchmarks but −3.3 pt BBH and −1.0 pt EvalPlus, with 3 stages no better than 2; healing after a single aggressive prune recovers roughly half of the in-domain gap (52% at 75% on Qwen3.6-35B; 68.75% MT: −0.082 → −0.038) and HOPE's full-parameter 1B-token SFT "narrows the gap but does not close it."

### Cited Findings
- SlimQwen Table 5 (all pruned to 23A2B from Qwen3-Next-80A3B; one-stage = 400B tokens; progressive = 40B + 360B): MMLU 75.86 one-stage → 76.30 joint / 77.14 width-first / 77.39 depth-first; MMLU-Pro 52.97 → 53.12 / 52.80 / 53.22; MMLU-Redux 75.41 → 76.93 / 77.07 / 78.01; BBH 73.97 → 71.40 / 75.22 / 70.70; GSM-8K 85.22 → 86.05 / 84.00 / 85.82; EvalPlus 70.07 → 70.58 / 71.40 / 69.08; C-Eval 83.87 → 83.57 / 82.01 / 82.97; CMMLU 82.26 → 82.62 / 82.76 / 83.01 — [SlimQwen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08738)
- SlimQwen Table 9: three-stage schedules (20B + 20B + 360B) "achieve performance comparable to the two-stage setup ... a two-stage progressive pruning strategy is already sufficient"; depth-first 3-stage MMLU 77.29, width-first 3-stage 76.46 — [SlimQwen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08738)
- SlimQwen's progressive schedules are defined over depth and width only ("Depth-first allocates half of the layer reduction to the first stage ... Width-first executes half of the width reduction in the first stage"); the expert reduction is 512→256 (50%) via partial-preservation merging with 1,024 calibration samples — [SlimQwen §3, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08738)
- SlimQwen Table 2: after 400B tokens of continual pretraining, one-shot expert methods converge: REAP pruning MMLU 69.11 / EvalPlus 53.59 vs merging variants 68.73–69.74 / 48.00–55.29; "no single one-shot pruning or merging method establishes" dominance — [SlimQwen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08738)
- SlimQwen Table 1: pruned init + KD after the same budget: MMLU 75.67 vs random-init 65.06 (teacher 85.22) — [SlimQwen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08738)
- HOPE §4.5: full-parameter SFT on Qwen3.5-122B-A10B 50%-pruned checkpoints, LiveCodeBench traces from the unpruned model, lr 1e-6, ~2M tokens/step, 500 steps (~1B tokens): "HOPE outperformed REAP both before and after SFT ... fine-tuning narrows the gap but does not close it" (numbers only in Figure 6) — [HOPE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- Half-the-Experts healing at 75% pruned: HumanEval+ 0.787→0.841 (52% of the 10.3-pt gap), MBPP+ 0.659→0.685 (23%), general ppl 30.9→9.4; routed experts frozen; "parameter-efficient recovery ... In the coding domain, with the routed experts held frozen" suffices, "in pointed contrast to the translation-domain finding of Martin et al. (2026) (their §4.3: 'parameter-efficient methods are insufficient to recover entire pruned parameter blocks')" — [Half the Experts §5.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- Martin et al.: no-retraining 68.75% → −0.082 xCOMET; with synthetic distillation at the same 68.75% → −0.038 (54% of gap recovered); supervised FT on FLoRes recovers less (−0.038 at k=22 but −0.130 at 87.5% vs −0.071 with distillation) — [Martin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28042)
- MoE-Pruner (2024, weight-level not expert-level): Mixtral-8x7B 50% sparsity with expert-wise distillation "keeping 99% of original performance" — [MoE-Pruner](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12013) (search summary; not read in full)
- Cerebras lists "iterative pruning schedules" as future work, unmeasured — [Cerebras blog](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/reap)
- Task-Specific Expert Pruning (2022, Switch Transformer era) "progressively drop the non-professional experts for the target downstream task" to a single expert, "preserve 99.3% benefits from MoE across six ... tasks" — [Task-Specific Expert Pruning](https://deepai.org/publication/task-specific-expert-pruning-for-sparse-mixture-of-experts) (search summary; fine-tuned, task-specific, not a general LLM result)

### Inferences
- The SlimQwen result is the only evidence that staging helps, and it is (a) at pretraining scale (400B tokens, far beyond a Thor budget), (b) staged over depth/width with experts apparently cut once, (c) mixed in sign (BBH −3.3 pt). It does not establish that staging the expert ratio itself raises the tolerable ratio.
- Healing recovers ~50% of the in-domain gap at 69–75% in two independent settings; one-shot 75% + heal ≈ one-shot 62.5% unhealed (Qwen3.6-35B: 0.841 vs 0.860 HumanEval+). Extrapolation: if a second prune→heal cycle also recovered ~50% of its own increment, two cycles of 30%-of-remaining (total 51%) would land near a one-shot 50%, not above it; there is no evidence a progressive schedule lets a general model exceed 50% at the one-shot-50% quality.
- The recovery mechanism matters for whether iteration can compound: Half-the-Experts recovered via router + attention/shared-expert LoRA with experts frozen, so a second prune starts from re-routed statistics that already compensate; Martin et al. needed full-parameter FT. For GLM-5.3-Flash (shared expert present, fused routed experts), the Half-the-Experts recipe is the directly applicable one.

### Gaps
- No measured prune→heal→prune for MoE experts anywhere; this is an open experiment.
- SlimQwen does not state at which stage the 512→256 expert merge happens, so even its progressive result cannot be attributed to staged expert removal.
- HOPE's SFT numbers exist only as a figure; the magnitude of "narrows but does not close" is unknown.

## Q3. Layer-adaptive expert allocation: gain, which layers tolerate pruning, learned allocation

### Takeaway
Non-uniform allocation is worth 0–3 pt for strong criteria (EvoESAP on REAP: Code Avg 0.461→0.462, Math 0.646→0.648 at 50% on ERNIE) and large only when rescuing weak criteria (Frequency on ERNIE 50%: MATH-500 +19.6%); learned/global allocations (DiEP, CHOPE) report that shallow layers are most sensitive and deep layers most prunable, but CHOPE over-pruned the earliest layers and collapsed, and the project's own exact-DP estimate is −4.98% reconstruction error at 50% shrinking to −3.24% at 75%.

### Cited Findings

Code Avg and MC essentially unchanged (−0.4% and −0.2%)"; "On Qwen3, EvoESAP most strongly benefits weaker criteria"; at 50% on Qwen3 Frequency improves Code Avg +6.6%, WildBench +7.2%, Math +9.5%, MC +6.0% — [EvoESAP §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06003)

- REAP paper: merging's functional-subspace collapse "most pronounced in late layers" (PCA variance Table A4); REAP itself prunes a uniform fraction per layer — [REAP App. B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- Internal (this project, GLM-5.3-Flash, from banked saliency): exact min-plus DP with a 16-expert floor gives −4.98% gross reconstruction error at 50% (85–238 experts/layer), −3.83% at 65%, −3.24% at 75%; greedy water-fill made it worse (+3.5%) because f_L(k) is non-convex; not shippable because `num_local_experts` is a single scalar in glm5_next/vLLM/GGUF and the CUDA server (`N_ROUTED_EXPERT = 144`) — `/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md` (unpublished)

### Inferences
- Across sources the allocation gain for a REAP/HOPE-quality order is a few percent of the metric and shrinks with ratio (internal DP: 5.0% → 3.2% from 50% to 75%); it does not change the ratio ceiling. It is a second-order lever behind criterion and calibration corpus.
- Direction of tolerance disagrees by model: deep layers most prunable (Mixtral/DiEP), middle layers (GPT-OSS/Martin), early layers must be protected (CHOPE collapse, DiEP). Extrapolation: for GLM-5.3-Flash the safe prior is a floor on early layers and letting late layers absorb extra pruning, but it must be measured.
- The learned/global methods (DiEP, CHOPE) are the ones that collapse; the search methods with a fixed within-layer order and a per-layer floor (EvoESAP, Martin) are the ones that are stable. Any non-uniform attempt here needs a per-layer floor ≥ top-k (8) and ideally ≥16.
- Deployability remains the blocker already recorded: a non-uniform checkpoint cannot be loaded by vLLM/GGUF/our CUDA server without patching the model definition.

### Gaps
- No source reports a non-uniform allocation result above 50% on an LLM except Martin et al. (MT only, with the allocation folded into the method; no uniform-vs-allocated split above 50%).
- EvoESAP's Appendix D (searched density shapes) was not read, so the early/middle/late shape it finds is not recorded here.

## Q4. Router handling after pruning: top-k renormalization, router retraining, router distillation

### Takeaway
Renormalizing top-k router weights to sum to 1 in the saliency calculation (REAP fix 2026-03-11) cuts REAP's mean coding-accuracy drop from 2.6% to 1.9% across five models; router-only KD (2603.02217) adds essentially nothing on top of REAP at 37.5% pruning on Qwen3-30B (MMLU 0.6483→0.6507, HumanEval 0.915→0.921, MBPP 0.622→0.618) while rescuing editing/merging methods (MoBE BBH-fewshot 0.002→0.472), and the one >30% field report using router-only KD (GLM-5.2 34%) found it "cannot fully restore the termination behavior" (loop rate 2x base, unchanged with 6x more KD data).

### Cited Findings
- REAP README, 2026-03-11: "Top-k router logits are now renormalized to sum to 1 for REAP saliency, controlled by `ObserverArgs.renormalize_router_weights`"; across ERNIE-4.5-21B, Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, GLM-4.5-Air, Llama-4-Scout on non-agentic coding, mean accuracy decrease 1.9% with normalization vs 2.6% without; README wording on whether released checkpoints used it is garbled — [CerebrasResearch/reap](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap)

- Half-the-Experts: on-policy calibration (profiling on the base model's own greedy outputs) lifts the 75%-pruned point 0.787→0.817 HumanEval+; a union guard protecting top general-traffic experts → 0.829 — [Half the Experts §5.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- MESA: "Pruning in this regime removes experts and forces the router to redistribute mass to the remaining experts, de-dispersing the routing distribution" — [MESA §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04453)

### Inferences
- The renormalization fix is a saliency-side change (which experts are chosen), not a post-hoc router fix; it is worth ~0.7 pt mean on coding at ≤50% and is free. Whether our pipeline includes it should be diffed (memory note already flags this).
- Router-only KD is close to a no-op for pruning (as opposed to merging/editing), because pruning leaves surviving experts intact and the router's relative logits among survivors are still valid; its measured value at 37.5% is within noise. At 50%+ the measured failure mode it cannot fix is termination/looping (GLM-5.2 evidence), which lives in the deleted experts.
- The healing recipe with measured >50% recovery (Half-the-Experts) trains the router jointly with the shared expert and attention LoRA; it is the router + always-on-path combination, not router-only, that recovered general ppl 3x. Extrapolation: for GLM-5.3-Flash, "per-expert healing" (scalar per surviving expert) is weaker than this recipe and should be replaced or augmented by it.

### Gaps
- No source reports router handling at ratios above 62.5% retention except the 75%-pruned Half-the-Experts heal (router full + LoRA) and Martin et al. (full FT); no router-only KD result exists above 37.5% pruning on a general benchmark suite.
- Router KD Tables 4–6 (Mixtral, and the 75% retention setting) were not transcribed.

## Q5. Saliency criteria: REAP vs HOPE vs output-error vs Fisher vs gate-free norms — which generalises best beyond 50%?

### Takeaway
At 50% HOPE (second-order pᵀFp) is the best-ranked criterion in the only dense sweep (rank 1.58 vs REAP 2.42; SWE-Bench Pro 43.84 vs 39.61 on Qwen3.5-122B), and REAP is the most robust first-order criterion at 62.5–75% on Qwen3.6-35B (0.787 vs MAN 0.720 vs collapse for routing mass/random), but the winner flips by family (routing mass beats REAP by 16.5 pt on Gemma-4 at 50% and by 0.063 xCOMET on GPT-OSS), gate-free MAN/MSAN win task-agnostic rankings, and no criterion has measurements above 50% on a general suite; nothing in the literature tests Fisher or true output-error criteria above 50%.

### Cited Findings

- HOPE at 50% can lose badly on single benchmarks: Qwen3.5-35B Evol-CA MMLU 29.77 (HOPE) vs 50.15 (REAP) vs base 65.15, while LCB 48.01 vs 28.49 — [HOPE Table S1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)

- The evidence ordering for >50% general use: (1) second-order HOPE at 40–50% is the best-measured; (2) REAP is the safest first-order fallback and degrades gracefully to 75% on Qwen-family; (3) frequency/routing-mass is bimodal (best on GPT-OSS/Gemma, catastrophic on Qwen3-Coder/Kimi-K2 at 50%); (4) random collapses at 50%. Extrapolation: on GLM-5.3-Flash (sigmoid noaux_tc router, 288 experts), the gate-weighted family (REAP/HOPE) is the prior; a routing-mass control should still be run because the family-flip is unexplained.
- HOPE's advantage is on cooperative/agentic workloads and grows with ratio by construction (more pairs broken); but its MMLU loss at 50% on Qwen3.5-35B shows the quadratic objective can sacrifice a minority domain not represented in calibration — directly relevant to the ballast problem (Q6).
- No output-error (e.g. layer-reconstruction search) or Fisher criterion has a >50% LLM measurement; Enumeration/reconstruction-loss pruning (Mosaic's baseline) collapses on code at 50% on Mixtral (HumanEval 1.22), so reconstruction criteria are not a known escape.

### Gaps
- No criterion comparison above 50% on any general-purpose benchmark suite; only in-domain code (Qwen3.6-35B, Gemma-4) and MT (GPT-OSS).
- The "Yamaguchi et al. 2026" biomedical study (arXiv 2607.01444) compares six methods over "multiple pruning ratios" but its abstract page gives no numbers; full text not read.
- Fisher-information expert criteria: none found in 2025–2026 MoE-LLM literature.

## Q6. Protecting thin domains: reserving per-domain experts so minority capabilities don't collapse

### Takeaway
Two formal treatments exist, both at ≤50%: MESA (minimax over per-domain fractional routing-mass loss, iteratively boosting the worst-hit domain) turns REAP's −16.75 pt GPQA into +1.01 at 25% on gpt-oss-20b at the cost of AIME25 (−13.34 vs −3.34), and Mosaic Pruning (cluster experts by cross-domain performance profile, keep one per cluster) keeps Mixtral HumanEval at 71.34 vs 1.22 for a single-corpus criterion at 50%; the cheaper levers with measurements are rebalancing the calibration corpus (C4-only calibration kills code at 50%; general text at 4.76% of our corpus keeps only 48.7% mass) and a union guard reserving top general-traffic experts (+4.2 pt HumanEval+ at 75% pruned).

### Cited Findings
- MESA algorithm: compute per-domain router mass R ∈ R^{L×E} from 1,000 calibration examples per domain across 6 domains; iterate T=20 steps: pick pruning set P of lowest combined scores, compute ℓ_d = fraction of domain d's routing mass inside P, boost scores of experts serving d* = argmax ℓ_d by η₀=0.15; objective min_P max_d ℓ_d — [MESA Alg. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04453)
- MESA results, gpt-oss-20b 25% (32→24), Δ vs base: GPQA MESA +1.01 / REAP −16.75 / EvoESAP −20.31 / Random −14.72; AIME25 −13.34 / −3.34 / −20.00 / −43.34; GSM8K +4.01 / +0.45; MMLU −4.50 / −6.65; IFEval −1.75 / −0.79 / −17.60; MESA beats REAP on 7 of 11, REAP on 4; GPQA gap significant (z=3.57, p<0.001) — [MESA Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04453)
- MESA mechanism statement: "experts retain subtle domain-specific affinities. For example, the Math domain might route 3.8% of its tokens to Expert A, while the Code domain routes only 2.4%. A global importance score ... destroys the domain-specific signal ... pruning based on global scores accidentally removes critical domain specialists" — [MESA §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04453)
- MESA cross-check: on Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct "all methods degrade proportionally and no domain-specific imbalance emerges" — the trade-off is specific to over-dispersed routers — [MESA abstract](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04453)
- Mosaic Pruning: K-means over hidden states discovers latent domains; experts get a per-domain reconstruction-error performance vector; Spearman-rank similarity → agglomerative clustering into r−m clusters; pick the highest Activation-Variability-Score expert per cluster. Mixtral 8→4 (50%): HumanEval 71.34 vs Enumeration 1.22; MBPP 10.89 vs 1.55; GSM8K 28.35 vs 22.37; Qwen1.5-MoE 60→40 (33%): avg 55.74 vs 38.60; terms the baseline failure "functional collapse" — [Mosaic Tables 1, 4, 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19822)
- REAP: domain-specific calibration "substantially improves coding within the target domain" (Fig. A8) while general-mix calibration "best preserves accuracy across tasks" (Fig. A9); C4-only calibration at 50% → Qwen3-30B Code Avg 0.217 (base 0.558) — [REAP App., Table A8](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- Half-the-Experts: coding-heavy calibration at 50% raises general-chat ppl +131% (5.20→12.00) while code ppl +2.1%; union guard "protecting the top general-traffic experts before filling the budget by saliency" at 75% pruned: 0.787→0.829 HumanEval+ (on-policy alone 0.817) — [Half the Experts §5.1, §5.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- Hy3-REAP-48e (75%, 64 code samples): factual knowledge collapsed while code stayed strong; card attributes it to pruning "experts that held world knowledge, since they scored low on code data" — [sapidlabs/Hy3-REAP-48e](https://huggingface.co/sapidlabs/Hy3-REAP-48e)
- Kimi-K3-REAP: adding 0.50M Chinese tokens to an English-only calibration flipped 9.63% of keep/drop slots vs 2.96% for adding English — [runrunway/Kimi-K3-REAP-448experts](https://huggingface.co/runrunway/Kimi-K3-REAP-448experts)
- PreMoe: generalist mask = equal-weight union of per-domain specialist profiles; at 75% the generalist loses far more (LCB 20.22 vs specialist 49.63) — a static union of domain masks does not protect every domain at extreme ratios — [PreMoe](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17639)
- Yamaguchi et al. (biomedical, 4 models × 6 methods): "moderate pruning preserves in-domain utility without immediate reliability decline"; "hallucination risks increase at extreme pruning ratios"; cross-domain "both utility and reliability degrade rapidly" — [arXiv 2607.01444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01444)
- Internal: under the shipped GLM-5.3-Flash REAP50 mask, retained saliency mass by domain: general 0.4865 vs agentic 0.7466, code 0.7283, math 0.7133, science 0.7204; general is 4.76% of calibration routed tokens; a bucket-balanced re-mask recovers only +0.028 for general — "re-masking cannot create information the calibration never collected" — `/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md` (unpublished)

### Inferences
- MESA is the formal version of "reserve per-domain experts": it is a constraint on per-domain routing-mass loss, solved greedily by score boosting. It is cheap (needs only per-domain router mass, which our banked saliency already has by bucket) and composes with REAP/HOPE scores. Its measured cost is that protecting the worst domain shifts loss onto the previously-best domain (AIME −13 pt); at 50%+ with 7 domains the minimax solution will be strictly worse on code/math than plain REAP.
- The per-domain mass-retention statistic our audit already computes (general 0.49 vs ~0.72 for the rest) is exactly MESA's ℓ_d; running MESA's loop offline on the banked accumulators is a zero-GPU experiment.
- The calibration-corpus lever dominates: both REAP (C4 vs code) and Half-the-Experts (131% general ppl) show the mask follows the corpus, and our audit shows the general bucket is under-sampled 5–6x relative to other domains. Rebalancing the corpus is prerequisite to any domain-reservation method; reservation then handles the residual.

### Gaps
- No domain-reservation method is measured above 25% (MESA) or 50% (Mosaic, on 8-expert Mixtral); nothing at 50%+ on a 128–288-expert model.
- MESA's gpt-oss result depends on over-dispersed routing (entropy 90.8% of max); GLM-5.3-Flash's routing entropy has not been measured, so it is unknown whether the MESA regime applies.

## Q7. Routing entropy / load balance at >50% and new failure modes

### Takeaway
No paper measures routing entropy or load balance after >50% pruning; the documented post-pruning failure modes are (a) looping/non-termination that teacher-forced perplexity hides (GLM-5.2 34%: loop rate 3.6%→7.2%; Gemma-4 at 50%: "free-running generation collapses into repetition" while perplexity looks fine; 75%-pruned Qwen3.6 variants degenerate on 2–6 of 8 smoke prompts), (b) perplexity-accuracy decoupling (25%-keep code ppl 5.45 < 37.5%-keep 5.89 yet 7 pt lower pass@1; random pruning gives the lowest WikiText ppl and near-worst accuracy on gpt-oss), (c) confident factual errors and rising hallucination at extreme ratios, and (d) under over-dispersed routers, pruning "de-disperses" routing and can raise math accuracy above base while collapsing science.

### Cited Findings
- MESA entropy measurements: Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct mean per-token routing entropy 2.23 bits = 74.3% of log₂8; early layers ~80%, late ~65%; per-layer std 0.28–0.60 bits. gpt-oss-20b (λ_aux=0.9): 4.54 bits = 90.8% of log₂32, >82% in every layer, each expert ≈ k/E = 12.5% probability per token "regardless of token content"; diagnostic threshold H̄ > 0.85·log₂E; "Pruning in this regime removes experts and forces the router to redistribute mass ... de-dispersing the routing distribution"; MESA at 25% raises GSM8K to 92.87 vs base 88.86 — [MESA §3.1, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04453)
- MESA Table 1 (gpt-oss-20b 25%): WikiText-2 PPL vs GSM8K: base 126.02 / 88.86; MESA 107.14 / 92.87; REAP 145.04 / 89.31; RM-Uniform 83.29 / 83.24; Random 40.95 / 78.54 — "PPL and task accuracy are uncorrelated in over-dispersed models" — [MESA Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04453)
- GLM-5.2-504B keep-168 + router-KD: at n=50 results "looked like parity"; at n=2000 loop rate 0.072 vs 0.036 (z≈5, p<0.0001); codex/opencode prompts ~13% vs ~6%; median output length 1267 vs 1207; brevity prompt halves length but does not reduce looping; min_p 0.05 + repetition_penalty 1.10 → 2.3% — [0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B-W4A16](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B-W4A16)
- Half-the-Experts: "teacher forcing hides the compounding routing errors, and free-running generation collapses into repetition"; on Gemma "the templated-perplexity ranking across criteria anti-correlates with the pass@1 ranking"; chat template inflates teacher-forced ppl up to ~30x per sample; 75%-pruned routing-mass/contrastive/random models degenerate (empty or looping) on 2/8, 6/8, 5/8 smoke prompts; the 50%-keep random model passes 7/8 smoke prompts yet scores 0.311 — [Half the Experts §5.4, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- Half-the-Experts: "the 25%-keep model has lower code perplexity than the 37.5% one (5.45 vs. 5.89) yet loses seven points of pass@1. Past the flat region, next-token fit on code stops predicting whether the model can still complete a function" — [Half the Experts §5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- REAP: frequency pruning (which removes the least-used experts and therefore maximally concentrates load) is the method that collapses at 50% (Qwen3-Coder-480B Code 0.010; Kimi-K2 0.056); REAP's conditional mean deliberately averages over routed tokens "so usage frequency does not dominate" — [REAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- HOPE: "certain pairs of experts are co-selected far more often than independent chance would predict"; at 40–50% "many such cooperative pairs are broken simultaneously, compounding damage in ways that first-order importance scores cannot predict"; prune-set stability across calibration trials >0.95 Jaccard — [HOPE §1, §4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)
- Hy3-REAP-48e (75%): fluent throughout, confident wrong factual answers — [sapidlabs/Hy3-REAP-48e](https://huggingface.co/sapidlabs/Hy3-REAP-48e)
- Yamaguchi et al.: "hallucination risks increase at extreme pruning ratios ... evaluating pruned MoE models solely on utility is inadequate for high-stakes deployment" — [arXiv 2607.01444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01444)
- MoE-Pruner uses the coefficient of variation of per-expert activation counts as a load-balance score and reports that MoEs trained from scratch "usually have more experts and imbalanced load" — [MoE-Pruner](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12013) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Load concentration is the mechanism by which frequency-based criteria collapse: removing the least-used half forces all traffic onto the busiest experts, and generation collapses even when MC survives. REAP/HOPE avoid this by construction but no one has measured the resulting post-prune load distribution; at >50% with top-8 on 72–144 survivors the effective routing space shrinks combinatorially (C(144,8) vs C(288,8) ≈ 2⁸ fewer subsets), which is the Router-KD paper's stated reason fine-grained MoEs are hurt most.
- The new failure mode that appears with ratio is termination/looping, visible only in free-running sampling at n≥2000 prompts; it is not fixable by router-only KD and is partially maskable by min_p/repetition penalty. Any >50% evaluation here must include a loop-rate measurement at large n and must not rely on perplexity (memory notes already document multi-EOS and attractor traps).
- GLM-5.3-Flash's router regime (sigmoid, noaux_tc, 288 experts) should be classified by MESA's entropy diagnostic before choosing a criterion: if H̄ > 0.85·log₂288 ≈ 6.9 bits, REAP-style global scores are in the unreliable regime and per-domain protection is mandatory rather than optional.

### Gaps
- No measurement of routing entropy, expert load CV, or top-k gate mass distribution after >50% pruning in any source.
- No source quantifies looping/termination at ≥50% on a general model with n≥2000 prompts; the GLM-5.2 data point is at 34%.
- The Yamaguchi biomedical paper's per-ratio hallucination numbers were not retrieved (abstract only).
