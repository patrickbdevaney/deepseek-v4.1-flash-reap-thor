# Long-context routing drift, affected-rate vs position, and long-context survival under pruning + quantization

*Research notes, 2026-10-17. Scope: does a MoE expert-prune mask chosen at 2k–8k calibration tokens stay the right mask at 32k/128k/1M; does routing shift with position; how do DSA / MLA / KDA models differ; what do long-context evals show for heavily pruned or quantized giant MoEs; and how to analyse our captured GLM-5.3-Flash teacher routing (42 layers x 16 rows x 8192 tokens). Every number is tagged measured (M) or hypothesised/inferred (H). Local measurements from this repo are cited by file path and flagged LOCAL.*

**Headline.** No paper plots routing drift against absolute token position at 32k+; the literature on this exact question is empty. The closest measured facts are (a) REAP's own 30 % prune of a KDA-hybrid (Kimi-Linear-48B) calibrated at <=16,384 tokens held LongBench v2 (0.368 -> 0.372) and lost 3.4 pts on FRAMES, (b) routing depends on context mainly in middle layers and is near token-identity in layer 0 (Jaccard 0.83 same-token vs 0.09 different-token at L0, converging to ~0.6 vs ~0.2 mid-stack on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B), (c) prefill expert profiles of a 411-token and a 2,500-token sequence are near-identical in deeper layers but diverge during generation, and (d) the one position-coupled structure with catastrophic pruning consequences is the attention-sink / "Super Expert" coupling at the first token (3 of 6,144 experts -> ppl 8.70 -> 59.86). Long-context failure under compression is, in every measured case, a cliff in *length* rather than a slope in *depth*, and the measured cliffs come from attention-side precision (KV cache, RoPE part of MLA, linear-attention state) far more than from weights or experts.

## KQ1. Prior measurements of routing-decision drift vs position / context length

### Takeaway
Nobody has published expert-selection entropy or top-k overlap *as a function of absolute token position* for DeepSeek/Qwen/Mixtral-class models at long context. What is measured: routing is token-identity-dominated in the input layer and context-dominated in middle layers; prefill routing profiles of short vs long inputs are near-identical in deep layers; routing diverges during *generation*, not prefill; and our own 2k vs 8k saliency ladder (LOCAL) shows a 4x length change moves the keep-set far less (Jaccard 0.9745) than resampling tokens at the same length (0.8380).

### Cited Findings
- (M) Layer-wise routing locality on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-FP8 (40 layers: 30 GDN linear-attention + 10 full-attention, 256 experts top-8): Jaccard of the two top-8 sets at aligned positions, same-token vs different-token, within-group: L0 0.828 / 0.087; L4 0.674 / 0.122; L8 0.664 / 0.177; L14 0.618 / 0.223; L20 0.597 / 0.212; L26 0.575 / 0.194; L34 0.666 / 0.116; L38 0.616 / 0.207. Averages 0.649 (same-token) vs 0.179 (different-token); random baseline 0.016. Token identity dominates at L0, context contribution peaks mid-stack (~14x random at L14). No significant difference between GDN and full-attention layers (p ~ 0.84, 0.89). — [arXiv 2604.17182](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17182v1)
- (M) Same paper, drift after a fork: same-token Jaccard between sibling continuations falls from 0.578 immediately after the fork to 0.294 after 200 generated steps; different-token from 0.257 to 0.086. The paper does not vary prefix length. — [arXiv 2604.17182](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17182v1)
- (M) "Myth of Expert Specialization" (gpt-oss-20b and 5 other MoEs): same token "apple" in daily vs coding context has high expert overlap in layer 1 and decreasing overlap in later layers (Fig. 4, qualitative only, no numbers). Fig. 9: a 411-token HLE question and a 2,500-token Wikipedia passage have near-identical per-expert activation profiles in deeper layers during *prefill* and diverge as *generation* proceeds, with divergence appearing earlier in later layers. OpenMoE's "context-independent specialization" claim is contradicted. — [arXiv 2604.09780](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09780)
- (M) Same paper, duplication experiment: duplicating an input 1x-12x (and up to 32x) pulls cross-domain expert-usage similarity toward the intra-domain baseline, increases logit magnitude and collapses sampling randomness — i.e. repeated content changes routing statistics. Fig. 24: at K = 1200 of 2048 directions, >40 % of tokens switch away from the rank-6 expert (DeepSeek-V2-Lite, layer 10, 50k Nemotron-CC-Math tokens). — [arXiv 2604.09780](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09780)
- (M) "Polysemantic Experts, Monosemantic Paths" (Qwen3-30B-A3B, GPT-OSS-20B, GLM-4.5-Air, OLMoE, Granite-4-Tiny, DeepSeek-V2-Lite): 500 top-1 routing paths for the token ":" in three syntactic roles follow visibly distinct trajectories through 48 layers; router weights correlate with hidden-state magnitude (layer-wise Pearson avg ~0.60; GLM-4.5-Air +0.11 to +0.63). Position information is carried mainly by the "content channel"; the paper reports no dependence of routing on sequence position or length. — [arXiv 2604.17837](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17837)
- (M) Router-position independence: one spatial-structure study argues the router itself ignores position; position enters only via RoPE-carrying embeddings, and a probe recovers only which half of the sequence a token is in, not exact position. — [arXiv 2504.04444](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.04444)
- (M) Prefill-vs-decode expert activation correlates 0.70–0.92 per expert across three MoE models (ELDR). — [arXiv 2607.00466](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00466); expert-pair patterns in prefill vs decode heatmaps are similar by Spearman rho. — [arXiv 2510.05497](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05497)
- (M) Routing entropy decreases consistently with depth (steeper for non-English) in multilingual routing analysis — depth, not position. — [arXiv 2510.04694](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04694v2)
- (M) DOT-MoE (MoEfication of Qwen2.5-7B): its advantage over CMoE is "consistent across context windows from 2K to 32K" — the only paper found that reports a routing method across a 2k–32k length sweep, and it is about a constructed router, not a native one. — [arXiv 2606.01666](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01666)
- (M) Routing is load-skewed at fixed length: MoE-GPS batchwise skewness 1.388 / 1.402 / 1.990 on Mixtral 8x7B at seq 512 only; DeepSeek-R1 decode skew measured at seq 2048 only. No paper reports per-expert load at 4k vs 32k vs 128k. — [arXiv 2506.07366](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07366)
- (M, LOCAL) Sequence-length ladder on GLM-5.3-Flash saliency (12 layers, 131,072 tokens/arm, keep top-144 of 288): keep-set Jaccard S=8192 vs S=2048 = mean 0.9745 (min 0.9583); CONTROL (S=2048 vs S=2048 on disjoint tokens) = mean 0.8380 (min 0.8194). Split by attention type: KDA layers 0.9757 vs control 0.8391; DSA layers 0.9722 vs control 0.8356 — indistinguishable. — `/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/93-seqlen-ladder-results.md`
- (M, LOCAL) Capture constraint: one 16,384-token forward through a single MoE layer died from 93.0 GiB headroom; 8,192 uses ~57 GiB; so teacher routing was captured at 8,192 x 16 rows x 42 layers. — `/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/scripts/capture_teacher_routing.py`

### Inferences
- (H) Routing drift with position, if it exists, should be a *middle-layer* phenomenon: the input layers route on token identity (position-invariant by construction) and the context contribution peaks mid-stack. Our analysis should therefore report affected-rate vs position per layer group (early / mid / late), not pooled; a pooled curve will be dominated by position-invariant layers and hide a mid-layer trend.
- (H) The strongest measured driver of routing change is *generation* (decode drift after a fork: 0.578 -> 0.294 in 200 steps) and *content repetition*, not prefill position. Our 8192-token teacher-forced capture measures prefill routing only. The affected-rate most relevant to "agentic daily driving" at 100k is the one on *generated* tokens deep in context, which the current capture does not contain.
- (H) The local ladder result (0.9745 across 4x length vs 0.8380 control) is the best available evidence that the keep-set is length-stable to 8k. It says nothing beyond 8k and is a saliency-ordering result, not an affected-rate result; both can be true and the mask still mis-serve a sub-population of deep-context tokens if those tokens are rare in the saliency average.

### Gaps
- No paper plots top-k overlap, routing entropy, or expert-load distribution against absolute position at >=32k for any DeepSeek/Qwen/Mixtral/GLM model. "Myth" Fig. 9 is the closest (411 vs 2,500 tokens, qualitative).
- No measurement of routing at 128k–1M exists for any open model; the longest length at which routing has been characterised in any form is the 32k DOT-MoE sweep.

## KQ2. Does short-context calibration cause disproportionate long-context degradation? Pruned-MoE long-context evals

### Takeaway
Only one expert-pruning paper (REAP) reports any long-context benchmark: a 30 % REAP prune of Kimi-Linear-48B-A3B (a KDA hybrid, calibrated at <=16,384 tokens) scored LongBench v2 0.372 vs 0.368 baseline and FRAMES 0.523 vs 0.557 (-3.4 pts), with prompts truncated at 131,072. No RULER/NIAH-vs-length curve for a pruned MoE exists; the "context length where the gap opens" is unmeasured for pruning. Calibration studies vary sample count and domain, never sequence length.

### Cited Findings
- (M) REAP (v3): Kimi-Linear-48B-A3B-Instruct, REAP 30 %: LongBench v2 0.368 -> 0.372; FRAMES 0.557 -> 0.523 (gpt-4.1-2025-04-14 judge). Prompts >131,072 tokens truncated in the middle. Calibration: max sequence length 16,384, 24,414 samples. Authors state the compressed model keeps performance "at context lengths far beyond" the 16,384 calibration limit. — [arXiv 2510.13999v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v3)
- (M) REAP calibration config: <=110B models use 1,024 samples packed to 2,048 tokens; >=110B models use 12,228 samples at max 16,384 tokens, no truncation/packing. SWE-Bench inputs limited to 65,536. REAP saliency = mean over tokens where expert j is top-K of g_j(x) * ||f_j(x)||_2; after pruning, the router renormalises over the surviving experts; no analysis of which tokens are rerouted. — [arXiv 2510.13999v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v1)
- (M) Cerebras REAP cards: GLM-4.6-REAP-218B-A32B (40 % pruned) lists a 202,752-token window; GLM-4.5-Air-REAP-82B-A12B (25 %) lists 131,072; neither card reports a long-context benchmark. — [HF cerebras/GLM-4.6-REAP-218B-A32B](https://huggingface.co/cerebras/GLM-4.6-REAP-218B-A32B); [Cerebras blog](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/reap)
- (M) HOPE (Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, GLM-4.5-Air; 10–50 % pruning): benchmarks are Tulu3 dev suite + SWE-Bench Pro; no long-context benchmark. Prune-set stability across 3 random trials >0.95 Jaccard for all methods; across calibration *domains* only 0.60 (HOPE) / 0.61 (REAP); ~8k prompts (REAP) / ~10k (HOPE) needed for 99 % prune-set agreement (Qwen3.5-122B, 50 %). — [arXiv 2609.18916v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v2)
- (M) Calibration-dataset robustness study (gpt-oss-20b, layer-wise reconstruction pruning, 64 sequences default): "minimal benefit from scaling calibration data beyond small sample sizes"; English-only calibration held across 7 languages; pruned-with-C4 avg 0.503 vs baseline 0.464 (MathQA/MMLU). Sequence length not stated or varied; no long-context eval. — [arXiv 2610.15578](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15578)
- (M) MoE-Pruner (2–256 calibration samples), MoNE (100/500/1000 samples), Wanda++ (8/8 to 128/2048 samples/context): all vary count, none varies length as a long-context experiment; all evaluate short-context tasks or perplexity. — [arXiv 2410.12013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12013); [arXiv 2507.00390](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00390); [arXiv 2503.04992](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04992v4)
- (M) SHAPE (Qwen3-30B-A3B, GPT-OSS-20B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, 20/40 % pruning): authors note that tasks needing "longer dependency chains" are more sensitive to expert removal; no long-context benchmark run. — [arXiv 2606.09886](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09886)
- (M) Dense sparsity + long context (MATCH, NIAH 8k–128k on sparsified Qwen3 / Phi-3): the proposed fix gains most at 32k–128k, implying sparsified baselines lose most there; weight sparsity, not expert pruning. — [arXiv 2606.29844](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29844)
- (M) Router-expert mismatch after compression (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 at 62.5 % retention; Mixtral 8->5): routing-probability L1 distance vs original is larger for pruning (REAP, CFES) than for editing/merging, and top-8 overlap with the original falls with depth for all methods (Fig. 2, 100 ELI5 samples; numbers not in text). REAP on Qwen3: MMLU 0.7112 -> 0.6483, MedMCQA 0.5420 -> 0.4100, HumanEval 0.939 -> 0.915, GSM8k 0.863 -> 0.880. REAP on Mixtral 8->5 collapses: GSM8k CoT 0.689 -> 0.036, MMLU 0.677 -> 0.283. — [arXiv 2603.02217v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217v1)
- (M, LOCAL) MiMo-V2.6-Flash first-order affected-rate at <=4,095 tokens: 1.13 % of routed slots / 8.71 % of tokens overall; layer 7 alone 18.18 % of slots. — `/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/99-long-context-assurance.md`

### Inferences
- (H) The REAP Kimi-Linear data point is the single most relevant prior: same attention family as GLM-5.3-Flash (KDA hybrid), 30 % prune, 16k calibration, evaluated to 131k. Its FRAMES loss (-3.4 pts, multi-document retrieval+reasoning) alongside a LongBench v2 no-change is consistent with the local hypothesis that *retrieval* is attention-side and safe while *use of retrieved content* (expert work) is where loss appears. Our 50 % prune is beyond anything evaluated at long context.
- (H) Because routing divergence after pruning compounds with depth (Router KD Fig. 2), the teacher's first-order affected rate (what we captured) is a lower bound on the student's actual routing deviation at any position; the gap between first-order and realised deviation should grow with both depth and, plausibly, position.
- (H) Prune-set instability across calibration *domains* (Jaccard 0.60) dwarfs the length effect (LOCAL 0.9745 across 4x). The dominant mask risk at long context is therefore that long documents are a *domain* under-represented in calibration (long code repos, long agentic traces), not that position per se changes routing.

### Gaps
- No RULER/NIAH-vs-length table for any expert-pruned MoE; the length at which a pruned model's gap opens is unmeasured in the literature.
- No calibration study varies sequence length as the independent variable and evaluates long context.
- REAP's claim of generalisation "far beyond" 16,384 rests on two benchmarks with mid-truncation at 131k, one of which dropped 3.4 pts.

## KQ3. Position-dependent experts: BOS / attention-sink experts and long-range aggregators

### Takeaway
There is strong measured evidence for first-position-coupled experts: "Super Experts" (<0.5 % of experts, e.g. 3 of 6,144 in Qwen3-30B-A3B, 10 of 15,677 in DeepSeek-R1) receive markedly larger router scores on the sink/first token, produce the massive activations that form the attention sink, and their removal is catastrophic (ppl 8.70 -> 59.86; Math-500 88.0 -> 1.4; repetitive output to max length). Separately, "driver" experts activate disproportionately at sentence starts and ends. No paper identifies experts specialised in long-range aggregation.

### Cited Findings
- (M) Super Experts: identified by max down_proj output magnitude (>99.5th percentile across experts, >=1/10 of model max, in layers with massive activations). Counts: Qwen3-30B-A3B 3/6,144 (L1 E68, L2 E92, L3 E82); DeepSeek-R1 10/15,677; DeepSeek-V2-Lite 2 (L3 E54, L4 E38); Mixtral-8x7B 1 (L1 E3). Sink tokens appear at the first token across domains; their router scores on SEs are "markedly larger" while non-sink tokens' router scores are relatively uniform. SE sets are identical across Base / Instruct / Thinking variants and across WikiText-2, C-Eval, GSM8K, HumanEval, Pile-of-Law. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- (M) Pruning SEs: Qwen3-30B-A3B WikiText-2 ppl 8.70 -> 59.86 (random 3 experts: 8.71); DeepSeek-R1 ppl 3.33 -> 5.18; non-reasoning avg accuracy -21.7 to -27.2 %; GSM8K -53 to -74 %; Qwen3-30B-A3B Math-500 88.00 -> 1.40, AIME24 80 -> 0; DeepSeek-R1 Math-500 97.60 -> 4.00. Attention-sink decay rate stays ~90 %+ after SE pruning (sink disappears). Output becomes repetitive until max length. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- (M) 2026 attention-sink survey restates: pruning 3 of 6,144 experts in Qwen3-30B-A3B causes catastrophic degradation; sink formation is tied to this sparse expert subset. — [arXiv 2604.10098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10098)
- (M) Driver experts by relative position (Mixtral, DeepSeek-MoE, Qwen1.5-MoE; five equal bins 0–20 % … 80–100 % of sequence; DeepSeek on SA and MMLU): tokens at the start of a sentence have disproportionately high causal weight, the middle contributes least, and the end is also strong. No BOS-specific experiment; no sequence-length sweep. — [arXiv 2601.10159v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10159v2)
- (M) OpenMoE "drop-towards-the-end": with fixed expert capacity, later-position tokens are dropped more; on RedPajama/TheStack few drops even past position 1500, but large drop ratios on multilingual and instruction data; assignments fixed early in pretraining. This is a capacity-factor training artefact, not an inference-time routing property of dropless models. — [arXiv 2402.01739](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01739)
- (M) Capacity-induced prefix bias: oversubscribed-expert overflow is dropped from the sequence end, so suffix tokens get less training signal; Random Token Selection mitigates. — [arXiv 2109.10465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.10465)
- (M) Attention sink / massive-activation mechanism: self-concentration from the causal mask plus value-non-mixing produce sinks and MAs at the initial position; sinks can also appear at non-initial positions, which fixed-position protections miss (RotateKV). — [arXiv 2609.09085](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.09085); [arXiv 2501.16383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16383)

### Inferences
- (H) REAP's saliency g_j * ||f_j|| should rank Super Experts very high (their down_proj outputs are by definition the largest in the model), so a REAP mask is unlikely to prune them; HOPE's pairwise re-weighting could in principle demote one. This is checkable offline against our mask: compute per-expert max |down_proj output| on the first 8 positions of each captured row and assert every SE candidate is in the keep-set. This is a cheap, high-value assertion.
- (H) The sink coupling is a *position-0* effect, so a position-binned affected-rate should treat bin [0, ~8) separately; even one pruned-expert hit there is qualitatively different from a hit at position 5,000.
- (H) No evidence for "long-range aggregator experts"; if they exist they would show up as experts whose routing share rises with position in middle layers. That is exactly what the per-expert, per-bin share from our capture can test (see KQ4).

### Gaps
- No paper tests whether SE routing is tied to absolute position vs the BOS token identity (e.g. a sink appearing mid-sequence after a document boundary).
- No measurement of experts whose selection frequency grows with position.

## KQ4. The affected-rate metric: definition, threshold, binning with confidence intervals

### Takeaway
"Affected rate" is not a named metric in the literature; the nearest published quantities are routing-probability L1 distance and top-k overlap vs the original model (Router KD), REAP's gate-weighted output-norm saliency, and the Super Expert result showing that a 0.05 % slot-share can be catastrophic — so a raw slot-fraction has no validated quality threshold. For 16 rows x 8,192 tokens the independent unit is the row; CIs must be row-clustered (bootstrap over rows, paired within-row contrasts), and a same-position across-row control sets the noise floor, mirroring the local ladder design.

### Cited Findings
- (M) Published post-compression routing metrics: token-level L1 distance between original and compressed routing probabilities, and top-8 set overlap by layer, measured on 100 ELI5 samples; overlap falls with depth for all six compression methods. — [arXiv 2603.02217v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217v1)
- (M) REAP saliency is gate-weighted: mean of g_j(x) ||f_j(x)||_2 over tokens where j is in top-K; renormalisation factor assumed ~1 because gate values are small. — [arXiv 2510.13999v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v1)
- (M) A 0.05 % expert share (3/6,144) can be catastrophic when it coincides with the sink token, and random experts of the same count cost ~0 (ppl 8.71 vs 8.70). — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- (M) Prune-set Jaccard >0.95 across random calibration trials; 0.60 across calibration domains; 8k–10k prompts for 99 % agreement — the published scale of mask noise. — [arXiv 2609.18916v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v2)
- (M) Long-context compression failures present as a length *cliff* with pooled depth marginals spanning only 55–66 %; authors argue methods should report where the cliff falls relative to a full-precision baseline rather than averaging over a sweep. — [NeurIPS 2026 workshop, Salammagari](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/173781)
- (M, LOCAL) Existing first-order affected-rate: MiMo 1.13 % of slots / 8.71 % of tokens at <=4,095 tokens, layer 7 = 18.18 %; ladder control floor 0.8380 vs 4x-length 0.9745. — `/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/99-long-context-assurance.md`, `/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/93-seqlen-ladder-results.md`
- (M, LOCAL) Capture format: teacher top-8 expert ids per token per layer, 42 layers x 16 rows x 8,192 tokens, real documents only (no concatenation). — `/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/scripts/capture_teacher_routing.py`

### Inferences (recommended methodology; all H)
- **Define three rates per (layer, bin):** (1) slot-rate = pruned slots / (8 x tokens); (2) token-rate = tokens with >=1 pruned slot / tokens; (3) gate-mass-rate = sum of teacher gate weight on pruned slots / sum of all gate weight — (3) is the one aligned with REAP's saliency and should be the primary; also record rank-stratified rates (was the pruned expert the token's top-1/top-2 or its 7th/8th), since a pruned top-1 is a much larger perturbation than a pruned 8th. If the capture stores only ids and not gate values, (3) is unavailable and that is itself a finding worth noting.
- **Bin position logarithmically** so the early-sequence regime is not swamped: [0,8), [8,64), [64,256), [256,1k), [1k,2k), [2k,4k), [4k,8k). The [0,8) bin isolates the sink regime (KQ3); [0,2k) is the calibration regime; [2k,8k) is extrapolation. Report per layer and per layer-group (early L0–L7 / mid / late), and per attention type (KDA vs DSA layers), because the literature says the context effect is mid-stack.
- **Independence unit is the row, not the token.** Tokens within a document are strongly correlated (same topic, same expert pool), so a token-level binomial/Wilson CI on 16 x 8,192 tokens would be wildly over-confident. Use a cluster bootstrap: resample the 16 rows with replacement (e.g. 2,000 draws), recompute each (layer, bin) rate, take the 2.5/97.5 percentiles. Expect wide intervals with n=16; that is honest.
- **Make the drift test paired and within-row:** for each row r and bin k compute d_{r,k} = rate(r,k) − rate(r, reference bin [256,1k)); test the 16 values of d_{r,k} with a sign test / Wilcoxon, and fit a per-row slope of rate on log-position with row fixed effects. Pairing cancels document-level level differences, which are the dominant variance (the ladder control shows row/sample identity moves masks by ~16 %).
- **Add a control that mirrors the ladder:** split each row into two halves of tokens *at the same positions* across different rows (across-row, same-position variance) and compare with the within-row across-position variance. Only if across-position variance exceeds across-row same-position variance is there drift to explain.
- **Threshold:** none exists. Use three decision rules instead: (a) any non-zero hit in bin [0,8) on a Super-Expert candidate -> block the mask; (b) a monotone positive slope of gate-mass-rate vs log-position whose 95 % cluster-bootstrap CI excludes zero in >= 25 % of layers -> treat the mask as position-sensitive and extend calibration (KQ7); (c) a flat curve within the control floor -> the mask is as good at 8k as at 2k, and the extrapolation to 1M rests on the bounded-state argument (DSA top-k = 2048, fixed KDA state), which is unmeasured beyond 8k.
- **Report the per-expert share-vs-position matrix** (288 experts x bins per layer): experts whose share rises monotonically with position are the candidate "long-range" experts; check whether any of them are pruned.

### Gaps
- No literature defines affected-rate, its threshold, or its relationship to downstream quality; the mapping from affected-rate to task loss has to be fitted locally (e.g. per-layer affected rate vs per-layer KD loss).
- Decode-time affected rate is not measurable from a teacher-forced capture.

## KQ5. Long-context survival under extreme (2–3 bit) weight quantization on giant MoE

### Takeaway
Measured long-context weight-quantization results stop at 4-bit: at 64k–128k, 4-bit methods lose 3–16 % on RULER on average (up to −30.7 % for Llama-3.1-8B BNB-nf4; −10.3 % Qwen-2.5-72B GPTQ-int4), while 8-bit is <1 % and the gap is absent at 8k. For 2-bit and below on giant MoEs the only published evidence is short-context: naive 1.58-bit DeepSeek-R1 produces infinite repetition/gibberish until the first 3 layers and attention are kept at 4–6 bit, and KL-divergence vs size tables; no NIAH/RULER-vs-length curve exists for any IQ2/IQ3 DeepSeek/Kimi/GLM quant.

### Cited Findings
- (M) EMNLP 2025 systematic study (Llama-3.1 8B/70B, Qwen-2.5 7B/32B/72B; FP8, GPTQ-int8, AWQ-int4, GPTQ-int4, BNB-nf4; RULER/OneRuler 8k/64k/128k, NoCha to 128k): average long-context drop FP8/GPTQ-int8 <1 %, AWQ-int4 ~1.8 %, GPTQ-int4 ~2.7 %, BNB-nf4 ~6.9 %. At 8k all methods ~= BF16; gap opens at 64k and 128k. 4-bit RULER drops >=3 % on every model, up to 16 % overall; GPTQ-int4 Llama-3.1-8B −21.2 %, Qwen-2.5-72B −10.3 %; BNB-nf4 Llama-3.1-8B −30.7 %, Qwen-2.5-7B −29.7 %, Qwen-2.5-32B −15.7 %; OneRuler BNB-nf4 Llama-3.1-70B −32 to −66 % (paper inconsistent). NoCha drops steepen beyond ~75k (GPTQ-int8 Llama-70B −11 to −13 %). Non-English drops up to 5x English. Larger models more robust but not guaranteed. Proposed (unverified) cause: RoPE rounding-error accumulation at long context. No repetition failure described. — [arXiv 2505.20276](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20276v3)
- (M) Unsloth DeepSeek-R1 1.58-bit (131 GB from 720 GB): naive uniform quantization of all layers "breaks the model entirely, with infinite repetitions and gibberish"; fix = first 3 dense layers and attention at 4/6-bit, MoE at ~1.5-bit (MoE is 88 % of size). Short-context evaluation only. — [Unsloth blog](https://unsloth.ai/blog/deepseekr1-dynamic); [X post](https://x.com/danielhanchen/status/1883901952922448162)
- (M) Unsloth Dynamic 2.0 KLD (Gemma-3-27B reference table): IQ1_M ~0.83 KLD, IQ2_XXS ~0.54, Q4_K_XL ~0.02; dynamic Q2_K_XL reduces KLD ~7.5 % vs static. DeepSeek-V3.1 Aider Polyglot pass-2: dynamic 5-bit 70.7 %, dynamic 1-bit 55.7 % (short context). — [Unsloth docs](https://docs.unsloth.ai/basics/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs); [Aider page](https://unsloth.ai/docs/fr/bases/unsloth-dynamic-2.0-ggufs/unsloth-dynamic-ggufs-on-aider-polyglot)
- (M) Community IQ3_XXS Gemma-4-31B produced leaked control tokens and repetition loops at 8k/24k while IQ3_XS worked — a 2–3-bit repetition failure mode, non-MoE, anecdotal. — [HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/mradermacher/model_requests/discussions/2579)
- (M) NVFP4 DeepSeek-V4-Flash card: MoE linear layers only, 1M window, "<0.5 % degradation" on GPQA-Diamond, AA-LCR, tau2-Telecom, SciCode, IFBench — AA-LCR is the only long-context item and no per-length breakdown. — [dev.co listing](https://dev.co/ai/llms/deepseek-v4-flash-nvfp4)
- (M) Massive activations / sink tokens are the known obstacle to low-bit quantization; sink-token KV entries are quantization-sensitive and keeping the initial token in FP16 helps (RotateKV, citing KIVI/KVQuant). — [arXiv 2501.16383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16383)
- (M) Low-bit quantization hurts more on better-trained (more tokens/param) models. — [ACL 2025, arXiv 2411.17691](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.17691)

### Inferences
- (H) Weight quantization at 4-bit degrades long context *gradually* with length (slope), KV/attention-side compression degrades it as a *cliff* (KQ6). On a 2–3-bit giant MoE both are present; the published short-context repetition failure of naive 1.58-bit R1 is the same symptom the Super-Expert ablation produces (repetition to max length), suggesting that extreme MoE quantization and sink/SE damage share a failure mode: corrupt the first-token / early-layer path and the model loops.
- (H) The protective recipe is consistent across sources: keep early dense layers, attention/KV projections, router, and SE-bearing experts at higher precision; apply the aggressive bits to late-layer routed experts.

### Gaps
- No NIAH/RULER-vs-length measurement for any <=3-bit quant of DeepSeek-R1/V3/V4, Kimi-K2, or GLM; the bit-width at which NIAH collapses on a giant MoE is unknown.
- No study separates expert-weight quantization from attention/KV quantization at long context on an MoE.

## KQ6. KV / attention-compression interactions: MLA latent, DSA indexer, KDA state precision

### Takeaway
Attention-side precision degrades long context more sharply than weight precision: KV-cache quantization moves the needle-retrieval cliff inward 2–3x (Qwen-2.5-72B from 64–96k usable at FP16 to 32k at int8 or int4, bit width irrelevant); 2-bit KV causes 6–9 pt LongBench collapse; the RoPE part of the MLA KV cache spans ~100x the range of the latent part and is the quantization-sensitive component (SnapMLA keeps it BF16); DSA at top-k 2048 is measured near-lossless to 128k (−0.35 RULER pts) whereas GDN-style linear layers lose 8–11 RULER pts at 128k even with half the layers full-attention; FlashKDA stores the KDA state in bf16 but accumulates in fp32 with chunk 16 to keep exp(cumsum(g)) within bf16 range.

### Cited Findings
- (M) KV-cache quantization retrieval cliff (Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B; FP16/int8/int4; 4k–128k, 5 depths, 190 runs): Qwen retrieves at all depths through 96k (lenient) / 64k (strict) at FP16 and only through 32k once quantized; Llama falls from partial at 64k to none; int8 and int4 give identical pass/fail on Llama and differ by one cell on Qwen. Failure is a cliff in length, not depth (pooled depth marginals 55–66 %). — [NeurIPS 2026 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/173781)
- (M) KIVI on LongChat-7B NIAH: 16-bit 96.3, KIVI-4bit 96.3, KIVI-2bit 85.3, InfLLM-4x 34.0. — [arXiv 2407.01527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01527)
- (M) 1-bit K and V on Llama-3.1-8B: RULER 84.2 -> 47.8; DGAP restores to 83.2. — [arXiv 2607.16248](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16248)
- (M) MLA-converted Llama2-7B on LongBench: 4-bit KV −0.2 to −0.4 %; 2-bit −6.2 to −9 % (not native DeepSeek). — [arXiv 2502.14837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14837)
- (M) SnapMLA (DeepSeek-V3.1, LongCat-Flash-Thinking): latent c_KV values concentrated within ~±10^1, RoPE k_R up to ~±10^3 with outlier tails; FP8 raises RoPE-component MSE by ~an order of magnitude; design = FP8 content part + BF16 RoPE part. FP8 vs BF16 on long-generation tasks: DeepSeek-V3.1 AIME-25 87.92 -> 85.42, GPQA-D 84.15 -> 82.57, MATH-500 98.8 -> 98.2; up to 1.91x throughput; sweep 16k–128k. — [arXiv 2602.10718](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10718)
- (M) GLM-5 report, efficient-attention ablation on GLM-9B (continual-trained 190B tokens; 64k / 128k): dense RULER 85.35 / 75.28; SWA-Interleave 65.94 / 44.93; SWA-Pattern 83.72 / 69.59 (−5.69 @128k); GDN 76.76 / 64.00 (−8.59 / −11.28); SimpleGDN 81.76 / 67.03 (−3.59 / −8.25); RepoQA@128k SimpleGDN −7.33; "even when half of the layers retain full attention." DSA on GLM-4.7-Flash RULER 4k->128k: baseline 97.44/96.72/95.83/92.96/85.34/79.21; full DSA 96.77/96.25/96.69/93.45/87.06/78.86 (−0.35 @128k; +1.72 @64k). MLA vs DSA base at 128k: MQ-NIAH 100/100, MV-NIAH 95.5/97.0. DSA described as "lossless by construction". — [arXiv 2602.15763](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15763)
- (M) DeepSeek-V3.2: DSA lightning indexer has a small number of heads, ReLU, "can be implemented in FP8", top-k 2048 KV tokens per query, indexer warm-up 1,000 steps; "do not observe substantial performance degradation" on short- and long-context tasks vs V3.1-Terminus; AA-LCR +4 pts; no RULER table. — [arXiv 2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02556)
- (M) Kimi Linear (48B-A3B, 3:1 KDA:MLA, 1.4T tokens), 128k: RULER 84.3 vs MLA 81.3 vs GDN-H 80.5; MRCR 29.6 vs 22.6; LongBench v2 35.0 vs MLA 36.1; FRAMES 58.8 vs 60.5; authors say long-context retrieval "remains the primary bottleneck for pure linear attention". KDA state d_k x d_v per head = 128 x 128. — [arXiv 2510.26692](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26692)
- (M) Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B (GDN hybrid) RULER-NIAH, independent measurement: near-perfect to 128k, "substantial decay beyond 256k", 80.78 % at 1M; Qwen3-4B-Instruct 48.16 % @256k, 24.69 % @1M; Qwen2.5-14B-1M 89.97 % @256k. — [arXiv 2603.23516](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23516)
- (M) FlashKDA kernel: recurrent state stored in bf16, state updates with fp32 FMA; chunk size 16 (vs FLA's 64) "keeps the range of exp(cumsum(g)) within bf16 precision". FlashInfer's bf16 GDN state backend only for K=V=128. — [FlashKDA docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/moonshotai/flashkda); [FlashInfer](https://docs.flashinfer.ai/generated/flashinfer.gdn_decode.gated_delta_rule_decode_pretranspose.html)
- (M) Gated DeltaNet retains retrieval where other linear models collapse (S-NIAH-1 81.8 % at 2k in a linearization study) — i.e. linear attention retrieval is fragile and the delta rule is what rescues it. — [arXiv 2504.14366](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14366)
- (M) Community llama.cpp on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: q8_0 KV cache ~free, q4_0 worse; long context degraded tool-calling regardless of quant (informal). — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1u0isbo/snapshots/2026-06-13T02%3A56%3A06.64152Z)

### Inferences
- (H) For GLM-5.3-Flash (34 KDA + 11 DSA layers per third-party description), the long-context risk ordering from the measurements is: KDA-layer state precision and the bf16 range of the cumulative gate (hard kernel constraint) > RoPE part of the DSA/MLA KV cache > latent part > expert weights. If any of our NVFP4/overlay work touches k_pe, the KDA gate cumsum, or KV storage, that is where to run the NIAH length-cliff first.
- (H) DSA's top-k = 2048 bounds the attention set, so beyond ~2k the hidden-state statistics the router sees are plausibly saturated; this is the mechanism behind the LOCAL 0.9745 ladder result and is the argument for extrapolating to 1M — but it is measured only to 8k locally and to 128k (DSA lossless) in the GLM-5 report.
- (H) The DSA indexer's FP8 is a vendor design point at 128k; nothing measures indexer precision vs retrieval at 1M. The GLM-5 Table 6 "warm-up only" row (71.35 @128k vs 79.21) shows the indexer *selection quality*, not its precision, is the lever.

### Gaps
- No measurement of KDA/GDN recurrent-state bit-width vs long-context accuracy (bf16 vs fp32 state) at any length.
- No measurement of DSA indexer precision (FP8 vs BF16) vs retrieval at >128k.
- No NIAH@1M from the vendors for Kimi Linear or GLM-5.3-Flash; the only 1M data point for a GDN hybrid is third-party (Qwen3-Next 80.78 %).

## KQ7. Making a short-calibrated mask robust at long context

### Takeaway
No paper proposes or tests position-stratified or long-sequence calibration for expert pruning. The measured levers are: (1) calibration *token count and domain diversity* dominate mask stability (0.60 Jaccard across domains; 8–10k prompts for 99 % agreement) while 4x sequence length barely moves it (LOCAL 0.9745); (2) post-hoc router-only recalibration (Router KD: 3,000 C4 samples x 512 tokens, ~2 h) gives small gains on fine-grained MoEs and nothing for collapsed ones; (3) REAP's own giant-model recipe uses 16,384-token unpacked samples. Chunked-attention calibration that fits memory is unaddressed in the literature.

### Cited Findings
- (M) Router KD: updates only router parameters; token-level KL from teacher softened next-token distribution; c4, max 3,000 samples, 1 epoch, batch 2 x grad-accum 4, lr 5e-5, max seq len 512, T=1.0; ~2 h on Qwen3-30B-A3B, ~40 min on Mixtral. Gains on Qwen3 across all six compression methods; marginal on Mixtral; no help when compression collapses scores. REAP -> REAP-R on Qwen3: MMLU 0.6483 -> 0.6507, WinoGrande 0.5683 -> 0.5841, HellaSwag 0.4198 -> 0.4245, MedMCQA 0.4100 -> 0.4109, MBPP 0.6220 -> 0.6180. — [arXiv 2603.02217v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217v1)
- (M) REAP giant-model calibration: 12,228 samples, max 16,384 tokens, no packing/truncation (Kimi-Linear run: 24,414 samples). — [arXiv 2510.13999v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v1); [v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v3)
- (M) Calibration diversity: HOPE prune-set Jaccard 0.60 across calibration domains vs >0.95 across seeds. — [arXiv 2609.18916v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v2); calibration resembling the pretraining distribution holds across 7 languages, and "scaling calibration data beyond small sample sizes" gives minimal benefit (gpt-oss-20b). — [arXiv 2610.15578](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15578)
- (M) MoE Pathfinder: pruned-model quality is unimodal in the number of k-means clusters K used to build the calibration set — too little or too much diversity both hurt. — [arXiv 2512.18425](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18425)
- (M) Post-pruning fine-tuning permits more aggressive pruning (theory + experiments on fine-tuned SMoE). — [arXiv 2405.16646](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16646v3)
- (M) Every Token Counts (HSA-UltraLong, 8B MoE): pre-trained at 8k, mid-trained to 32k, near-perfect S-NIAH at 16M — length generalisation of *attention* far beyond training length is achievable; the paper reports no routing analysis. — [arXiv 2511.23319](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.23319)
- (M, LOCAL) Sequence length costs +2.4 % wall-time for 4x length and moves the mask by ~2.5 %; token count/domain is the first-order lever; corpus caps at 16,384 real tokens; 16k forward does not fit. — `/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/93-seqlen-ladder-results.md`, `/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/scripts/capture_teacher_routing.py`

### Inferences
- (H) **Position-stratified calibration is implementable without a longer forward:** compute saliency accumulators separately for position buckets within the 8,192-token rows (the capture already supplies positions) and check whether the top-144 keep-set from the [4k,8k) bucket differs from the [0,2k) bucket by more than the 0.838 control floor. If it does not, a stratified mask is unnecessary; if it does, take the union-weighted mask (max-normalised saliency across buckets) rather than the pooled one, so that experts salient only deep in context are not averaged away.
- (H) **Chunked-attention calibration that fits memory:** the OOM is the quadratic DSA/attention term in one 16k forward, not the MoE. Because DSA attends to top-k = 2048 keys, a chunked forward that carries the full KV cache but computes attention for 2,048-query blocks at a time (prefix-chunked prefill, the same as serving-side chunked prefill) reproduces the exact 16k–128k hidden states at bounded activation memory; for the KDA layers the recurrent state carries over naturally. This gives true long-position routing captures without approximation, and is the standard inference trick rather than a research contribution. Verify with a bit-exactness check against an unchunked 8k forward before trusting it.
- (H) **Mixing a few long sequences** is the cheapest robustness lever supported by the literature's emphasis on domain diversity: long documents are a domain (repos, long agentic traces) under-represented in a 16,384-capped corpus; a handful of genuinely long sequences, chunk-prefilled, adds that domain to the saliency average. Concatenation is not a substitute (fake boundaries), as the local notes already state.
- (H) **Post-hoc router recalibration on long sequences** (Router KD with long-position tokens in the KD set) directly targets routing drift without touching experts; measured gains are small (sub-point on Qwen3) but the cost is ~hours, and unlike mask changes it can be applied after surgery. The local global-router-KD plan (wiki 98) is the right vehicle; its KD set should include positions >2k.
- (H) Decision order: (1) run the offline affected-rate-vs-position analysis (KQ4) on the captured 8k rows; (2) only if a positive slope or a mid-layer hotspot appears, run chunk-prefilled captures at 32k–128k on a few long documents; (3) regardless, assert Super Experts are kept; (4) reserve router KD on long positions as the post-surgery remedy.

### Gaps
- No published method or result for position-stratified or long-sequence calibration of expert pruning; everything above KQ7 "Inferences" is design reasoning, not measured.
- No published comparison of pooled vs stratified saliency masks at any length.
