# Catastrophic single-point failures in MoE expert pruning and a "never-prune guard" checklist

Scope note (research run 2026-10-17). Every number carries the model it was measured on; nothing below was measured on a 1T-class checkpoint except the REAP Kimi-K2 / Qwen3-Coder-480B rows and the Super-Expert ablation on DeepSeek-R1 (671B). The pipeline this feeds already banks, per layer: per-expert per-domain REAP saliency (mean of g_j·‖f_j‖ over routed tokens), routing counts, gate mass, and a top-8 routing capture (expert ids + gate values per position) over 16 rows × 8,192 tokens. Each guard below says what it computes from those and whether it needs a new forward pass. Items marked **[extrapolation]** are my inference, not a measured result.

## KQ1. Super Experts: identification method, lists, coincidence with attention sinks / massive activations, detectability from position-0 gate mass

### Takeaway
Super Experts (SEs) are identified by the *maximum* down_proj output magnitude of each expert (> P99.5 of all experts' maxima AND > a_max/10, restricted to the shallow layers where massive activations are born), not by router score; they are <0.5 % of experts, data-agnostic and post-training-invariant, fire on the first (sink) token with "exceptionally large" router scores, and their removal collapses the attention sink and the model (Qwen3-30B-A3B ppl 8.70→59.86, MATH-500 88.0→1.4; DeepSeek-R1 MATH-500 97.6→4.0, AIME 0). Position-0 gate mass in a routing capture is a valid *proxy* signature, but the paper's own criterion needs a per-expert max-activation statistic that a mean-saliency bank does not contain.

### Cited Findings
- Identification criterion (Eq. 6): for every expert compute a(l,e) = max output magnitude of its down_proj; let 𝒜 be the set of all a(l,e). Expert (l,e) is an SE iff a(l,e) > P99.5(𝒜) AND a(l,e) > (1/10)·a_max AND l ∈ L, "the set of layers responsible for the formation of massive activations". No router-score term is used in the criterion. — [arXiv 2507.23279 (ICLR 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Counts and proportions (Table 1/2): Qwen3-30B-A3B 3 of 6,144 (0.05 %; L1 E68, L2 E92, L3 E82); DeepSeek-R1 10 of 15,677 (0.06 %); DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat 2 of 1,782 (0.11 %; L3 E54, L4 E38); Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct 1 of 256 (0.39 %; L1 E3). Down_proj output maxima: 744.0 (Qwen3-30B-A3B top expert), 616.0 (DeepSeek-R1). — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- DeepSeek-R1 SE list (Table 7, extracted text garbled; one reading): L8 E24, L8 shared expert, L12 E190, L13 E64, L14 E202, L14 shared expert, L22 shared expert, L33 E64, L33 shared expert, L35 shared expert — i.e. roughly half the R1 SEs are *shared* experts, and they sit in layers 8–35, not only layers 1–3. Treat the exact indices as low-confidence. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Paper internal inconsistency: Table 7 lists Qwen3 L3 E82 under "outlier experts" while Tables 2 and 8 list it as an SE. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Pruning cost, non-reasoning (Table 3): Qwen3-30B-A3B avg 70.22→55.00 (−21.68 %), WikiText-2 ppl 8.70→59.86, GSM8K 89.61→42.38; DeepSeek-V2-Lite avg 60.27→43.90, ppl 6.31→10.75, GSM8K 37.83→9.78; Mixtral-8x7B avg 67.84→49.38, ppl 3.84→6.23. Random pruning of the same number of experts: avg 70.36 / 60.30 / 67.82 and ppl 8.71 / 6.31 / 3.86 (no change). — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Pruning cost, reasoning (Tables 4–5): DeepSeek-R1 avg 75.64→1.81; MATH-500 97.60→4.00; AIME24 79.33→0.00; AIME25 66.33→0.00; LiveCodeBench 63.44→0.00; GPQA 71.50→5.05; ppl 3.33→5.18 (random: avg 75.53, ppl 3.35). Qwen3-30B-A3B thinking: avg 69.37→4.02; MATH-500 88.00→1.40; AIME24 80→0; HumanEval 43.90→0.00; GPQA 61.62→18.69; ppl 8.70→59.86. Outputs degenerate into repetition ("that's the" repeated; introductory phrase repeated to max length). — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Note the ppl/benchmark decoupling: R1's ppl rises only 3.33→5.18 while every reasoning benchmark goes to ~0. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Mechanism: SEs produce the massive activations in the residual stream; attention converts them into sinks; after SE pruning the attention-sink decay rate D_sink "remains consistently high, at approximately or even exceeding 90 %" (Qwen3-30B-A3B, C4), and Figure 8 shows the sink on token 0 disappearing. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Router-score signature: "the sink tokens consistently appear as the first token"; on the sink token the SEs receive "exceptionally large" / "markedly larger" router scores while non-sink tokens' scores "remain relatively uniform across experts" (Fig. 6, App. F, measured on C4 and WikiText-2). No numeric router-score values are printed. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Outlier experts in *final* layers (distinct from SEs) are dataset-dependent; pruning Qwen3-30B-A3B L47 E8/E48/E100 gives ppl 8.71 vs 8.70 — harmless on ppl. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Massive activations (dense LLMs, the precursor paper): appear at layer 2 in LLaMA2-7B/Mistral-7B, layer 4 in LLaMA2-13B, layer 9 in LLaMA2-70B, persist to the last few layers; magnitudes 2622 vs median 0.2 (LLaMA2-7B), 7100 vs 0.3 (Mixtral-8x7B, "four orders of magnitude"); trigger tokens: starting token, first "." / "\n" (LLaMA2-7B), delimiters plus "and"/"of" (Mixtral-8x7B), some numeric tokens (70B); live in fixed hidden dims (Mixtral 2070, 3398). Zeroing them: LLaMA2-7B ppl 5.47→inf, 13B 4.88→5729, zero-shot 68.95→36.75 %; setting them to their mean: no change (5.47→5.47) — they act as "fixed but important biases". — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- Attention-sink survey (2026) restates: sink formation in MoE is tied to Super Experts; pruning 3/6,144 is catastrophic; massive activations bias routing logits; sinks also appear on strong delimiters (periods, newlines) and can "dynamically emerge at non-initial positions"; Qwen3-Next uses gated attention to reduce sinks and prevent expert collapse. — [arXiv 2604.10098](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10098v1)
- Official REAP repo exposes a `SINGLETON_SUPER_EXPERTS` flag that "force[s] super and outlier experts into singleton clusters" for the merging path, citing 2507.23279; the README does not say how it detects them nor whether the pruning path protects them. — [CerebrasResearch/reap](https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap)
- One community REAP pipeline (Qwen3-Coder-Next) sets `preserve_super_experts: true` ("protected from pruning regardless of their average REAP score"; "they rarely fire but are critical when they do") but reports no numbers (results "TBD"). — [mtecnic/research-test](https://github.com/mtecnic/research-test-Qwen3-Coder-Next-REAP-AWQ)

### Inferences
- The SE criterion is a **max** statistic; REAP saliency is a **mean**. The banked per-domain saliency therefore cannot reproduce the criterion unless the saliency pass also stored a running per-expert max of ‖down_proj output‖. If it did not, SE detection needs one more forward pass (cheap: hook down_proj outputs, a few hundred tokens suffice since SEs are data-agnostic; the authors say no specific calibration set is needed).
- Position-0 gate mass is a usable proxy from the top-8 capture: because position 0 sees no context, its routing is a deterministic function of the first token in every layer (causal attention). If all 16 rows start with the same BOS/[gMASK] token, the 16 position-0 samples are identical and give exactly one sink-routing profile per layer: the experts that dominate the gate at position 0 in the shallow layers are the SE candidates. Dominance (top-1 gate ≫ rest) is the signature the paper describes; an absolute threshold is not published.
- On DeepSeek-R1, SEs include shared experts and extend to layer 35 of 61. For a 1T-class model with a shared expert (GLM-5.3 has one; memory: "97.5 % routed experts"), the shared expert must be kept at full fidelity and the SE search window must not be limited to layers 1–3. **[extrapolation]**
- Massive activations also fire on delimiter tokens ("."/"\n", and in Mixtral on "and"/"of"), so a position-0-only guard can miss the intra-row sink tokens in packed rows; bin the capture by token id as well as position.

### Gaps
- No published routing *frequency* of SEs (whether they sit in the frequency tail). The paper reports rarity by count, not by routing share.
- No numeric router-score magnitudes on the sink token (only the qualitative "exceptionally large").
- No SE profile exists for any GLM, Kimi, Qwen3-235B, DeepSeek-V3/V4 or any hybrid linear-attention (KDA/Gated-DeltaNet) model; no evidence on whether gated-attention models (Qwen3-Next) still have SEs.
- The REAP repo's internal SE detector (which statistic/threshold) could not be read from the README.

## KQ2. Are Super Experts stable across domains / sequence lengths, and do REAP/HOPE saliency scores already rank them high?

### Takeaway
SE sets are identical across WikiText-2, C4, C-Eval, GSM8K, HumanEval, Pile-of-Law and MedDialog and across Base/Instruct/Thinking variants, and are insensitive to the threshold (P95–P99.9, 0.07–0.1·a_max); nothing is published on sequence-length dependence or on where REAP/HOPE rank them, so they need an explicit guard rather than an assumption of safety.

### Cited Findings
- "The distribution of SEs remains highly consistent across these varied domains and OoD datasets" (Figs. 14–19, 24–29: WikiText-2, C-Eval, GSM8K, HumanEval; Pile-of-Law and MedDialog for Qwen3-30B-A3B); SE sets are "fully consistent sets across all models and training stages" for Qwen3-30B-A3B Base / original / Instruct-2507 / Thinking-2507 (App. K); SE counts unchanged for thresholds P95–P99.9 and 0.07–0.1·a_max on C4 and WikiText-2 (App. I). — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- REAP saliency: S_j = (1/|𝒳_j|) Σ_{x∈𝒳_j} g_j(x)·‖f_j(x)‖₂ over tokens where j is in the top-k; lowest S_j pruned; pruning is uniform per layer (25 % or 50 % "in each layer"). — [arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v1)
- Unified scoring (2606.15716): S_j(b,α,β) = (1/N_j^b) Σ_t 1[j∈E_t]·g^α·‖f‖^β; Frequency=(0,0,0), SEER=(0,1,0), EAN=(0,0,1), REAP=(1,1,1), MAN=(1,0,1). "The dominant effect [of calibration dependence] comes from the routing-frequency term." — [arXiv 2606.15716](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15716)
- HOPE: the QP "assigns a higher cost to pruning experts which participate in cooperative clusters, causing them to be retained even if their individual importance (diagonal of FF) is lower" — i.e. HOPE can also *demote* an expert whose cluster is weak. — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v1)
- Causal audit (OLMoE, Qwen1.5-MoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite; n=200 positions, zero-ablation of top/bottom expert by metric): no observational metric (utilization, activation norm, mean routing weight, activation std) predicted ΔL after Bonferroni; all |d| < 0.17 across 60 metric–layer cells; only the per-token routing-weight control at OLMoE L15 reached d=+0.231 (p=0.0013). — [arXiv 2606.10703](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10703v1)
- Half-the-Experts: a direct causal-ablation estimator of per-expert importance was "noise-dominated" (split-half reliability r=0.05 per layer); "magnitude-family signals are load-bearing". — [arXiv 2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721)

### Inferences
- Because REAP averages over routed tokens, an SE that is enormous on the ~1 sink token per sequence but ordinary elsewhere has its mean diluted by sequence length: at 8,192-token rows the sink contribution is 1/8192 of the mean. Whether an SE ranks high therefore depends on how large its *non-sink* outputs are, which is unmeasured. Treat SE rank under REAP as unknown; do not rely on it. **[extrapolation]**
- With b=1 (frequency-normalised) criteria, a rarely-routed SE is not penalised for rarity, which is in its favour; with Frequency (b=0) it is actively at risk.
- Sequence-length stability: the sink token is position 0 and massive activations are input-agnostic, so the SE identity should be length-invariant; the *mean* saliency is not. **[extrapolation]**

### Gaps
- No paper measures where SEs rank under REAP, HOPE, MAN or Frequency. This is a cheap offline check against the local mask (see checklist G1).
- No sequence-length sweep of SE identity exists.

## KQ3. Position-coupled experts ("driver experts", sentence starts/ends)

### Takeaway
"Driver experts" are defined causally (KL shift under a unit-norm perturbation of routing logits), concentrate in middle layers (peak ~layers 8–12 on 16–47B models), and their causal weight is disproportionately carried by tokens at the start of a sentence; down-weighting them (×0.8 on logits, not pruning) costs 5–9 points on math and 8–11 on sentiment. No paper bins routing *share* by position and prunes on it; the start-of-sentence coupling is the measured fact, the sentence-end claim is not supported.

### Cited Findings
- Driver-expert definition: perturb expert e_i's routing logits with a unit-norm vector and take CE(e_i) = D_KL(P‖Q_i) between clean and corrupted outputs; threshold not stated. Models: Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeek-MoE-16B, Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B. — [arXiv 2601.10159](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10159v1)
- Positional finding: tokens are split into five equal positional bins per sentence; "tokens at the start of a sentence carry disproportionately high causal weight", the central part contributes least (Fig. 4; percentages only in the figure). Nothing is reported about sentence-end tokens. — [arXiv 2601.10159](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10159v1)
- Layer profile: causal influence rises early, peaks mid-network (appendix: ~layers 8–12), dips, rises again in deep layers; DeepSeek has the highest causal rate, Mixtral the lowest. — [arXiv 2601.10159](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10159v1)
- Cost of down-weighting (logits ×0.8, Table 2): Math acc Mixtral 0.8013→0.7522, DeepSeek 0.8462→0.7581, Qwen 0.8117→0.7503; MMLU 0.6323→0.6247 / 0.4761→0.4701 / 0.6011→0.5914; sentiment 0.5137→0.4307 / 0.6080→0.5146 / 0.6134→0.5030 (F1). — [arXiv 2601.10159](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10159v1)
- Driver and domain experts "may share members"; MoE gating forms a small set of "generalist experts" with peaked activations across tasks. — [arXiv 2601.10159](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10159v1)
- Massive-activation trigger tokens beyond position 0: first "." or "\n" (LLaMA2-7B), delimiters and "and"/"of" (Mixtral-8x7B). — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- Sinks "can dynamically emerge at non-initial positions"; BERT heads attend to [CLS] early, [SEP] mid, periods/commas deep. — [arXiv 2604.10098](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10098v1)
- Routing is near token-identity at layer 0 and context-dependent mid-stack (Jaccard 0.83 same-token vs 0.09 different-token at L0, converging to ~0.6 vs ~0.2 mid-stack on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B) — from the sibling note. — [local note: long_context_routing_drift.md]

### Inferences
- A position-binned guard is computable from the top-8 capture: for each expert, the fraction of its gate mass coming from (a) absolute positions 0–7 of each row, (b) the first token after each segment/document boundary inside packed rows, (c) the first token after "."/"\n". An expert with a large share of its mass in these bins (and a small total share) is a sink/driver candidate. No literature threshold exists; the SE signature is "dominant at position 0", so the defensible rule is a dominance test at position 0, not a share cut-off. **[extrapolation]**
- Driver experts are *mid*-layer and were only down-weighted, not removed; their 5–9 point math cost at ×0.8 suggests removal would be worse, but this is unmeasured. **[extrapolation]**

### Gaps
- No paper prunes position-coupled experts and reports the cost; no share threshold ("X % of mass in first N tokens") exists in the literature.
- No sentence-end / EOS-coupled expert analysis was found.

## KQ4. Domain-exclusive experts and the frequency tail

### Takeaway
Frequency-based pruning collapses coding (Qwen3-Coder-480B 50 %: EvalPlus 0.011 vs REAP 0.873; Kimi-K2 50 %: 0.124 vs 0.863; Qwen3-30B 25 % with C4 calibration: 0.000 vs REAP 0.599 vs gate-free MAN 0.868) because frequency is the calibration-dependent term, whereas norm-weighted criteria keep rarely-routed, large-output experts. A per-domain exclusivity score computed from the banked per-domain saliency is a direct guard; the only measured guard of this kind ("union guard" of top general-traffic experts) raised 25 %-keep HumanEval+ 0.787→0.829.

### Cited Findings
- REAP Table 2/3, Eval+/LiveCode/Code-avg: Qwen3-30B 50 % Frequency 0.704/0.236/0.470 vs REAP 0.821/0.293/0.557; GLM-4.5-Air 50 % Frequency 0.546/0.104/0.325 vs REAP 0.755/0.352/0.553; Qwen3-Coder-480B 50 % Frequency 0.011/0.012/0.011 vs REAP 0.873/0.415/0.644 (25 %: 0.792 vs 0.884); Kimi-K2 50 % Frequency 0.124/0.000/0.062 vs REAP 0.863/0.429/0.646 (25 %: 0.524 vs 0.889). "Frequency-based pruning suffers from a sharp degradation in quality at 50 % compression." — [arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v1)
- Task-agnostic (C4 calibration) 25 % pruning, Eval+/LCB/WildBench/avg: Qwen3-30B Frequency 0.000/0.000/0.632/0.411, REAP 0.599/0.137/0.600/0.619, MAN 0.868/0.346/0.570/0.707 (full model Eval+ 0.871); OLMoE Frequency 0.000/0.000/0.127, REAP 0.000/0.000/0.263, MAN 0.009/0.000/0.260; ERNIE Frequency 0.254/0.055 vs MAN 0.343/0.077. "Frequency, SEER, EAN, and MoNE nearly collapse on EvalPlus and LiveCodeBench." — [arXiv 2606.15716](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15716)
- Half-the-Experts "union guard": protecting top general-traffic experts before filling the domain budget raised 25 %-keep HumanEval+ from 0.787 to 0.829 (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B); on Gemma-4-26B-A4B "a handful of experts among 3,840 saw no calibration token"; on Qwen every expert received traffic. — [arXiv 2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721)
- Pre-registered causal test on Command A+ (218B, 128 experts, top-8+1 shared, NVFP4 and BF16): "routing mass does not predict modularity"; the Arabic family of 16 experts is the one robust module (+1.80 nats on-target, ~6× ppl, off-target ≤ +0.43); the code family raised code NLL +0.49 but HumanEval accuracy did not change (0.00, n=60) and bled +0.62 into math; masking up to 32 of the most-shared experts lowered MMLU-Pro by only ~0.11 with no cliff. — [arXiv 2606.25092](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25092)
- DBES routing specialisation S_spec by domain, last layer: GLM-4.6 L88 Medical 0.72 / Legal 0.45 / Code 0.35 / Knowledge 0.10; DeepSeek-R1-0528 L57 Medical 0.68 / Math 0.50; Qwen3-30B-Thinking L47 Medical 1.90. DeepSeek and GLM domain isolation S_iso "generally < 0.4" ("distributed collaboration") vs Qwen ("modular specialization"). — [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- Attribution-guided pruning (2606.18304): "router weight and token usage are weakly correlated with true contribution, and some frequently routed experts are harmful"; uses a minimum-channel floor m below which an expert is dropped — no protected-expert mechanism. — [arXiv 2606.18304](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18304)
- Local sibling finding: REAP 50 % on Kimi-K2 kept single-turn code flat but BFCL multi-turn fell 0.355→0.164; chat-only calibration "lost most coding ability" on GLM-5.3-Flash. — [local note: sota_shipped_artifacts.md / healing_recovery.md]

### Inferences
- The banked per-domain saliency S_{j,d} supports an exclusivity score directly: excl_j = max_d S_{j,d}·n_{j,d} / Σ_d S_{j,d}·n_{j,d} (mass-weighted). The literature supports the *direction* (keep high-norm rare experts) but gives no exclusivity threshold; the 2606.25092 result that the Arabic module is real while code is entangled suggests exclusivity is most reliable for language/script domains and for Qwen-class models, less so for GLM-class "distributed" routers.
- A per-domain exclusivity guard is complementary to, not a replacement for, a protect_frac top-slice: the top-slice keeps generalists (which 2606.25092 shows are causally redundant in bulk), the exclusivity guard keeps specialists that the aggregate saliency under-weights because their domain is a small share of the corpus (memory: "gap is corpus imbalance (general 4.76 %)"). **[extrapolation]**
- The cleanest measured guard remains per-domain *mass retention*: for each domain d and layer, Σ_{kept} S_{j,d}·n_{j,d} / Σ_{all} S_{j,d}·n_{j,d} ≥ floor. No paper fixes the floor; REAP's 50 % results show it can be met at ~0.9+ retention on code when calibration includes code. **[extrapolation]**

### Gaps
- No paper reports a per-expert exclusivity distribution or a validated threshold; no head-to-head of "exclusivity guard" vs "top-slice guard".
- The frequency-tail collapse is shown at the benchmark level; the papers do not name the removed load-bearing experts.

## KQ5. Layer-level guards (early dense-like layers, final layers)

### Takeaway
Three independent sources point to layer-level floors: cross-layer HOPE (CHOPE) that "tended to prune a large number of experts in the earliest layers" collapsed SWE-Bench Pro (Qwen3.5-35B 25 %: 44.58→27.29±17.51) and the authors propose a floor of at least M = routing-width experts per layer; the causal audit finds late-layer ablations are 5–40× more damaging than early ones (OLMoE L15 P95 ΔL +2.633 nats vs ≤ +0.709, gap norm 41×); DBES shows thinking-tuned models re-specialise the *last* layers ("terminal surge", GLM-4.6 L88 Medical S_spec 0.72 vs 0.49 mid-stack). Early layers are where SEs live, late layers are where specialist circuits and the largest single-expert ΔL live; both ends need floors.

### Cited Findings
- CHOPE (global budget, per-layer count uncapped, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B / 122B-A10B, Evol-CA calibration): "tended to prune a large number of experts in the earliest layers" and "occasionally experienced performance collapse". SWE-Bench Pro: 35B base 41.82; 25 % HOPE 44.58 vs CHOPE 27.29 ± 17.51; 50 % HOPE 42.33 vs CHOPE 22.95 ± 1.19; 122B 10 % 52.44 vs 46.04. Proposed constraint (C.6): require at least M experts (the routing width) to survive in every layer; no results with the constraint reported. The main HOPE method prunes each layer independently with a uniform budget. — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v1)
- Causal audit progressive ablation: layers 0–9 mean ΔL at k=7 ≤ +0.083 nats; layer 15 ΔL +0.155 at k=2 and +0.431 at k=7; position-level P95 at L15 k=7 +2.633 nats vs ≤ +0.709 elsewhere; gap norm 0.0041 (L0) → 0.1697 (L15), ~41×; "early-layer redundancy renders most selection criteria interchangeable". — [arXiv 2606.10703](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10703v1)
- DBES Table 10 S_spec (first / mid / last layer): GLM-4.6 Medical 0.15 / 0.49 / 0.72, Legal 0.11 / 0.58 / 0.45, Code 0.10 / 0.39 / 0.35, Knowledge 0.05 / 0.19 / 0.10; DeepSeek-R1-0528 Medical 0.06 / 0.31 / 0.68, Math 0.06 / 0.22 / 0.50; Qwen3-30B-Thinking Medical 0.96 / 0.85 / 1.90 vs Instruct 0.45 / 0.85 / 0.82. "CoT-finetuned models … exhibiting a 'Terminal Surge' where deep layers are repurposed for highly isolated, domain-specific reasoning"; Instruct models follow "Low-High-Low". DeepSeek-R1 follows "progressive separation" with no terminal re-integration. — [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- REAP: expert functional-space contraction under *merging* is "dramatic in late layers, where experts are more specialized" (Llama-4 L47 PC1 span −800…600 collapses to a cluster; Qwen3 L47 128 experts span −200…300 collapse to "a minute region"); pruning preserves the geometry. — [arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v1)
- Massive activations are born in a shallow layer (L2 for 7B dense, L4 for 13B, L9 for 70B) and persist to the last few layers; SEs sit at L1–3 (Qwen3-30B), L3–4 (V2-Lite), L1 (Mixtral), and up to L35 on R1. — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2); [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Unsloth 1.58-bit DeepSeek-R1: uniform quantisation of all layers "breaks the model entirely, with infinite repetitions and gibberish"; keeping the first 3 dense layers and attention at 4–6 bit fixes it (quantisation analogue of an early-layer floor). — [Unsloth blog](https://unsloth.ai/blog/deepseekr1-dynamic)
- Final-layer outlier experts (dataset-dependent) are harmless to prune on ppl (8.71 vs 8.70). — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)

### Inferences
- Under a uniform per-layer budget (REAP/HOPE default) the HOPE floor (≥ top-k experts per layer) is never binding; it only matters once per-layer budgets are non-uniform (memory note: "uniform layer alloc costs 5 %", so the local pipeline is presumably non-uniform and therefore exposed to the CHOPE failure).
- The causal audit's late-layer P95 (+2.6 nats per ablation at the 95th-percentile position) is the quantitative face of "late-layer specialist circuits": pruning late layers trades a small mean loss for fat tails, which is the signature of looping/degeneration rather than ppl. **[extrapolation]**
- The terminal-surge numbers argue for a per-domain mass-retention floor that is *per layer*, with the last ~10 % of layers held to a tighter retention than mid-stack, specifically for the thinking variant. **[extrapolation]**

### Gaps
- No paper gives a numeric per-layer keep floor other than HOPE's "≥ M" proposal (untested).
- No layer-wise pruning-sensitivity curve exists for any 100B+ model.

## KQ6. Looping / degeneration: incidence, why perplexity hides it, protocol, pre-prune predictors

### Takeaway
Measured looping after pruning: GLM-5.2-504B REAP keep-168/256 (34 %) + router-KD loops on 7.2 % of 2,000 held-out agent prompts vs 3.6 % for the teacher (z≈5), ~13 % vs ~6 % on codex/opencode prompts, natural-EOS 0.928 vs 0.965; the pilot at n=50 "looked like parity". Teacher-forced perplexity hides it because it never lets routing errors compound (random 50 % prune on Qwen3.6: code ppl 4.82 < base 5.38 yet −57.9 pass@1). No validated pre-prune predictor exists; the only post-hoc hypotheses are "termination behaviour carried by the pruned experts" (0xSero) and removal of "high-flow, domain-neutral experts" (Half-the-Experts).

### Cited Findings
- Protocol: 2,000 held-out prompts "harvested from genuine coding-agent traces (codex, opencode, cursor, claude-code)", "raw sampling, no max_tokens, no timeout" so "loops are detected, never truncated"; metrics: attractor/loop rate, natural-EOS rate, distinct-4, median length. Results pruned vs teacher: loop 0.072 vs 0.036, EOS 0.928 vs 0.965, distinct-4 0.880 vs 0.921, median length 1267 vs 1207 tokens; "z≈5, p<0.0001"; ~1 % of prompts loop on the teacher intrinsically; at n=50 "looked like parity". — [0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B-W4A16](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B-W4A16)
- Router-KD (75 gate matrices, ~0.016 % of params, KL to teacher, lr 5e-5) "cannot fully restore the termination behavior carried by the pruned experts"; 6× more KD data and a knowledge-augmented expert set both failed; full-data floor KD gave 8.6 %. Serving mitigations: min_p 0.05 + rep-penalty 1.05 → 4.9 %; 1.10 → 2.3 % (distinct-4 0.95); a brevity system prompt halves length (1267→507) but "does not reduce looping". — [0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B-W4A16](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B-W4A16)
- Half-the-Experts (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Gemma-4-26B-A4B; keep 0.75/0.5/0.375/0.25): "teacher forcing hides the compounding routing errors, and free-running generation collapses into repetition"; 8-prompt greedy smoke test with a repetition/emptiness detector: at 25 % keep routing-mass 2/8, contrastive 6/8, random 5/8 collapse; the 50 %-keep random model passes 7/8 yet scores 0.311; contrastive 50 % "emits repetition loops on real tasks and scores 0.207"; a clean 8/8 smoke can still score 0.09 pass@1, so the smoke test "fails in both directions" and was not used as a gate. Random 50 % Qwen: code ppl 4.82 vs base 5.38, HumanEval+ −57.9 pts; REAP 25 %-keep ppl 5.45 < 37.5 %-keep 5.89 yet 0.787 vs 0.860 pass@1; Gemma templated ppl ranking "anti-correlates" with pass@1; chat template inflates teacher-forced ppl up to ~30× per sample (170.8 vs 12.2). Post-hoc attribution of the contrastive collapse: removal of high-flow, domain-neutral experts — "not test[ed] as a predictor". — [arXiv 2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721)
- SE pruning produces exactly this symptom: "repetitive and uninformative outputs", MATH-500 responses degenerate into repeated "that's the"; R1 ppl only 3.33→5.18 while accuracy → 0. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Naive 1.58-bit R1 gives "infinite repetitions and gibberish" until the first 3 dense layers and attention are kept at 4–6 bit. — [Unsloth blog](https://unsloth.ai/blog/deepseekr1-dynamic)
- REAP: merged models are "more repetitive" by n-gram diversity and diverge faster from the baseline than pruned ones. — [arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v1)
- Local sibling notes: 75 %-pruned variants degenerate on 2–6 of 8 smoke prompts; "</think>-restart loops"; multi-EOS GGUF trap makes models talk to themselves. — [local notes: expert_pruning_beyond_50.md; memory gguf-multi-eos-token-trap]

### Inferences
- Looping has two documented mechanical routes: (i) sink collapse (SE removal → attention has no anchor → degenerate repetition), and (ii) loss of termination behaviour (EOS / `</think>` / `<|user|>` emission) that is carried by specific experts. Route (i) is covered by the SE guard. Route (ii) is computable from the top-8 capture *if the rows contain chat-formatted turns*: bin each expert's gate mass by whether the current token is a terminator (`</think>`, `<|observation|>`, EOS, `<|user|>`) or the token immediately preceding one; experts whose mass share on terminator-adjacent positions is anomalous (e.g. > 5× their overall share) are termination carriers. No literature threshold; mark as a candidate predictor to validate against the n≥2000 loop-rate protocol. **[extrapolation]**
- The n=50 vs n=2000 result sets the protocol: a loop-rate delta of 3.6 points at base rate 3.6 % needs ≳ 1,000 prompts per arm for z ≈ 3.5 (binomial), hence n ≥ 2,000 and no max_tokens.
- Perplexity is structurally blind to both routes because teacher forcing re-anchors every step; any guard evaluation must include free-running generation.

### Gaps
- No paper identifies which pruned experts carried termination behaviour, and no pre-prune statistic has been validated as a looping predictor.
- The 0xSero loop-detection rule (what counts as an attractor) is not published.

## KQ7. Attention-sink interaction with KV/attention compression

### Takeaway
The sink token's routing in the SE layers must be preserved (pruning any SE collapses the sink, D_sink ≥ 90 %); KV-cache eviction/quantisation methods that drop or low-bit the sink token fail independently, so SE preservation and sink-token preservation are two separate necessary conditions on the same mechanism. Massive activations behave as fixed biases (set-to-mean is lossless, set-to-zero is fatal), which bounds what weight quantisation of an SE may change: its magnitude, not its exact value.

### Cited Findings
- "MoE models rely on these experts to induce sinks, and those sinks must be kept intact during sparse attention or KV compression." — [arXiv 2507.23279 via bytez summary](https://bytez.com/docs/arxiv/2507.23279/paper); D_sink ≥ 90 % after SE pruning — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- Survey: sink-token preservation is applied to "KV compression, sparse attention, and precision-aware quantization protection"; preserving initial anchors is "essential to prevent the catastrophic collapse of model performance"; sinks are "a primary bottleneck for quantization"; gated attention reduces post-quantisation degradation; sinks are weak in layers 0–1 and grow in intermediate/deep layers. — [arXiv 2604.10098](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10098v1)
- KVSink (sink-preserving KV quantisation) and NOVA-KV (first sink tokens + recent tokens in BF16, middle quantised) exist as sink-aware KV methods; NOVA-KV reports GPT-OSS-20B at 8K context where 2-bit did not beat BF16 per decode step. — [arXiv 2508.04257](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.04257); [arXiv 2608.04074](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04074)
- Massive activations: set to mean → ppl unchanged (5.47→5.47); set to zero → inf; they are "fixed but important biases"; adding explicit learnable attention K/V biases removes them at equal ppl (3.04) in GPT-2. — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- Qwen3-Next uses gated attention to reduce sinks "and prevent expert collapse". — [arXiv 2604.10098](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10098v1)

### Inferences
- For the local stack (NVFP4 experts, memory notes on NVFP4 dense overlay and W4A4 serving): an SE's down_proj output of ~600–750 is a weight×activation product; NVFP4's block scaling keeps the weight magnitude, but any *activation* quantisation path (W4A4/FP8 activations) on the SE layers must retain the 10⁴× outlier range. Keep SE-layer activations unquantised or verify the sink survives after quantisation (D_sink check). **[extrapolation]**
- Memory note "DSA no-op below 2048 ctx" and any sparse-attention / KV-eviction plan for Thor serving must whitelist position 0 (and delimiter sinks) in every softmax layer; linear-attention (KDA) layers have no sink and are unaffected. **[extrapolation]**

### Gaps
- No paper measures SE behaviour or sink structure in hybrid KDA/Gated-DeltaNet models (GLM-5.3 class), nor in gated-attention MoEs.
- No measured interaction between expert pruning and KV quantisation on the same checkpoint.

## KQ8. Ranked never-prune guard checklist

### Takeaway
Seven guards, ranked by measured severity × confidence. G1–G3 are hard vetoes supported by catastrophic measured outcomes; G4–G6 are soft floors expressed as mass retention; G7 is a post-prune gate, not a prune-time guard. Total hard-veto cost is tiny (expected ≲ 0.2 % of experts for G1+G2 plus top-k per shallow layer for G3); the soft floors cost whatever the retention threshold forces.

### Cited Findings (what each guard rests on)
- G1 severity: SE removal = ppl 8.70→59.86, MATH-500 88→1.4, R1 avg 75.6→1.8. — [arXiv 2507.23279](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279)
- G2 severity: zeroing massive activations = ppl inf / 5729. — [arXiv 2402.17762](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17762v2)
- G3 severity: early-layer over-pruning = SWE-Bench Pro 44.58→27.29±17.51; proposed floor ≥ routing width per layer. — [arXiv 2609.18916](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916v1)
- G4 severity: frequency-tail removal = EvalPlus 0.873→0.011 (480B), 0.863→0.124 (K2); union guard +4.2 pts. — [arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v1); [arXiv 2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721)
- G5 severity: late-layer P95 ΔL +2.633 nats; terminal-surge S_spec 0.72 (GLM-4.6 L88 Medical). — [arXiv 2606.10703](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10703v1); [arXiv 2605.18498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18498)
- G6/G7 severity: loop rate 3.6→7.2 % (13 % agentic), invisible at n=50 and in ppl. — [0xSero](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B-W4A16); [arXiv 2607.16721](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16721)

### Gaps
- Thresholds for G2 dominance, G4 exclusivity/retention, G5 and G6 have no literature support; they must be calibrated locally against G7.
- Expected protected-expert counts for G4–G6 cannot be stated without running the computation on the banked statistics.
- Whether the local saliency bank stores a per-expert max (needed for G1 without a new pass) must be checked in the pipeline code; nothing in the literature substitutes for it.
