# The DOMAIN-SPECIALIST strategy for extreme (60–75%) MoE expert pruning: multi-domain specialists, routing-mass bounds, and collateral damage

One sentence up front: every >50% result that holds its in-domain score is a one-domain (or at most "reasoning-cluster") specialist with no healing up to 62.5% and healing beyond; the only controlled multi-domain-vs-single-domain sweep (PreMoe, DeepSeek-R1, 3 domains + dialogue) shows the extra domains cost ~1 pt at 50%, ~3 pt at 62.5% and ~13 pt at 75%, and the only direct routing-coverage measurement (Qwen3.6-35B) shows the union of just two domains' top-half expert sets already covers 77% of all experts — so a 4-domain specialist above ~60% cannot be built by "keeping each domain's experts"; it has to be built by a shared ranking that accepts a measured per-domain mass loss, and general-text ballast is not what forbids it (general text is the least domain-isolated traffic in every measured model, i.e. it rides on the shared core) — it is the knowledge long tail (MMLU/ARC/factual MC) that dies first and is not recoverable without expert-level fine-tuning.

Sibling notes: the one-shot/general-purpose frontier and the full >50% table with healing are in `expert_pruning_beyond_50.md` in this directory; this file does not repeat its criterion/allocation sections and focuses on the specialist reframing.

## Key Question 1: Full table of every >50% expert-pruning result (ratio, calibration domain(s), kept-domain delta, sacrificed-domain collateral, healing)

### Takeaway
Six primary sources report numbers above 50% (PreMoe and EASY-EP on DeepSeek-R1/V3, Half-the-Experts on Qwen3.6-35B and Gemma-4-26B, Martin et al. on GPT-OSS-20B, plus a 50%-with-guard GLM-4.7-Flash community card that documents the collateral). All are one-domain or one-cluster specialists; the kept-domain delta at 62.5% is −0.6 to −3.0 pt (math, code), the sacrificed-domain delta at the same ratio is −8 to −12 pt (GPQA/LCB off a math mask) or 6x general perplexity, and at 75% the sacrificed domains collapse (USMLE −66 pt, FinanceIQ −48 pt, LCB −21 pt on R1; general ppl 6x; MC/ARC/MMLU −30 to −50 pt on GLM-4.7-Flash at only 50%). No result above 62.5% is within 5% in-domain without healing.

### Inferences
- The kept-domain cost at 62.5% is bounded at roughly −1 to −3 pt for math and code *when the eval and the calibration are the same narrow distribution*; the sacrificed-domain cost at the same ratio is roughly 3–10x larger (−8 to −12 pt on R1's non-target reasoning benchmarks, 6x general perplexity on Qwen3.6). The decision is therefore not "does 62.5% work" but "which 8–12-pt losses are acceptable".
- The three families diverge at 75%: DeepSeek-R1 math degrades gracefully (−3.2 pt), Qwen3.6 code needs healing (−10.3 → −4.9), Gemma-4 collapses to zero. GLM-5.3-Flash and Kimi-K2 (the 763B–1T candidates) have no measured point above 50%; the DBES effective-rank evidence below suggests they sit closer to DeepSeek than to Qwen in redundancy, which argues for the graceful end but is an extrapolation.
- Knowledge-heavy "domains" (USMLE, FinanceIQ, MMLU-Pro, ARC, KMMLU) are the ones that collapse first *even when they are the calibration target* (R1 USMLE −66 pt in-domain at 75%), which means "finance" and "science" in the user's priority set are not specialist-safe at 75% the way code and math are.

### Gaps
- No primary source evaluates a ≥480B model above 50%; PreMoe's 70% and EASY-EP's 75% are on DeepSeek-R1/V3 (671B) only, with no agentic benchmarks except AgentBench-OS.
- Martin et al. measured no non-translation capability at any ratio, so the GPT-OSS 75%/87.5% rows have no collateral number at all.
- Half-the-Experts' union-guard point (0.829) reports no general-perplexity figure, so whether guarding general traffic *buys back* general text (vs only protecting code) is unmeasured.

## Key Question 2: Multi-domain specialists — pruning for 2–4 domains at once above 50%, and the marginal cost per added domain

### Takeaway
No source prunes for 2–4 domains above 50% and reports a table; the closest controlled evidence is PreMoe's DeepSeek-R1 generalist (3 reasoning domains + dialogue) vs per-domain specialists, where the multi-domain mask costs 1.0 pt average at 50%, 2.8 pt at 62.5% and 13.4 pt at 75% — i.e. each added domain is nearly free to ~60% and the cost is super-linear beyond. EASY-EP's 6-domain mixed mask at 50% on R1 costs 5.1 pt average (−7.6% rel, driven by finance −20.9 and LCB −3.5) but is free on V3-0324 (+0.8), so the price of mixing depends on the model as much as on the domain count.

### Cited Findings
- PreMoe DeepSeek-R1, specialist vs generalist average (MATH-500/GPQA/LCB): 50% 78.73 vs 77.69 (−1.04); 62.5% 73.89 vs 71.07 (−2.82); 75% 62.83 vs 49.46 (−13.37); the generalist's LCB is the fastest casualty (61.03 vs 56.99 at 62.5%, 49.63 vs 20.22 at 75%) — [PreMoe Tables C.8, C.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17639)
- PreMoe generalist construction: average token-level utility within each domain, combine with weights w_d (default 1/D), take top-M; mixture sensitivity at 50% is smooth: 1:1:1 avg 76.02; math-heavy 2:1:1 → MATH 97.20, LCB 62.87; code-heavy 1:1:2 → MATH 95.60, LCB 67.65; science-heavy 1:2:1 → GPQA 72.73, LCB 63.60 — a 2x weight on a domain moves its benchmark ~2–4 pt and the others ~1–2 pt the other way — [PreMoe Table D.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17639)
- PreMoe: generalist MMLU-Pro 71.36 → 77.58 by adding more calibration data (vs 82.29 full), i.e. 6 of the 11-pt gap closes with data, not with a different mask criterion — [PreMoe §4.4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17639)
- EASY-EP R1, 50%: per-domain masks avg 66.55 vs 6-domain mixed mask avg 61.47 (−5.08); per-benchmark mixed: AIME24 75.94 (vs 79.17 specialist), AIME25 61.98 (68.33), HMMT 42.50 (45.31), LCB 57.63 (61.11), GPQA 70.36 (70.12), USMLE 91.20 (91.67), FinanceIQ 57.95 (78.80), Agent-OS 34.17 (37.92) — [EASY-EP Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06792v2)

- EASY-EP cross-domain transfer: "similar-domain pairs transfer best (math/science, code/OS-agent, science/medicine)"; off-domain results are typically worse than the mixed mask — [EASY-EP §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06792v2)

- MESA, capture-regime finding: under decode-time calibration the four domains routed almost identically (per-domain routing-mass correlation 0.57–0.86) and the minimax objective "had no per-domain signal to grip"; under prefill calibration cross-domain correlation collapsed to ≈0 (math–knowledge −0.068, science–knowledge 0.004, instruction–knowledge −0.066) and the MESA-vs-REAP pruned-set Jaccard was 0.252 — [MESA §4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04453)
- Half-the-Experts union guard: "protecting the top general-traffic experts before filling the budget by saliency" at 75% pruned lifts HE+ 0.787→0.829; on-policy calibration alone 0.817 — [Half the Experts §5.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- GLM-4.7-Flash K-Guard at 50% (two-domain Korean+code guard): the protected set could not fit — 20 of 46 layers exceeded the 32-expert budget and 185 protected experts were dropped; result: coding macro −12.6, Korean −17.5, academic −28.6; the plain code-only 25% prune kept coding at −2.0 — [KGuard-REAP-32E card](https://friendli.ai/models/buugiiiiii/GLM-4.7-Flash-KGuard-REAP-32E)

### Inferences
- The "marginal cost of the k-th domain" is not additive in expert count; it is a function of how much of the k-th domain's top set is already inside the union. On R1, math+science+code+dialogue at 62.5% cost 2.8 pt vs one domain, so a 4-domain reasoning-cluster specialist at ~60–65% on a DeepSeek-style 256-expert model should land within ~3–5 pt of the single-domain rows (extrapolation from one model). On the Qwen3.6 evidence (two domains' top-half sets cover 77% of experts), a 4-domain specialist cannot be built by union above ~25% removal at all; it must be built by a shared mass ranking, which is exactly what makes each kept domain lose 20–30% of its routing mass at 50% (see KQ3).
- Code+agentic and math+science are the two cheap pairs (EASY-EP transfer); text ballast rides the shared core (KQ4). The expensive additions are the knowledge-bearing ones (finance, medicine, legal) and vision, which overlap least with the reasoning cluster and so cost the most experts each.
- The model matters more than the domain count: mixing was free on V3-0324 and cost 7.6% rel on R1 at the same ratio and same masks. A 763B–1T target should be tested at 50% mixed-vs-specialist before any >50% plan is built on it.

### Gaps
- No multi-domain mask has been evaluated above 50% in any paper; the PreMoe generalist at 62.5%/75% is the only data and it covers three reasoning domains plus dialogue — no agentic, finance, vision.
- EASY-EP has no mixed-domain row at 75% (64 experts).
- No source measures how the achievable ratio moves as domains are added at fixed quality (the "does each domain pull the ratio back toward 50%" curve); it can only be read off PreMoe's three ratios.

## Key Question 3: Routing-mass coverage — how concentrated is routing per domain in 256–384-expert models, and what floor does that put on experts-per-domain

### Takeaway
On the one fine-grained model with a published retention curve (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, 256 experts, top-8), keeping the top 50% of experts per layer retains only 88.8% (WikiText) / 81.3% (code) of a domain's routing mass, with the worst layer at 64.2%, and the top-32 experts carry only 38% of softmax mass — load-balanced routers leave "little slack"; our own GLM-5.3-Flash measurement (0.69–0.72 retained at 50% for kept domains, up to ~0.80–0.82 with the mass criterion) is in the same regime. No paper reports the "experts needed for 95%/99% of a domain's mass" figure for DeepSeek-V3/R1, Kimi-K2, Qwen3-235B or GLM-4.5/5 directly; DBES gives per-domain KL-from-uniform and effective-rank instead, which show Qwen3-235B routing is far more concentrated (and more redundant) than DeepSeek-R1 or GLM-4.6.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (256/8), 500K tokens per domain: normalized activation entropy 0.889 (WikiText) and 0.915 (code); Gini 0.581 and 0.492; dead experts per layer median 0, max 1; "keeping the top 50% of experts per layer retains only 88.8% (WikiText) and 81.3% (code) of routing mass, and the worst layer retains just 64.2%"; Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (512 experts, k=10): entropy 0.911, Gini 0.527, dead experts median 1 of 512 — [Training-Free Halving](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04575)
- Same model: mean softmax mass by rank — top-1 expert 0.046, top-8 0.182, top-32 0.380 ("remarkably flat"); renormalization is a 5.50x amplification at k=8 — [Training-Free Halving Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04575)

### Inferences
- Floor estimate for a single domain (extrapolation from the Qwen3.6-35B Lorenz data: top-50% → 81–89% of mass, Gini ≈ 0.5–0.58): reaching 95% of one domain's mass needs roughly 65–75% of the experts, and 99% needs nearly all of them. A 65%-removal specialist (35% kept) would retain on the order of 65–75% of its *own* domain's mass on that model; on GLM-5.3-Flash, which is flatter (0.69–0.72 at 50% with the mean criterion, ~0.80 with mass), the same removal would leave roughly 55–65%. These are extrapolations; no source publishes the curve beyond 50%.
- The routing-mass argument therefore does not give a hard "N experts per domain" bound; it gives a soft one: beyond ~50% the removed experts are not idle experts but low-weight contributors to every token (top-32 of 256 carry only 38% of mass), and the model survives because the renormalized survivors approximate them — which is why the specialist results hold at 62.5% in-domain despite ~30% mass loss, and why the "which experts, not how much" ablation (uniform weights +49.7% ppl vs random selection +1667%) says selection quality, not coverage, is what matters.
- Architecture prior for the 763B–1T targets: Kimi-K2 (DeepSeek-style, 384 experts) and GLM-5.x (GLM-style) should be expected to look like DeepSeek-R1/GLM-4.6 in DBES terms — low per-domain concentration (S_spec 0.1–0.5), low isolation (<0.4), high effective rank (0.45–0.59). That profile means (a) per-domain masks will overlap heavily (good for multi-domain), (b) there is less redundancy to prune than in Qwen3-235B (R_eff 0.24–0.31, "cliques"), so the Qwen specialist ratios are optimistic for these families. The REAP Kimi-K2 MC drop at 50% (−13.7 vs −5.8 on Qwen3-Coder) is consistent with this.

### Gaps
- No per-domain retention curve (fraction of experts covering 90/95/99% of mass) exists in print for DeepSeek-V3/R1, Kimi-K2, Qwen3-235B or GLM-4.5/5; DBES reports KL/rank/isolation only, and the Halving paper's Figure 1(b) curve is not numerically tabulated beyond the 50% point.
- Standing-committee and MoE Lens numbers are on 64–128-expert models; whether the "1–5 experts carry most mass" regime holds at 256–384 experts is not measured (the Halving paper's top-8 = 18% of mass on 256 experts suggests it does not).

## Key Question 4: The ballast question — can general text be one of the specialist domains, or is it what makes >50% impossible

### Takeaway
General text is not the diffuse outlier the premise assumes: on Qwen3.6-35B WikiText routing is *more* concentrated than code (entropy 0.889 vs 0.915, Gini 0.581 vs 0.492; top-50% retains 88.8% vs 81.3%), and in every DBES model the general-knowledge set has the *lowest* domain isolation (it uses the shared core everyone else uses). What makes >50% fail on general evals is the knowledge long tail (MMLU/ARC/factual MC) that lives in the many low-mass experts, plus corpus imbalance — in our own GLM-5.3-Flash run general text was 4.76% of calibration tokens and retained 0.49 mass vs 0.71–0.75 for the heavy buckets. General text as a specialist domain costs little in experts; general *knowledge* at >50% is unrecoverable.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: WikiText entropy 0.889, Gini 0.581, top-50% retains 88.8% of mass; code entropy 0.915, Gini 0.492, top-50% retains 81.3%; WikiText-ranked top-128 retains only 38.0% of code mass; union of the two top-128 sets = 198/256 — [Training-Free Halving](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04575)

### Inferences
- Two different things are called "general text". (1) Generic fluency/syntax/chat-register traffic is concentrated on the shared core (committee + high-mass experts) and is cheap to keep: it is the *least* isolated domain and the one whose top set overlaps everyone else's. (2) Factual/world knowledge is stored across the long tail of low-mass experts and is the first casualty of any >50% prune, regardless of whether general text is in the calibration mix (Kimi-K2 MC −13.7 at 50% with C4 present; GLM-4.7-Flash MMLU −40 pt with general replay present). A "text ballast" domain therefore does not pull the ratio back toward 50% for fluency; it is the MC/knowledge evals that will show the loss, and a specialist plan must declare them sacrificed.
- Our 0.49 general retention is primarily a corpus artifact (4.76% of tokens), not evidence that general text is intrinsically diffuse; the fix (balanced recalibration) was already identified in the audit and is prerequisite to any specialist pass. The Halving data suggests that with balanced calibration general-text mass retention at 50% should be *higher* than code's.
- Perplexity on general text is a weak proxy for the loss (Half the Experts: ppl and pass@1 decouple; MESA: random pruning gives the *lowest* ppl); the specialist decision should be gated on an MC/knowledge suite and a loop/degeneration rate, not on dNLL.

### Gaps
- No source measures routing concentration of general text vs code vs math on a ≥256-expert DeepSeek/GLM-style model; the WikiText-vs-code comparison is Qwen3.6-35B only and DBES has no "general text" set (its "Knowledge" set is HLE, a hard exam).
- No source reports what fraction of general-text *knowledge* (MMLU-type) survives a general-text-calibrated specialist above 50% — every >50% row either did not measure MC or measured it off a non-general mask.

## Key Question 5: Agentic/tool-use — does it survive at 60–70% as a target domain, and what breaks first

### Takeaway
No source measures agentic capability above 50%. At 50% with agentic trajectories in the calibration mix, single-turn and SWE-style agentic coding hold (Qwen3-Coder-480B SWE-Bench −1.8 pt, Kimi-K2 +2.2 pt; HOPE SWE-Bench Pro 43.84 vs REAP 39.61 on Qwen3.5-122B), but multi-turn tool use is the first thing to break: Kimi-K2 BFCL Multi-Turn 0.355→0.164 at 50% while Non-Live held 0.840→0.785, and τ²-bench pass^3 (consistency over three runs) drops faster than pass^1. The documented failure modes are degenerate repetition (invalid-patch rollouts restarted), loops caught by τ²-bench's LLM loop detector, and loss of long-generation ability on reasoning models.

- Ordering of agentic failure at 50%, from the only large-model data: multi-turn state tracking (BFCL Multi-Turn) breaks first and hardest, then consistency across runs (pass^k), then live/single-turn function formatting, with task-level SWE patches last. ## Key Question 6: Sacrifice candidates — which domains' experts overlap least with the priority set (freeing the most experts), and which are cheap to re-add by LoRA

### Takeaway
The measured overlap structure says: medical and legal are the most isolated domains in every model (DBES S_iso 0.57–0.64, S_spec 0.9–1.1 on Qwen; highest on DeepSeek/GLM too), so their experts are the cheapest to drop; finance is intermediate and is badly hurt by mixing (R1 FinanceIQ 78.8 specialist → 57.9 mixed at 50%); math↔science and code↔agent are near-free pairs; vision has no overlap measurement in any text-MoE study. Re-adding a sacrificed domain by LoRA on attention/shared-expert is unsupported: the one LoRA heal that worked restored the *kept* domain, and the translation authors state parameter-efficient methods cannot recover pruned expert blocks.

- UMoE (prune-regrow-finetune on Qwen3-30B/3.5-35B): after domain SFT, pruning the 64 least-salient experts costs Direct-SFT only 0.12 pt but UMoE 5.0 pt — domain fine-tuning *concentrates* a domain into fewer experts; the method itself requires expert training — [UMoE](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11444)

### Inferences
- Ranking the user's seven domains by "experts freed per domain sacrificed", from the overlap data: medical/legal (not in the set, already free) > finance (moderately isolated, and it pays most under mixing, so keeping it is expensive) > vision (unmeasured in text-MoE studies; our own 0.755 retention at 50% is middling) > science (its experts are the most exclusive of the reasoning trio: removing them costs GPQA −12.7 while removing math's costs −3.4) > general knowledge long tail (can't be "sacrificed" selectively — it goes with the ratio) > code/agentic and math (the core; cheap pairs). Finance and science are therefore the two priority-set members whose retention most constrains the ratio; dropping finance alone is the single cheapest trade (extrapolation from EASY-EP/DBES).
- "Re-add later by LoRA" has no supporting datapoint. The LoRA-on-attention/shared-expert mechanism re-routes and re-weights *surviving* experts; a domain whose specialist experts are gone has nothing to route to. The only evidence of post-hoc domain injection without expert training is router-side up-weighting of existing domain experts (+1.6 to +5.5 pt), which presupposes they survived. Treat re-adding finance or science as requiring expert fine-tuning (impossible on FP4-source experts) or a merged-in dense adapter, not a LoRA patch.

### Gaps
- No study measures expert overlap between vision tokens and text domains in a natively multimodal text-MoE (GLM-5.3-Flash class); the only vision-MoE routing reference in the literature set is DeepSeek-VL2, cited but not analyzed.
- No study attempts to re-add a sacrificed domain to a pruned MoE by any parameter-efficient method; the claim that it is cheap is unsupported.

## Key Question 7: The distillation lever at high ratio — is 65–75% specialist pruning feasible with forward-pass healing plus LoRA on attention/shared expert, or does it require expert fine-tuning

### Takeaway
At 75% the two healing recipes measured recover about half the in-domain gap by very different means: Half-the-Experts' LoRA (attention + shared expert + full router, experts frozen byte-identical, 95.2M params, 1,863 examples, <3 h) recovered 52% of the HumanEval+ gap and 70% of the general-ppl excess on Qwen3.6-35B; Martin et al. needed full-parameter FT on 10k distilled examples for translation on GPT-OSS-20B and state LoRA is insufficient. Neither recipe gets a 75% specialist back inside 5% of base in-domain (−4.9 pt code; −4.1% rel MT), and nothing has been healed at 60–65%. The LoRA-only route is compatible with frozen FP4 experts; it is the one the FP4 constraint permits, and it has one positive datapoint and one negative.

### Cited Findings
- Half the Experts, Qwen3.6-35B 75% pruned: LoRA rank 64 on attention, linear-attention and shared-expert projections, router matrices trained in full, routed experts frozen ("verified byte-identical after training"); data = base model's answers to the 2,047 calibration prompts (1,863 fit the sequence length), loss masked to the assistant turn; HE+ 0.787→0.841 (52% of the 10.3-pt gap), MBPP+ 0.659→0.685 (23%), pooled McNemar p = 0.027; code ppl 5.45→4.85; general ppl 30.9→9.4; 95.2M trainable params (0.86%); "under three hours on the target device" — [Half the Experts §5.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- Half the Experts: the expert tensors are "fused three-dimensional parameters that the LoRA implementation cannot wrap", which is why experts were frozen — the recipe was forced by tooling, not chosen — [Half the Experts §5.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- Half the Experts calibration-side levers at 75% without training: on-policy calibration 0.787→0.817; union guard 0.829; both "remain more than six HumanEval+ points below the 2-bit base (0.896)"; a 37.5%-keep on-policy point scores 0.835 — [Half the Experts §5.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)
- Martin et al., GPT-OSS-20B: synthetic distillation = base model generates En→X targets for 10k English passages; full FT with TRL, seq 2,048, effective batch 16, lr 1e-5, 3 epochs, bf16, 4×A6000; 68.75%: 0.859→0.904 (55% of gap); 75%: 0.902 (−0.039); 87.5%: 0.871 (−0.071) where the unhealed model errors on 100% of inputs; supervised FT on FLoRes only is worse at high ratios (87.5%: −0.130 vs −0.071); "parameter-efficient methods are insufficient to recover entire pruned parameter blocks" — [Martin et al. Tables 1, 3, 5, 6, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28042)
- HOPE: SFT of REAP- and HOPE-pruned Qwen3.5-122B on 1B tokens of unpruned-model LiveCodeBench traces "narrows the gap but does not close it"; HOPE stays ahead of REAP after SFT — [HOPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18916)
- Router-only KD (REAP-R) on Qwen3-30B-A3B at 37.5% pruned: MMLU 0.6483→0.6507, HumanEval 0.9146→0.9207, MBPP 0.6220→0.6180; no router-only result above 37.5% — [Router KD, as transcribed in expert_pruning_beyond_50.md](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02217v1)
- PreMoe: no healing at any ratio; question+trace calibration vs question-only is worth 10.9 pt average on the R1 specialist — the "forward-pass" lever here is calibration content, not fine-tuning — [PreMoe Table D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17639)
- EASY-EP: no healing; CommonCrawl calibration gives AIME24 0.00 on both models, input+output calibration is best — [EASY-EP Tables 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06792v2)
- Internal: FP8 teacher logits for the GLM-5.3-Flash eval token set are banked (`teacher_from_cache: true`), so KD on that set is feasible without re-running the 321 GB teacher; NVFP4 is +1.56% ppl vs pruning +21.0% at 50% — [memory: reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md)

### Inferences
- The feasibility question splits by what is being recovered. In-domain generation quality on a code/math specialist at 75% is recoverable to within ~5 pt by re-routing + shared-pathway LoRA (one datapoint), and the same recipe pulls general perplexity most of the way back — consistent with KQ4's picture that fluency lives on the shared core. Capabilities whose experts were removed (translation into a specific language, factual knowledge) needed full FT (Martin) or were never recovered (nobody measured MC after healing). With FP4-source experts the LoRA route is the only one available; it should be expected to buy ~half the in-domain gap and most of the general-ppl excess, and nothing on sacrificed domains.
- Extrapolating the two 75%-with-heal points (−4.9 pt code, −4.1% rel MT) and the unhealed 62.5% points (−0.6 to −3.0 pt), a 60–65% four-domain specialist with LoRA/router healing should land in the −3 to −6 pt band on its kept generative domains, with knowledge MC −10 to −20 pt and finance/science −10 to −25 pt. This is an extrapolation across two model families; no direct point exists.
- "Forward-pass healing" (ExFold-style output rescaling, router recalibration from the banked saliency) has no published result at any ratio above 50% in this literature set; the closest is PreMoe's calibration-content effect (+10.9 pt from using traces), which is pre-prune, not post-prune.

### Gaps
- No healing result exists at 60–65% on any model; the only healed points are 68.75–87.5%.
- No healed model above 50% has a post-heal MC/knowledge or agentic benchmark; whether LoRA/router healing restores any sacrificed-domain capability is unmeasured.
- No source heals a model whose experts are FP4/INT4 quantized; Half-the-Experts healed a Q8-evaluated bf16-trained model and Martin a bf16 model.
