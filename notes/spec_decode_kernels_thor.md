# Speculative decoding and CUDA kernel-level levers for giant pruned+quantized MoE decode on Jetson AGX Thor (sm_110a)

Scope: lossless or quality-matched levers only; every number carries hardware / model / quant / runtime / batch where the source gives it. `LOCAL` = the user's own measurements in `~/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/*.md` and `~/glm-5.3-reap/wiki/`. `[Spark]` = DGX Spark GB10 (sm_121, same 128 GB LPDDR5X / 273 GB/s class but a different SM and a different FP4 tensor-core path) — comparable device, not Thor. `[extrapolated]` marks projections. Several primary pages were blocked (NVIDIA forum HTML 403, x.com article 402, Mirage Medium post 403); where that happened the finding is taken from a second source and flagged.

Headline: on this device class, speculative decoding on a fine-grained MoE at batch 1 is bounded by the *expert union* of the verification batch, and the 2026 literature (EcoSpec, "Limits of Speculation", MoESD, S2-MoE, DraftExpert, AcceptMoE, EVICT) now states the same cost model the user measured locally. Kernel-level, the Thor engine is already at 67% of its streaming bandwidth after the MoE fix and the NVFP4 dense overlay; the remaining ranked levers are (1) bytes/token on the *non-expert* path (attention is the binding term on both GLM-5.3-Flash and the 753B target), (2) batched/fused per-layer attention kernels so a K-wide verify actually amortises the non-expert 42–63% of B_tok, (3) only then expert-aware speculation with a DFlash-class drafter, (4) megakernel/graph levers worth ≤5–15% and not first.

## Key question 1: Why spec-decode gains shrink with expert count / active size on giant MoE at batch 1, and what the 2026 literature says the fix is

### Takeaway
The mechanism is settled in both LOCAL data and 2025–2026 papers: verifying m tokens routes to `E·(1−(1−k/E)^m)` distinct experts, so expert bytes scale almost linearly in m exactly where speculation needs them flat; at batch 1 only the non-expert bytes amortise. The published fixes are all "shrink the verified expert union" — cost-aware draft-tree selection (EcoSpec, lossless, 1.15–1.36× avg at B=1 on 120B–671B MoE), draft-tree trimming (EVICT, lossless, 1.21× over EAGLE-3), or restricting the verifier's eligible experts (AcceptMoE, S2-MoE — NOT lossless, 1.29× / up to 5.3× on offloaded edge) — and none of them has been measured on a Thor/Spark-class device with the model resident.

### Cited Findings
- LOCAL cost curve (GLM-5.3-Flash REAP-50 NVFP4, CUDA server, Thor, 45 layers, batch width K): cost of a K-wide verify in AR-step units K=2 **2.164**, K=3 2.198, K=4 2.636, K=8 4.977, K=16 9.477. With the native MTP block's 72.3% acceptance every depth is a net loss (depth 2 = 0.95×); 85% acceptance → 1.02×, 95% → 1.13×, a perfect drafter → 1.27×. Per-width dprof at width 4 (ratio to width 1, 0.25 = perfect): `attn:kda` 0.54, `attn:mla` 0.46, `mla:absorb_q` 0.25, but `ffn:moe` **0.82** (~45% of the step). Distinct experts touched by M tokens with 144 experts top-8: 8.0 (M=1), 7.8 (M=2), 7.4 (M=4), 6.6 (M=8), 5.4 (M=16) per token — [LOCAL glm53-spec-decode-moe-wall](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-spec-decode-moe-wall.md)
- LOCAL expert-union fit: "Measured verify-cost curve sits ON the expert-union bound: cost(K) ≈ 0.58·144·(1−(1−8/144)^K)/8 + 0.42; K=3 predicts 2.06× vs 2.20× measured. Break-even at K=3 needs per-token accept p ≥ 0.70; 1.3× needs p ≈ 0.95" — [LOCAL glm53-kda-launch-bound](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-kda-launch-bound.md)
- LOCAL GGUF path: GLM-5.3-Flash IQ3_M on Thor with llama.cpp partial offload (`-ngl 24`), native MTP: 46.5% accept at depth 2, 58.5% at depth 3, but 2.99 → 1.73 / 1.85 tok/s because batch cost is flat below 32 tokens (`op_offload_min_batch_size`) — [LOCAL glm53-mtp-serving-path](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-mtp-serving-path.md), [LOCAL llamacpp-small-batch-offload-cliff](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/llamacpp-small-batch-offload-cliff.md)
- MoESD gives the same formula: expected activated experts `N(t) = E·(1−((E−K)/E)^t)`, with the verify pass processing `B·γ` tokens; "SD is ineffective for MoE under small batches" and gains appear only at moderate batch where most experts are already loaded. Peak 2.29× was Qwen2-57B-A14B (64 experts, top-8) at HumanEval/greedy/γ=4 with a 0.5B draft in vLLM on 80 GB GPUs; no batch-1 number is given — [MoESD, arXiv 2505.19645](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19645v1)
- EcoSpec names the mechanism "expert scattering": confidence-driven draft selection picks tokens that route to disjoint experts, enlarging the per-step expert union. Batch-1 greedy, 8×H200, HF Transformers prototype, γ=4: EAGLE-3 on Qwen3-235B-A22B touches 23.7 experts/layer on average (AR = 8) with 0.832 s verify; EcoSpec 20.5 experts, 0.730 s. GPT-OSS-120B 11.6 → 10.6 experts (AR = 4); DeepSeek-V3.1 31.4 → 31.2. Speedups vs AR: Qwen3 1.36× (EAGLE-3 1.22×), GPT-OSS 1.31× (1.14×), DeepSeek-V3.1 1.15× (MTP 1.10×). Acceptance length barely changes (2.41 → 2.32 on Qwen3); the speedup is lower verification cost. Lossless (verification rule unchanged). At batch 4 the advantage shrinks to 1.09×/1.06× and at batch 8 both methods are slower than AR — [EcoSpec, arXiv 2607.12696](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12696v1)
- "Limits of Speculation" fits verification time as `T = β₀(m) + α(m)·Ū + c·L_ctx` where Ū is the per-layer unique-expert count; Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B (128 experts, top-8), EAGLE-3, 1×A100, bf16, **B=1**, 925,752 rounds; R² 0.967–0.999; α ≈ 0.078–0.084 ms/expert for m ≤ 4 and ≈ 0.264–0.268 ms/expert for m ≥ 5 (a ~3.2× jump in per-expert cost once the verify microbatch exceeds 4). An oracle with full knowledge of the reference text plateaus at **2.34×** with mean accepted length ~2.1 and mean draft length ~2.8 even when the budget is 12: "a high token-acceptance probability alone no longer guarantees a speedup" — [arXiv 2609.22156](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22156v1)
- S2-MoE (llama.cpp, Jetson Orin NX 16 GB / AGX Orin / RTX 4090, experts offloaded to SSD or CPU): self-draft uses fewer experts per token (OLMoE 2 vs 8, DeepSeek 1 vs 6, Qwen3 3 vs 8, GPT-OSS 1 vs 4), a utility rule `U = p_i·T_AR / (new experts·T_exp + draft cost)` admits candidates while U ≥ 1 (R² 0.943–0.999 vs measured verify latency), and "reuse-aware expert gating" biases the target router toward already-loaded experts — explicitly "a controlled target-routing approximation rather than a strictly lossless transformation" (PPL ratio 1.012–1.013, top-1 agreement 89.1–93.0%). Orin NX HumanEval: DeepSeek 0.55 → 2.07 tok/s (3.76×), OLMoE 2.30 → 12.08 (5.25×), Qwen3 0.47 → 1.22 (2.60×), GPT-OSS 0.95 → 1.65 (1.74×); RTX 4090 range 1.2–2.9×. States that MoESD's batch-scale gains "largely diminish in edge inference settings, where batch sizes are typically one or two and verification remains memory-bound" — [S2-MoE, arXiv 2608.15018](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15018v2)
- DraftExpert (experts offloaded to CPU/Flash; RTX 4090 + CPU, Snapdragon 8 Elite NPU with llama.cpp Q4_0): draft = shared expert + router top-1 + one trained accelerator-resident draft expert per layer; verify cost is the union `U_l(D) = ⋃ TopK_l(h)`; drafting stops when confidence < τ and marginal expansion Δ_i > budget. Exact-mode lossless. Acceptance 83–85% at K=4; 1.29–1.61× over AR-with-offload on DeepSeek-V2-Lite / Moonlight-16B-A3B (64 routed + 2 shared, top-6) — [DraftExpert, arXiv 2607.24434](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1)
- AcceptMoE restricts the verifier's eligible expert set per verification block (self-sizing from router scores and offline commitment probabilities): **not lossless** (−0.27 pp mean accuracy vs EAGLE-3 across 12 model-task pairs); 1.290× at batch 1 in SGLang with all experts resident, 2.06× under physical offloading, H2D traffic −73.6–77.1% — [AcceptMoE, arXiv 2608.02989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02989)
- EVICT trims the draft tree to a cost-effective prefix before target verification using offline-profiled verification cost; training-free, lossless; up to 2.35× over AR and 1.21× average over EAGLE-3 on "diverse MoE backbones" (SGLang; hardware/batch not in abstract) — [arXiv 2605.00342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00342)
- Router-side fix needs retraining: routers trained with a global load-balancing loss + shared experts + a consistency loss + autoregressive expert selection show much higher expert coactivation, and the best model is 21% faster under SD at on-par accuracy — [Expert coactivation, arXiv 2609.22471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22471)
- Field data on 128 GB devices (all `[Spark]` unless stated), previously compiled in the companion note: dense Qwen3.8-27B vLLM MTP n=3 2.2×/2.16×/1.91× (BF16/FP8/NVFP4); Gemma-4-26B-A4B NVFP4 MTP +81%; GLM-5.3-Flash UD-IQ3_XXS llama.cpp MTP n=2 15.5 → 20.8 tok/s (+34%, 95% acceptance, mean accepted 2.70, "n=2 beats n=3/n=5"); MiMo-V2.5 310B NVFP4 + DFlash 22.3 → 27.6 prose (+24%) / 45.4 code / 66.9 JSON; DeepSeek-V4-Flash + DSpark 17.06 → 13.25 (−22%) or 8.37 (−51%); Qwen3.5-397B-A17B NVFP4 4×RTX PRO 6000 MTP=2 50.5 → 39.6 (−22%); Thor TRT-Edge-LLM Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MTP +26% but DFlash −30% — [thor_serving_performance.md Q6](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/thor_serving_performance.md), primary sources [Weschera README](https://github.com/Weschera/GLM-5.3-Flash-Unsloth-1x-DGX-Spark/blob/main/README.md), [Classmethod DSV4](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-deepseek-v4-flash-0731-llama-cpp/), [Qwen3.8-27B Spark study](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/comprehensive-qwen3-8-27b-study-on-dgx-sparks-quantization-speculative-decoding-and-tp-dp-scaling/381102), [MiMo DFlash thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/mimo-v2-5-dflash-speculative-decoding-on-a-2x-dgx-spark-pair-22-67-tok-s-depending-on-workload/375607), [SM120 report](https://discuss.vllm.ai/t/sm120-rtx-pro-6000-nvfp4-moe-performance-report-qwen3-5-397b/2536), [TRT-Edge-LLM benchmarks](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-Edge-LLM/0.10.1/user_guide/performance/performance-benchmarks.html)
- Jetson AI Lab's own tutorial states the MoE caveat without data: a smaller DFlash draft size can win when "parallel verification activates extra experts in an MoE model"; recommended starting depths MTP 3, DFlash 15, DSpark 4; Thor Qwen3.8-27B dense Q4_K_M llama.cpp MTP depth 3 ≈ 13 → 35 tok/s — [jetson-ai-lab speculative decoding](https://www.jetson-ai-lab.com/tutorials/speculative-decoding/)
- ds4 on `[Spark]` (DeepSeek-V4-Flash IQ2-class, 81 GiB): target eval ≈ 60 ms, a 2–5-row DSpark verify ≈ 83–97 ms, "each accepted draft saves about 55 ms of weight traffic" — i.e. verify width 5 costs ~1.5× a single step on a 256-expert top-6 model whose B_tok is dominated by dense/Q8 parts — [antirez/ds4 #773](https://github.com/antirez/ds4/issues/773)

### Inferences
- The ranking of field results by (active size, expert count) is exactly the expert-union model: dense 27B (nothing to scatter) 2.2×; 26B-A4B (128 experts top-8 but tiny experts, so expert bytes are a small share of B_tok) +81%; GLM-5.3-Flash (A18B, 256 experts) +34% only at n=2; DSV4-Flash (256 experts, top-6, A13B) negative with a 4–8-token DSpark verify; Qwen3.5-397B negative. The share of B_tok that is routed experts, not the model size, is the predictor.
- The LOCAL K=2 point (2.164 AR-steps for a 2-wide verify) sits far ABOVE the expert-union bound the user later fitted (0.58·1.94+0.42 = 1.55 at K=2) while K=3 sits on it (2.07 vs 2.20). A 2-wide verify costing 2.16 steps means the *non-expert* 42% did not amortise at width 2 at all. The note says "every non-gemm kernel is called per token with offset pointers" — on a 34-layer KDA model that was already launch-bound (238 launches/token for `kda:gates`), per-token non-gemm kernels make the non-expert cost ~linear in K. Re-measuring the K=2..4 curve after `gemv_multi` and with a chunked KDA kernel (see Q4) is the cheapest way to test whether the real ceiling is 1.27× or the expert-union ceiling (K=3 perfect-acceptance 4/2.56 = 1.56×, K=7 8/4.25 = 1.88× under the same 58/42 split).
- EcoSpec-style expert-aware drafting is worth ~10–15% of verify cost on 128–256-expert models (23.7 → 20.5 experts) and is lossless; AcceptMoE/S2-MoE gating is worth more but changes outputs (top-1 agreement ~89–93% is in the same band as the NVFP4 overlay's 90.1%), so it is only admissible under the user's "quality-matched" clause with a measured PPL/flip budget.
- The 2026 literature has no measurement of expert-aware SD on a Thor/Spark-class device with the model resident; all edge results (S2-MoE, DraftExpert) are offloading regimes where expert *loads* cost ms each and the union penalty is 10–100× larger than resident DRAM reads, so their 2–5× numbers do not transfer.

### Gaps
- No 2026 paper measures spec decode on a 144–192-expert REAP-pruned model; the only such data is LOCAL.
- "Limits of Speculation" and EcoSpec are A100/H200 results; the m≤4 vs m≥5 per-expert cost jump (3.2×) may be an A100 grouped-GEMM artefact rather than a universal law — unverified on Thor.
- The LOCAL discrepancy between the measured K=2 verify cost (2.164) and the fitted expert-union bound (1.55) is not explained in the notes and was not re-measured after the KDA launch fixes.

## Key question 2: Draft choices measured on MoE targets — native MTP, EAGLE-3, DFlash, Medusa typical acceptance, n-gram, self-speculation

### Takeaway
On MoE targets the ranking by measured acceptance is DFlash/DFlash2 (5–8 accepted tokens/step on code) > native MTP (2.5–3.3 at depth 2–3) ≈ DSpark > EAGLE-3 (≈2–2.8 on MoE) > n-gram (workload-dependent, no MoE data); but because the verify cost is set by the expert union, the *net* winner on a resident giant MoE is whichever drafter gets the most accepted tokens per *distinct expert touched* — which favours short, high-confidence drafts (MTP n=2 on GLM-5.3-Flash) or expert-aware trees, not long DFlash blocks, unless the non-expert share of B_tok is large.

- Draft-side cost on the CUDA server: the draft head's shared `lm_head` is 1.269 G per drafted token, larger than the rest of the MTP block (0.371 + 0.067 G); NVFP4-ing it is system-level lossless (a draft error costs a rejection, not a wrong output) and lifts the projected 1.38× to 1.49× at depth 3 (projection made before the batch curve was measured) — [LOCAL glm53-cuda-server-spec-batch](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-cuda-server-spec-batch.md)

### Inferences
- Draft quality is not the long pole on GLM-5.3-class MoE: the native head already sits at 72% / accepted ≈2.5–2.7, DFlash2 would raise that to ≈5.8, yet both must pay the union cost. The useful figure of merit is accepted tokens per verify cost; on the LOCAL 58/42 split with 144 experts, MTP n=2 (2.25 tokens / 2.07 cost = 1.09×) and DFlash2 block 8 (≈5.8 / 4.25 = 1.36×) are both small, and DFlash2's 2.18 GiB BF16 drafter (which cannot be published after fine-tuning under NC-ND) eats resident memory the REAP target needs.
- Typical acceptance (+11% LOCAL) is the only drafter-side lever with a measured Thor win on a MoE, but it is quality-changing at T>0; it belongs in a "quality-matched" tier with a measured flip rate, alongside AcceptMoE-style gating.
- Self-speculative top-2 drafting on a resident model is unattractive: it saves no bytes on the non-expert 42–63% of B_tok, and the literature only shows it winning when expert *loads* (offload) dominate.

### Gaps
- No measurement anywhere of EAGLE-3, DFlash or n-gram on a REAP-pruned MoE; LOCAL has MTP only. No n-gram-on-MoE numbers at all.
- DFlash paper's per-model batch-1 table and whether any MoE target is included could not be verified (abstract only).

## Key question 3: The batch-of-one escape — how much of B_tok a K-wide verify amortises on the LOCAL byte breakdown, ceiling vs the measured 1.27×

### Takeaway
On the user's post-overlay B_tok the non-expert share (KDA 47.4% of the original 19.76 G, MLA 13.1%, lm_head 6.4%) is what a K-wide verify can amortise; under a clean expert-union model that ceiling is 1.29× (K=1) … 1.56× (K=3) … 1.88× (K=7) at perfect acceptance, not 1.27×, and ≈1.0–1.1× at the native head's 72% — the measured curve is worse than the union model at K=2 because per-token KDA/MLA kernels did not batch, and the published cost models (Limits of Speculation, S2-MoE) say verify cost also jumps ~3× per expert once the microbatch exceeds 4 on grouped-GEMM paths.

### Cited Findings

## Key question 4: CUDA kernel levers on sm_110a — fused MoE, NVFP4 GEMV, CUTLASS block-scaled via `110a`, CUDA graphs, megakernels, KDA fusion, KV kernels, bandwidth

### Takeaway
What is proven on Thor: a warp-per-row NVFP4 MoE GEMV with hardware `cvt.fp4x2` unpack and x-reuse across rows (LOCAL 4.71× on `ffn:moe`, 82% of streaming), launch fusion on launch-bound linear-attention ops (+4.8%), and NVFP4 on every dense matrix (−50.6% B_tok, +42% tok/s). What does not exist on Thor as of Oct 2026: block-scaled FP4 tensor-core MMA through any public toolchain (CuTe DSL 4.8 refuses it for sm_110a; PTX docs list the block-scale form for sm_100a/sm_100f/sm_110f, not sm_110a), so "870 TFLOPS FP4" is not reachable by CUTLASS — BF16 `tcgen05` is, so W4A16 (dequant-to-BF16 then tensor core) is the only tensor-core FP4 path. CUDA graphs and megakernels address launch gaps and per-kernel ramp/tail; LOCAL (0.4% gap) and ds4-on-GB10 (300 launches moved to graph: flat) show those gaps are already closed in hand-written engines, leaving ≤10–15% `[extrapolated]` for a full megakernel.

- CUDA graphs are the right fix only when host launch gaps exist; the LOCAL KDA problem was 238 tiny kernels each too short to reach bandwidth (ramp + tail per kernel), which graphs do not remove and fusion did. ## Key question 6: Reconciling "sub-3-bit buys fit, not speed" (GB10 decode flat at 16–20 tok/s) with bandwidth-bound theory

### Takeaway
The plateau is bandwidth-bound after all — but on the bytes that do not shrink with expert bit-width: on GB10 a 2-bit DSV4-Flash still reads 10–13 GB/token (dense Q8 projections, attention, shared experts, lm_head, routing) for a 45–55 ms floor at the measured 232 GB/s, and ds4's nsys shows no launch gaps; the LOCAL Thor finding that KDA/attention is 47–60% of B_tok is the same structure. The second, separate regime is the vLLM NVFP4 kernel path (64% efficiency vs 90% for FP8 on the same dense model), where dequant kernel efficiency, not bytes, caps speed.

### Cited Findings
- `[Spark]` ds4 (DSV4-Flash IQ2-class, 81 GiB): 231–234 GB/s read probe; execute 45.0–45.7 ms of a 55–56 ms step (encode 9.8–10.6 ms); "each token reads roughly 10–13 GB of weights (dense layers plus 6 routed experts across 61 layers)" → floor 45–55 ms; "plain decode on GB10 sits at ~85–90% of the physically achievable memory bandwidth"; fusion (−61 launches) and graphing (−300 launches) both flat; "weight bytes read per token is the stated limit" — [ds4 #773](https://github.com/antirez/ds4/issues/773)
- `[Spark]` 1 → 2 → 3-bit curve flattens at ~16–19 tok/s on DSV4-Flash / GLM-5.3-Flash GGUF (2-bit only 3.6–5.2% / ~17% faster than 3-bit for 13–20% fewer bytes) — [thor_serving_performance.md Q2](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/thor_serving_performance.md) citing [Classmethod DSV4](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-deepseek-v4-flash-0731-llama-cpp/) and [Weschera](https://github.com/Weschera/GLM-5.3-Flash-Unsloth-1x-DGX-Spark/blob/main/README.md)
- LOCAL: experts are 83% of resident bytes but 24.1% of B_tok on GLM-5.3-Flash; KDA 47.4%; "REAP+NVFP4 quantised where the disk was, not where the bandwidth is" — [LOCAL glm53-cuda-server-roofline](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-cuda-server-roofline.md); NVFP4-ing the dense path halved B_tok and gave +42% only after the x-reuse fix (first attempt +1.4%) — [LOCAL glm53-activation-traffic-finding](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-activation-traffic-finding.md)
- `[Spark]` dense efficiency vs bits: Gemma-4-31B 84% (bf16) → 62% (int4) of the bandwidth bound; Qwen3.8-27B vLLM FP8 90% vs NVFP4 64% — [Gemma 4 Spark thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/gemma-4-day-1-inference-on-nvidia-dgx-spark-preliminary-benchmarks/365503), [Qwen3.8-27B study](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/comprehensive-qwen3-8-27b-study-on-dgx-sparks-quantization-speculative-decoding-and-tp-dp-scaling/381102); SGLang NVFP4 dense 85% of bound — [ai-muninn](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-bandwidth-ceiling-85-percent)
- LOCAL: half2 MoE math −1.6%; inline fp8 scale decode −19% ("instruction-bound, not shared-bandwidth-bound"); hardware FP4 unpack vs constant-memory LUT was part of the 4.71× — [LOCAL glm53-decode-gap-profile](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-decode-gap-profile.md)

### Inferences
- "Bits buy fit not speed" holds because expert bits only touch the ~25–40% of B_tok that is routed experts; on GLM-5.3-Flash going from 3-bit to 2-bit experts cuts B_tok by ~8%, matching the ~5–17% measured. The lever that does move the plateau is the bit-width of the *always-read* path (attention projections, shared expert, lm_head), which is why the LOCAL dense overlay was worth 42% and why the 753B target's 12.9B attention parameters (≈8 GB/token at NVFP4) are its binding term `[extrapolated]`.
- Dequant ALU is a real but secondary term: it shows up as the 62–64% NVFP4 efficiency in Marlin/vLLM paths and in the LOCAL instruction-bound sub-ops; it is fixed by hardware unpack + x-reuse, not by fewer bits.
- Memory latency at small tensors and launch overhead are visible only in launch-bound sub-ops (LOCAL KDA gates at 7% of BW, `mla:indexer` 12–14 GB/s); on GB10's GGUF engines they were measured as negligible.

### Gaps
- No per-kernel bandwidth-vs-bits sweep exists on Thor for the same model (4-bit does not fit beside 2-bit for the giant models); the GB10 plateau evidence comes from one engine's own read probe and an un-itemised 10–13 GB/token estimate.

## Key question 7: Proposed inference stack for a ~100 GiB REAP-50 1T-class model on Thor, with projected tok/s per lever

### Takeaway
Build on the LOCAL custom CUDA server (the only stack with measured Thor results at this size), in this order: (1) bytes on the always-read path, (2) one fused/batched kernel per attention layer so verify width m amortises it, (3) hardware-unpack NVFP4/2-bit MoE GEMV with x-reuse (proven), (4) only then a short-depth native-MTP or expert-aware DFlash2-class verify on the slot-state engine, (5) graphs/megakernel last. Projected AR decode for the 753B REAP-50 at ~2.2-bit experts + NVFP4 attention is ~9–12 tok/s `[extrapolated]` from B_tok ≈ 17 GB/token; spec decode could add 1.3–1.9× `[extrapolated]` only if lever (2) lands, versus the measured ≤1.0× on the Flash engine today.
