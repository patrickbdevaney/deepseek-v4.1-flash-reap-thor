# Quantize Before You Prune Past Fifty

**DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 is the only 763B-1T candidate that closes the byte budget on one Thor, and the best recipe for it does not push expert pruning past 50%.** The recipe is bit-exact HOPE/REAP deletion of 192 of 384 experts per layer on a balanced seven-domain corpus with per-modality vision ranking and never-prune guards, a short block-wise heal on frozen FP4 experts, then trellis-class requantization of the survivors to about 2.9 bits per weight, with the 189 GiB Engram tables served from NVMe by O_DIRECT reads. That lands at **about 113.7 GiB** (weights 101.5, KV 0.1, reserve 12), with a 1M-token context costing under 1 GiB of KV, and an expected +10-20% text perplexity versus the unpruned model (extrapolated: each axis is measured separately, the stack is not). The literature does not support pruning a generalist much past 50%: every >50% result is a single-domain specialist, and the only evidence that progressive re-ranking moves the cliff is an 8-expert Mixtral. Past 50%, each extra pruning point costs more perplexity than the bits it frees (derived below). GLM-5.3-753B fits only by pushing both pruning (55-61%) and quantization (2.0-2.5 bpw) past their measured cliffs, is text-only, and decodes at about 5-9 tok/s; MiMo-V2.6-Pro-MOPD needs 75-82% expert removal and decodes at 1.5-2.7 tok/s, so it is a no-go. The real blockers are logistics: 51 GB of free disk against a 475 GiB checkpoint, no one having booted V4.1 on sm_110a, and a sub-4-bit expert kernel that does not yet exist. For decode, closing the gap between the 116 GB/s the local server realizes and the 190-200 GB/s its kernels reach is worth more than every expert-side byte reduction combined.

## V4.1-Flash is the only 1T-class target whose bytes close

The byte budget is weights plus KV at target context plus a 12 GiB runtime reserve, all inside 117 GiB. Thor exposes 122.83 GiB, a full `drop_caches` leaves about 118-119 GiB, and a pure-CUDA server has run a 180B at 107.6 GiB resident while a further 5.5 GiB hard-reset the box ([local note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/structural_and_memory_budget.md)). The three candidates differ sharply in where their bytes sit. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 is 475.24 GiB on disk (763.2B parameters), but only 268.95 GiB is routed experts (56.6%); 189.13 GiB is Engram lookup tables and the non-expert, non-drafter floor is 9.77 GiB. Its routed experts ship as MXFP4 (E2M1 with an E8M0 scale per 32 values, 4.25 bits per weight including scales), and its KV cache is **890 bytes per token** ([arXiv 2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19969); header-derived figures in [target_models.md](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/target_models.md)). MiMo-V2.6-Pro-MOPD is 527 GiB with 93.9% of its bytes in QAT-trained MXFP4 experts and 51,200 B/token of KV (57 times V4.1). GLM-5.3 is 703.7 GiB of FP8 (96% routed experts, 89,856 B/token of MLA latent) and has no vision tower ([GLM-5.3 repo](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3), [go/no-go note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/glm53_753b_go_nogo.md)).

Engram is the structural surprise. It reads 48 rows of 264 bytes per token (12.4 KiB), at addresses that are a pure function of the token ids, so it adds almost nothing to per-token bandwidth while holding 40% of the checkpoint. On Thor, host and device are one pool, so pinned-host offload frees nothing: a 4 GiB pinned allocation cost 4.09 GiB of GPU-visible memory, and mmap drags 64 KiB of page cache per touched row. An O_DIRECT row store measured **1.13-1.30 ms per token** for 48 concurrent reads on this machine, about 1% of a 100 ms decode step ([engram note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/engram_off_device_thor.md)). Resident Engram is impossible even at MXFP4 (97.3 GiB), and removing it roughly doubles perplexity (code 1.91 to 4.16, prose 6.45 to 9.06) ([LibertAI EXL3 card](https://huggingface.co/LibertAIDAI/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017-EXL3-2.9bpw)). So V4.1 is viable only with Engram on NVMe, and then it is the best-shaped model in the set: active parameters are about 16.4B per decode token, against about 40B for GLM-5.3 and about 42B for MiMo-Pro.

The table below shows the memory arithmetic. Expert bytes scale as kept fraction times bits per weight over 4.25 for V4.1 and MiMo, and as 724.8B times bits over 8 for GLM. The fixed term is 9.79 GiB for V4.1 (attention 4.80, embeddings and head 2.47, shared experts 1.32, vision tower 0.90, router and hyper-connection parameters 0.30), 15.2 GiB for MiMo (vision kept, MTP and audio dropped), and 19.42 GiB for GLM (attention, indexer, dense and shared experts at FP8, embeddings at BF16, MTP dropped). At the 50% generalist ceiling the three targets would need 247 GiB (MiMo, native), 169 GiB (GLM at 4 bpw) or 134 GiB (V4.1, native) of experts, which is why the quantization axis has to carry the weight. V4.1 reaches 50% at 3.0 bpw with a razor-thin 0.2 GiB spare, so the practical operating point is 2.9 bpw with a 3 GiB margin. Neither GLM nor MiMo gets near their pruning ceiling at any bit-width above 2.0. GLM-5.3 reaches exactly REAP-50 only at 1.96 bpw with FP8-protected attention, or at 2.2 bpw if attention, dense and shared experts also go to NVFP4 and KV to FP8 (the "protect attention" precondition then fails). The two-line summary for MiMo-Pro is that 4.25 bpw needs 82% removal, 3.0 bpw needs 75%, and holding 50% would need 1.5-1.6 bpw, below the lowest point on its published ladder (2.0 bpw: +43.7% perplexity) ([AesSedai ladder](https://huggingface.co/AesSedai/MiMo-V2.6-Pro-MOPD-GGUF), [MiMo go/no-go note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/mimo_pro_go_nogo.md)).

Quality ranking inside each model follows the quant-versus-prune economics developed in the next section. For V4.1, ranks 3 and 4 tie on expected damage but differ in character: the 224-expert recipe is quant-heavy and the 128-expert recipe is prune-heavy with no requantization, and the latter is the only one that vLLM's fused router accepts without a patch (legal expert counts are 1, 2, 4, ..., 128, 192, 256, 320, 384; a count such as 224 or 160 needs a torch fallback) ([vLLM kernel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vllm-project/vllm/main/csrc/moe/topk_softplus_sqrt_kernels.cu)). For GLM-5.3 the measured anchors are grim: the unpruned model at 2.30 bpw (Unsloth UD-IQ1_S) costs **+72% perplexity** with mean KLD 0.69 and top-1 agreement 72.6%, and a REAP-50 MLX build at 4-bit costs **+75.6%** ([Unsloth GLM-5.3](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/glm-5.3), [pipenetwork](https://huggingface.co/pipenetwork/GLM-5.3-REAP50-MLX-4bit)). Stacking them gives an extrapolated 1.6-2.2x perplexity, nothing like "within 10%". A trellis quantizer would do far better than GGUF (EXL3 at 2.75 bpw on unpruned GLM-5.3 shows KL 0.086, versus 0.374 for GGUF at 2.70, on different test sets) ([Mia-AiLab](https://huggingface.co/Mia-AiLab/GLM-5.3-EXL3-2.75bpw-TensorFold)), but EXL3 has no sm_110 evidence at all. For MiMo-Pro the 75% rank-1 recipe stacks the literature's worst measured specialist loss on a +9.8% perplexity requantization of experts that were QAT-trained at 4 bits, on a model with no shared expert to absorb rerouted mass. MiMo-V2.6-Flash REAP-50 (88 GiB, 23,040 B/token of KV) is the sane fallback and fits 1M context at FP8 KV with about 9.5 GiB spare under an 8 GiB runtime, or 103 GiB total at 128k BF16 under the 12 GiB reserve; it loses 8 Artificial Analysis index points to Pro (38 versus 46) before any compression ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/mimo-v2-6-pro)).

## Fifty percent is the pruning ceiling, and quantization is cheaper down to 2.9 bits

Every lever has a measured stopping point, and the evidence is lopsided. For expert deletion, **no generalist survives past 50% within 10%**. REAP at 50% on Qwen3-Coder-480B costs 1.7 points of code average and 5.8 points of multiple-choice, but fine-grained general models lose 14-22 points of MC at the same ratio (Qwen3-30B-A3B 0.721 to 0.503, GLM-4.5-Air 0.747 to 0.554, Kimi-K2 0.780 to 0.643 with BFCL multi-turn 0.355 to 0.164) ([REAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)). The highest shipped ratio on any 400B+ model is 50%. Beyond it the only measurements are specialists: DeepSeek-R1 at 62.5% keeps MATH-500 within 0.6 points but loses 8.6 on GPQA and 8.1 on LiveCodeBench, and at 75% GPQA falls 27.8 points ([PreMoe](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17639)); Qwen3.6-35B at 75% loses 10.3 points of HumanEval+ while general perplexity rises roughly 6x ([Half the Experts](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)). A multi-domain mask is nearly free to about 60% (PreMoe's R1 generalist costs 1.0 points at 50%, 2.8 at 62.5%, 13.4 at 75%), which is the best evidence for pushing a little past 50%, but it is a four-domain reasoning mix on a model with a shared expert, not a seven-domain generalist with vision. HOPE is the right criterion: at 50% it ranks 1.58 versus REAP's 2.42 across methods, and lifts SWE-Bench Pro on Qwen3.5-122B from 39.61 to 43.84 ([HOPE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18916)).

The case for progressive pruning is real but narrow. On Mixtral-8x7B, one expert per layer per round with re-ranking moves C4 perplexity at 50% from 14.74 (one-shot) to 10.44, and to 9.76 with 1M tokens of fine-tuning per round; on MMLU one-shot collapses to chance at 37.5% dropped (13.55) while iterative holds 45.63, and iterative at 62.5% (perplexity 12.55) beats one-shot at 50% ([Jaiswal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05586)). Re-ranking alone supplies 86% of that gain, and it is the only result in the literature where staging moves the cliff rather than only improving quality at ratios both schedules survive; on larger fine-grained models, staged recovery (SlimMoE, SlimQwen, Puzzle) beats one-shot only at a fixed ratio, with 2-3 stages capturing all of it ([SlimQwen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08738)). Mixtral has 8 experts. Nothing exists for 128-384 experts.

Healing has a clear ceiling too. A LoRA (rank 64 on attention and the shared expert, full router, experts frozen, 1,863 self-generated examples, 95.2M trainable parameters, under 3 hours) recovers **52% of the HumanEval+ gap at 75%** on Qwen3.6-35B and cuts general-chat perplexity from 30.9 to 9.4 against a base of 5.20 ([Half the Experts](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)). That recovers routing and ballast, not deleted capacity; the translation study needed full-parameter distillation on 10k examples and states that parameter-efficient methods cannot recover pruned expert blocks ([Martin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28042)). Router-only KD is a no-op on a REAP mask (Qwen3-30B at 62.5% keep: average 0.5456 to 0.5454), though it fixes quantization mis-routing (1.5-bit Mixtral perplexity 9.66 to 7.69; [router note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/router_post_prune_rules.md)). ExFold is a serving-time fold that keeps every expert resident, so its 61.50 to 68.75 recovery compares a static pool with a dynamic one and is not a healing result; REAP at 25% keep on the same Qwen3-30B collapses to 22.25 versus 69.04 and ExFold reports no permanent-pruning result there ([ExFold](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24938v1), [local analysis](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/exfold_routing_recovery.md)). Merging and factorization are closed levers here: REAM trails REAP by 4 points on bf16 Qwen3-30B and by **13 points at 48% on a DeepSeek-V4-Flash class model with FP4 experts**, because merging dequantizes and re-snaps to the FP4 grid, and MoBE-style factorization yields only 24-30% with training and a custom kernel.

Quantization is the cheaper axis for longer. On the same 1T MoE (Kimi-K2-Thinking, INT4-source, imatrix GGUF), perplexity versus the 4.55 bpw baseline reads: 4.06 bpw +2.5%, 3.25 bpw +7.4%, 2.92 bpw +14.0%, 2.26 bpw +41%, 1.83 bpw +72.6%, with attention and shared experts held at q8_0 ([ubergarm Kimi-K2-Thinking](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Thinking-GGUF)). The BF16-source sibling is 2-30 points better at matched bits (2.92 bpw +7.8%, 1.83 bpw +43%), which is the double-quantization premium for a 4-bit source ([ubergarm Kimi-K2-Instruct](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Kimi-K2-Instruct-GGUF)). The V4.1-specific datum is much more favorable: LibertAI's EXL3 trellis build of 1017 at 2.9 bpw (experts about 3 bpw, layers 18-22 at 2 bpw) moves perplexity from 1.8749 to 1.9094 on code (+1.8%) and 6.4277 to 6.4520 on prose (+0.4%) against the dequantized base ([LibertAI](https://huggingface.co/LibertAIDAI/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017-EXL3-2.9bpw)). Trellis codes (QTIP lineage: Llama-3.1-405B at 3.52 to 4.12 at 3 bits and 5.08 at 2 bits) are the 2-3 bit frontier ([QTIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235v4)), and the quantizer choice matters enormously: MLX 3-bit experts reportedly collapse the V4-Flash model entirely ([pipenetwork](https://huggingface.co/pipenetwork/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-MLX-REAP50)), while MiMo-Pro's GGUF ladder reads +2.7% at 3.5 bpw, +9.8% at 3.0, +19.6% at 2.5 and +43.7% at 2.0 ([AesSedai](https://huggingface.co/AesSedai/MiMo-V2.6-Pro-MOPD-GGUF)). Locally on GLM-5.3-Flash, REAP-50 costs +21.0% perplexity, one GGUF bit-width step +1.46% and NVFP4 +1.56%, so pruning is about 14x more damaging per unit of compression (local measurement, [GLM-5.3-Flash REAP memory](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/reap-criterion-and-corpus-audit.md)).

These curves let me state the marginal trade explicitly (my derivation, cross-model proxies, extrapolated). On V4.1 each extra pruning point near 50% costs about 0.6 points of perplexity on the measured 0909 curve (+7.1% at 37.5% to +14.7% at 50%), and the curve is convex. Moving from 192 to 160 experts frees enough bytes to lift the expert width from 3.0 to 3.6 bpw, worth about 0.5 perplexity points per pruning point on the Kimi quant curve between 3.2 and 3.8 bpw. Below 2.8 bpw the quant curve steepens to roughly 3 perplexity points per pruning-point-equivalent of bytes. The two marginal costs cross near **50% pruning and 2.9-3.0 bpw**, which is why that is the optimum for V4.1. GLM-5.3 is forced past the knee on the steep side, so its optimum is more pruning plus 2.2-2.5 bpw, with everything else bad. Perplexity is also the wrong ruler for the tails. Teacher-forced perplexity hides looping (GLM-5.2 REAP at 34%: loop rate 3.6% to 7.2%, 13% on agentic prompts) and multi-turn tool collapse (Kimi-K2 BFCL multi-turn halves at 50%), and it can rate a broken pruned model above an intact one ([Half the Experts](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16721)); the GLM-5.2 loop-rate figures come from [0xSero](https://huggingface.co/0xSero/GLM-5.2-504B-W4A16). The pruning side therefore carries an uncounted tail risk that the quant side does not.

The remaining levers are small, and the first three cost nothing on a quantized source. Expert-width slimming is training-free and beats whole-expert deletion by 5-13 points at 50-60% expert-parameter reduction on models up to 80B (TENP 59.06 versus 48.02 at 60% on Qwen1.5-MoE), but for FP4 sources it is bit-exact only for gate/up channels (whole rows) and for down_proj in aligned blocks of 32 (MXFP4) or 256 (K-quants) ([width-slimming note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/expert_width_slimming.md)); it composes with deletion only super-additively before healing and is untested above 80B. Layer dropping removes 12-25% of layers at a 2-7 point cost on Mixtral-class models and collapses long-chain reasoning; the 203 GB Engram cannot be dropped. KV: FP8 on MLA is near-lossless (DeepSeek-R1 GSM8K 0.9157 to 0.9154, [vLLM](https://vllm.ai/blog/fp8-kvcache)), and V4.1 already ships FP4 KV through QAT. Per-expert mixed precision recovers 0.3-13 points at matched bits (the "+20-25 points" figure was against a uniform 2.0-bit GPTQ baseline), GGUF cannot express it, and it saves resident bytes without reducing decode bytes because bytes scale with routing-weighted width ([per-expert note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/per_expert_mixed_precision.md)). The Engram table itself quantizes to MXFP4 (97.3 GiB) with about 12% relative L2 error and GSM8K 0.967 versus 0.966 ([NVIDIA NVFP4 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-NVFP4)), which helps disk, not the pool.

Proven versus extrapolated can be stated flatly. Proven on this machine: GLM-5.3-Flash REAP-50 with HOPE, a balanced corpus and per-expert heal at 93.6 GiB NVFP4, with the +21% perplexity bill above; an NVFP4 dense overlay (-50.6% bytes per token, +1.56% perplexity, 0.138% confident flips); bit-exact removal of experts from FP4 checkpoints (REAP on Kimi-K2-W4A16, LibertAI on V4.1 at 256/272 experts, NVIDIA ModelOpt on MXFP4 gpt-oss); and Engram row reads at 1.2 ms. Measured elsewhere but not stacked: V4.1 REAP to 192 experts (+14.7% text perplexity with plain mean saliency and a 262k-token calibration corpus), V4.1 trellis requantization at 2.9 bpw (+0.4-1.8%). Extrapolated, in decreasing confidence: the 50%-plus-trellis stack on V4.1; a balanced seven-domain mask beating +14.7%; frozen-expert block-wise healing recovering routing damage on a 384-expert model; any generalist at 58-67%; progressive staging extending the cliff on a fine-grained model; healing a model that is both pruned and sub-4-bit.

## A five-stage pipeline that stops pruning when the gates say stop

The pipeline is built for V4.1 and ports to the others by changing the model adapter. It is constrained by three facts: only bit-exact operations are free on FP4 experts, a 763B-class student never fits resident for end-to-end backward, and perplexity cannot be the gate. Stage 0 is preparation. The calibration forward must run on the checkpoint's own `inference/model.py`, because `transformers` has no `deepseek_v41`; carry `(x[B,S,4,5120], ffn_pre)` between layers instead of `(x, topk)`, add the shared compressed-KV and index state from layers 2/8/14/20, and apply Engram before blocks 1 and 14 with rows gathered by O_DIRECT from shards 47-48 (never calibrate with Engram off; the saliency would describe a different model). At 8192 tokens by 16 rows the peak is about 47 GiB (bf16-dequant layer) or 28 GiB (MXFP4 resident with per-expert dequant), provided the indexer scores are evaluated row-wise (a naive 16-row indexer tensor is 64 GiB). 1017 is a minor update with new Engram tables, so rankings must be recomputed rather than transferred (a ranking carried between V4-Flash revisions showed per-row cosine as low as 0.946) ([v41 delta note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/v41_architecture_pruning_delta.md), [LibertAI REAP card](https://huggingface.co/LibertAIDAI/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-REAP-256E)). Cache the teacher once: top-token logits via 12 importance-sampled tokens per position (about 36 bytes per token, unbiased and matching full KD within noise) rather than top-K, which is biased ([arXiv 2503.16870](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16870v2)).

| Stage | Action | Experts kept / bytes | Data and method | Gate before promotion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Port calibration forward to the reference model code, Engram row gather, teacher cache, Super-Expert max-norm pass | full model streamed | 5M routed tokens, 7 domains, 8k rows; 12-sample teacher logits | forward matches reference on real weights |
| 1 | Pass 0 on full model; HOPE under guards and `protect_frac` 0.04; bit-exact slice | 384 to 230 (40%), or 212 (45%) if the ratio-extension arm is run | per-domain, per-modality banked accumulators | Tier 1 |
| 2 | Re-rank pass on the pruned model; second slice | 230 to 192 (50%); optional 212 to 148 to 128 | same corpus, same guards; renormalization and bias replay after each slice | Tier 1 each stage, Tier 2 at the last candidate |
| 3 | Block-wise heal, experts frozen | no byte change; adapters about 0.2 GB | 2-8M self-distilled tokens from the teacher, thin domains included | Tier 2 vs pre-heal |
| 4 | Trellis requantization of survivors, dense path held at 4-8 bits | 192 experts at 2.9 bpw (101.5 GiB) | Hessians from the healed model on the same corpus | Tier 2, then Tier 3 |

The calibration corpus is the part most likely to decide the outcome. Equal-by-domain mixtures beat usage-proportional and every single source (+4.6 to +18.8 points over C4 on dense models, with the value of balance rising with ratio), the knee of the per-domain curve is at 17-25% share, and 0% general text costs 44 MMLU points at 50% pruning ([dose-response analysis](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/calibration_mixture_dose_response.md), [arXiv 2606.03328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03328)). Use general 20%, agentic 15%, code 15%, vision 14%, math 12%, science 12%, finance 10%, measured in routed tokens rather than bytes, with every pool at least 10% and at least 500k routed tokens (about 5M tokens total), stratified chunk order (blocked order left media at 74-85% of a 33-hour pass), and 8k sequences (a 4x length change moves the keep-set Jaccard only to 0.9745, against 0.838 for resampling at fixed length). Vision needs special handling: V4.1 carries a second router bias `gate.bias_vl` correlated -0.05 with the text bias, text-only ranking at 272 experts is **worse than random on vision** (+15.9% versus +7.2% caption perplexity), and plain mean saliency at 256 experts gives +27.4% caption perplexity against +5.5% text. The fix is per-modality normalization before combining (caption +8.9% at the price of text +14.1% in LibertAI's 50/50 variant), so enforce a vision floor through the banked per-bucket accumulators rather than a global average ([vision note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/vision_retention_pruning.md)). Use licence-clean sources only (Docmatix MIT, PixMo-docs ODC-BY; avoid ChartQA GPL, ShareGPT4V and APIGen-MT CC-BY-NC, R1-Distill-SFT CC-BY-NC-SA).

Stages 1 and 2 are the pruning stages. Run pass 0 on the full model with the router replayed exactly: scores are `sqrt(softplus(xW/gate_temp))`, the bias (or `bias_vl` for image tokens) only selects, the weights are the unbiased scores renormalized and multiplied by 1.5. Upstream REAP hard-codes softmax and unbiased top-k, which is wrong for noaux_tc routers, so the observer is a rewrite. Fix the gate definition before every observation pass, and delete rows, apply the reference-mass renormalization and replay the bias immediately after every prune. Select with HOPE (pᵀFp with an F matrix of 384² per layer, 22.5 MiB total) under hard guards: Super Experts (per-expert max down_proj norm above the 99.5th percentile and a tenth of the global maximum, which needs one extra hooked pass because a mean-saliency bank does not contain it), sink-token routing at position 0 in the shallow layers, a per-layer floor of at least the routing width, domain-exclusive experts kept to at least 0.9 per-domain mass retention, tighter late-layer retention, candidate termination carriers, and the shared expert never touched ([guard checklist](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/never_prune_guards.md), [Super Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23279): removal takes Qwen3-30B perplexity 8.70 to 59.86). Use `protect_frac` 0.04, which consumes 35-42% of the mask at 50% and 60-70% at 70%; above 60% it must shrink to floors and low-priority specialist pools must be dropped outright rather than thinned. Keep the allocation uniform per layer (searched allocation buys 0-3 points and `num_local_experts` is one scalar in most stacks). For a 50% target, a front-loaded two-stage cut (384 to 230 at 40%, re-rank, 230 to 192) costs two passes. The ratio-extension schedule from the progressive note is 45%, 30%, 13.5% of remaining, landing at 212, 148, 128 experts; pass cost is about 5.5 hours for GLM-5.3-Flash and an extrapolated 6-10 hours for V4.1, so a three-stage run is 13-20 hours of calibration plus 0.5-1.5 hours of surgery per stage ([progressive schedule note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/progressive_schedule_design.md)). Do not heal between stages: a gradient heal barely changes later selections and the student is not resident anyway. Slice MXFP4 weights and their E8M0 scales by expert index, slice `gate.weight`, `gate.bias` and `gate.bias_vl`, and re-link the 46,372 `weight.scale` tensors that assign-loading leaves stale.

Every stage ends in a gate, and promotion never rests on perplexity. Tier 1 (about an hour) is 500k held-out tokens stratified at 50k or more per domain: paired mean KL with a document-bootstrap interval, top-1 agreement (0.5% needs about 43k tokens, times 1.2-9.3 for within-document clustering), confident-flip rate, plus a 120-prompt greedy loop screen. Fail on a pooled agreement drop worse than 0.5%, any domain below 0.90x the best domain (the GLM ballast collapse was 0.579 versus 0.916), flips above twice the previous stage, or loop rate above twice the teacher's. Tier 2 (6-8 hours, every other stage) adds per-domain KL at 0.5% resolution, a 300-prompt loop test, 100 BFCL multi_turn_base items (the Kimi collapse detector), 1,000 PopQA, 1,600 log-prob MMLU items, 32k RULER, and for vision a 2,048-token caption perplexity plus paired ChartQA/DocVQA/TextVQA. Tier 3 (3-5 days) is the n=2000 uncapped loop run, full BFCL multi-turn, 64k RULER and 0.2% per-domain KL ([eval protocol](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/eval_protocol_pruned_moe.md); sample-size method from [Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). Stop at the last stage that clears Tier 2. The surviving count then determines the bit-width the bytes can afford. On V4.1 with a 3 GiB margin, 230 experts afford 2.43 bpw, 212 afford 2.64, 192 afford 2.91, 160 afford 3.49, 148 afford 3.78 and 128 afford the native 4.25 (iso-byte frontier, from the arithmetic above). Every stage is therefore a shippable candidate, and the marginal analysis in the previous section picks 192.

Stage 3 is the heal, done once, on the final mask, in high precision with experts still bit-exact FP4. The measured-feasible design is block-wise rather than end-to-end: stream each layer from NVMe once, co-stream the teacher layer, train LoRA (rank 16-64) on attention and the shared expert plus the full router and per-expert scalars, with the experts frozen and no FP4 backward needed (gradients flow through dequantized BF16 weights, the NeMo `MXFP4GroupedMM` pattern, [NeMo](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/automodel/nemo-automodel/nemo_automodel/components/quantization/mxfp4)). Estimated cost on Thor is 2M tokens in 1-2 hours and 8M tokens in 4-10 hours at about 75 GB resident, with about 0.5 hours per million tokens of teacher-logit caching; the end-to-end LoRA variant on cached logits is a short polish at about 110 GB peak, which must run with the vLLM server stopped ([layer-streamed note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/layer_streamed_healing_infra.md), EfficientQAT's Block-AP at [arXiv 2407.11062](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11062v3)). All of this is extrapolated: no framework streams FP4 MoE experts on aarch64, and layer-local healing on a pruned MoE is unmeasured (OverRep shows local MSE loses about 11 points of generation retention against logit KD unless a short end-to-end pass follows, [arXiv 2609.06974](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06974)). The data is self-distilled output from the unpruned teacher across the seven-domain mix with the thin domains included, because plain SFT on an off-distribution set can leave a model worse than no tuning ([healing note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/healing_recovery.md)), and nothing rescues a domain whose experts were deleted unseen. Run the 1-layer resident-versus-streamed gradient diff before any long run; an existing streaming trainer had a silent-gradient bug. Stage 4 is quantization, last, because P-KD-Q ordering measured perplexity 5.05 against 24-53 for every quantize-first order (a small dense NF4 study, [extreme-quantization note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/extreme_quantization.md)). Fit a trellis-class quantizer layer by layer to the healed experts with Hessians collected on the balanced corpus through the healed model, hold attention, shared experts, gates, hyper-connection parameters and the first layers at 4-8 bits (on DeepSeek-V3 the recipe that put attention at 2 bits lost 14.7% where a protected one at fewer bytes lost 0.9%, [Pareto note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/prune_quant_pareto_frontier.md)), and re-run Tier 2. Quantize the dense FP8 tensors to NVFP4 only as a separately gated decode lever, because on V4.1 they are FP8 sources and pay a second quantizer.

## Showstoppers: disk, Engram, FP4 sources, and a runtime nobody has booted

The first blocker is disk. The Thor NVMe has 51 GB free of 936 GB, with 312 GB in the Flash REAP workspace and 212 GB of models. V4.1 calibration needs the whole 475 GiB (510 GB) checkpoint readable by range, including the 189 GiB of Engram tables that must stay on local NVMe for serving; GLM-5.3 would need at least 1.1 TB (704 GiB FP8 source plus about 357 GiB of REAP-50 FP8 master plus outputs). Reclaiming the Flash workspace and models directory would just barely hold V4.1's source and leave little for outputs, so a second drive is the safe answer, but the only attachment points are one M.2 Key M slot (PCIe Gen5 x4, the system drive's), one M.2 Key E Gen5 x1, QSFP28 and USB, and Engram latency over USB or a x1 lane is unmeasured ([engram note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/engram_off_device_thor.md)). The second is the FP4 source itself: V4.1 and MiMo experts are MXFP4 (MiMo trained with MXFP4 QAT and quantize-dequantize after each RL update, so the codes are the model), no master exists in higher precision, deletion, aligned width slimming and Engram offload are bit-exact, and merging, SVD and requantization pay a second quantizer. The BF16 round trip is lossless for block-FP4, so the entire cost is the second quantizer; the Kimi proxy puts it at +2-6 points around 2.75-2.9 bpw and +30 points at 1.83 bpw, and V4.1's own EXL3 result says a trellis quantizer pays almost nothing at 2.9. Third is Engram correctness: the 1017 tables changed and are not reusable from 0909; vLLM's `cpu_offload` uses UVA pinned memory, which on unified memory frees nothing, so every stack needs an NVMe row-store patch (existing Spark patches for vLLM and SGLang and exllamav3's `RowTable` are the donors); mmap is wrong because each touch pulls 64 KiB of page cache out of the shared pool.

The runtime is the largest unknown. No source reports V4 or V4.1 running on sm_110a with any stack. The local vLLM nightly aarch64 image (0.32.0rc1) contains `deepseek_v41` with the router compiled for SM 110, but MXFP4 MoE goes through Marlin (4- and 8-bit only), so the 2.9 bpw recipe cannot run there; sparse MLA on SM 11.0 falls to the unverified FlashMLA path; LibertAI needed `--enforce-eager` because CUDA graphs caused illegal memory accesses on GB10. The expert-count table limits stock vLLM to 128 or 192 (a 160-expert or 224-expert variant needs the torch top-k fallback 0xSero used). llama.cpp support is an open, unmerged PR (#28696) with a known `MUL_MAT_ID` CUDA crash and no two-level candidate mask ([PR](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28696)), exllamav3 ships x86_64 wheels only with no Thor report, and SGLang has no Thor build. The only credible path for the recommended recipe is the project's own CUDA server, whose DSV4-0731 backbone port is byte-identical but needs mHC (already written for GLM), an Engram gather, the compressed-sparse indexer and the encoder-decoder KV projection, a larger port than 0731, plus a new 2.9 bpw trellis GEMV with hardware FP4 unpack. This is a build project, not a configuration. sm_110a also lacks block-scaled FP4 tensor-core MMA (BF16 tcgen05 works, so the tensor-core path is W4A16 dequant-to-BF16), and `ncu` is blocked by `ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM`. Licences are mostly clean (V4.1 MIT, MiMo MIT, GLM-5.3 permissive with a security-review clause above US$10B MaaS revenue). Two operating rules also bind: never co-run any probe beside a vLLM server (a hard reboot cost 12 hours), and detach every multi-hour job via systemd/setsid, never as a child of an interactive session.

Two modelling caveats sit on top of the engineering ones. Most of the V4.1 quality numbers above are perplexity-only (LibertAI's REAP card states that no downstream benchmarks were run), so the actual MC, code and agentic damage of any V4.1 prune is unmeasured. And MiMo-Pro and GLM-5.3 have never been run through this pipeline at all.

## Decode: close the bandwidth gap before touching top-k

The goal is fast decode without quality loss, and the evidence ranks the levers in a different order than the intuition "fewer bytes means more tokens". Bytes only convert to speed if the kernels sit at the streaming wall. On the local GLM-5.3-Flash NVFP4 server, halving bytes per token from 19.76 to 9.76 GB bought 1.4% until each block reused the activation vector across R=5 output rows (an NVFP4 gemv reads 7.1 bytes of x per byte of weights), and decode then went from 8.36 to 11.86 tok/s, which is about **116 GB/s effective** against kernels that individually stream at 190-200 GB/s and a 235-247 GB/s ceiling; the wall exceeds the kernel sum by 0.4%, so CUDA graphs and megakernels are not the lever (a megakernel is worth at most 10-15%, extrapolated) ([decode-gap profile](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-decode-gap-profile.md), [activation-traffic finding](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/glm53-activation-traffic-finding.md)). The MoE GEMV rewrite (warp per row, hardware `cvt.fp4x2` unpack, x reuse) gave 4.71x on the expert phase and reached 82% of streaming; an inline fp8 scale decode instead of a lookup table was a 19% regression because the kernel is instruction-bound; launch fusion on launch-bound linear-attention ops added 4.8%. Sub-3-bit buys fit, not speed: on a DGX Spark, DeepSeek-V4-Flash decodes at 17.35 tok/s at 2.56 bpw against 16.56 at 2.93 bpw (3.6-5.2% for 13% fewer bytes), a two-point fit gives about 37 ms of byte-independent time per step on that stack, and 2-bit streaming kernels on Thor need R of 8-10 rows of x reuse or the INT2 lesson repeats ([Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-deepseek-v4-flash-0731-llama-cpp/), [decode-bytes note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/decode_bytes_per_step.md)). The 2.9 bpw recipe therefore needs its own warp-level trellis decode kernel before it earns anything beyond fitting.

For the recommended V4.1 recipe the per-token read (in GB, decimal, my arithmetic from header-derived parameter counts) is 4.51 GB of routed experts at native MXFP4 (8.49B active parameters), 3.08 GB at 2.9 bpw, plus 7.88 GB of FP8/BF16 dense reads (attention 5.14, shared experts 1.42, lm_head 1.32), or about 4.47 GB if those dense tensors go to NVFP4 at 0.62 bytes per weight. KV and Engram are negligible (890 B/token and 12.4 KiB/token).

All rows are estimates, not measurements. The local anchor is the DSV4-0731 REAP-180B engine at about 14.6 tok/s with an autoregressive wall near 17.9 ([MEMORY](/home/patrickd/.claude/projects/-home-patrickd/memory/MEMORY.md)). The ordering by quality cost per byte saved is: NVFP4 on the dense path first (-18 GB on GLM-Flash, measured +1.56% perplexity, with the lm_head revert the likely owner of most confident flips); FP8 KV (near-lossless, though V4.1 is already FP4 by QAT); then the closing of the 116 to 160-200 GB/s gap, which is lossless; then top-k reduction. Top-k reduction is real but unproven where it matters: with the normalization denominator held at the trained reference mass (k2), Qwen3.6-35B going 8 to 4 experts loses 0.35 MMLU points (p=0.66) against 4.65 with standard renormalization, and Qwen3.5-397B at 10 to 5 loses 0.55 (not significant) ([arXiv 2609.04575](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04575)); but it is softmax-only, nothing tests sigmoid or sqrtsoftplus `noaux_tc` routers, nothing tests a pruned pool, and perplexity and MMLU disagree about the best k2, so scan k2 in {k1, k, 2k} with paired tests before adopting it. Dynamic confidence-based skipping saves 5-15% of expert reads for 0.3-0.9 points and static k2 dominates it ([MC-MoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06270), [ZEDA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18643)). Intra-expert activation sparsity keeps only about 95% of benchmark score at 85% sparsity and is a gather that fights the row-reuse scheme, so it fails the "no compromise" bar ([arXiv 2605.08575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08575)). Expert caching and temporal locality do nothing for resident weights, since about 6 GB of other weights stream between consecutive visits to the same layer.

Speculative decoding is the least reliable lever on a fine-grained MoE. Verifying m tokens touches E(1-(1-k/E)^m) distinct experts, so expert bytes scale almost linearly in m. For V4.1 at 128 experts and top-6 that is 22.4 distinct experts at m=4 and 40.8 at m=8, and pruning makes the union fraction worse, not better. On GLM-5.3-Flash on Thor the measured verify cost is 2.16 autoregressive steps at K=2 and 2.64 at K=4, the native MTP head accepts 72.3%, and the net is 0.77-0.95x with a bound of 1.13x at 95% acceptance and 1.27x at perfect acceptance; the "+34%" figure is a DGX Spark GGUF number (15.5 to 20.8 tok/s, 95% acceptance) not reproduced locally ([spec-decode note](/home/patrickd/glm-5.3-reap/research_notes/Compressing%201T%20MoE%20for%20Thor/spec_decode_kernels_thor.md), [Weschera](https://github.com/Weschera/GLM-5.3-Flash-Unsloth-1x-DGX-Spark/blob/main/README.md)). V4.1's DSpark drafter (about 14B parameters, 3 stages of 128 experts top-3) was a net slowdown (-22% to -51%) or at best +1.8% on a Spark ([Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-deepseek-v4-flash-0731-llama-cpp/)). The measured verify curve at K=2 sits 40% above the clean expert-union model because per-token attention kernels did not batch, so the lever is a single fused m-token attention kernel, not a better drafter; with that, the union ceiling is 1.56-1.88x, and for the V4.1 recipe the byte model (non-expert share about 59% after the dense overlay) gives cost(m=4) of about 2.1 and cost(m=8) of about 3.4 autoregressive steps, i.e. roughly 1.2-1.5x at best (extrapolated). Expert-aware tree selection (EcoSpec: unique experts per verify 23.7 to 20.5, lossless) is the published way to shrink the union ([EcoSpec](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.12696)). Typical-acceptance sampling adds about 11% on Thor's DFlash at temperature 0.3 but is not lossless. Build order for the speed work: bytes on the always-read path, then one fused m-token kernel per attention layer, then the hardware-unpack sub-4-bit expert GEMV, then, only after re-measuring the K-curve, a short-depth drafter; graphs and megakernels last. The same stack on GLM-5.3-753B projects to 9.2 tok/s autoregressive from about 17 GB per token with NVFP4 attention at 67% of streaming (extrapolated), against about 4.8 tok/s with FP8-protected attention at the 0.47 efficiency the project's own gate uses; MiMo-Pro reads 36.6 GiB per token, 1.45-1.68 tok/s at the 57-66 GB/s the GGUF stacks have achieved.

## Conclusion

The task framing assumes the lever to pull is deeper expert pruning, but the numbers say the opposite. The best method is the one that forces the least pruning: pick the model whose bytes can be removed bit-exactly (V4.1's Engram off-pool, FP4 experts deleted whole), cap deletion at the measured 50% ceiling with the strongest criterion and the broadest calibration, and let a trellis quantizer absorb the rest, because 2.9-3.0 bpw is where quantization and pruning have equal marginal cost. A 66.7% bit-exact variant remains the fallback if the custom 2.9 bpw kernel proves too costly to build, and progressive re-ranking is the instrument for finding out, in measured gates, whether a 384-expert model has any cliff left to move; nothing in the literature says it does, and a two-pass experiment answers it. The two other candidates fail for different reasons: GLM-5.3-753B because 40B active parameters and a text-only architecture meet both cliffs at once, and MiMo-Pro because 494 GiB of QAT-trained 4-bit experts leave no room to maneuver.

The sharpest open questions are all cheap to answer relative to their value. A 192-expert V4.1 mask built with the balanced corpus and per-modality ranking either beats the +14.7% LibertAI baseline or it does not, and that measurement decides whether the premise holds. A 2-hour microbenchmark of a 1-layer dequant plus grouped-GEMM forward and dgrad on Thor decides whether block-wise healing is a weekend job. And a k2 scan on the existing GLM-5.3-Flash server decides whether top-k reduction is free. The deeper lesson from the correction of earlier findings is that most of the apparent upside in the literature (the 2-bit-plus-50% collapse that was an unpruned naive-INT2 model, the ExFold healing that was a dynamic pool, the +20-25 point mixed-precision gain that was against a 2.0-bit baseline) dissolved on inspection. What survives is smaller and more mechanical: delete exactly, quantize smartly, heal the router, and measure the tails, since perplexity hides them.
